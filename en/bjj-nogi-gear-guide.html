    No-Gi vs Gi Training: Gear Differences

    No-gi training eliminates the gi collar and sleeve grips, forcing you to rely on body positioning and grip fighting. No-gi gear must be form-fitting to prevent grip holds on loose fabric. Rash guards, compression shorts, and spats are the standard no-gi uniform.

    Rash Guards: What You Need

    Rashguards are compression shirts worn for no-gi training and grappling. They provide skin protection against mat burn, reduce friction between bodies, and help regulate body temperature. Look for materials with high nylon and spandex content for durability. Fitted (not loose) design is critical.

    Compression Shorts: Fit and Function

    Compression shorts provide support and protection during no-gi training. They should be form-fitting to prevent grip opportunities. Most athletes wear compression shorts under their no-gi shorts (double-layer protection). Look for moisture-wicking materials and reinforced crotch stitching.

    Spats: Thigh and Leg Protection

    Spats are tight-fitting leg coverings that protect your legs during no-gi rolling. They prevent direct friction with the mat and provide hip and thigh protection. Many athletes wear spats under their shorts for additional protection. Choose spats with reinforced knee and hip areas.

    No-Gi Outfit Setup

    Standard no-gi attire: compression shorts or spats (under), no-gi shorts (over), rash guard (top). Some athletes add a second layer of spats under their shorts for extra grip prevention. Wear nothing metal that could cut or scratch your training partner.

    Sizing No-Gi Gear

    No-gi gear should fit snugly — not tight enough to restrict movement, but form-fitting enough to prevent grip opportunities. Measure your chest, waist, and inseam. If between sizes, size down rather than up — no-gi gear loses elasticity over time and washing.

    How long does it take to learn Nogi Gear Guide?

    Most practitioners develop functional competency with Nogi Gear Guide within 3–6 months of consistent drilling. Mastery — the ability to execute reliably in live rolling against resisting opponents — typically takes 1–2 years.

    Is Nogi Gear Guide effective for beginners?

    More Questions

    What's the difference between rashguards and t-shirts for BJJ?

    Rashguards are designed to be tight-fitting and moisture-wicking, preventing chafing and providing a smoother surface for grips. Regular t-shirts can become heavy when wet and may hinder movement or get caught on opponents.

    Are spats really necessary for no-gi BJJ?

    While not strictly mandatory, spats offer significant benefits. They protect your skin from mat burn, provide compression for muscle support, and can prevent your opponent's gi pants (if they are wearing them in a hybrid setting) from getting a good grip.

    What kind of shorts are best for no-gi BJJ?

    Look for shorts made of durable, quick-drying material with a good range of motion, often referred to as 'MMA shorts' or 'grappling shorts.' Avoid loose-fitting gym shorts or board shorts with excessive pockets or drawstrings that can be grabbed.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: What kind of shorts are best for No-Gi BJJ so they don't get grabbed easily by my opponent?

    Opt for board shorts or wrestling shorts with a smooth, slick material and no external pockets or dangling strings. This design minimizes grip points, allowing your limbs to slide away more effectively when an opponent attempts to secure a hold.

    Q: Why do I need a rashguard for No-Gi BJJ and what's the best way to wear it to prevent chafing?

    A rashguard creates a smooth, friction-reducing layer between your skin and your opponent's body, minimizing mat burn and chafing, especially during positional scrambles. Wearing a properly fitted rashguard that is snug but not constricting will ensure it stays in place and effectively protects your skin.

    Q: Are spats really necessary for No-Gi BJJ, or can I just wear regular athletic leggings?

    Spats are designed with durable, flexible fabric that offers compression and protection against mat burns and abrasions, unlike many regular athletic leggings. Their specific construction helps maintain leg control by providing a consistent surface for grips and preventing slippage during grappling exchanges.

No-Gi BJJ Gear Guide: Rashguards, Shorts, Spats

No-gi BJJ gear: rashguards, compression shorts, spats, and complete no-gi equipment guide.

Contents

    No-Gi vs Gi Training: Gear Differences

    No-gi training eliminates the gi collar and sleeve grips, forcing you to rely on body positioning and grip fighting. No-gi gear must be form-fitting to prevent grip holds on loose fabric. Rash guards, compression shorts, and spats are the standard no-gi uniform.

    Rash Guards: What You Need

    Rashguards are compression shirts worn for no-gi training and grappling. They provide skin protection against mat burn, reduce friction between bodies, and help regulate body temperature. Look for materials with high nylon and spandex content for durability. Fitted (not loose) design is critical.

    Compression Shorts: Fit and Function

    Compression shorts provide support and protection during no-gi training. They should be form-fitting to prevent grip opportunities. Most athletes wear compression shorts under their no-gi shorts (double-layer protection). Look for moisture-wicking materials and reinforced crotch stitching.

    Spats: Thigh and Leg Protection

    Spats are tight-fitting leg coverings that protect your legs during no-gi rolling. They prevent direct friction with the mat and provide hip and thigh protection. Many athletes wear spats under their shorts for additional protection. Choose spats with reinforced knee and hip areas.

    No-Gi Outfit Setup

    Standard no-gi attire: compression shorts or spats (under), no-gi shorts (over), rash guard (top). Some athletes add a second layer of spats under their shorts for extra grip prevention. Wear nothing metal that could cut or scratch your training partner.

    Sizing No-Gi Gear

    No-gi gear should fit snugly — not tight enough to restrict movement, but form-fitting enough to prevent grip opportunities. Measure your chest, waist, and inseam. If between sizes, size down rather than up — no-gi gear loses elasticity over time and washing.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How long does it take to learn Nogi Gear Guide?

    Most practitioners develop functional competency with Nogi Gear Guide within 3–6 months of consistent drilling. Mastery — the ability to execute reliably in live rolling against resisting opponents — typically takes 1–2 years.

    Is Nogi Gear Guide effective for beginners?

    Yes. Nogi Gear Guide is part of the core BJJ curriculum and taught at all belt levels. Beginners should focus on the fundamental mechanics and concepts before refining advanced entries.

    How often should I drill Nogi Gear Guide?

    3–5 times per week is ideal for rapid skill acquisition. Even 10 focused repetitions per session compounds over time — consistency matters more than volume.

    What positions connect to Nogi Gear Guide?

    BJJ is a linked system. Nogi Gear Guide flows naturally to and from related positions. Study transitions in both directions to build a complete positional game.

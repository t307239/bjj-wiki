    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my opponent keep escaping my Butterfly Guard to Back Take when I try to hook their leg?

    This often happens because your hip is not actively driving into their hip to create a stiff frame, which allows them to shrimp away. Ensure your hooking foot's heel is pulled towards your glute and your knee is pointed towards their hip to maintain consistent pressure and prevent their escape.

    Q: How can I effectively transition to the back from Butterfly Guard when my opponent is posturing up really high?

    When they posture up, you need to use their upward momentum against them by extending your legs to create space and then immediately shooting your hips forward and upward. Simultaneously, scoop their nearest arm and pull it across their body to secure your grip for the back take.

    Q: What's the best way to maintain control of the back during a Butterfly Guard to Back Take against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on securing your hooks deeply by driving your heels into their hips and squeezing your knees together to prevent them from creating space. Maintain constant pressure by keeping your chest tight to their back and driving your hips forward, making it difficult for them to turn or escape.

    More Questions

    How do I prevent my opponent from passing my butterfly guard when I try to take their back?

    Maintaining tight hip control and actively using your hooks to drive into your opponent's legs is crucial. If they try to posture up, use your butterfly hooks to elevate their hips and prevent them from stabilizing.

    What are common mistakes when transitioning from butterfly guard to the back?

    A common mistake is not committing to the hip bump or shrimp to create the necessary space to get your leg over. Another is losing your grips on their legs or pants, which allows them to escape.

    How do I finish the back take and secure the position if my opponent tries to roll out?

    As you secure the back, immediately work to establish your seatbelt grip and bring your chest to their back. If they try to roll, adjust your body to maintain control and continue to drive your hooks in to prevent them from escaping.

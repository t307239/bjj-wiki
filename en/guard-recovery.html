Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

Q: Why do I struggle to keep my opponent's arm controlled when I'm trying to do an armbar from guard in BJJ?

When your opponent's arm is slipping out, it's often because your hips aren't sufficiently tight to their shoulder. Ensure your hips are elevated and pressing into their shoulder socket, creating a strong fulcrum, and that your legs are actively squeezing to maintain that pressure and prevent them from posturing up.

Q: How can I effectively prevent my training partner from passing my guard when they are much bigger and stronger than me in BJJ?

To counter a larger opponent's guard pass, focus on maintaining a strong, tight Closed Guard by keeping your heels dug into their hips and your knees together, preventing them from creating space to drive forward. Utilize your legs to actively shrimp and create angles, breaking their posture by pulling their head down and keeping their weight distributed away from your center line.

Q: What is the most important biomechanical principle to understand for a proper hip escape (shrimping) in BJJ to create space?

The key to an effective Hip Escape is to first drive your knee of the side you are escaping towards the mat, creating a pivot point. Simultaneously, extend your opposite leg and push off the mat with your foot, allowing your hips to drive away perpendicular to your opponent's base, creating the necessary space.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How do I prevent my opponent from reguarding when I attempt a knee cut pass?

    Maintain constant pressure and keep your chest tight to their hip. As you cut your knee, use your free leg to 'post' on their far hip or thigh, preventing them from bridging or shrimping effectively.

    What is the most common mistake people make with the knee cut pass?

    A common mistake is not cutting the knee deep enough, allowing the opponent to maintain their guard structure. You need to get your knee almost parallel to their hip line to effectively break their base.

    How can I finish the knee cut pass if my opponent is strong and resists heavily?

    Focus on establishing a strong cross-face and underhook. Once you have those, drive your shoulder into their neck and use your hips to generate power, forcing them to give way.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why do I feel like my knee is going to get stuck or injured when I try to do the BJJ Knee Cut Pass?

    This often happens when you drive your knee too far forward and inward, creating a direct shearing force on your knee joint. Instead, keep your cutting knee slightly behind your hip and aim to drive your hip and shoulder into your opponent's thigh, using your shin as a wedge to control their leg.

    Q: How can I effectively use my hips and weight distribution during the BJJ Knee Cut Pass to prevent my opponent from reguarding?

    As you cut your knee, actively drive your hips down and forward, creating a strong base and preventing your opponent from bridging or shrimping. Your chest should be pressed firmly against their hip line, and your weight should be distributed over their leg, not just on your knee.

    Q: My opponent's legs seem too wide for me to get my knee inside for the BJJ Knee Cut Pass, what adjustments can I make?

    If their legs are wide, focus on initially collapsing one of their legs by stepping your cutting foot slightly outside their hip and driving your shoulder into their thigh, forcing them to bring their legs closer together. Once they adjust, you can then re-engage to cut your knee inside.

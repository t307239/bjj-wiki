    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck get strained when I transition from an armbar attempt to a triangle choke in BJJ flow drills?

    Neck strain often occurs when you try to force your head through the opponent's armpit to establish the triangle. Instead, focus on keeping your head tight to their shoulder and using your hips to drive their arm across your body, creating space for your head to naturally follow.

    Q: How can I effectively transition from an armbar attempt to a triangle choke when my training partner is much larger than me in BJJ flow drills?

    Against a larger opponent, prioritize controlling their posture by clamping your legs high on their back and squeezing your knees together. This leverage allows you to use their weight against them to break their grip on your arm and create the necessary angle to lock up the triangle.

    Q: What are the key body positioning adjustments I need to make to smoothly flow from an armbar setup to a triangle choke without giving up the submission in BJJ flow drills?

    The critical adjustment is to prevent your opponent from posturing up by keeping your hips close and your shin across their shoulder as you transition. As you fall back for the triangle, ensure your 'locking' leg's heel is firmly planted on your own knee to maintain tightness and prevent them from escaping.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How do I transition smoothly from an armbar attempt to a triangle choke?

    The key is to maintain pressure and control as you abandon the armbar. As your opponent defends the armbar by posturing up or pulling their arm out, use that momentum to swing your leg over their head and establish the triangle.

    What are common mistakes when flowing from armbar to triangle?

    A frequent error is losing hip control or giving up too much space when the armbar is defended. This allows the opponent to escape or create a better defensive position, so always focus on keeping your hips tight and your legs engaged.

    When is it better to commit to the armbar versus transitioning to the triangle?

    Commit to the armbar if you have a strong grip, good leverage, and the opponent is unable to defend effectively. If they start to posture up or pull their arm out in a way that opens up their neck and head, that's your cue to transition to the triangle.

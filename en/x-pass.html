X-Pass: The Essential BJJ Guard Pass for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The X-Pass is a dynamic guard pass initiated from an open guard position, aiming to break down the opponent's guard and secure Side Control. It's designed to bypass their legs and establish immediate positional dominance.

White belts often struggle with the X-Pass due to a lack of understanding of body mechanics, leading to ineffective pressure and easily defended attempts. They tend to rely on brute force rather than precise movements.

The core mechanical principle is to use your hips to create a base and leverage, driving your shoulder into their hip to break their structure and allow your leg to snake through.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Starting from a standing or kneeling position facing an opponent in open guard, secure a strong collar grip with your right hand, pulling their lapel towards your right hip. Simultaneously, establish a deep pant grip on their left pant leg, just above the knee.
    2. Drive your left knee towards the mat, creating a wide, stable base with your feet hip-width apart. Your left hip should be low, angled towards your opponent's left hip.
    3. With your right collar grip, pull their upper body towards your right side, disrupting their balance. Your left hand grips their left pant leg at the knee or thigh.
    4. Initiate a powerful hip drive, pushing your hips forward and upward, while simultaneously driving your right shoulder into their left hip pocket. Your weight should be transferring onto your right shoulder and hip.
    5. As your shoulder pressure breaks their hip structure, extend your right leg forward, sliding it between their legs. Your right knee should aim to be near their left hip bone.
    6. Immediately follow by driving your left knee towards the mat on the opposite side of their legs, creating a base for your left leg to follow and secure control.
    7. Transition to side control by driving your hips forward and securing a cross-face grip with your left hand, while your right arm underhooks their far armpit. Keep your chest tight to their body.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly driving your head forward instead of your shoulder into the opponent's hip can cause your neck to hyperextend, risking cervical spine strain or disc injury. The correct movement is a shoulder-to-hip connection.
    • Overextending your knee when trying to snake your leg through can lead to a knee ligament sprain (ACL/MCL) if your opponent applies a sudden twist or counter. Keep your knee bent and controlled as it enters.
    • Failing to establish a stable base with your hips and legs can result in you falling forward onto your face or hands, risking wrist fractures or facial contusions. Maintain a wide, low base throughout the movement.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip drive and shoulder-to-hip connection with no opponent. Focus on creating a strong base and driving motion. (10 reps)
    2. Solo drill: Practice the leg snake motion without an opponent, focusing on smooth extension and placement. (10 reps)
    3. Partner drill (no resistance): Opponent holds a passive guard. Practice the entire X-Pass sequence, focusing on grip accuracy and body positioning. (5 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (25% resistance): Opponent offers light resistance to your initial grips and hip drive. Focus on breaking their structure. (5 reps per side)
    5. Partner drill (50% resistance): Opponent actively tries to maintain their guard and resist your hip drive. Focus on maintaining pressure and completing the pass. (5 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Attempt the X-Pass during live rolling against a resisting opponent, focusing on timing and execution. (3-5 attempts per round)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is attempting to establish an open guard (e.g., spider guard, de la riva guard) and their hips are slightly away from you.
    • When you have secured a strong collar and pant grip that allows you to break their posture and off-balance them.
    • When you have established a solid base and can effectively drive your weight forward without compromising your own structure.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard retention: If the X-Pass is initiated, the opponent can immediately re-establish their guard by bringing their hips back and creating frames with their forearms, preventing the leg from entering.
    • Hip escape/Shrimping: The opponent can shrimp their hips away to create space, allowing them to re-establish their guard or transition to a different guard.
    • Knee shield: The opponent can place their knee between your hips and chest, creating a strong frame that prevents you from driving your shoulder through and passing.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to X-Pass?

    Your knee might hurt because you are not maintaining a bent and controlled leg as it snakes through. This can lead to hyperextension or a twisting force if your opponent resists. Ensure your knee remains slightly bent and your shin stays parallel to the mat as it enters, driving your hips forward to create space rather than forcing the leg through.

    Q: How do I X-Pass against a much bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are crucial. Focus on securing the deepest possible collar and pant grips to control their posture. Instead of a direct, forceful hip drive, use a more controlled, explosive push off your back leg, aiming to off-balance them and create a small window for your leg to pass. Use your shoulder to their hip pocket to break their base.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt an X-Pass instead of a knee-slice?

    The X-Pass is best attempted when your opponent's legs are extended or they are attempting to create distance with an open guard. It excels at breaking down their structure when they are trying to use their feet on your hips. The knee-slice pass is generally more effective when the opponent's legs are closer and you can pin one leg, driving your knee across their body.

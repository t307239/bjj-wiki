    More Questions

    Why is shrimping important in BJJ?

    Shrimping is a fundamental movement that creates space between you and your opponent. This space is crucial for escaping bad positions, setting up sweeps, and transitioning to more dominant positions.

    How do I generate power when shrimping?

    Power comes from your hips and core. Drive your hips away from your opponent by pushing off your feet and contracting your abdominal muscles, creating a fluid, explosive motion.

    What if I can't get enough distance when shrimping?

    Ensure your knees are bent and your feet are flat on the mat to get good leverage. Try to push off your opponent's hips or legs if they are too close, and focus on a strong hip drive rather than just pushing with your arms.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why do my hips feel stuck and I can't effectively shrimp out from underneath someone in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu?

    Your hips are likely stuck because you're not creating enough space by driving your knees towards your chest and simultaneously extending your hips away from your opponent. Ensure you're lifting your hips off the mat by engaging your glutes and core, then pushing your feet away while keeping your knees bent and tucked.

    Q: How can I use the shrimping technique to create space when a heavier opponent is completely crushing me on the bottom in BJJ?

    To shrimp effectively against a heavier opponent, focus on creating a strong frame with your forearms against their hips or shoulders to prevent them from sinking their weight further. Then, explosively drive your hips away by extending one leg and tucking the other knee towards your chest, using the momentum generated to create distance.

    Q: What is the correct foot placement and hip movement to make my shrimping technique more powerful and efficient in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu?

    Place your feet hip-width apart with your knees bent, then independently lift your hips by driving through your feet and simultaneously tucking one knee towards your chest while extending the opposite leg. This creates a powerful, asymmetrical push-off that propels your hips laterally away from pressure.

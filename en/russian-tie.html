The Russian Tie: A Biomechanically Precise BJJ Takedown for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Russian Tie is typically initiated from a collar-and-sleeve grip exchange or when an opponent attempts to tie your collar. It aims to break their posture and create an angle for a takedown.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of weight transfer and leverage, leading to lunging or ineffective grips.

The key mechanical insight is using your opponent's forward momentum against them by creating a strong, stable structure with your body and controlling their head and arm.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a collar grip with your right hand on the opponent's left collar, thumb inside. Your left hand grips their right sleeve near the elbow.
    2. Step your left foot slightly forward, aligning your left shoulder with their right hip, creating a 45-degree angle to their center of mass.
    3. With your right hand, pull their collar down and slightly across their body, while simultaneously using your left forearm to push their right elbow back and away from their body.
    4. Shift your weight onto your left foot, driving your hips back and down, creating a lever with your left leg as the fulcrum.
    5. As they resist or step forward, drive your right shoulder into their chest, specifically targeting the sternum area, and continue to pull their collar down.
    6. Simultaneously, extend your left arm to further push their right elbow away, breaking their base and posture.
    7. Execute a hip bump by driving your left hip forward and upward, while simultaneously stepping your right foot across their body and rotating your hips, guiding them to the mat.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Lunging forward with straight arms: This can hyperextend your elbow or shoulder joint, leading to ligament tears.

    Correct: Maintain a slight bend in your elbows and use your body weight to drive, not just arm strength.

    • Gripping too loosely on the collar: This allows the opponent to easily escape the grip and counter, potentially causing a wrist sprain.

    Correct: Secure a firm grip with your thumb inside the collar, creating a solid anchor point.

    • Not creating a sufficient angle: Trying to execute the tie straight on will result in a stalemate or you being pushed over.

    Correct: Step your lead foot to create a 45-degree angle, aligning your shoulder with their hip for leverage.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo grip and angle practice: Stand in front of a dummy or a sturdy post, practicing the collar and sleeve grips and the initial 45-degree body angle. (50 reps)
    2. Partnered grip and frame drill (0% resistance): With a partner, practice the grips and the initial pull/push mechanics without any movement or resistance. Focus on the feeling of the frame. (20 reps)
    3. Slow-motion execution (25% resistance): Execute the entire Russian Tie motion with a partner who offers minimal, controlled resistance, focusing on each biomechanical step. (10 reps per side)
    4. Controlled entry and hip bump (50% resistance): Practice entering the Russian Tie and executing the hip bump, with your partner gently falling to the mat. Focus on weight transfer. (10 reps per side)
    5. Takedown integration (75% resistance): Work with a partner who provides more realistic resistance, focusing on completing the takedown and maintaining control. (5 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling application (90% resistance): Attempt the Russian Tie during sparring, focusing on timing and execution without excessive force. (5 attempts per round)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is posturing up slightly after you've attempted a collar grip.
    • When an opponent reaches for your collar, creating an opening for a tie-up.
    • When you have a strong collar grip and are looking to break their posture to set up a takedown.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Sprawl and circle out: If the opponent initiates the tie and you feel them driving in, immediately sprawl your hips back and circle your hips to the side away from their drive, breaking the connection.
    • Grip release and underhook: As they pull your collar, quickly release your grip and attempt to secure an underhook on their near arm, preventing them from establishing leverage.
    • Stiff arm and step back: If they are pulling your collar strongly, stiff arm their pulling arm with your opposite arm and take a large step backward to create distance and break their posture control.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Russian Tie?

    Wrist pain during the Russian Tie often stems from gripping too tightly with your fingers extended and not using your forearm as a brace. When your opponent pulls, this puts excessive torque on your wrist. Ensure you have a firm grip with your thumb inside the collar and keep a slight bend in your elbow, using your forearm to absorb some of the pulling force.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, the Russian Tie relies even more heavily on perfect timing and biomechanics rather than pure strength. Focus on their weight distribution; as they step forward or shift their weight, that's your moment to initiate. Use your hips and core to create the leverage, pulling their head down and across while driving your shoulder into their chest at a 45-degree angle.

    Q: The ideal time to initiate the Russian Tie is when your opponent is momentarily off-balance or when they are attempting to establish a collar grip on you. Look for opportunities when they are slightly reaching forward or when their posture is not fully squared. This allows you to exploit their forward momentum and create the necessary angle for the takedown.

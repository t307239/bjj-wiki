The Ultimate Russian Tie Takedown Guide for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Russian Tie is a standing control position initiated from a collar tie, aiming to off-balance and take down an opponent. White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of leverage and weight distribution.

The key mechanical insight is using your opponent's forward momentum against them, creating a rotational force through precise body positioning and grip placement.

It's a fundamental setup that bridges the gap between striking and grappling, establishing superior control before initiating the takedown.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip: Right hand grabs opponent's left collar, left hand grabs opponent's right wrist.
    2. Stance: Feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, hips squared or slightly turned towards opponent's left.
    3. Posture: Maintain a strong upright posture, head up, looking at opponent's face.
    4. Weight Shift: Begin to shift your weight onto your lead (left) leg, driving your hips slightly forward and to your left.
    5. Arm Action: Pull opponent's collar down and slightly across their body with your right arm, while simultaneously using your left arm to control their wrist and guide their arm.
    6. Hip Rotation: Externally rotate your left hip and drive it into the opponent's right hip, creating a wedge.
    7. Push/Pull: Apply a controlled inward pull with your right arm and a slight outward push with your left arm, forcing them to pivot.
    8. Takedown Execution: As they pivot, step your right foot behind your left and drive through their center of mass, finishing with a sweep of your left leg if needed.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Neck Crank: Grabbing the back of the head and pulling down forcefully can cause hyperextension of the cervical spine. The correct grip is on the collar, not the head.
    • Knee Valgus Collapse: Stepping in too wide and driving with a straight leg can cause the knee to collapse inwards, damaging the MCL. Keep knees slightly bent and drive with hip power.
    • Low Back Strain: Hunching over with a rounded back and attempting to lift with the legs strains the lumbar spine. Maintain an upright posture and use hip drive, not back lift.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Grip & Posture Drill: Practice establishing the collar and wrist grip with correct posture for 30 seconds, 10 repetitions.
    2. Shadow Russian Tie Movement: Without a partner, practice the hip rotation and weight shift, simulating the pull and push action for 1 minute, 5 repetitions.
    3. Light Resistance Grip & Pull: With a partner standing, practice establishing the grips and applying the initial pull/push, focusing on control, 25% resistance, 10 reps per side.
    4. Controlled Pivot Drill: Partner resists slightly, allowing you to practice the pivot and hip drive, 50% resistance, 5 reps per side.
    5. Takedown Setup Drill: Practice initiating the Russian Tie and completing the takedown with minimal resistance, focusing on smooth transitions, 75% resistance, 3 reps per side.
    6. Live Takedown Situational Rolling: Start from a standing position, allowing attempts at the Russian Tie and other takedowns, 90% resistance, 5 minutes.

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is posturing up from a closed guard or half guard and you can establish a collar tie.
    • When your opponent attempts a collar tie on you, creating an opportunity to counter-grip and initiate the Russian Tie.
    • When your opponent is leaning forward slightly, making them susceptible to off-balancing.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Sprawl and Hip Escape: If the opponent initiates the Russian Tie and drives forward, sprawl your hips back and drive your hips to the side to break their base and escape the tie.
    • Underhook and Circle Out: Secure an underhook on the side of the tie and circle your hips out, using the underhook to prevent them from driving forward and creating space.
    • Grip Break and Stance Adjustment: Focus on breaking the collar grip by pulling your collar down and away, then adjust your stance to a wider, more stable base to resist the pull.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to Russian Tie?

    Wrist pain during the Russian Tie often stems from gripping the opponent's wrist too tightly or with an improper angle. Ensure your left hand is controlling the opponent's wrist from the outside, with your palm facing their palm, and avoid extending your wrist beyond a neutral position. Focus on using your forearm and shoulder to control their arm, not just your wrist.

    Q: How can I use the Russian Tie effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, the Russian Tie relies even more heavily on leverage and timing. Instead of trying to overpower them, focus on their forward momentum. Use your body weight to create angles, driving your hips into their side as you pull their collar. Your goal is to get them off balance and rotating, rather than trying to lift them.

    Q: When is the best moment to transition from the Russian Tie to a takedown?

    The optimal moment to transition is when you feel your opponent begin to pivot or become unstable from your pull and hip drive. Look for their weight to shift onto one leg, or for them to take a step to regain balance. This is your cue to commit to the takedown, stepping your feet to secure your base and drive through their center.

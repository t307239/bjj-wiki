The Russian Tie: A White Belt's Guide to BJJ Takedowns

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Russian Tie is a standing tie-up technique used to control an opponent's posture and initiate a takedown, typically from a collar-and-sleeve grip.

Many white belts struggle because they rely on brute strength rather than leverage, leading to lost balance and ineffective attempts.

The key is to off-balance your opponent by creating a strong angled pull and stepping in to disrupt their base, not by muscling them over.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a collar grip with your right hand on the opponent's left lapel, just below the collarbone. Your left hand grips their right sleeve, near the elbow.
    2. Step your left foot slightly forward and to the outside of the opponent's right foot, creating a 45-degree angle between your bodies.
    3. Simultaneously, pull down on the collar grip with your right hand and pull forward on the sleeve grip with your left hand, creating a rotational force.
    4. Lower your hips by bending your knees, aligning your pelvis closer to the opponent's hips.
    5. Shift your weight onto your lead (left) foot, driving your hips forward and slightly to your right.
    6. Use the combined pull and your forward hip drive to break the opponent's posture and off-balance them to their right side.
    7. Step your right foot in to establish a dominant position, often transitioning into a double-leg takedown or controlling their guard.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly extending your arms to "pull" the opponent: This can hyperextend your elbows, damaging the ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) and causing pain.
    • Standing too upright and trying to "muscle" the opponent over: This puts excessive strain on your lower back, risking disc compression or a pulled muscle.
    • Stepping directly forward instead of at an angle: This squares you up, making you vulnerable to sweeps and compromising your ability to create leverage, potentially leading to knee valgus strain.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the grip establishment, footwork angle, and hip movement without a partner. 10 reps each side.
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Partner stands passively. Focus on grip accuracy, angle, and the pulling motion. 10 reps each side.
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance. Focus on generating the pull and hip drive to create a slight off-balance. 5 reps each side.
    4. Dynamic partner drill: Partner resists slightly more, allowing you to feel the off-balance. Focus on the step-in and follow-through. 5 reps each side.
    5. Controlled sparring (0-25% resistance): Attempt the Russian Tie in a live situation, focusing on proper execution without worrying about scoring. 5 attempts.
    6. Live rolling (50-75% resistance): Integrate the Russian Tie into your rolling, aiming for successful takedowns while maintaining control. 5 attempts.

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is reaching for your collar or sleeves, creating an opening for grip fighting.
    • When you have established a strong collar grip and can control their head position.
    • When you are standing slightly off-balance and need to quickly initiate a takedown to regain control.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape and Angle Change: If the opponent attempts the Russian Tie, immediately step your hips away to your right and pivot your body to your left, breaking the angle of their pull.
    • Grip Break and Posture Up: As they pull, use your free hand to pummel inside their grip on your collar and simultaneously drive your head up, straightening your posture.
    • Whizzer (Overhook): If they successfully initiate the pull, quickly establish an overhook on their pulling arm (your right arm for them) and use it to resist their forward pressure and potentially counter.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to do the Russian Tie?

    Shoulder pain often stems from an improper collar grip. Instead of gripping the lapel with your palm facing inward, ensure your palm faces outward and grip the fabric firmly at the sternum. Also, avoid reaching too far and exposing your shoulder joint; keep your elbow tucked and pull with your lats and back, not just your arm.

    Q: How do I perform the Russian Tie effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paraMount. Focus on the angle of attack: step in at a sharp 45-degree angle to their side. Use your hips to drive forward and under their center of gravity, rather than trying to pull them straight down. Lowering your base and using your entire body's momentum is more effective than arm strength.

    Q: When is the best time to use the Russian Tie in BJJ?

    The Russian Tie is best utilized when you have a secure grip on the opponent's collar and sleeve, and they are either reaching for you or have their posture slightly compromised. It's ideal for initiating a takedown early in a match or when you feel a need to change the pace and pressure.

The Elevator Sweep is a fundamental sweep initiated from Closed Guard, aiming to lift your opponent and transition to a superior position.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength rather than leverage, leading to stalemates or injury.

The key to the Elevator Sweep is generating upward momentum through hip extension and a precise rotation of your torso.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a strong two-on-one grip on your opponent's right sleeve with your left hand, and their left sleeve with your right hand. Your elbows should be tucked to your sides.
    2. Secure a "seatbelt" grip on the back of your opponent's neck with your right hand, palm facing their head.
    3. Simultaneously, place your left foot firmly on your opponent's right hip, with your knee pointing slightly upwards.
    4. Place your right foot on your opponent's left hip, creating a stable base and preventing them from posturing up.
    5. Begin to drive your hips upwards towards the ceiling, initiating a "biscuit" or hip extension movement.
    6. As your hips lift, use your left arm (on the sleeve grip) to pull their right arm across your body, creating a fulcrum.
    7. Simultaneously, use your right arm (seatbelt grip) to pull their head down and slightly towards your left shoulder.
    8. Initiate a powerful rotational movement of your torso to your left, bringing your right knee towards your opponent's right hip as you elevate.
    9. Drive your hips and chest upwards, using the combined leverage of your grips and hip extension to lift your opponent off the mat.
    10. As your opponent becomes airborne, immediately drive your right knee into their hip and transition to a top position like side control or mount.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Elbow Extension: Extending your elbows forcefully to pull sleeves can strain or tear the ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) in your elbow, especially when facing resistance.
    • Correct: Maintain a slight bend in your elbows and use your entire body to generate the pulling motion, focusing on scapular retraction and hip drive.
    • Over-Rotation of the Spine: Attempting to lift your opponent by violently twisting your lower back without proper hip engagement can lead to lumbar disc herniation or facet joint injury.
    • Correct: Initiate the rotation from your hips and core, ensuring your spine remains relatively neutral as you drive upwards.
    • Knee to Face Impact: Driving your knee forcefully into your opponent's face while sweeping can cause orbital bone fractures or nasal damage, and can also injure your own knee if misaligned.
    • Correct: Aim to drive your knee into your opponent's hip or rib cage to create leverage, and keep your knee aligned with your hip to protect it.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Extension Drill: Practice the hip extension movement (point 5) without any partner, focusing on generating upward force. 20 reps.
    2. Solo Grip and Hip Drill: Practice the sleeve and seatbelt grips (points 1 & 2) and the hip lift (point 5) without a partner. 20 reps.
    3. Partnered Grip and Hip Lift (No Pressure): With a passive partner, practice the grips and hip lift. They remain still. 50% resistance. 10 reps.
    4. Controlled Sweep Drill (Passive Partner): With a passive partner, execute the full Elevator Sweep. They offer minimal resistance, allowing you to focus on mechanics. 75% resistance. 10 reps per side.
    5. Light Resistance Sweep Drill: Drill with a partner who offers light, predictable resistance. Focus on overcoming their initial defense. 90% resistance. 10 reps.
    6. Live Rolling Application: Attempt the Elevator Sweep during live rolling with a resisting opponent. Focus on timing and setup. 100% resistance. 3-5 attempts per round.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is postured up slightly in your closed guard, creating a gap.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard by pressuring forward with their weight.
    • When your opponent has a deep grip on your collar or sleeves, which you can then break and use for leverage.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The "Stand Up" Counter: If your opponent feels the sweep coming and attempts to stand up by releasing grips and straightening their legs, immediately release your grips and stand up yourself, creating a scramble.
    • The "Stack Pass" Counter: If your opponent anticipates the sweep and tries to "stack" you by driving their weight forward and down, keep your hips tight to their body and attempt to shrimp out to the side, creating space to reset or transition.
    • The "Grip Release and Base" Counter: If your opponent is able to maintain their base and resist the lift, immediately release your grips, bring your knees to your chest, and establish a solid defensive base to prevent them from passing.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try the Elevator Sweep?

    This pain typically arises from attempting to lift your opponent solely with your back muscles instead of your hips and core. When you arch your lower back excessively without proper hip extension, you place immense strain on the lumbar spine. Focus on initiating the upward movement by driving your hips towards the ceiling, engaging your glutes and hamstrings, which provides a much stronger and safer power source.

    Q: How can I use the Elevator Sweep against a much larger and heavier opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing become paraMount. You must focus on breaking their posture and creating a strong fulcrum with your grips. Instead of trying to lift their entire weight, aim to unbalance them by pulling their upper body down and across as you elevate your hips. This creates a tipping point, making them fall due to their own momentum, rather than you directly lifting them.

    Q: When is the best moment to attempt the Elevator Sweep from closed guard?

    The optimal time to attempt the Elevator Sweep is when your opponent momentarily loses their posture and becomes slightly unbalanced, often when they are trying to create space to pass or re-establish their base. Look for a slight forward lean or a moment where their weight is not perfectly distributed. Avoid attempting it when they are completely stacked or have a very strong, stable base.

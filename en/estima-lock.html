The Estima Lock is a leg lock submission initiated from a position where your opponent's leg is trapped between your own legs, typically from a half-guard or deep-half-guard scenario. It targets the knee joint for a submission.

White belts often struggle because they rush the setup or apply pressure incorrectly, leading to inefficient mechanics or injury. Understanding the precise angles and weight transfers is crucial for success and safety.

The core principle is to create a strong fulcrum with your hips and control your opponent's knee rotation, while simultaneously isolating their foot and ankle with your arm to apply rotational pressure.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a deep-half-guard, secure your opponent's right leg with your left arm, hooking behind their knee. Your left forearm should be flush against the back of their thigh.
    2. Place your right foot on their left hip, creating a frame and preventing them from easily posturing up or stacking. Your right knee should point towards the ceiling.
    3. With your left hand, grip your own right shin, creating a secure 'figure-four' or 'S' grip around their trapped leg. Ensure your left elbow is tight to your body.
    4. Shift your hips to your left, lowering your base and creating a slight angle away from your opponent. Your hips should be perpendicular to their trapped leg.
    5. Drive your right knee slightly forward, aiming it towards their left hip. This helps to control their hip rotation.
    6. With your left hand gripping your right shin, begin to extend your right leg slightly while simultaneously pulling your opponent's foot towards your chest. This action isolates their ankle.
    7. Apply rotational pressure by driving your hips into your opponent and rotating your torso slightly counter-clockwise, while continuing to pull their foot towards your chest. This stresses the knee joint.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying pressure too early before the leg is properly isolated: This can result in hyperextension or torsion of the opponent's knee ligament (ACL, MCL) or twisting of the tibia and fibula, causing severe sprains or fractures. The correct action is to ensure the leg is securely trapped and the ankle isolated before initiating the rotational pressure.
    • Allowing the opponent's hips to remain square or too close: This prevents proper hip angling and fulcrum creation, potentially leading to a knee-on-belly transition or escape. It can also put excessive shear force on the knee. The correct action is to actively angle your hips and create space with your framing leg.
    • Over-extending your own leg or knee: This can lead to injury to your own knee joint (ligament damage) if the opponent escapes or counters forcefully. The correct action is to maintain control of the trapped leg and use hip and torso rotation rather than relying solely on leg extension.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip angling, grip formation, and leg isolation with no opponent. Focus on the feel of the 'figure-four' grip and hip movement. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the entire sequence from deep-half-guard to the Estima Lock finish. Focus on correct body positioning and weight transfer. (25 reps per side)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to the setup, allowing you to practice initiating the sweep and trapping the leg. Focus on maintaining frames and hip control. (10 reps per side)
    4. Controlled Estima Lock drilling: Partner allows the leg to be trapped and the ankle isolated. You execute the lock with very light pressure, allowing them to tap early. (15 reps per side)
    5. Live rolling (0-25% resistance): Attempt the Estima Lock from deep-half-guard. Focus on recognizing the opportunity and executing the setup without seeking the submission aggressively. Partner can defend lightly.
    6. Live rolling (50-75% resistance): Work on integrating the Estima Lock into your game. Your partner will actively defend and attempt to escape, requiring you to maintain your position and mechanics under pressure.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is in deep-half-guard and you have successfully isolated one of their legs between your own.
    • When your opponent attempts a stack pass from half-guard and you can trap their leg as they drive forward.
    • When transitioning from a failed sweep in half-guard and you can secure their leg before they recover their base.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Escape by turning into the attacker: If the opponent is applying the lock, turn your hips towards their attacking leg. This relieves pressure on your knee. Then, use your free leg to push their hip away and create space to stand or recover guard.
    • Straighten your leg and drive forward: Attempt to straighten your trapped leg and drive your hips forward into the attacker. This can disrupt their fulcrum and create an opportunity to escape or transition to a more dominant position.
    • Knee-on-belly or stack defense: If the attacker is isolating your leg, attempt to drive your free leg to their hip and transition to a knee-on-belly position or a stack pass. This forces them to address your immediate threat, potentially releasing the leg lock.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do the Estima Lock?

    Your knee might be hurting because you are not properly isolating the opponent's ankle before applying rotational pressure. This can cause your own knee to hyperextend or twist. Ensure your grip is tight on their foot and ankle, and that your hips are angled correctly to create a stable fulcrum. Only then should you apply the rotational force.

    Q: How can I Estima Lock a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paraMount. Focus on using your hips to create a strong fulcrum, angling them significantly away from their trapped leg. Ensure your 'figure-four' grip is extremely tight on their shin and ankle to prevent them from using their size to muscle out. Drive your hips into them and rotate your torso to apply the lock, rather than relying on brute strength.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to the Estima Lock from half-guard?

    The ideal time to transition to the Estima Lock from half-guard is when your opponent is attempting to pass your guard by driving forward or stacking. As they commit their weight, you can use their momentum to trap their leg between your own. This timing capitalizes on their forward pressure, making the setup more effective and harder for them to defend.

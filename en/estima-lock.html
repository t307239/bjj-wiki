The Estima Lock is a leg lock submission typically initiated from a top-side position, aiming to hyperextend the opponent's knee. White belts often struggle due to a lack of control and understanding of leverage points.

The key is to isolate the leg and create a fulcrum point, using your body to generate torque safely and effectively.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From side control, secure a "seatbelt" grip on the opponent's far arm with your near arm, and your far arm under their far armpit, controlling their torso.
    2. Your near knee drives into their hip crease, preventing them from turning into you, maintaining a perpendicular body angle.
    3. Your far leg slides underneath their hips, aiming to trap their leg between your torso and your thigh.
    4. Secure a "S-grip" or "figure-four" grip on the opponent's trapped leg: your hand grips your own bicep, and your other hand grips their ankle or shin, controlling the foot.
    5. Your hips pivot towards their head, creating a perpendicular angle to their body, while your chest presses into their hip.
    6. Drive your hips forward and slightly upwards, extending your torso while keeping your grip tight on their leg.
    7. Apply pressure by straightening your torso and extending your hips, creating a hyperextension force on the opponent's knee joint.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly "pulling" the leg with your arms: This strains your shoulder and elbow joints, and fails to generate proper leverage. The correct movement is hip extension.
    • Allowing the opponent's knee to bend: This can lead to a "bending" of the knee joint under pressure, risking anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) tears. Maintain control of the ankle/shin to prevent flexion.
    • Not establishing a strong base or hip angle: This allows the opponent to shrimp or bridge out, potentially causing your own knee or ankle to be twisted. Keep your hips perpendicular and your base wide.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip pivot and grip transition on your own leg, focusing on the S-grip and hip movement. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the entire sequence from side control, focusing on achieving the correct body angles and grips without any pressure. (10 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to secure the position and apply a very gentle, controlled "tap" pressure. (5 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to defend, but allows you to isolate the leg and apply controlled pressure. Focus on maintaining your base and hip angle. (5 reps per side)
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner actively defends and applies pressure to escape. Focus on maintaining your grips and hip control as they try to counter. (3 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Attempt the Estima Lock in a controlled rolling scenario, prioritizing technique over submission. Tap early if caught. (Focus on executing 1 attempt)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is turtled and you have established side control.
    • When the opponent attempts to shrimp away from your side control, exposing their leg.
    • When the opponent is trying to bridge and roll, and you can isolate their leg as they move.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Leg defense: The opponent can try to pull their leg out by creating space with their free leg or by bridging their hips. The defender must maintain a tight grip and control the ankle.
    • Hip escape: If the defender fails to establish a strong perpendicular base, the opponent can shrimp their hips away and relieve pressure, or even escape to guard.
    • Re-guarding: If the attacker's base is weak, the opponent can try to bring their free leg back into guard to defend.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do the Estima Lock?

    Your knee is likely hurting because you are trying to force the lock by pulling with your arms, or you are not achieving the correct perpendicular hip angle. The Estima Lock should be applied by extending your hips, creating a hyperextension force on the opponent's knee, not by pulling their leg with your upper body. Ensure your hips are driven forward and your torso is straight.

    Q: How do I Estima Lock against a bigger opponent who has a lot of weight?

    Against a larger opponent, establishing a strong perpendicular base and a tight S-grip is paraMount. Use your body weight to drive into their hip, preventing them from using their size to bridge effectively. Focus on isolating their leg and using your hip extension to create the torque, rather than trying to match their strength with brute force.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to an Estima Lock from side control?

    The ideal time to transition to an Estima Lock from Side Control is when your opponent is attempting to shrimp away from your pressure. As they create space to move their hips, you can slide your leg underneath theirs and secure the trap. Also, if they are turtling, it presents an opportunity to isolate a leg as they try to defend.

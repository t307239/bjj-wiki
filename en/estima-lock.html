The Estima Lock is a submission typically initiated from the 50-50 guard or a similar leg entanglement position. It aims to hyperextend the opponent's knee joint for a tap.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body awareness and an instinct to 'muscle' the submission, leading to poor mechanics and injury risk.

The key insight is using hip elevation and precise weight transfer to create leverage, rather than brute strength, to isolate and attack the knee.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From the 50-50 guard, secure your opponent's leg between your own legs. Your right leg should be inside their right thigh, and your left leg outside their right thigh.
    2. Grip your opponent's right ankle with your left hand, thumb pointing upwards, palm facing your opponent's shin.
    3. With your right hand, grip your own left forearm, creating a strong handle. This is your 'figure-four' grip.
    4. Elevate your hips off the mat by bridging your glutes upwards. Your pelvis should angle towards your opponent's hips.
    5. Drive your right knee towards the mat, creating a fulcrum point on your opponent's upper thigh, just above the knee.
    6. Simultaneously, pull your opponent's ankle towards your chest using your figure-four grip.
    7. Shift your body weight forward, driving your hips into your opponent's thigh while maintaining the ankle pull. This isolates the knee joint.
    8. Continue to drive your hips forward and slightly upwards, creating a controlled extension of the knee. Apply pressure slowly and listen for the tap.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying the Estima Lock by simply pushing the leg away with your feet: This can strain your own knee ligaments (ACL/MCL) as you lack the proper hip engagement to isolate the opponent's knee.
    • Grabbing the foot instead of the ankle with a loose grip: This allows the opponent to escape and can lead to wrist hyperextension injuries in your gripping hand.
    • Jerking the ankle upwards without hip elevation: This puts excessive torsional force on the opponent's knee and can cause meniscus tears or ligament damage.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling (0% resistance): Practice the hip elevation and figure-four grip creation with a training dummy or a willing partner holding still. Focus on smooth hip movement and grip security. 20 repetitions.
    2. Partner drilling, no resistance (0% resistance): With a partner lying on their back, practice entering the 50-50 guard and securing the Estima Lock position without any pressure. Focus on grip accuracy and hip placement. 10 repetitions per side.
    3. Controlled entry and grip (25% resistance): Your partner allows you to establish the 50-50 guard and grip. They provide minimal resistance to your hip elevation and pull, allowing you to feel the mechanics. 5 repetitions per side.
    4. Static control and gentle pressure (50% resistance): Your partner maintains a static position, allowing you to establish the lock and apply very light, controlled extension. They tap immediately to any discomfort. 5 repetitions per side.
    5. Dynamic entry with light defense (75% resistance): Your partner offers light resistance to your entry and attempts minor adjustments. Focus on maintaining your grips and hip control through their movements. 3 repetitions per side.
    6. Live rolling, focus on Estima Lock attempts (90% resistance): During live rolling, actively seek opportunities to enter the 50-50 guard and attempt the Estima Lock. Your partner will defend realistically but will not actively try to counter your specific submission attempt. 5 attempts.

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When you have successfully established the 50-50 guard and have isolated your opponent's leg.
    • When your opponent attempts to stack you from the 50-50 guard, creating an opportunity to elevate your hips.
    • During scrambles where you can secure a leg entanglement that resembles the 50-50 position.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Straightening the attacked leg and 'swimming' the foot out: The opponent can attempt to straighten their leg forcefully, creating space to remove it from the entanglement. This requires a strong base from the attacker.
    • Hip escape and creating distance: The opponent can attempt to hip escape away from the attacker's hips, reducing the leverage of the Estima Lock.
    • Turning into the attacker and flattening out: The opponent can turn their body to face the attacker and attempt to flatten their body to the mat, relieving pressure on the knee.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my opponent's leg feel slippery and hard to grip for the Estima Lock?

    This is common, especially in no-gi. Ensure your grip is on the ankle bone or just above it, where there's less fabric and more bony prominence. Use your forearm to create friction against their shin if needed. Consider a slight 'cupping' motion with your gripping hand to prevent slippage. Focus on securing the grip before initiating the hip elevation.

    Q: How can I Estima Lock a much heavier opponent with good base?

    Against a heavier opponent, rely more on precise hip elevation and leverage than brute pulling strength. Drive your hips upwards and forwards with maximum power, using your glutes and core. Your opponent's weight can actually work against them if you create a strong fulcrum with your knee and pull their ankle towards your chest, isolating their knee joint effectively.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to the Estima Lock from other leg entanglements?

    The Estima Lock is most effective when the opponent's leg is already somewhat controlled and isolated, such as in the 50-50 guard. If you are in a position like the single-leg X-Guard and your opponent is posturing up, you can transition by bringing your inside leg over their hip and securing the ankle to enter the 50-50 position, creating the opportunity for the Estima Lock.

Tripod Sweep: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Tripod Sweep is initiated from the Closed Guard, aiming to disrupt your opponent's base and achieve a dominant top position.

White belts often fail by relying on brute strength or improper weight distribution, leading to ineffective sweeps and potential injury.

The core principle is leveraging your leg as a post (the tripod) while using your hips and free leg to unbalance and elevate your opponent.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a strong closed guard by interlocking your feet behind your opponent's hips, ensuring your knees are tight.
    2. Secure a double sleeve grip, pulling your opponent's arms towards your chest to break their posture.
    3. Release your left foot from the guard and place it firmly on the mat, creating your first 'leg' of the tripod, perpendicular to your opponent's hip.
    4. Simultaneously, bring your right knee towards your chest, creating a strong frame against your opponent's hip/thigh with your right shin and foot.
    5. With your left foot planted and your right leg framing, use your hips to bridge slightly, lifting your opponent's base.
    6. As your opponent's weight shifts forward, drive your right shin and foot upwards, pushing their hip away and up.
    7. Simultaneously, extend your left leg slightly and use your planted left foot to 'hop' or shift your hips to your left, creating an angle.
    8. As their base collapses, drive your hips forward and up, using your framing right leg to elevate their hip, rolling them over your left hip.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Placing your planted foot too far forward and attempting to 'pull' your opponent over: This can strain your knee ligaments (MCL/LCL) due to excessive rotational force. Instead, keep your planted foot angled and use your hips to drive.
    • Arching your back excessively while bridging: This can lead to lumbar strain or disc injury. Maintain a neutral spine and focus on hip extension. Instead, engage your glutes and core for the bridge.
    • Letting your framing knee collapse inwards: This puts your knee joint at risk of valgus stress, potentially tearing the ACL. Keep your knee aligned with your foot and actively push away.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling of leg placement and hip bridge motion (0% resistance, 50 reps).
    2. Practicing the tripod leg setup and framing motion without a partner (0% resistance, 50 reps per side).
    3. Partner drills: Focus on establishing closed guard and executing the leg placement and framing steps (25% resistance, 10 reps).
    4. Adding the hip bridge and leg drive motion with partner's light resistance (50% resistance, 10 reps).
    5. Executing the full sweep motion with controlled partner reaction (75% resistance, 10 reps).
    6. Live rolling, focusing on applying the Tripod Sweep when opportunities arise (90% resistance, 5 attempts).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is postured up and leaning forward in your closed guard.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard by stepping their legs inside.
    • When your opponent is holding your hips tightly, creating a strong base you can disrupt.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • To defend: If your opponent attempts the sweep, immediately widen your base and drive your weight forward onto your knees, preventing them from elevating your hip. Keep your hips heavy and close to the mat.
    • If they establish the tripod and begin to drive: Quickly bring your knees back towards your chest and try to regain a tight closed guard or transition to a different guard like half guard by framing with your shin.
    • If they successfully elevate your hip: Immediately try to sprawl your hips back to the mat and break their grip, or roll with them to prevent them from settling into a dominant position.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Tripod Sweep?

    Knee pain often occurs if you're relying on pulling your opponent down with your arms or if your framing knee collapses inwards. Ensure your planted foot is angled correctly and your framing leg actively pushes their hip away, keeping your knee aligned with your foot. Focus on hip drive rather than pulling.

    Q: How do I Tripod Sweep a much bigger and heavier opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on timing and leverage. Break their posture aggressively with your sleeve grips and pull them forward. Your goal is to use their forward momentum against them. Ensure your tripod leg is firmly planted and your framing leg drives their hip upward with maximum hip extension, not just leg strength.

    Q: When is the best time to use the Tripod Sweep instead of a scissor sweep?

    The Tripod Sweep is most effective when your opponent is postured up and leaning forward, or when they are trying to establish a strong base by placing their hands on the mat or your hips. The Scissor Sweep is better when your opponent is more upright and their legs are closer together, allowing you to sweep their base outwards.

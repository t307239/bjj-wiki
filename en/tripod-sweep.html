Tripod Sweep: A White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

The Tripod Sweep is initiated from Closed Guard and aims to invert your opponent's base, allowing you to sweep them to your guard.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength instead of proper leverage and timing, leading to stalled sweeps and wasted energy.

The key is to create an off-balance angle using your leg as a lever, forcing their weight onto their base and then sweeping it away.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip: Establish a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip on their left arm with your left hand.
    2. Hip Angle: Shrimp your hips to your left, creating a 45-degree angle between your torso and your opponent's center line.
    3. Leg Placement: Place the sole of your right foot on their left hip bone, knee pointing towards their left shoulder.
    4. Weight Transfer: As you begin to lift your hips, pull their left arm towards your chest while simultaneously driving your right foot into their hip.
    5. Base Disruption: Use your left leg to hook their right leg behind the knee, preventing them from posting it.
    6. Sweep Motion: Simultaneously extend your hips upwards and pivot your body counter-clockwise, pulling their arm and pushing their hip.
    7. Transition: As they fall, immediately bring your left leg over their body to establish guard, or transition to mount if possible.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Knee Drive: Driving your right knee outwards and upwards towards their shoulder can hyperextend your knee joint, risking ACL or meniscus tears. Instead, ensure your knee points towards their shoulder and your drive is primarily horizontal, pushing their hip outwards.
    • Arm Yanking: Pulling their left sleeve with excessive upward force can strain your shoulder or elbow joints, especially if they resist strongly. Focus on a controlled pull that guides their arm across your body, not a raw yank.
    • Premature Hip Extension: Extending your hips before establishing sufficient leg leverage and grip control can lead to them posturing up or passing your guard. Wait until you feel their weight shift and their base is compromised before initiating the hip extension.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Movement (0%): Practice the hip escape and leg placement without a partner. Focus on the 45-degree angle and foot-to-hip connection. (10 reps)
    2. Grip and Leg Placement (25%): Partner lies on their back. Practice establishing grips and placing your right foot on their hip, ensuring your knee points correctly. (10 reps)
    3. Controlled Sweep Motion (50%): Partner remains passive. Execute the hip drive, arm pull, and leg hook. Focus on the timing of the hip extension and sweep. (10 reps)
    4. Assisted Sweep (75%): Partner offers slight resistance to the sweep. Focus on maintaining your base and driving through the motion. (10 reps)
    5. Light Resistance Sweep (90%): Partner actively tries to prevent the sweep but allows you to execute the mechanics. Focus on adapting to their minor adjustments. (10 reps)
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Attempt the Tripod Sweep during live rolling, prioritizing technique over outcome. (3 minute rounds, 5 rounds)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is postured up in your closed guard, creating space.
    • When they are attempting to pass your guard and are leaning forward, compromising their base.
    • When you have established strong collar and sleeve grips and can control their posture.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Posture Up: If they attempt the sweep, immediately drive your hips down and posture up, creating a strong base. This prevents your weight from being lifted.
    • Leg Post: If they hook your leg, quickly post your free leg on the mat and push off to create space and prevent the sweep.
    • Base Shift: If you feel the sweep coming, shift your weight to your base leg and widen your stance, making it harder for them to invert your base.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do the Tripod Sweep?

    Your knee might be hurting because you are hyperextending it or driving it outwards instead of guiding it. Ensure your right foot is placed on their hip bone, with your knee pointing towards their shoulder. The primary force should be a horizontal push into their hip, not an upward knee drive.

    Q: How can I Tripod Sweep a much bigger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paraMount. Focus on creating a significant angle with your hips (45 degrees) and using their arm as a lever. Pull their arm across your body and drive your foot into their hip, disrupting their base before committing to the sweep. Don't try to lift them; aim to unbalance them.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Tripod Sweep in closed guard?

    The ideal time is when your opponent is postured up and leaning forward, creating an opening. If they are too low or too far back, the sweep is unlikely to work. Look for moments when they are actively trying to break your guard or pass, as their forward pressure will be easier to exploit.

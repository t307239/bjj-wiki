The Lockdown: BJJ's Most Controlling Half Guard Position

Contents

    How to Enter the Lockdown

    1. Establish the half guard: From your back, use your legs to capture one of your opponent's legs between your own legs.
    2. Underhook the ankle: Slide your bottom foot behind your opponent's ankle and hook it with your instep (not just your heel).
    3. Figure-four your legs: Bring your top foot over and hook it behind your own ankle, creating a figure-four lock around your opponent's leg.
    4. Extend and torque: Straighten both legs outward while rotating the locked leg — this stretches your opponent and breaks their posture.
    5. Frame and control the upper body: Use your arms to frame against your opponent's hip and push them away, or underhook their far arm to set up Old School or Electric Chair.

    Key Details & Common Mistakes

    • The Lockdown is most effective with no gi or with pants — in gi, grips on your opponent's clothing can make it harder to entangle.
    • The extension is key: many beginners pull with bent legs instead of extending straight. The power comes from the full leg extension and hip rotation.
    • Keep your hips low and angled to the side — a flat base on the mat makes the Lockdown much weaker.
    • Common mistake: Locking too high on the thigh instead of at the ankle/lower shin reduces control significantly.

    Variations

    Half Guard Lockdown: The standard version described above, used to set up sweeps and attacks.

    Lockdown to Old School Sweep: From Lockdown, underhook the near leg, post up on your free elbow, and sweep your opponent forward by pushing their knee away.

    Lockdown to Electric Chair: From Lockdown, hip out and come up onto your hip, reaching under your opponent's far leg to apply the Electric Chair submission or sweep.

    When to Use

    The Lockdown is best deployed when you are underneath a heavy, pressure-based passer who is attempting to flatten you out in the half guard. It immediately neutralizes their weight by trapping their leg and shifting their base.

    It works particularly well against opponents who drive their knee through the middle or try to knee-slice from half guard. The Lockdown stops that motion cold and reverses the pressure back on them.

    Counters & Defense

    Step over (Saulo pass): The passer pulls their knee free and steps over your lockdown, switching to the other leg to pass.

    Roll through: A knowledgeable opponent can roll into the Lockdown direction to escape and take the back.

    Underhook battle: Fighting for the underhook before the Lockdown is fully set prevents the bottom player from coming up effectively.

    More Questions

    How do I escape the lockdown if my opponent has it?

    The primary escapes involve creating space by extending your leg, attempting to shrimp out, or transitioning to a different guard. Sometimes, a well-timed knee slice or pressure can also break the lockdown.

    What are the main advantages of using the lockdown?

    The lockdown excels at controlling your opponent's base and mobility, preventing them from passing your half guard. It also sets up numerous sweeps and submissions by limiting their movement and creating leverage.

    When is the best time to transition into the lockdown?

    The lockdown is most effective when your opponent is trying to pass your half guard, especially when they are pressuring forward and their legs are within range. It's a reactive position that capitalizes on their forward momentum.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Is the Lockdown effective in gi BJJ?

    The Lockdown works in gi, but it is less effective because the gi fabric can interfere with leg entanglement and opponents can post using gi grips. It shines most brightly in no-gi grappling where 10th Planet techniques are most commonly used.

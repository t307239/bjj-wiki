Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

Q: Why do my shoulders feel like they're going to pop out when I'm trying to escape side control in BJJ?

This often happens when you're bridging or shrimping incorrectly. Instead of just pushing with your legs, ensure you're driving your hips into the mat and creating space by rotating your body, tucking your chin, and keeping your elbow close to your ribs to protect your shoulder joint from hyperextension.

Q: How can I stop my opponent from passing my guard effectively when they're much heavier than me in BJJ?

Focus on maintaining a strong, active guard by keeping your knees tucked tight to your chest and your feet planted on their hips or shoulders. Utilize your legs to constantly push and pull, creating angles and preventing them from establishing a stable base, which will make it harder for their weight to be an advantage.

Q: What's the proper way to grip fight for an armbar from guard so my opponent can't just pull their arm out easily in BJJ?

When setting up an Armbar from guard, ensure your grips are tight and locked around their arm and shoulder, not just their wrist. Specifically, use your bicep grip on their upper arm and your wrist grip on their hand, pulling their elbow towards your chest to break their posture and control the limb's leverage.

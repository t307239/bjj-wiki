Cobrinha

BJJ Athlete5x World ChampionBlack Belt

Rubens Charles, known as "Cobrinha" (Portuguese for "little snake"), is a Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu black belt and one of the most celebrated lightweights in the sport's history. A student of Shaolin (Cicero Costha) and Alliance, Cobrinha won 5 IBJJF World Championships at featherweight and was a dominant force in the lighter weight classes for over a decade. He is particularly celebrated as a pioneer and master of the De La Riva guard and berimbolo system.

Biography

Born in Brazil and later based in the United States (Los Angeles), Rubens "Cobrinha" Charles built his BJJ career through the Shaolin (Cicero Costha) and Alliance systems. He earned his black belt and became one of the defining lightweights of the 2000s and 2010s.

Cobrinha's training environment in Los Angeles brought him into contact with some of the best competition preparation programs in BJJ. His 5 World Championships (2007, 2008, 2009, 2010, 2012) at featherweight marked him as the premier featherweight of his era.

He later founded his own academy (Cobrinha BJJ) and has trained numerous high-level competitors, extending his influence as a coach well beyond his competitive years.

Technical Style

Cobrinha's game was defined by flexibility, creativity, and a guard system that was ahead of its time. Core elements:

  • De La Riva guard: Cobrinha was one of the most dangerous De La Riva players ever — using DLR to control space, create sweeps, and transition to back takes.
  • Berimbolo: Cobrinha was one of the early pioneers of the berimbolo rotation, which became a defining technique of the light featherweight and featherweight divisions in the 2010s.
  • Flexibility-based guard: His extreme flexibility allowed guard positions and transitions unavailable to less flexible practitioners — his hips and back mobility were legendary.
  • Submission game: Triangle choke, omoplata, and armbar were primary submissions from his guard system.

De La Riva Mastery

Cobrinha's De La Riva guard was arguably the most sophisticated of its era. He used the DLR hook not just to hold position but as a dynamic control point for:

  • Sweeping opponents who tried to knee-slice or torreando past.
  • Transitioning to X-guard when opponents straightened the DLR leg.
  • Back takes via DLR berimbolo rotations.
  • Triangle and omoplata attacks when opponents reached for collar control.

Berimbolo Pioneer

The berimbolo — an inverted rotation from De La Riva guard that takes the opponent's back — became a defining technique of lightweight BJJ in the 2010s. Cobrinha, along with the Miyao brothers and other lightweight specialists, was one of the key figures who popularized and developed the berimbolo rotation in competition.

His ability to execute the berimbolo from multiple positions and chain it with back-take sequences made him one of the most dangerous guard players of his generation.

Championship Record

Cobrinha won the IBJJF World Championship at featherweight in 2007, 2008, 2009, 2010, and 2012 — five world titles in six years. He also won multiple Pan-American Championships and Brazilian Nationals, establishing himself as the dominant featherweight across nearly a decade of competition.

Legacy

Cobrinha's legacy is threefold: competitive excellence (5 World Championships), technical innovation (DLR and berimbolo development), and coaching success (his academy produced multiple high-level competitors). He remains one of the most respected names in BJJ and a reference point for technical lightweight play.

⚡ Learning from Cobrinha

  • Study his De La Riva guard entries and transitions — his DLR is textbook for controlling space and creating sweep opportunities.
  • Cobrinha's flexibility is unique, but his guard principles (creating angles, using the DLR hook as a lever) are applicable at all levels.
  • Watch his berimbolo mechanics slowly to understand the rotation and weight transfer before attempting at speed.
  • His competition footage from the World Championships (2007–2012) at featherweight shows the most complete lightweight game of that era.
Q: What is Cobrinha known for in BJJ?

Cobrinha is best known for his De La Riva guard mastery, berimbolo innovation, and 5 IBJJF World Championship titles at featherweight. He was one of the most technically advanced lightweight BJJ practitioners of his generation and a pioneer of the inverted guard/berimbolo system that became widespread in the 2010s.

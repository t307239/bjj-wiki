Closed Guard is a fundamental BJJ position where you control your opponent from your back, legs locked around their waist.

White belts often struggle by having their legs too loose or trying to 'squeeze' with their muscles instead of using leverage.

The key is creating a tight "box" with your legs and using your hips to generate angles and pressure.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From supine position, bring knees towards chest, hips slightly elevated off the mat.
    2. Lock your ankles behind opponent's back, ensuring your shins create a strong "box" around their waist.
    3. Maintain a neutral spine, avoiding arching your lower back.
    4. Control opponent's posture by gripping their collar (either one or both lapels) with both hands.
    5. Alternatively, grip their sleeves, keeping elbows tight to your body.
    6. Use your hips to "hitchhike" and create slight angles, forcing them to adjust their weight.
    7. Keep your knees slightly bent, never locked straight, to maintain flexibility and pressure.
    8. Ensure your feet are not crossed, but rather ankles are interlocked behind the opponent's back.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Gripping opponent's ankles: This often leads to their legs slipping out and can strain your hip flexors and lower back if you pull too hard without proper control.
    • Locking knees straight: This compromises your ability to adjust angles and creates a "dead" guard, making it easier for opponents to stack you, potentially injuring your knees and spine.
    • Trying to "squeeze" with leg muscles: This fatigues you quickly and lacks the structural integrity of a proper closed guard, leading to weak control and potential shoulder or elbow injuries for you when they resist forcefully.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice closing the guard from various positions on your back for 5 minutes, focusing on hip movement and leg lock.
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice establishing closed guard and holding it for 30 seconds, focusing on grip and leg tightness. Repeat 10 times.
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Practice closing guard and maintaining it while the partner gently tries to open your legs. Repeat 10 times.
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Focus on maintaining closed guard while the partner attempts to break posture and move their hips. Repeat 5 minutes.
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Live rolling, focusing solely on establishing and maintaining closed guard for 2 minutes.
    6. Live rolling (100% resistance): Full sparring, prioritizing the use of closed guard for control and submission attempts for 5 minutes.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When opponent is attempting to pass your guard and enters within your leg reach.
    • When you are on your back and can bring your knees to your chest.
    • When opponent is postured up and you have an opportunity to "hug" them with your legs.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Pummeling: When opponent tries to step their leg inside, counter by driving your shin across their thigh, creating a "frame" to prevent them from establishing a dominant leg position.
    • Hip Escape: If opponent starts to "stack" you, immediately hip escape to the side, creating space to re-establish guard or transition.
    • Posture Control: If opponent is postured too high, use collar grips to pull them down, bringing their hips closer and making them vulnerable to sweeps or submissions.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to keep my Closed Guard tight?

    This typically happens when you over-arch your lower back to create tension, rather than using your hips and leg structure. Ensure your hips are slightly elevated off the mat and your ankles are locked behind your opponent's back, creating a secure "box." Focus on engaging your core and using your glutes to drive your hips forward, not just flexing your back.

    Q: How can I use Closed Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, your primary goal is to prevent them from using their weight advantage to "stack" you. Focus on maintaining a tight "box" with your legs and using your collar grips to break their posture. Continuously "hitchhike" your hips to create off-angles, making it difficult for them to maintain a stable base and allowing you to set up sweeps or submissions.

    Q: When should I switch from Closed Guard to other guards like Half Guard or Open Guard?

    You should consider transitioning from Closed Guard when your opponent consistently manages to "stack" you or has very strong posture control that you cannot break. If they start to create significant space or "base out" effectively, it's often a sign to transition to a guard that offers more mobility, such as Half Guard or a form of Open Guard, to re-establish a dominant position.

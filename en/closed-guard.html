Closed Guard: The Foundation of BJJ Control

WHITE★☆☆☆☆Beginner
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

Closed Guard starts from your back, encircling the opponent's hips with your legs. It aims to control distance, prevent passes, and set up attacks.

White belts often struggle by keeping their hips too flat or their legs too loose, losing control.

The key is active hip flexion and precise leg angles to create a tight, unescapable structure.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Start on your back, knees bent, feet flat on the mat.
    2. Secure a Collar Grip with your right hand on the opponent's gi collar, near the sternum.
    3. Simultaneously, secure a Sleeve Grip with your left hand on the opponent's right sleeve, near the wrist.
    4. Bring your right knee across the opponent's hip, creating a 45-degree angle with their midline.
    5. Place your left foot on the opponent's right hip bone, applying slight outward pressure.
    6. Curl your right foot under your left shin, creating a secure lock around the opponent's waist.
    7. Flex your hips upward, driving your knees towards your chest and creating a "shell" around their hips.
    8. Maintain tension in your legs, keeping your heels close to your glutes and your back tight to the mat.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Hyperextended Knees: Arching your back excessively to create space allows the opponent to drive forward, leading to knee hyperextension and potential ACL/MCL tears. Maintain a neutral spine and use hip flexion to control.
    • Wrist Strain from Sleeve Grips: Grabbing too high on the sleeve or pulling straight back with a bent wrist can cause carpal tunnel or wrist sprains. Grip lower on the forearm and pull with a straight arm, using your body weight.
    • Low Back Compression: Allowing the opponent to stack your hips too flat against the mat can compress your lumbar spine, risking disc injury. Actively flex your hips and keep your glutes tight to lift your hips.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Guard Setup: Practice closing your guard from a lying position 20 times per side, focusing on hip angle and leg lock (0% resistance).
    2. Guard Retention Drill: Partner lies on top, you practice maintaining closed guard as they try to create space with their hands (25% resistance), 10 reps.
    3. Grip Fighting: Practice securing collar and sleeve grips from closed guard while your partner resists (50% resistance), 15 reps.
    4. Basic Sweep Prep: Practice transitioning to a scissor sweep setup, holding the position for 10 seconds, 10 reps per side.
    5. Controlled Pass Defense: Partner attempts a simple pass (e.g., knee slice), you focus on maintaining closed guard and recovering. (75% resistance), 5 reps per side.
    6. Live Rolling: Execute closed guard for 2-minute rounds, focusing on maintaining control and attempting submissions (100% resistance), 3 rounds.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent attempts to pass your guard from standing or kneeling.
    • When you have successfully swept an opponent and they are on their knees.
    • When an opponent tries to establish side control from a top half-guard position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Bump Sweep: Secure a collar and sleeve grip. Bring your right knee into their hip and your left foot to their opposite hip. Hip escape to the right, driving your right knee into their hip as you bump them over.
    • Scissor Sweep: Secure a collar and sleeve grip. Place your left foot on their right hip. Swing your right leg over their back, hooking their right armpit. Simultaneously, pull their collar and push their hip with your left foot as you scissor your legs.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to hold my opponent's sleeve in Closed Guard?

    Your wrist might be hurting because you're gripping too high on the sleeve or pulling with a bent wrist. For a biomechanically sound grip, aim for the opponent's forearm, just above the elbow. When pulling, keep your arm straight and use your entire body's weight to drive them forward, rather than just your forearm muscles.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, focus on maximizing the "frame" your legs create. Keep your heels glued to your glutes and your knees driving inward to create a strong, tight structure. Use precise hip flexion to "shell" their hips, preventing them from posturing up. Actively control their posture with collar and sleeve grips, pulling them into your guard.

    Q: The most common error is not achieving sufficient hip flexion and leg curl. Ensure your heels are pulled tightly towards your glutes, and your knees are driving aggressively towards your chest. Your left foot should be actively posting on their hip bone (not just resting) to create leverage and prevent them from easily posturing up or driving forward.

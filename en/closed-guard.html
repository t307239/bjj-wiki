The Ultimate Guide to BJJ Closed Guard: Control & Attack

🥋 White Belt ★☆☆☆☆ Beginner

The Closed Guard is a foundational position in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, characterized by wrapping your legs around your opponent's torso and locking your ankles. It provides excellent control, limits your opponent's movement, and creates numerous opportunities for sweeps, submissions, and transitions, making it a cornerstone of any BJJ practitioner's game.

Contents

    How to Execute

    1
    Establish Position: From your back, bring your knees to your chest and wrap your legs around your opponent's waist or lower back, pulling them close.
    2
    Lock the Guard: Cross your ankles firmly, ensuring your feet are locked behind your opponent's back, creating a strong, inescapable hold.
    3
    Break Posture: Use your legs and arms (grips on sleeves/collar) to pull your opponent forward, breaking their posture and bringing their head closer to your chest.
    4
    Secure Grips: Establish dominant grips, typically on the opponent's collar and sleeve, to maintain control and prevent them from posturing up or passing.
    5
    Control Hips: Actively use your hips (shrimp, bridge) to create angles, off-balance your opponent, and set up your attacks while keeping their hips pinned to the mat.
    6
    Maintain Pressure: Continuously apply pressure with your legs and core, making it difficult for your opponent to breathe, adjust, or initiate their own attacks.

    Key Details & Tips

    1
    Posture Control is Key: Your primary goal is to break your opponent's posture, limiting their ability to strike, initiate passes, or generate power.
    2
    Active Hips: Don't be a passive guard player. Use hip movement (shrimp, bridge) to create angles and off-balance your opponent for sweeps and submission setups.
    3
    Grip Fighting: Always be looking to establish and maintain dominant grips on your opponent's sleeves, collars, or head to control their upper body and prevent their attacks.
    4
    Common Mistake: Many beginners keep their legs too loose or their ankles unlocked, allowing the opponent to easily posture up or break the guard with minimal effort.

    Variations

    1
    High Guard: Elevating your hips and bringing your knees closer to your chest, wrapping your legs higher up on your opponent's back for tighter control and easier arm attacks.
    2
    Attacks from Closed Guard: Transitioning directly into an Armbar, Triangle Choke, or Omoplata from the Closed Guard without breaking the lock, utilizing the control it offers.
    3
    Flower Sweep Setup: Utilizing a specific grip and hip movement to off-balance and sweep the opponent when they attempt to posture up, turning their defense into your offense.

    When to Use

    This technique works best when you are on your back and your opponent is kneeling or standing over you, providing an immediate control solution. It's ideal for controlling a larger or stronger opponent, as it neutralizes their strength and limits their movement. The Closed Guard serves as a safe and effective starting point to initiate a wide range of offensive techniques, including sweeps and submissions, while minimizing your own exposure to attacks.

    Counters & Defenses

    1
    Posture Up: Straighten your back, lift your head, and create space by pushing down on the opponent's hips to prevent the guard player from breaking your posture.
    2
    Stand Up & Break Grips: Stand up with good posture, break the ankle lock by pushing down on the opponent's knee, and strip any grips on your sleeves or collar to initiate a guard pass.
    3
    Stack Pass: Drive your opponent's knees towards their chest and stack them, putting immense pressure on their diaphragm and forcing them to open their guard to alleviate the discomfort.

    Q: What are the primary goals of the BJJ Closed Guard?

    The main goals of the BJJ Closed Guard are to control your opponent's posture, limit their movement, create opportunities for sweeps to achieve a top position, and set up various submissions like Armbars and chokes, all while minimizing your own exposure to attacks and maintaining a safe defensive posture.

    More Questions

    What's the biggest mistake beginners make when passing butterfly guard?

    The most common mistake is not securing a strong grip on the opponent's legs or hips. Without control, they can easily re-guard or sweep you.

    How do I prevent my opponent from hooking my leg when I try to pass?

    Focus on controlling their knee and ankle with your hands, preventing them from getting that deep hook. Alternatively, you can use your shin to block their hook before it's established.

    What are some common follow-up submissions after passing butterfly guard?

    Once you've cleared the guard, common submissions include side control chokes like the arm triangle or cross-collar choke, or transitioning to mount for an Americana or triangle choke.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my opponent's leg keep coming back to my chest when I try to pass their butterfly guard, and how can I prevent this?

    Your opponent is likely using their feet and ankles to create a frame against your hips and chest, preventing you from driving forward. To counter this, focus on establishing a strong grip on their ankle or shin with one hand, while using your other hand to control their knee or hip, thereby breaking their leg frame and creating an opening to advance.

    Q: How can I effectively break my opponent's posture when they are in butterfly guard and I'm trying to pass?

    When your opponent has good posture in butterfly guard, their hips are often elevated. To break their posture, use your shoulder or chest to drive down onto their upper back while simultaneously pulling their knees towards their chest with your arm grips on their ankles or shins, forcing their hips to the mat and their head down.

    Q: What is the most common mistake white belts make when trying to pass butterfly guard, and how can I avoid getting stuck in this position?

    A common mistake is trying to force a pass by driving straight forward without establishing control of the legs and hips first. Instead, focus on isolating one leg by gripping the ankle and pulling it towards you, while using your body weight to pin their hip to the mat, creating a clear path to advance your hips and pass.

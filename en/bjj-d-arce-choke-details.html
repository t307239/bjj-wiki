    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck feel like it's being crushed and not actually choked when I attempt the D'Arce choke?

    This often means you're not getting the proper angle; the D'Arce choke works by compressing the carotid arteries. Ensure your forearm is digging into their neck on the side of your bicep, and your other arm is securing your own bicep to pull their head into the choke, creating a vise-like grip.

    Q: What's the best way to maintain control and prevent my bigger opponent from escaping the D'Arce choke?

    To maintain control against a larger opponent, ensure your bicep is firmly tucked behind their head, preventing them from posturing up or creating space. Simultaneously, keep your hips tight and drive your chest into their shoulder to maintain constant pressure and prevent them from relieving the choke.

    Q: What specific body movement makes the D'Arce choke effective and not just a neck crank?

    The effectiveness comes from compressing the carotid arteries on both sides of the neck, not the trachea. To achieve this, drive your chest into their shoulder and pull your hips back, creating a powerful pulling motion that tightens the grip of your forearm and bicep around their neck, restricting blood flow.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is the main difference between a D'Arce choke and a Anaconda choke?

    The D'Arce choke is entered from the front, with your arm going under the opponent's armpit and around their neck. The Anaconda choke is entered from the side, with your arm going over the opponent's shoulder and around their neck.

    How do I prevent my opponent from defending the D'Arce choke by turning into me?

    To counter their turn, you need to maintain a strong head grip and use your legs to create a "seatbelt" grip on their hips, preventing them from creating space and turning out. Driving your shoulder into their neck also helps maintain control.

    What is the most common mistake people make when applying a D'Arce choke?

    A very common mistake is not getting the choking arm deep enough under the opponent's armpit and across their neck. This allows them to escape by shrugging their shoulder or turning their head, so focus on getting that arm as far across as possible.

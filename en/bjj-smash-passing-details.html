    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is the main goal of a smash pass in BJJ?

    The primary objective of a smash pass is to break down your opponent's guard by collapsing their legs and hips, creating pressure to advance your position to side control or mount. It's about overwhelming their structure rather than trying to slip through.

    How do I avoid getting my arm trapped when doing a smash pass?

    To avoid arm traps, maintain strong head and shoulder pressure, keeping your weight distributed forward and low. Focus on keeping your elbows tucked and your arms connected to your body, preventing your opponent from isolating and attacking them.

    When is a smash pass most effective?

    Smash passes are most effective against opponents who rely heavily on a closed guard or half guard with a strong butterfly hook. They are also useful when your opponent is trying to create space or hip escape, as your pressure can counter these movements.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my opponent's knee dig into my ribs when I try to Smash Pass, and how can I stop it?

    Your opponent's knee digs in because you're likely not creating enough downward pressure with your chest and shoulder. To counter, drive your sternum into their hip bone and maintain a strong shoulder-to-shoulder connection, preventing them from shucking you off.

    Q: How can I prevent my opponent from using their legs to frame and stop my Smash Pass, especially when they have a strong guard?

    To defeat strong leg frames, focus on collapsing their hips by driving your chest and shoulder into their pelvis, not just their legs. Simultaneously, use your free arm to control their ankle or shin, preventing them from creating space and establishing a solid base.

    Q: When I attempt a Smash Pass, my opponent often ends up turning into me and reversing my position; what specific body mechanics should I focus on to avoid this?

    To prevent reversals, ensure your head is always posting on the mat or their hip bone to maintain your base, and keep your hips heavy and driving forward. As you pressure down, actively use your shoulder to turn their hips away from you, creating a strong anchor and preventing them from turning into your space.

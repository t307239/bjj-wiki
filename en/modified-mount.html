Modified Mount is a dominant BJJ position achieved from a standard mount, aiming to improve control and set up submissions. White belts often struggle because they focus on squeezing rather than stable hip pressure and base.

The key mechanical insight is to use your hips to create a "bridge" that prevents your opponent from bridging out, while simultaneously keeping your weight forward.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From standard mount, establish hooks with your shins around the opponent's hips. Ensure your knees are wider than shoulder-width for base.
    2. Place your hands on the mat slightly wider than your shoulders, fingers pointing forward, creating a tripod base.
    3. Shift your weight forward by extending your hips towards the opponent's head, lowering your hips closer to the mat.
    4. Bring your knees inward slightly, creating a "seatbelt" effect around their hips, preventing them from bridging.
    5. Your torso should be upright, creating a "post" that resists forward pressure from the opponent.
    6. Maintain a slight inward knee pressure to keep their hips controlled, preventing them from creating space.
    7. Your chest should be pressed lightly onto their upper chest, allowing you to feel their movements and react.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Knee hyperextension: Attempting to "lock" your legs by straightening them completely can lead to hyperextension of the knee joint, damaging the ACL or PCL.
    • Wrist sprains/fractures: Placing excessive weight directly onto outstretched wrists with fingers pointed straight forward can cause wrist hyperextension or compression injuries.
    • Lower back strain: Arching your lower back excessively to try and stay "up" without proper hip engagement can strain the erector spinae muscles and intervertebral discs.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice shifting weight forward and bringing knees inward without a partner. (20 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Opponent lies flat. Practice entering Modified Mount from standard mount, focusing on hip pressure and base. (10 reps each side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Opponent offers minimal resistance to your weight shift. Focus on maintaining position. (25% resistance, 5 reps)
    4. Partner drill (moderate resistance): Opponent attempts to bridge or shrimp. Focus on maintaining hip pressure and base. (50% resistance, 5 reps)
    5. Partner drill (advanced resistance): Opponent actively tries to escape. Focus on maintaining control and weight distribution. (75% resistance, 5 reps)
    6. Live rolling: Implement Modified Mount during sparring, focusing on maintaining the position and transitioning to submissions. (100% resistance, 5 minutes)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When you have established a stable standard mount and your opponent is trying to bridge.
    • When you feel your opponent is creating space by pushing your hips up.
    • As a transition to set up armbars or chokes from mount.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Opponent bridges: If the opponent bridges, immediately widen your base by pushing your knees out and lower your hips to the mat to absorb the pressure.
    • Opponent shrimps: If the opponent shrimps, use your hands to "post" on the mat and drive your hip into their hip to prevent them from creating space.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to get into Modified Mount?

    Your lower back is likely hurting because you are arching your spine excessively to maintain height instead of using your hips and core. To fix this, focus on driving your hips forward and down, almost "sitting" on your opponent's upper body. Engage your core muscles to support your spine, and keep your chest pressed lightly onto their upper chest.

    Q: How can I use Modified Mount against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, the key is not to out-muscle them, but to use precise weight distribution and hip pressure. Focus on keeping your hips heavy and low, preventing them from using their size to bridge or shrimp effectively. Use your knee pressure to "seatbelt" their hips, and maintain a strong base with your hands on the mat, slightly wider than shoulder-width.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from standard mount to Modified Mount?

    The optimal time to switch to Modified Mount is when you feel your opponent starting to create space or attempt a bridge. As soon as you sense their hips lifting or pushing into yours, initiate the weight transfer forward and inward. This proactive adjustment prevents them from escaping before they gain momentum.

Mastering the Modified Mount in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

Modified Mount is a dominant position established from the standard mount, offering superior control and attack angles. It's crucial for controlling opponents and setting up submissions.

White belts often struggle because they focus on 'holding' the opponent rather than actively controlling their base and weight distribution. This leads to them being easily bucked off or having their base compromised.

The key mechanical insight is to use your shin and knee as a wedge, creating a strong, unstable platform for your opponent while maintaining your own stable base through hip pressure and core engagement.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From standard mount, secure your opponent's hips with your hands on their hip creases, fingers pointing inwards. Maintain a hip-to-hip connection.
    2. Shift your weight slightly to your right hip, allowing your left leg to begin to slide.
    3. Slide your left shin across your opponent's torso, aiming to place it just above their hip bone on their left side.
    4. As your left shin moves, drive your right knee forward, tucking it towards their left armpit. This creates a "figure four" control of their torso.
    5. Immediately transfer your weight forward onto your opponent's chest, driving your hips down and slightly forward. Keep your right knee firmly planted.
    6. Engage your core and pull your opponent's hips towards you with your hands, preventing them from bridging or creating space.
    7. Maintain a wide base with your right knee and the foot of your left leg (planted securely on the mat), ensuring your hips are lower than their shoulders.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly placing your knee: Allowing your right knee to flare outwards or lifting it too high can lead to your opponent bridging and escaping, or a potential knee hyperextension if they roll aggressively.
    • Losing hip connection: If you allow your hips to lift off your opponent's body, they can create space to shrimp or bridge, and you risk losing the position entirely.
    • Neglecting core engagement: Without a braced core, your weight will feel light, making you susceptible to escapes and preventing you from generating the necessary pressure to control your opponent.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip shift, shin slide, and knee tuck motion without a partner. Focus on smooth transitions and body awareness. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner, execute the Modified Mount transition from standard mount. Focus on correct weight transfer and hip pressure. (20 reps)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to practice the mechanics of securing the position. Focus on maintaining hip contact. (20 reps)
    4. Position hold drill: Start in Modified Mount and hold the position for 30 seconds against light resistance. Focus on maintaining base and controlling their hips. (5 sets)
    5. Controlled transition drill: Transition from standard mount to Modified Mount and then back to standard mount, with your partner offering light, predictable resistance. (15 reps)
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Attempt to establish and maintain Modified Mount during rolling. Focus on applying the learned biomechanics and reacting to your partner's movements.

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When you have successfully achieved standard mount and your opponent is actively trying to buck you off or create space.
    • When your opponent is turtling or trying to recover guard from mount, creating an opportunity to adjust your base.
    • As a transitional position when moving from one dominant position to another, offering better control for submissions.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip escape and bridge: The opponent attempts to shrimp their hips away and bridge their hips upward, creating space to escape the mount.
    • Knee block and roll: The opponent uses their knee to block your advancing knee and attempts to roll you over, usually by turning into your base.
    • Arm-trapping and escape: The opponent uses their arms to create frames and push your weight off, then shrimps away to recover guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get into Modified Mount?

    Knee pain often occurs if your knee is not properly aligned with your hip and ankle. Ensure your right knee is tucked towards their left armpit and your shin is across their torso. Avoid letting your knee flare outwards or forcing it into an unnatural angle. Focus on driving your hips forward and down, not just pushing with your knee.

    Q: How do I maintain Modified Mount against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, prioritize hip pressure and weight distribution. Instead of just 'holding' them, actively drive your hips down and forward, using your shin and knee as a wedge to disrupt their base. Keep your base wide with your right knee and left foot, and use your arms to pull their hips into you, preventing them from using their weight effectively.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to Modified Mount from standard mount?

    The ideal time to transition is when you feel your opponent attempting to escape or create space. If they are actively bridging or shrimping, it signals an opportunity to adjust your position and secure a more dominant control. Don't force the transition if your standard mount is secure; wait for the opening.

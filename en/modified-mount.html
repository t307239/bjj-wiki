Modified Mount is a dominant top position achieved after passing the guard, securing control over your opponent's upper body and hips.

White belts often struggle by not establishing a strong base, leading to their opponent escaping or reversing.

The key is to connect your hips to your opponent's hips while maintaining upper body pressure, creating an unshakeable platform.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a standard mount, shift your hips slightly towards your opponent's left side (if they are on their back).
    2. Place your right knee on the mat, directly beside their right hip, creating a "figure four" with your legs.
    3. Your left knee remains elevated, positioned over their left hip bone, maintaining a wide base.
    4. Secure a "collar and sleeve" grip: right hand grips their left collar, left hand grips their right sleeve.
    5. Drive your chest down onto their chest, keeping your shoulders lower than their hips.
    6. Distribute your weight by leaning your upper body forward, with your hips pinned to their hips.
    7. Maintain a base width roughly equal to your opponent's shoulder width, ensuring stability.
    8. Keep your elbows tucked close to your body to prevent them from creating frames.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Placing both knees too far apart: This can lead to knee hyperextension or ligament tears (ACL/MCL) if the opponent bridges or shrimps suddenly.
    • Gripping too loosely on the collar/sleeve: This allows the opponent to easily escape grips, potentially leading to a reversal and strain on your wrist joints.
    • Slouching your upper body: This reduces your control and makes you susceptible to hip escapes and sweeps, potentially causing lower back strain from unstable positioning.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice transitioning into Modified Mount from a simulated guard pass, focusing on hip connection and knee placement. (50 reps, 0% resistance).
    2. Partner drilling (static): Assume Modified Mount on a compliant partner. Focus on achieving the correct hip angle, knee placement, and grip without movement. (20 reps, 10% resistance).
    3. Partner drilling (slow movement): Practice transitioning to Modified Mount after a controlled guard pass. Partner offers minimal resistance to escapes. (15 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Controlled positional sparring: Begin from a neutral position. Your goal is to achieve Modified Mount. Partner defends actively but without full aggression. (5 rounds, 50% resistance).
    5. Positional sparring with escape focus: Start in Modified Mount. Partner attempts to escape. You focus on maintaining the position and preventing escapes. (5 rounds, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Implement Modified Mount during regular sparring. Focus on achieving and maintaining the position. (3 rounds, 100% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • After successfully passing an opponent's guard and establishing a dominant top position.
    • When your opponent is turtled or attempting to bridge out of mount.
    • When you have achieved a strong base and your opponent's hips are accessible.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Knee-to-elbow frame: Opponent places their forearm between your knee and hip, pushing your knee away to create space for escape.
    • Hip escape (shrimp): Opponent uses their legs to create a frame and shrimps their hips away, breaking your connection and allowing them to regain guard or escape.
    • Bridging: Opponent attempts to lift their hips explosively to dislodge your weight and create an opportunity to reverse or escape.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my hip hurt when I try to get into Modified Mount?

    This often occurs when you are trying to force your hip into position rather than using your opponent's body as a pivot. Ensure your supporting leg's knee is pointed slightly outwards and your hips are aligned with their hips, rather than trying to jam it in at an unnatural angle. Focus on a smooth, connected movement.

    Q: How do I maintain Modified Mount against a much heavier opponent?

    Against a heavier opponent, your connection is paramount. Instead of relying on pure weight, focus on driving your chest into their chest and keeping your hips glued to theirs. Use your forearm on their hip to maintain that connection, and keep your base wide and stable. Think of it as pinning them to the mat with your body rather than just sitting on them.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from standard mount to Modified Mount?

    The optimal time is when you notice your opponent is actively trying to bridge or shrimp out of your standard mount. Their defensive movements create an opening to shift your weight and hips. Also, if you feel your base is becoming unstable in standard mount, the Modified Mount offers a more secure, grounded position.

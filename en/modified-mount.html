Mastering the Modified Mount in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Modified Mount is a dominant control position achieved from a standard mount, offering superior leverage and submission opportunities.

White belts often struggle by neglecting weight distribution, leading to a loss of control and increased risk of reversal.

The key is maintaining a tight base with hips low and chest heavy, preventing the opponent from creating space or bridging effectively.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From standard mount, with opponent's arms trapped, shift your weight slightly forward.
    2. Place your left knee closer to the opponent's left hip, creating a tighter angle.
    3. Drive your right knee across their torso, aiming to get it past their shoulder line.
    4. Lower your hips until they are almost touching the opponent's hips, creating a "hip-to-hip" connection.
    5. Grip both of the opponent's arms at the bicep with your hands, palms facing inward.
    6. Maintain a tight chest-to-chest connection, preventing them from posturing up.
    7. Ensure your weight is distributed forward, driving your sternum into their chest.
    8. Keep your feet actively posting on the mat, providing a stable base and preventing them from shrimping out.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Your knees are too high, allowing the opponent to bridge and escape: This strains the lumbar spine and can cause facet joint impingement. Maintain hips low, below their armpits.
    • You are posting your feet too wide, creating a weak base: This allows the opponent to easily hip escape or shrimp out, potentially injuring your hip flexors or adductors. Keep feet closer together, posting firmly on the mat.
    • You are leaning back, negating forward pressure: This puts excessive strain on your shoulders and can lead to shoulder impingement when the opponent attempts to escape. Drive your chest into their chest with hips forward.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip and knee movement from mount to modified mount without a partner, focusing on smooth transitions. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Static partner drilling: With a compliant partner, practice assuming the modified mount position, focusing on achieving the correct body angles and weight distribution. (20 reps, 25% resistance)
    3. Controlled movement drilling: With a partner, move between standard mount and modified mount, allowing the partner to resist slightly with light bridging. (15 reps, 50% resistance)
    4. Submission setup drilling: Practice transitioning to common submissions (e.g., armbar, Ezekiel choke) from the modified mount, with the partner offering minimal defense. (10 reps per submission, 75% resistance)
    5. Positional sparring: Focus on maintaining the modified mount for a set duration or until a submission is secured, with the partner actively trying to escape. (3 rounds x 2 minutes, 90% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the modified mount into live rolling scenarios, aiming to achieve and maintain the position against a resisting opponent. (3 rounds x 5 minutes, 100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have established a secure standard mount and the opponent is trying to create space by pushing your knees apart.
    • When the opponent is turtling or attempting to bridge, and you need a more stable control position.
    • When you are looking to transition to submissions that require tighter chest-to-chest pressure.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape/Shrimping: If the opponent's hips are too close, create space by shrimping your hips away and bringing your knees back towards your chest to re-establish guard.
    • Bridging: If the opponent's weight is too far forward, bridge your hips up forcefully, lifting their weight and creating an opportunity to escape or reverse.
    • Elbow Frame: If the opponent is driving their chest into yours, create a frame with your elbow between your chest and their sternum to generate space and prevent them from crushing you.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to get into Modified Mount?

    Your lower back pain likely stems from trying to achieve the modified mount by arching your back excessively instead of driving your hips forward. When you round your back and lift your hips, you place immense pressure on your lumbar spine. To fix this, focus on hinging at your hips and driving your pelvis towards your opponent's hips, keeping your spine neutral and your chest heavy.

    Q: How can I maintain Modified Mount against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, the key is to maximize your leverage by staying low and heavy. Instead of trying to match their strength, focus on controlling their structure. Drive your hips into their hips to prevent them from bridging, and use your chest pressure to keep them from posturing up. Actively use your feet to anchor yourself to the mat, and consider using an Underhook on one of their arms if they try to create space.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from standard mount to Modified Mount?

    The optimal time to transition is when you feel the opponent beginning to resist your standard mount by attempting to bridge or create space with their legs. This resistance often indicates they are trying to escape, and the tighter control of the modified mount can shut down those escape attempts. It’s also effective when you are ready to initiate a submission, as the close pressure of modified mount sets up many attacks.

Osoto Gari: The Powerful Rear-Hipped Major Outer Reap

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

Osoto Gari is a fundamental standing technique initiated from a clinch, aiming to off-balance and throw your opponent backward.

White belts often struggle by trying to force the throw with raw strength instead of proper leverage and body mechanics.

The key insight is to use your body as a lever, rotating your hips to unbalance your opponent before extending your reaping leg.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a standard collar and sleeve grip: right hand on opponent's left lapel, left hand on opponent's right sleeve. Maintain a slightly upright posture.
    2. Step your right foot forward, angling your body 45 degrees to your opponent's left. Your right hip should be near their left hip.
    3. Transfer your weight onto your left leg. Simultaneously, pull their left lapel down and towards your own left shoulder.
    4. As you pull, step your left foot backward, creating a base with your feet shoulder-width apart. Your left knee should be slightly bent.
    5. Initiate a hip rotation: turn your hips to your right, driving your right hip into their left hip pocket.
    6. Extend your right leg behind and slightly to the outside of your opponent's left leg, aiming to sweep their calf or ankle area.
    7. As your right leg extends and your hips rotate, drive your opponent backward and over your extended right leg, completing the throw.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly extending your reaping leg with a straight knee: This puts excessive stress on the knee joint, leading to potential ligament tears (ACL/MCL) from hyperextension or twisting forces. The correct movement involves a slightly bent knee as the pivot point.
    • Trying to 'pull' the opponent down with arm strength alone: This can lead to shoulder impingement or rotator cuff tears. The power comes from hip rotation and weight transfer, not just arm pulling.
    • Maintaining a completely upright posture during the reap: This compromises your base and balance, making you susceptible to being thrown yourself. You must lower your center of gravity and create a stable base with bent knees.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Shadow drilling: Practice the steps without a partner, focusing on body mechanics and hip rotation. (10 reps, 0% resistance).
    2. Static grip drilling: With a willing partner holding a static grip, practice your footwork, hip angle, and weight transfer. (10 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    3. Controlled entry drilling: Partner offers minimal resistance to your entry and grip. Focus on smooth transitions and hip engagement. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    4. Step-by-step progression: Practice the initial entry and hip turn, then the reap, then the final drive. Partner offers light resistance to each step. (5 reps per step, 75% resistance).
    5. Controlled throw attempts: Partner cooperates fully to allow the throw, focusing on the mechanics of the sweep and weight transfer. (10 reps per side, 90% resistance).
    6. Live rolling introduction: Attempt Osoto Gari during live rolling, prioritizing safe execution and understanding timing over successful throws. (5 attempts, 100% resistance, stop if unsafe).

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • Opponent is off-balanced forward or to their side.
    • Opponent is leaning into you with their weight on their lead leg.
    • You have established a strong collar and sleeve grip with control of their posture.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Opponent steps their reaping leg back and widens their base: This negates the reap by creating a stable platform.
    • Opponent pulls you into them and drives forward: This turns the attempted Osoto Gari into a forward pressure situation, often leading to a guard pass attempt.
    • Opponent hops their reaping leg over your attacking leg: This is a common defense that can transition into a sweep or guard retention.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do Osoto Gari?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are extending your reaping leg completely straight and locking it out. This puts immense pressure on the knee joint and ligaments. For Osoto Gari, your reaping leg should be slightly bent at the knee, acting as a pivot and sweep point, not a rigid extension. Focus on rotating your hips to unbalance your opponent first.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, focus on using their own weight against them. Your primary goal is to break their posture and unbalance them significantly before attempting the reap. Use your grips to pull their lapel down and towards your body, and use your hip rotation to drive into their center of gravity. A strong hip connection is crucial to leverage their size.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt Osoto Gari during a BJJ match?

    The optimal time to attempt Osoto Gari is when your opponent is slightly off-balanced, perhaps after they've attempted a failed sweep or pass, or when they are leaning forward. Look for moments where their weight is not firmly planted on both feet. Establishing control of their sleeve and collar to break their posture is also a prerequisite.

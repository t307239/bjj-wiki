Osoto Gari: Your First Major BJJ Takedown

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

Osoto Gari is a major outer reaping throw, typically initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to unbalance your opponent and take them to the mat.

Many white belts struggle because they rely on brute strength and forget the fundamental principle of leverage and timing.

The key is to create a strong base and use your opponent's forward momentum against them by reaping their leg and driving your hips.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip: Secure a Collar Grip with your right hand (palm facing opponent's lapel, thumb inside) and a Sleeve Grip with your left hand on opponent's right sleeve (forearm parallel to the mat).
    2. Stance: Establish a stable, shoulder-width apart base with knees slightly bent, hips low.
    3. Foot Placement: Step your left foot forward, placing it outside and slightly in front of your opponent's right foot.
    4. Unbalancing: Use your collar grip to pull opponent slightly forward, and your sleeve grip to guide their right arm across their body.
    5. Reaping Leg: Swing your right leg in a wide arc behind your opponent's right leg, aiming to make contact with your shin or calf behind their knee.
    6. Hip Drive: Simultaneously, drive your hips forward and slightly to your left, lowering your center of gravity.
    7. Finishing: As your right leg sweeps their right leg, continue the hip drive and slight pull, lowering your body towards the mat while maintaining control of grips, guiding them down.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly extending your lead leg too straight when reaping can cause a hyperextension injury to your own knee joint if your opponent resists or moves unexpectedly.
    • The correct action is to keep a slight bend in your reaping leg and move it dynamically, not lock it out.
    • Trying to lift your opponent with your arms instead of driving your hips forward can strain your lower back and shoulders, leading to muscle tears.
    • Focus on hip drive and body leverage, using your arms for control and direction, not for lifting.
    • Rushing the movement without proper unbalancing can lead to losing your own base and falling, potentially twisting an ankle.
    • Ensure your opponent is off-balance before initiating the sweep and maintain a wide, stable base throughout the motion.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Shadow drilling: Practice the footwork, hip motion, and arm movements without a partner. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance).
    2. Grip and Static Placement: Have a partner stand passively. Practice securing grips, stepping in, and placing your reaping leg. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance).
    3. Controlled Sweep: With a cooperative partner, execute the Osoto Gari, focusing on the biomechanics. Partner offers minimal resistance. (10 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Controlled Unbalancing: Partner actively tries to maintain balance but doesn't counter aggressively. Focus on creating the unbalance before the sweep. (15 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Semi-Live Takedown Practice: Execute Osoto Gari with a partner who attempts to defend or resist. Focus on maintaining control and finishing the takedown. (20 reps per side, 75% resistance).
    6. Live Takedown Round: Incorporate Osoto Gari into live rolling rounds, aiming to use it when the opportunity arises. (5 min round, 90% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When opponent is moving forward towards you, creating a natural unbalance.
    • When you have established a strong collar and sleeve grip and can control their posture.
    • When opponent's weight is on their lead leg and you can easily access their trailing leg.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Foot block/Inside reap defense: If opponent attempts Osoto Gari, immediately step your reaping leg backwards and slightly inwards, creating a strong base and blocking their sweep with your shin.
    • Single leg defense: As they reach to reap, duck your head and shoot for a single leg takedown on the same side they are attacking, disrupting their balance and taking the dominant position.
    • Pulling counter: If they initiate the pull too early, use your sleeve grip to pull them forward and off-balance yourself, then transition to your own takedown or sweep.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do Osoto Gari?

    Your knee is likely hurting because you are locking your reaping leg straight and hyperextending it. When you swing your leg to reap, keep a slight bend in your knee throughout the motion. This allows for dynamic movement and absorbs any resistance without stressing the joint. Focus on driving your hips forward to generate the sweep, rather than forcing your leg into position.

    Q: How can I perform Osoto Gari against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, you must focus on leverage and timing, not strength. Use your grips to control their posture and create forward momentum. Step in close and drive your hips low and forward into their center of mass. Your reaping leg should sweep behind their knee, not their ankle, to disrupt their base. The key is to use their size against them by unbalancing them significantly.

    Q: I keep falling over when I try Osoto Gari, what am I doing wrong?

    You are likely falling over because you are not establishing a solid base and you are committing your weight too early before your opponent is sufficiently off-balance. Ensure your feet are shoulder-width apart and your knees are bent. Only commit your weight and drive your hips forward once you feel their weight shift forward or to the side due to your grip control and foot placement. Maintain a strong connection with your grips throughout the movement.

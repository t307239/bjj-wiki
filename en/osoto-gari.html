Osoto Gari: The Essential BJJ Takedown for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

Osoto Gari is a major outer reaping throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to off-balance and sweep an opponent's leg to achieve a takedown. White belts often struggle because they rely on brute strength rather than coordinated body mechanics and proper weight transfer.

The key to a successful Osoto Gari is to create a strong, unmovable base while simultaneously disrupting your opponent's base, then using your hips and legs to execute the sweep.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grips: Establish a right-side collar grip with your left hand (thumb-in, knuckles facing opponent) and a right-sleeve grip with your right hand (palm facing up, fingers gripping the fabric below the elbow).
    2. Stance: Adopt a staggered stance, left foot slightly forward, with knees bent and hips low, creating a wide, stable base (approximately shoulder-width apart).
    3. Break Posture: Use your collar grip to pull your opponent's head down and towards your left shoulder, collapsing their posture and bringing their weight forward over their left leg.
    4. Foot Placement: Step your right foot forward and slightly to the outside of your opponent's right leg, ensuring your right foot is flat on the mat, heel pointing towards their hip.
    5. Hip Engagement: Turn your hips inward by rotating your torso counter-clockwise, bringing your right hip into contact with your opponent's right hip.
    6. Reaping Action: As your hips engage, extend your left leg back and slightly to the side, creating a sweeping motion with the back of your left calf across the front of your opponent's right ankle or lower shin.
    7. Weight Transfer & Sweep: Simultaneously, drive your weight forward through your right hip and leg, while continuing the reaping motion with your left leg, lifting and pushing their right leg outwards and backwards to unbalance them.
    8. Completion: As their right leg is swept, drive them forward and down with your upper body and hip pressure, following them to the mat to secure a dominant position.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Knee Hyperextension: Attempting to kick your leg out too straight and stiffly, especially if your opponent is resisting by leaning back, can lead to hyperextension of the knee joint. The correct action is a controlled sweeping motion with a slightly bent knee, maintaining contact with the opponent's leg.
    • Ankle Sprain: Stepping your reaping leg too far behind your opponent's foot or across their ankle with insufficient hip rotation can cause your foot to twist awkwardly if they step back or shift weight, leading to an ankle sprain. Ensure your foot is placed correctly to sweep the lower shin or ankle, not behind it.
    • Lower Back Strain: Trying to force the sweep by bending excessively at the waist without proper hip engagement and weight transfer can strain the lumbar spine. The power comes from the rotation of your hips and the forward drive of your body, not from a hunching motion.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling (0% resistance): Practice the entire sequence of grips, footwork, hip rotation, and leg sweep motion without a partner. Focus on smooth transitions and correct body angles. (10 reps per side).
    2. Partner Resistance Drilling - Static (25% resistance): With a partner standing passively, practice the grips and initial posture break. They then offer minimal resistance as you execute the footwork and sweep motion, focusing on feel and timing. (5 reps per side).
    3. Partner Resistance Drilling - Dynamic (50% resistance): The partner actively resists slightly by not allowing posture to break easily or by stepping back minimally. You focus on adapting your pull and footwork to overcome this light resistance. (5 reps per side).
    4. Controlled Takedown Drilling (75% resistance): Partner actively tries to defend by moving their feet. You execute the Osoto Gari, aiming for a controlled takedown where the partner can safely absorb the fall. Focus on finishing the technique. (5 reps per side).
    5. Live Takedown Situational Sparring (90% resistance): Begin from a standing clinch and attempt Osoto Gari as part of a larger sequence. Partner actively defends, and you react. Focus on applying the technique under pressure. (3-minute rounds).
    6. Full Live Rolling (100% resistance): Integrate Osoto Gari attempts into regular rolling. Aim to use it when opportunities arise, accepting that not every attempt will be successful. Focus on learning from successful and unsuccessful attempts. (5-minute rounds).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is leaning forward, making their weight distribution unstable.
    • When you have established a strong collar grip and can effectively break their posture.
    • When your opponent is standing with their weight primarily on one leg, creating an opening for a sweep.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Inside Reap (Uchi Mata): If your opponent attempts an Osoto Gari, immediately step your right foot inside their right leg and perform an inside reap with your right leg, driving your right hip into their left hip to counter their sweep and potentially throw them.
    • Switch Step and Snap Down: As they initiate the Osoto Gari, step your left foot back and use your collar grip to snap their head down and to the left, simultaneously stepping your right foot forward to establish a forward base and transition to a guard pass attempt.
    • Level Change and Single Leg: If you feel the sweep coming, drop your level by bending your knees deeply and shoot your left arm across their body to secure a single-leg takedown on their right leg, disrupting their sweep and gaining control.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do Osoto Gari?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are hyperextending it during the reaping motion. This happens when you try to sweep with a stiff, straight leg instead of a controlled, slightly bent knee. Ensure you maintain a slight bend in your reaping leg and focus on rotating your hips to generate the sweep, keeping the movement fluid rather than forceful.

    Q: How can I perform Osoto Gari against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, your focus must be on using their weight against them and precise biomechanics. Establish a strong collar grip to break their posture and pull them forward, making their mass more manageable. Use your hip rotation to get your body in tight and your sweeping leg to target their ankle or lower shin, rather than trying to overpower them with leg strength alone.

    Q: When should I stop trying Osoto Gari if my opponent is defending well?

    You should stop attempting Osoto Gari if your opponent successfully establishes a solid base, has their weight distributed evenly, or is actively countering your initial posture break. Continuing to force the attempt when these conditions are met often leads to you overextending, losing your balance, or exposing yourself to a counter-takedown. Instead, transition to a different technique or focus on securing a better grip and posture.

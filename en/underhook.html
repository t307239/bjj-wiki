The Underhook: A Biomechanically Precise Guide for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Underhook is a fundamental control position, typically established from guard or Side Control, aiming to create leverage and dominate your opponent's posture.

White belts often struggle with the Underhook due to a lack of understanding of proper body mechanics, leading to ineffective grips and wasted energy.

The key mechanical insight is to use your entire body as a lever, driving your shoulder into your opponent's armpit to break their structure and control their movement.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From bottom guard, establish a closed guard on your opponent's hips with your legs.
    2. Simultaneously, extend your right arm and insert your right shoulder deep into your opponent's left armpit.
    3. Grip your own right bicep with your left hand, creating a reinforced "frame" with your right arm.
    4. Drive your right hip down and slightly towards your opponent's left hip, creating a wedge.
    5. Keep your chest tight to your opponent's chest, preventing them from posturing up.
    6. Your opponent's left arm should be trapped between your right shoulder and your chest.
    7. Maintain a stable base by keeping your feet planted on the mat or on your opponent's hips, depending on the guard.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Shoulder Insertion: Reaching with the arm only and not driving the shoulder into the armpit allows the opponent to easily pull their arm out, leading to a potential separation and loss of control.
    • Elbow Hyperextension: Trying to "pull" the opponent's arm with a straight arm can overextend your own elbow joint, risking ligamentous tears (e.g., UCL sprain).
    • Excessive Neck Flexion: Leaning the head too far forward to try and "pull" the opponent down can strain the cervical spine ligaments and muscles, leading to neck pain or injury.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Movement (0%): Practice the shoulder drive and bicep grip motion without a partner. Focus on the hip angling and chest connection. (20 reps)
    2. Partner Assisted (25%): With a partner standing, practice inserting the underhook. They offer minimal resistance, allowing you to focus on the insertion mechanics. (10 reps per side)
    3. Guard Retention Drill (50%): From closed guard, have your partner gently try to posture up. Practice maintaining the underhook and preventing them from breaking your guard. (1 minute rounds, 3 rounds)
    4. Side Control Transition Drill (75%): From side control, practice establishing the underhook to prevent your partner from escaping to guard. They actively resist. (30 second rounds, 5 rounds)
    5. Guard Passing Defense Drill (90%): From guard, practice establishing the underhook as your partner attempts a basic guard pass (e.g., knee-slice). Focus on preventing the pass. (1 minute rounds, 3 rounds)
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Integrate the underhook into your rolling. Focus on establishing and maintaining it during positional sparring. (5 minute rounds, 3 rounds)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard and their arm is within reach.
    • When you are in side control and your opponent is trying to shrimp away or create space.
    • When transitioning to mount or back control and you need to secure a dominant position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Arm Trap and Posture Control: If your opponent achieves the underhook, immediately bring your elbow to your hip and drive your shoulder into their chest to create space and prevent them from collapsing your posture.
    • Overhook Creation: If your opponent has an underhook, attempt to establish an overhook on their other arm to neutralize their leverage and create opportunities for sweeps or escapes.
    • Head and Arm Control: If the underhook is deep, use your opposite arm to secure their head and neck, forcing them to defend and potentially creating an opening to escape or transition.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to get an underhook in BJJ?

    Shoulder pain often occurs from reaching too far with your arm instead of driving your shoulder joint into the opponent's armpit. This "arm-fishing" can strain the rotator cuff. Ensure you are using your torso and hips to create the wedge, keeping your elbow slightly bent and your shoulder actively engaged in the socket.

    Q: How do I get an underhook against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is key. Don't try to muscle it. Instead, use your hip to drive into their hip, creating a slight angle. Your goal is to get your shoulder *under* their armpit, not just around it. Use your bicep grip to maintain connection and then use your legs to create space and drive.

    Q: When should I look for an underhook in BJJ, and when is it a bad idea?

    An underhook is excellent when you are on the bottom and want to prevent a pass or when you are on top in side control to maintain control and prevent escapes. It's a bad idea if you are reaching with a straight arm, as this leaves your shoulder vulnerable to attacks like kimuras or Armbars, or if you are not actively using your hips and core to create pressure.

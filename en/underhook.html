The Underhook is a fundamental BJJ control position, typically achieved from guard or during transitions, aiming to secure dominant body positioning.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength instead of precise leverage, leading to ineffective control and wasted energy.

The key mechanical insight is using your shoulder and scapula to create a strong, unmovable frame, driving your opponent's posture down and away.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a seated or kneeling position, establish a base with knees wider than shoulders. Turn your hips slightly towards your opponent.
    2. Reach your arm *under* your opponent's arm, aiming for the space between their torso and their armpit.
    3. Secure a grip on your own bicep with your opposite hand, creating a "frame" with your forearm pressed against their torso.
    4. Drive your shoulder into their chest or upper torso, using your scapula to create a strong posterior force.
    5. Simultaneously, slightly tuck your chin and drive your hips forward and slightly up, creating a lifting and pushing wedge.
    6. Maintain a narrow base with your feet or knees to prevent them from easily stepping around your hips.
    7. Keep your head up and your gaze forward, always aware of your opponent's upper body movement.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly reaching with a bent elbow and no bicep grip: This leaves your elbow vulnerable to hyperextension, potentially damaging the ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) or causing a sprain. The correct method involves securing your bicep for a stable frame.
    • Shrugging your shoulder upwards instead of driving into the torso: This creates an "air pocket" and allows the opponent to post their arm, negating your control. Drive your shoulder *into* their chest, using your scapula as a fulcrum.
    • Arching your back excessively while trying to lift: This strains your lumbar spine and provides no stable base. Instead, drive your hips forward and slightly up with a braced core.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice reaching the underhook position and securing the bicep grip, focusing on shoulder drive and hip drive without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Static partner drill: With a compliant partner, practice establishing the underhook, focusing on the precise grip and shoulder/hip pressure. (25 reps per side)
    3. Light resistance partner drill: Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the correct body mechanics and pressure. (50 reps total)
    4. Introduction to guard retention: Practice establishing and maintaining the underhook from guard against a partner attempting to pass. (5 minutes, 50% resistance)
    5. Transition drilling: Practice securing the underhook during common transitions (e.g., from failed sweep, scrambles). (10 minutes, 75% resistance)
    6. Controlled rolling: Focus on using the underhook to control posture and initiate sweeps or transitions in a live, but controlled, roll. (5 minutes, 90% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent attempts a guard pass and their arm is within reach.
    • From inside closed guard when the opponent is posturing up.
    • During scrambles and transitions where an opponent's arm is exposed.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The "Frame and Push": Immediately establish a strong frame with your forearm against their bicep and push their arm away while simultaneously driving your hips back, creating space to recover guard.
    • The "Elbow Escape": If they have a deep underhook, drive your elbow into their hip and shrimp your hips away, creating a pocket to bring your leg back into guard.
    • The "Head and Arm Control": If they secure a strong underhook, use your free arm to control their head and upper torso, preventing them from driving their shoulder into you and creating an angle to escape.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to get an underhook against a bigger opponent?

    Your shoulder likely hurts because you're trying to force the underhook with pure arm strength, which is ineffective against a larger opponent. Instead, focus on using your scapula and core. Drive your shoulder into their torso, creating a strong, stable frame, and use your hips to generate leverage. This biomechanical approach utilizes your body's larger muscle groups for power, not just your shoulder joint.

    Q: How do I get an underhook when my opponent is much heavier than me in BJJ?

    Against a heavier opponent, brute force is your enemy. Instead, emphasize precise timing and leverage. Wait for them to commit to a movement or a pass, creating a momentary opening. Then, use a quick, controlled reach, securing your bicep grip firmly. Drive your shoulder into their chest, not just their arm, and use your hips to create a wedge. Think of it as using your body as a lever, not a battering ram.

    Q: When should I prioritize getting an underhook over other grips in BJJ?

    Prioritize the underhook when your opponent is posturing up, especially from guard, as it’s crucial for controlling their upper body and preventing them from posturing effectively. It’s also a vital tool during Guard Passing attempts by your opponent, allowing you to disrupt their balance and create opportunities to recover or attack. Essentially, use it whenever you need to establish dominant control over their upper body.

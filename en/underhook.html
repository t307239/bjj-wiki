The Underhook: A White Belt's Guide to Essential BJJ Positioning

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The underhook is a fundamental position control in BJJ, typically established from guard or during scrambles, aiming to establish dominance and prevent opponent movement.

White belts often struggle with the underhook due to a lack of body awareness, leading to ineffective grips and misplaced weight, resulting in lost position or injury.

The key mechanical insight is understanding how to create a strong frame with your arm and use your hips to drive into your opponent, creating a powerful wedge.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a seated or kneeling position, approach your opponent's torso.
    2. Reach your non-dominant arm underneath their armpit, ensuring your elbow is tucked.
    3. Secure a deep grip on their far side tricep or shoulder blade with your hand, fingers pointing upwards.
    4. Simultaneously, drive your chest into their torso, creating a perpendicular angle between your bodies.
    5. Lower your hips towards the mat, creating a strong base and driving your shoulder into their chest.
    6. Use your free arm to establish a cross-collar grip or push on their hip to maintain distance and control.
    7. Keep your head up and your base wide, actively pressing your hips forward to maintain superior positioning.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Arm Extension Error: Extending your elbow fully when reaching for the underhook allows the opponent to easily break your grip and potentially attack your arm. This can lead to hyperextension of the elbow joint and ligament damage (e.g., UCL sprain).
    • Hunching Over: Rounding your back and hunching your shoulders exposes your neck and makes it difficult to generate power. This posture can lead to cervical spine strain and increased risk of choke attempts.
    • Lack of Hip Drive: Failing to drive your hips forward and instead relying solely on arm strength to maintain the underhook makes you easily susceptible to being pushed or swept. This can strain your lower back muscles (erector spinae) due to poor core engagement.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling (10 reps per side): Practice the underhook entry and hip drive motion without a partner, focusing on correct body angles and grip placement. (0% resistance)
    2. Partnered Static Grip Fighting (20 reps per side): With a compliant partner, practice securing the underhook grip and holding the position. Focus on maintaining the frame and hip drive. (25% resistance)
    3. Positional Sparring - Half Guard Entry (5 rounds, 30 sec each): Start from a neutral position and attempt to establish the underhook to enter half guard. Focus on the transition. (50% resistance)
    4. Positional Sparring - Guard Retention (5 rounds, 30 sec each): From guard, practice using the underhook to prevent your opponent from passing. Focus on hip escape and frame creation. (75% resistance)
    5. Positional Sparring - Top Control (5 rounds, 30 sec each): From a dominant position like side control, practice maintaining the underhook to control your opponent's posture and prevent escapes. (90% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling - Focused Rounds (3 rounds, 1 min each): Dedicate rounds to specifically working for and maintaining the underhook in a live rolling scenario. (100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent attempts to establish a strong cross-face or head-and-arm control from guard.
    • During scrambles when transitioning between offensive and defensive positions.
    • When your opponent is trying to flatten you out from guard or side control.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The "Whizzer" (Overhook): As your opponent attempts the underhook, immediately secure an overhook (arm over their back) and drive your shoulder into their neck/traps. This breaks their posture and allows you to counter their pressure.
    • Head Outside Single Leg Takedown: If your opponent is driving forward for an underhook, circle your hips away and secure a head-outside single leg takedown by driving your shoulder into their hip and securing their leg.
    • Grip Break and Re-establish: If the underhook is established, use your free hand to peel their grip off your tricep or shoulder. Simultaneously, use your hips to create space and circle away to break their base and re-establish your own.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to get an underhook?

    Shoulder pain often stems from trying to force the underhook with arm strength alone, rather than using your hips and core. When you reach, ensure your elbow stays tucked and your shoulder is not elevated. Drive your chest into your opponent's torso and use your hips to create a powerful wedge, rather than just extending your arm.

    Q: How can I get an underhook against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on speed and timing rather than brute force. Look for opportunities when they are slightly off-balance or shifting weight. Use your legs to shrimp and create angles, then explosively drive your shoulder into their torso as you secure the underhook grip, prioritizing a tight tricep or shoulder blade control.

    Q: When is the best time to use an underhook to sweep from guard?

    The underhook is excellent for sweeping when your opponent is posturing up or trying to apply pressure. Once you have the underhook and have broken their posture, use your hips to create a strong base and drive into them. This allows you to either off-balance them for a sweep or transition to a better position like Half Guard.

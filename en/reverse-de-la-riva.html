Reverse De La Riva Guard: A White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Reverse De La Riva Guard is an open guard position where you control one of your opponent's legs with your own leg, facing away from their hips. It aims to disrupt their base and create off-balancing opportunities for sweeps and submissions.

White belts often struggle because they lack a stable base and proper weight distribution, leading to easily being passed or falling out of position. They also tend to grip too loosely or apply force in inefficient directions.

The key mechanical insight is using your hooking leg to create a constant pulling tension against their shin while simultaneously using your free leg and hips to drive into their centerline, creating an angle for attack.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a seated open guard, establish a right de la riva hook: place your right foot on the outside of your opponent's left shin, angling your heel towards their knee. Your left leg is free to post or frame.
    2. Secure a left sleeve grip with your right hand and a left pant grip (above the knee) with your left hand. Keep your elbows tight to your body.
    3. Angle your hips slightly to your left, creating a 45-degree angle relative to your opponent's forward momentum. Your right hip should be closer to their left knee.
    4. Initiate the 'reverse' motion by driving your left hip *into* your opponent's left hip. This is a forward and slightly upward motion, not a backward lean.
    5. Simultaneously, pull their left sleeve with your right hand in a downward and slightly backward motion, keeping your elbow bent at 90 degrees.
    6. As you drive your hip and pull the sleeve, use your right de la riva hook to pull their left shin towards your chest, applying upward pressure on their ankle.
    7. Shift your weight onto your left hip, allowing your right leg to remain elevated and connected to their shin. Your body should be angled away from them, but your hips are driving forward into their space.
    8. Maintain constant tension on the sleeve and pant grip, and keep your hooking foot actively 'pulling' their shin.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect hip drive: Applying a backward lean instead of driving your hip forward into the opponent's centerline. This can lead to your knee or ankle ligaments being overextended if they push forward, causing a sprain.
    • Overextension of the hooking leg: Straightening your right leg completely while trying to hook their shin. This exposes your knee joint to hyperextension if the opponent steps back or twists, leading to ACL or MCL tears.
    • Loose grips: Holding the sleeve and pant grip with open hands or weak wrist control. This allows the opponent to easily escape the grips, negating the setup and leaving you vulnerable to a pass.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling (0%): Practice the hip drive and body angling motion without any grips or opponent. Focus on hip mobility and weight distribution. (10 reps)
    2. Solo drilling with imaginary opponent (0%): Add the sleeve and pant grip motions, simulating the pull and drive. Focus on timing and coordination. (10 reps)
    3. Partner drilling - no resistance (25%): Your partner stands, you establish grips and perform the hip drive and leg hook. Partner allows gentle movement. Focus on maintaining connection. (5 reps per side)
    4. Partner drilling - light resistance (50%): Partner offers slight resistance to your hip drive and sleeve pull. Focus on feeling the pressure and adjusting your body. (5 reps per side)
    5. Partner drilling - controlled pass attempt (75%): Partner attempts a basic guard pass (e.g., knee slice). You practice maintaining the Reverse De La Riva hook and hip angle to prevent the pass. (5 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling - focus on position (90%): In rolling, specifically aim to enter Reverse De La Riva. Focus on establishing the grips and hip angle, and preventing immediate passes. Do not focus on submissions yet. (2 minute rounds)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up while you are in a closed or half guard, creating space to open your guard and establish the hook.
    • When your opponent is attempting to pass your open guard from a distance, allowing you to intercept a leg.
    • When your opponent is applying pressure and trying to stack you, and you can use the Reverse De La Riva hook to create an angle and off-balance them.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Pass: If the opponent steps their hips away and begins to drive forward, immediately transition to a closed guard or attempt a sweep. Do not let your hooking leg get stuck.
    • Standing Up: If the opponent tries to stand up and pull their leg free, use your sleeve grip to control their posture and transition to a single leg takedown or a sweep attempt.
    • Knee on the Mat: If the opponent flattens your hooking leg by placing their knee on the mat, use your free leg to post and push off their hip, creating space to re-establish your guard or transition to a different position.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my right hip hurt when I try to do Reverse De La Riva?

    Your right hip likely hurts because you are not properly driving your left hip *into* your opponent's centerline. Instead, you are likely leaning back, which puts excessive strain on your hip flexors and can lead to impingement. Focus on a forward and upward hip drive, keeping your core engaged and your back relatively straight.

    Q: How do I use Reverse De La Riva against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a bigger opponent, leverage is key. Focus on using your sleeve and pant grips to control their posture and prevent them from simply smashing you. Drive your hip into their centerline with as much force as possible to off-balance them. Your hooking leg should be actively pulling their shin towards your chest to maintain the connection and disrupt their base.

    Q: When should I start using Reverse De La Riva instead of regular De La Riva?

    You should transition to Reverse De La Riva when your opponent is facing you directly and you want to attack their lead leg, or when they are trying to step around your regular De La Riva hook. It's also effective when you want to create a more aggressive angle for sweeps and submissions by driving into their space rather than away from it. The core principle is to use your hips to drive *into* their base.

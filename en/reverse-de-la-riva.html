Reverse De La Riva: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Reverse De La Riva Guard is an open-guard position where you control one of your opponent's legs with your own leg, facing away from them. It allows for sweeps and submissions by creating off-balancing angles.

White belts often struggle because they lack precise body control and rely on brute force rather than leverage. This leads to poor positioning and potential injury.

The key insight is using your hooking leg to create a fulcrum, allowing you to pivot your hips and unbalance your opponent with minimal effort. Your off-hooking leg acts as a brace.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Opponent is in your guard, typically standing or kneeling. Establish a strong collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (left sleeve) with your left hand.
    2. Shift your hips to your left, creating a 45-degree angle relative to your opponent's centerline. Your right knee should point slightly towards your opponent.
    3. Swing your right leg behind your opponent's right thigh, aiming to hook the back of their knee with your shin and ankle. This is the 'reverse de la riva hook'.
    4. Grip your opponent's left ankle with your left hand, palm facing up. Your left elbow should be bent, creating a frame.
    5. Your right foot should be flat on the mat, acting as a base and pivot point. Your left foot is free or can lightly brace against their hip.
    6. Maintain tension on the collar and sleeve grips, pulling them slightly towards you to draw them forward and off balance.
    7. As they step or lean forward, drive your left foot into the mat and pivot your hips counter-clockwise, using your right leg's hook to elevate and sweep them to their left.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Grabbing the opponent's foot with your hand instead of hooking their knee with your shin: This puts excessive strain on your wrist and elbow joints, potentially causing sprains or tendonitis. The correct action is to use your shin to hook behind their knee for stable control.
    • Letting your hooking leg drop or lose its connection: This compromises your base and allows the opponent to easily step out or pass your guard, risking knee hyperextension if you try to force the sweep. Maintain constant pressure with your shin and ankle against their thigh.
    • Overextending your hooking leg in an attempt to 'pull' them: This can lead to a knee injury as your knee joint is forced beyond its natural range of motion. Instead, focus on pivoting your hips and using your free leg to drive their hip away.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg placement and hip movement without an opponent. Focus on the hook and the hip pivot. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Partner stands or kneels. You practice establishing the hook and grips, then executing the hip pivot. Partner offers no resistance. (20 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers light pressure to resist your attempts to establish the hook. You focus on breaking their posture and securing the position. (30 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Sweep attempts (light resistance): Partner attempts to defend by posturing up or stepping away. You practice your timing to initiate the sweep when they offer an opening. (40 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Guard retention and sweep attempts: Partner tries to pass your guard. You focus on maintaining the Reverse De La Riva hook and attempting sweeps when opportunities arise. (5 minutes, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the Reverse De La Riva into your rolling. Focus on establishing the position and looking for sweep opportunities. (3 rounds, 90-100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up from your closed guard and you want to create space to open.
    • When your opponent is trying to pass your guard from a standing or kneeling position, and you can intercept a leg.
    • When you are in your De La Riva guard and your opponent switches their weight or base, creating an opportunity to reverse the hook.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Pummel (Inside/Outside): If the opponent tries to step their leg out, use your free leg to pummel inside or outside their leg, re-establishing control or transitioning to a different guard.
    • Hip Escape and Frame: If they are pressuring heavily, hip escape away and use your forearm to frame against their hip or chest to create space and reset your position.
    • Stand Up and Disengage: If they successfully break your hook, immediately stand up, create distance, and reset to a neutral guard or attempt a takedown.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do Reverse De La Riva?

    Your wrist likely hurts because you are incorrectly gripping your opponent's foot with your hand when you should be using your shin and ankle to hook their knee. This hand grip puts direct pressure and torque on your wrist and elbow, risking injury. Focus on the biomechanical action of using your leg to wrap around their thigh and behind their knee, using your hand for a lighter ankle grip or to control their pants.

    Q: How do I use Reverse De La Riva against a bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, the Reverse De La Riva becomes even more about leverage and timing than raw strength. Focus on getting your hook deep behind their knee to control their base. Use your grips to pull their weight forward and off-balance, creating an angle where their size is less of an advantage. Your goal is to pivot your hips and use their momentum against them, rather than trying to overpower them.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from De La Riva to Reverse De La Riva?

    The optimal time to switch from De La Riva to Reverse De La Riva is when your opponent attempts to step their leg out of your De La Riva hook, or when they shift their weight significantly to one side, creating an opening. You can also initiate the switch proactively if you feel your opponent is becoming too heavy on your De La Riva hook, by quickly pivoting your hips and re-hooking their leg from the opposite side.

This guard starts from an open guard scenario, aiming to control your opponent's base and off-balance them for sweeps or transitions.

White belts often struggle by overextending their limbs or creating insufficient frame, leading to easily passed guards.

The key is using your leg as a lever to subtly shift their weight and disrupt their balance, not brute force.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From an open guard, establish a Spider Guard Grip on the opponent's sleeve with your non-hooking hand, and a Collar Grip (opposite side lapel) with your hooking hand.
    2. Place your Right Foot (if right-handed) on the opponent's Left Hip crease, knee slightly bent, foot flexed.
    3. Your Left Leg hooks Behind the opponent's Right Leg, specifically around their hamstring or calf area, creating a "hook".
    4. Actively push your Right Foot into the hip to create distance and frame, while simultaneously pulling with your Spider Guard Grip to break their posture.
    5. Rotate your hips slightly Counter-clockwise to create an angle, bringing your Left Knee towards your Right Shoulder.
    6. Use your Left Hook to Sweep their right leg Outward and Upward, while simultaneously Driving your Right Foot into their hip.
    7. As they fall, transition your Left Hand to their Far Hip or Pants for control and to prevent them from posturing back up.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Gripping too tightly with your Spider Guard Grip on the sleeve: This can lead to Wrist sprains or Elbow hyperextension if the opponent forcefully pulls away. Instead, maintain a firm but flexible grip, ready to absorb their movement.
    • Extending your Hooking Leg straight: This exposes your Knee joint to Ligament tears (ACL/MCL) if the opponent steps over or applies pressure. Keep your hooking knee slightly bent and tucked.
    • Trying to lift the opponent with your legs: This can cause Lumbar strain or Hip flexor tears due to excessive force. Focus on off-balancing by pushing with your foot on the hip and sweeping with the hook.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Foot Placement Drill: Practice placing your right foot on the hip and left leg hooking behind the opponent's leg. 10 repetitions each side.
    2. Solo Hip Rotation and Frame Drill: From the hooked position, practice the hip rotation and pushing with the foot on the hip. 10 repetitions each side.
    3. Assisted Hook Sweep Drill (0% Resistance): Partner stands, you execute the hook sweep motion without them resisting. 5 repetitions each side.
    4. Assisted Sweep and Control Drill (25% Resistance): Partner offers light resistance to the sweep, you complete it and practice holding the top leg. 5 repetitions each side.
    5. Guard Retention Drill (50% Resistance): Partner attempts to pass, you focus on maintaining the Reverse De La Riva hook and framing. 3 minutes.
    6. Live Rolling (75-90% Resistance): Integrate the Reverse De La Riva into sparring, focusing on smooth transitions and maintaining the guard.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent steps forward with their outside leg, creating an opening for your hook.
    • When the opponent is posturing forward, making their hips accessible for your foot frame.
    • When transitioning from a failed sweep or a scramble where you end up in an open guard scenario.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee-Slice Pass Attempt: If they try to drive their knee across your body, use your hooking leg to "sit up" and bring your hips back, creating space and re-establishing the hook.
    • Stack Pass Attempt: If they try to stack you, push hard with your foot on the hip and use your hooking leg to elevate their leg, forcing them to back off.
    • Standing Up: If they try to stand up and disengage, use your Spider Guard grip to pull them back down or transition to a single leg takedown attempt.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my right knee hurt when I try to set up Reverse De La Riva?

    This often occurs if your right knee is collapsing inward (valgus stress) when you place your foot on the opponent's hip. Ensure your foot is flexed, and actively push with the heel, keeping your knee tracking over your second toe. This creates a stable frame and protects the medial collateral ligament (MCL).

    Q: How do I use Reverse De La Riva effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paraMount. Focus on creating a strong frame with your foot on their hip to prevent them from smashing you. Use your hooking leg to control their base and subtly shift their weight, rather than attempting to lift them. Prioritize sweeps that exploit their momentum, like a sweep that uses their own forward pressure against them.

    Q: While a Spider Guard grip on the sleeve is common and effective for breaking posture, it's not the only option. You can also use a **Collar Grip** or a **Wrist Grip** on their sleeve. The key is to have a grip that allows you to control their upper body and pull them into your sweep, ensuring you maintain your own balance.

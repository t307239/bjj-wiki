Reverse De La Riva: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

The Reverse De La Riva Guard is an open guard position where one leg is hooked behind the opponent's thigh, and the other is placed on their hip. It allows for sweeps and transitions to attacks.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body awareness and an impulse to push or pull without controlling frames and angles.

The key to Reverse De La Riva is using your hooked leg to control distance and your free leg to establish a strong hip frame, creating leverage for sweeps.

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From an open guard, establish a **right sleeve grip** on the opponent's left arm and a **left pant grip** on their left thigh.
    2. Angle your hips to your left, creating a 45-degree angle relative to the opponent's forward-facing position.
    3. Place your right foot on the opponent's left hip, creating a frame with your shin perpendicular to the mat.
    4. Hook your left foot's instep behind the opponent's right thigh, just above the knee, ensuring your shin is parallel to the mat.
    5. Maintain a slight bend in your left knee, keeping tension on the hook.
    6. Shift your weight onto your left hip, keeping your back relatively straight and your core engaged.
    7. Use your right foot on the hip to push and create space, while simultaneously pulling the opponent's sleeve grip to unbalance them to their left.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Hyperextending the knee: Reaching too far with the hooking leg and straightening the knee under pressure can cause ACL or MCL tears. Always maintain a slight bend in the hooking knee.
    • Cervical spine strain: Tilting the head backward excessively when the opponent stacks or drives forward can lead to neck injuries. Keep your chin tucked and maintain a neutral spine.
    • Wrist sprains: Gripping too tightly with an open palm and allowing the opponent to twist your wrist can cause ligament damage. Focus on a strong grip with fingers and thumb, maintaining a stable wrist angle.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hook Drill: Practice hooking your left leg behind your right thigh, then switching. Repeat 20 times per leg. (0% resistance).
    2. Hip Frame Drill: From your back, practice placing your right foot on a partner's hip and extending your leg to create a frame. Partner stands passively. 10 reps. (0% resistance).
    3. Combined Hook and Frame: From your back, practice establishing the Reverse De La Riva hook and hip frame against a passive partner's static stance. Hold for 10 seconds. 10 reps. (0% resistance).
    4. Controlled Sweep Setup: With a partner, practice initiating the sweep motion by pulling the sleeve and pushing the hip frame. Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to complete the sweep. 5 reps per side. (25% resistance).
    5. Guard Retention and Sweep Attempt: Partner actively tries to pass. Focus on maintaining the hook and frame, and attempt one sweep. Partner resists the sweep but doesn't aggressively pass. 5 reps per side. (50% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling with Focus: Implement Reverse De La Riva during rolling, aiming for sweep attempts. Partner will actively defend and attempt to pass. 2 minute rounds, 3 rounds. (75-90% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is posturing forward and creating space.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your closed guard or half guard, opening up their legs.
    • When you feel the opponent is off-balance and leaning too far forward.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee Slice Pass: When you feel the opponent's hook is weak, the defender can drive their knee across the opponent's body, aiming to slice it through to establish side control.
    • Stack Pass: The defender can use their hands to grip the opponent's shoulders, drive their weight forward, and stack the opponent's hips, collapsing the guard.
    • Leg Drag: The defender can reach across and grip the opponent's free leg (the one not hooked), pulling it to their side to establish a leg drag position and pass.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my back hurt when I try to set up Reverse De La Riva?

    Your back pain likely stems from rounding your lumbar spine excessively while trying to create the hook and frame. Instead, maintain a neutral spine and engage your core. Think of pushing your hips *into* the opponent's leg with your hooked foot, rather than arching your back to reach.

    Q: How do I maintain Reverse De La Riva against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, your frames become even more critical. Ensure your hip frame (your foot on their hip) is solid and perpendicular to the mat, preventing them from easily collapsing onto you. Use your sleeve grip to control their posture and create angles, preventing them from simply walking through your legs.

    Q: When should I switch from De La Riva to Reverse De La Riva?

    You typically switch to Reverse De La Riva when the opponent's weight shifts to their lead leg and they are facing away or partially turned from you, creating an opportunity to hook the *opposite* leg. It's a reaction to their movement and body positioning, not a predetermined sequence.

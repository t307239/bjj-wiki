Reverse De La Riva Guard: A White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Reverse De La Riva Guard is an open guard position used to control an opponent's leg from the outside, creating off-balancing opportunities. It's initiated when your opponent is in front of you, typically after they have attempted to pass your guard.

White belts often struggle with Reverse De La Riva because they lack the foundational body awareness to maintain structure and frame effectively. They tend to overextend or rely on brute strength instead of leverage.

The core principle is to use your outside leg as a hook and your inside leg to control distance, creating a dynamic off-balancing platform through hip rotation and weight shifts.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip: Secure a right sleeve grip with your left hand, and a right pant grip (just above the knee) with your right hand. Your opponent is facing you, slightly angled to their left.
    2. Initial Position: Sit up, with your right hip slightly closer to your opponent. Your left leg is extended, creating space.
    3. Hook Placement: Swing your right leg across your opponent's right leg, placing your right foot on their outer thigh, heel facing their hip. This is your 'hook'.
    4. Frame & Control: Extend your left leg to control distance, placing your left foot on your opponent's left hip or thigh. Use your left arm to frame against their right hip or upper thigh.
    5. Hip Angle: Rotate your hips to the left, creating an angle away from your opponent. Your base will be on your left hip.
    6. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your left hip, allowing your right leg to become lighter and more mobile.
    7. Opponent's Weight: Your opponent's weight will naturally be distributed forward as they try to pressure through your guard. You are using their forward momentum against them.
    8. Leverage Point: The point of leverage is your right foot hooked on their outer thigh, combined with your left leg pushing on their hip/thigh.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Error: Extending your left leg too far forward and pushing with your foot directly on the opponent's knee. Damage: High risk of hyperextension of the opponent's knee or your own ankle if they shift weight unexpectedly. Correction: Keep your left leg bent and use your foot to push on their hip or thigh, maintaining a controlled frame.
    • Error: Allowing your right foot hook to slip down towards the opponent's ankle and gripping their ankle directly. Damage: Increased risk of ankle locks for you, or a potential knee injury if they step over your hooked leg incorrectly. Correction: Maintain the hook high on the outer thigh, with your heel facing their hip.
    • Error: Turning your hips square to the opponent and pulling them towards you with your sleeve grip. Damage: This can lead to your opponent easily stacking you or passing your guard. It also strains your lower back. Correction: Maintain your angled hip position and use your grips to subtly shift their weight, not to pull them directly into you.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Setup (0%): Practice the hip rotation and leg swing to establish the hook and frame from a seated position. 10 reps each side.
    2. Partner Assisted Setup (25%): Your partner stands passively. Practice establishing your right hook on their right leg and framing with your left leg. 5 reps each side.
    3. Establishing the Hook (50%): Partner takes a step towards you. Practice swinging your right leg to hook their outer thigh. Focus on the hip rotation. 5 reps each side.
    4. Maintaining Frame and Hook (75%): Partner attempts a light pressure pass. Focus on maintaining your right hook and left leg frame. Resist their initial movement. 5 reps each side.
    5. Controlled Sweep Attempt (90%): With passive partner, practice initiating a sweep by shifting weight and using the hook to off-balance. Focus on the hip movement. 5 reps each side.
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Integrate Reverse De La Riva into sparring. Focus on establishing the guard and attempting one sweep or transition per round. 1 round.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent attempts a knee-cut pass or leg-drag pass, and you can intercept their leg.
    • When your opponent is posturing up and you have an opportunity to drop your hook.
    • When you are in open guard and your opponent is too close for a traditional open guard but not in a passing position yet.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: If the white belt's hips are too square and they attempt to pull you directly in, step your right leg over their head and stack them by driving your shoulder into their hip. Your left leg should push their hip away.
    • Knee Slice Pass: If the white belt's hook is loose or they don't maintain frame, step your right knee to the mat inside their left leg and drive your left knee forward to slice past their guard. Use your hands to push their hook off.
    • Leg Drag: If the white belt's hook is not secure and their leg is exposed, step your right foot across their body, grab their left ankle with your right hand, and drag their leg across their body while your left hand secures their hip.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my right hip hurt when I try to set up Reverse De La Riva?

    Your hip pain likely stems from your pelvis being too square to your opponent, creating a pinching effect. Ensure you rotate your hips to the left *before* swinging your right leg over. This creates space and allows your hip to pivot rather than being compressed. Think of it as sitting on your left hip at an angle.

    Q: How do I effectively use Reverse De La Riva against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, the key is controlling distance and timing. Use your left leg frame to create space and prevent them from crushing you. Focus on hooking their outer thigh *high* and using your hip rotation to off-balance them. Don't try to pull them; instead, use their forward pressure against them by subtly shifting your weight and using the hook to drive their knee towards their face.

    Q: I keep losing my Reverse De La Riva hook, what am I doing wrong?

    Your hook is likely slipping because you're not creating enough hip rotation or you're not cupping the opponent's thigh properly. Ensure your left hip is grounded and rotated towards your opponent. When you swing your right leg, aim to cup the *outside* of their thigh, with your heel pointing towards their hip. This creates a strong anchor point that is harder to dislodge. Don't just place your foot there; actively grip the fabric.

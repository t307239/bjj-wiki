Reverse De La Riva Guard: A White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

Reverse De La Riva is an open guard position where you control one of your opponent's legs with your own leg across their hip, facing away from them.

White belts often struggle by overextending or losing their base, making them vulnerable to passes.

The key is to maintain a strong frame and hip connection while using your controlling leg as a lever to disrupt their balance.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a seated open guard, establish a standard De La Riva hook with your left leg on the opponent's right thigh, knee pointed upwards.
    2. Simultaneously, place your right foot on the opponent's right hip crease, toes pointing towards their head.
    3. Grip the opponent's left ankle with your left hand, thumb inside the cuff for maximum control.
    4. Grip the opponent's right pants near the knee with your right hand, ensuring a strong connection.
    5. Angle your hips 45 degrees away from the opponent, creating space and preventing them from stacking you.
    6. Keep your left knee tight to your chest, creating a strong base and preventing leg entanglement.
    7. Use your right foot on their hip to push and create angles, while your left ankle grip pulls their leg towards you.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Extending your left leg straight out to hook their leg: This overstretches the knee joint and can lead to an ACL or MCL tear. Instead, keep your left knee bent and close to your body.
    • Allowing your opponent to step their hips into yours: This crushes your base and compromises your structure, risking lower back injury. Maintain a consistent distance with your right foot on their hip.
    • Gripping too loosely on the ankle or pants: This allows them to easily escape the control, potentially leading to a pass and a scramble where you could fall awkwardly, risking a twisted ankle or knee.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Isolation Drill: Practice placing your right foot on the hip and maintaining a 45-degree hip angle. (5 minutes, 0% resistance).
    2. Partnered Static Grip Drill: Opponent stands, you are seated. Practice establishing the De La Riva hook and the right foot on hip, gripping ankle and pants. (5 minutes per side, 10% resistance).
    3. Controlled Movement Drill: Opponent slowly walks forward and backward. You maintain the Reverse De La Riva position and adjust your hip angle. (5 minutes per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Basic Sweep Attempt (No Resistance): From the established Reverse De La Riva, practice initiating a sweep by pushing with the right foot and pulling with the left ankle grip. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Live Entry Drill: Start from a neutral seated guard. Attempt to enter Reverse De La Riva against minimal resistance. Focus on establishing grips and position. (5 minutes, 75% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling Entry: Focus on entering Reverse De La Riva during live rolling. Prioritize maintaining your structure and control over immediate submission or sweep. (5 minutes, 90% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up and their weight is forward, creating an opportunity to off-balance.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your open guard from a distance, allowing you to intercept their leg.
    • When you are transitioning from a closed guard and want to create dynamic open guard options.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee Slice Pass Defense: If they attempt a knee slice, maintain your right foot on their hip and use your left leg to push their knee away, creating space to reset.
    • Torreando Pass Defense: If they try to circle around, maintain your hip angle and use your right foot to block their hip movement, making it difficult for them to gain side control.
    • Standing Base Reset: If they try to step over your legs, immediately bring your legs back to a closed guard or shrimp your hips to create space and regain a strong defensive posture.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my left knee hurt when I try to enter Reverse De La Riva?

    Your left knee is likely hurting because you are overextending your leg to establish the De La Riva hook. Instead of straightening your leg, keep your left knee bent and close to your body, creating a secure hook with your shin and foot. This protects your knee joint from hyperextension and injury.

    Q: How do I maintain Reverse De La Riva against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, your hip angle and frame become even more critical. Keep your hips angled 45 degrees away and use your right foot on their hip as a strong, constant pushing frame. Grip their ankle and pants firmly, and use your body to create leverage rather than relying solely on brute strength. Focus on disrupting their base and balance.

    Q: When should I switch from De La Riva to Reverse De La Riva?

    You typically switch from De La Riva to Reverse De La Riva when your opponent is posturing up or trying to step their leg inside your De La Riva hook. The movement involves switching your hooking leg and placing your free foot on their hip, which is a natural transition to create new angles and offensive opportunities.

The Reverse De La Riva Guard is an open guard position designed to control your opponent's legs from an inverted angle, facilitating sweeps and transitions.

White belts often struggle by over-relying on strength or incorrect body positioning, leading to loss of control or injury.

The key is establishing a strong leg entanglement and using your hips to create angles and off-balance your opponent.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Starting from a seated open guard, establish a Left De La Riva hook with your right foot on the opponent's left thigh (anterior aspect).
    2. Simultaneously, place your opponent's left ankle lightly with your left hand, creating a loose grip.
    3. Shift your hips to your left, angling your pelvis approximately 45 degrees away from your opponent's center.
    4. With your right leg, reach across your opponent's body and secure a Left Outside De La Riva hook on their right thigh (posterior aspect).
    5. Your left foot should now be positioned behind your opponent's right knee, ready to peel their leg.
    6. Grip your opponent's left sleeve with your right hand, creating a frame to control their posture.
    7. Drive your left hip into your opponent's left hip, creating a wedge and initiating an off-balancing pressure.
    8. Extend your right leg slightly to push their left knee away, while simultaneously pulling their left sleeve to break their base.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Hyperextending Knee/Ankle: Grabbing the opponent's foot with your left hand and pulling their leg directly towards you without proper hip angle can force hyperextension of your own knee or ankle ligaments.
    • Neck Strain from Poor Posture: When transitioning, looking directly up at your opponent's face while your hips are not properly aligned can strain your cervical spine.
    • Shoulder Impingement: Attempting to grip their sleeve with your right hand by reaching over their shoulder without establishing a frame can lead to your shoulder being trapped or injured.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Mobility Drill: Perform 10 repetitions of hip escapes to the left and right, focusing on pelvic tilt and weight transfer without any resistance.
    2. Static Grip and Hook Placement: With a partner kneeling, practice establishing the De La Riva hook and Reverse De La Riva hook without movement (20 repetitions each side).
    3. Controlled Hook and Frame: Partner remains still. Practice placing the Reverse De La Riva hook and establishing the sleeve grip, then transitioning to the other side (10 repetitions each side).
    4. Assisted Leg Peel: Partner gently resists as you peel their leg with your left foot while maintaining hooks and sleeve grip (30 seconds, 5 repetitions).
    5. Controlled Sweep Attempt: Partner offers minimal resistance (25%). Practice initiating the sweep by driving your hip and pulling the sleeve (10 repetitions each side).
    6. Live Rolling (50% Resistance): Focus solely on maintaining and transitioning into the Reverse De La Riva guard, attempting sweeps only when the opportunity is clear.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent establishes a strong cross-collar grip and is pressuring forward.
    • When your opponent is trying to pass your guard from a knee-on-the-mat position.
    • When you have successfully framed and created space from your closed guard.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass Defense: If your opponent attempts to stack you, immediately widen your base and drive your hips towards the mat, creating space and preventing them from collapsing your guard.
    • Leg Peel Defense: If your opponent tries to peel your Reverse De La Riva hook, quickly bring your knee closer to your chest and use your free foot to block their passing leg.
    • Knee-Cut Pass Defense: As they attempt a knee-cut, shift your hips to the side of their passing knee and use your sleeve grip to pull their upper body across your body.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my left knee hurt when I try to set up Reverse De La Riva?

    Your left knee likely hurts because you are not properly angling your hips. When you place your Reverse De La Riva hook, your pelvis should be rotated approximately 45 degrees away from your opponent. This rotation prevents your knee from being forced into a valgus (inward) stress position, which can injure the MCL. Ensure your hips are leading the movement, not just your leg.

    Q: How do I use Reverse De La Riva effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on using your hips to create angles and break their base rather than relying on pure pulling strength. Your left hip acts as a wedge against their hip, and your right sleeve grip is used to off-balance their upper body. Continuously shift your weight to their unsupported side to create opportunities for sweeps like the Berimbolo or simple leg sweeps.

    Q: The optimal time to transition from Closed Guard to Reverse De La Riva is when your opponent is actively trying to break your guard by posturing up or stepping their legs in. As they create this space and forward pressure, you can use your legs to create distance and entangle their legs, turning their forward momentum into an opportunity to establish your Reverse De La Riva.

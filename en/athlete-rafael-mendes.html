Rafael Mendes

BJJ Athlete6x World ChampionBlack Belt

Rafael Mendes is a Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu black belt, 6x IBJJF World Champion, and co-founder of Art of Jiu-Jitsu (AOJ) Academy in Costa Mesa, California. Along with his brother Guilherme, Rafael became synonymous with the berimbolo — a De La Riva-based inverted rotation to the back — and dominated the featherweight division for nearly a decade. He is widely considered the most technically refined practitioner of his era and one of the greatest lightweights of all time.

Biography

Rafael Mendes grew up training BJJ in Araraquara, São Paulo under Cicero Costha alongside his brother Guilherme. The brothers moved to the United States and co-founded Art of Jiu-Jitsu Academy (AOJ) in Costa Mesa, California — which became one of the premier competition BJJ academies in North America.

Rafael won World Championships in 2009, 2010, 2011, 2012, 2014, and 2015 at featherweight, cementing his legacy as the most dominant featherweight of the berimbolo era. He retired from competition around 2015–2016 to focus on coaching at AOJ, which has produced numerous World Champions under his and Guilherme's guidance.

Technical Style

Rafael Mendes was known for an extraordinarily precise and systematic BJJ game. Key elements:

  • Berimbolo mastery: Rafael was the most technically refined berimbolo practitioner in competition history. His execution was clean, fast, and nearly impossible to defend for competitors who had not specifically prepared for it.
  • De La Riva guard: The berimbolo begins from DLR, and Rafael's DLR control was the foundation of his entire guard game.
  • Back control precision: After taking the back via berimbolo, Rafael's rear naked choke finishing was highly technical — patient seatbelt control and methodical choke application.
  • Guard passing: Rafael's passing was less celebrated than his guard game but equally effective — technical knee slice, torreando, and back-step combinations.
  • Submission precision: Rear naked choke from back, triangle from guard, and kimura were his primary finish weapons.

Berimbolo System

The berimbolo (from De La Riva guard — the guard player inverts under the passer's leg to take their back) was not invented by Rafael Mendes, but he and Guilherme systematized and popularized it to a level that fundamentally changed how featherweight BJJ was played worldwide.

Rafael's berimbolo mechanics:

  • DLR hook engagement: Establish deep De La Riva hook on the opponent's posting leg.
  • Back grip: Grip the back of the opponent's shorts or belt (gi) with the far hand.
  • Inversion: Invert under the opponent — roll backward and under their leg, getting your hips above their hip line.
  • Back take: Complete the rotation and establish back control — ideally with both hooks in or at minimum seatbelt control.

Against opponents prepared for the berimbolo, Rafael would flow to alternate back-take paths, triangle attacks, or leg locks — making his game difficult to gameplan against.

Art of Jiu-Jitsu (AOJ)

AOJ, co-founded by Rafael and Guilherme Mendes, is one of the most successful competition BJJ academies in North America. The academy has produced multiple World Champions and is a pipeline for high-level competition BJJ. Rafael's coaching focuses on the systematic, positional approach to BJJ that characterized his competition career.

Championship Record

Rafael Mendes won IBJJF World Championships in 2009, 2010, 2011, 2012, 2014, and 2015 at featherweight — 6 black belt world titles. He also won multiple Pan-Americans, Brasileiros, and ADCC titles. His 2015 World Championship was particularly notable as he was already largely focused on coaching at that point.

Legacy

Rafael Mendes's legacy is the berimbolo era — a period in BJJ (roughly 2009–2016) when the berimbolo dominated lightweight competition and changed how guard play was understood worldwide. He is also a highly successful coach whose AOJ academy continues to produce world-class competitors. His systematic, precise approach to BJJ remains a model for technical development.

⚡ Learning from Rafael Mendes

  • Study his berimbolo from both the setup (DLR engagement) and the inversion mechanics — many practitioners only learn the inversion without mastering the entry.
  • His De La Riva guard philosophy: the DLR hook controls the posting leg, and everything flows from that control.
  • Watch his back control finishing sequences — methodical, patient, and technically precise RNC application.
  • AOJ (Art of Jiu-Jitsu) has released excellent instructional content and competition footage online.
Q: Did Rafael Mendes invent the berimbolo?

No — the berimbolo was developed and used before Rafael Mendes, but he and his brother Guilherme systematized, refined, and popularized it to such a degree that they are most associated with it in competitive BJJ. Their execution made the berimbolo a standard technique in lightweight gi competition worldwide.

Q: Is Rafael Mendes still competing?

Rafael Mendes largely retired from competition around 2015–2016 to focus on coaching at Art of Jiu-Jitsu (AOJ) Academy. He has made occasional returns but is primarily known as a coach and instructor at this stage of his career.

Inside Heel Hook: A White Belt's Biomechanically Precise Guide

This technique attacks the opponent's heel from the inside, applying pressure to the knee and ankle joints.

White belts often rush this, forgetting control and focusing only on the leg, leading to injury and loss of position.

The key is isolating the heel and creating a fulcrum with your hips against their ankle, using your shoulder as a brace.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a 50-50 guard or similar leg entanglement, establish a "honeyhole" grip: your left hand grips the opponent's right foot's instep, thumb pointing towards their heel.
    2. Your right arm wraps under their calf, securing a "bicep-slicer" grip around their lower leg, forearm pressing into their shin.
    3. Sit up, pivoting your hips so your left hip is angled towards their knee and your right hip is angled towards their ankle.
    4. Drive your right shoulder into their hip to prevent them from turning away or stacking you, creating a stable base.
    5. Extend your legs, straightening your body to create tension.
    6. Simultaneously, curl your left leg upwards, pulling their heel towards your chest while keeping your right arm locked.
    7. Apply pressure by driving your hips forward and slightly upward, creating a rotational force on the ankle and a hyperextension force on the knee.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying the "honeyhole" grip with fingers wrapped around the sole of the foot, instead of thumb on the instep: This allows the opponent's foot to easily slip out, and can lead to a wrist sprain for the attacker if the opponent resists by pulling hard.
    • Failing to establish shoulder pressure against the opponent's hip: This allows the opponent to bridge or stack, creating dangerous torque on the attacker's knee and ankle, potentially leading to ACL or MCL tears.
    • Rushing the finish by "pulling hard" without proper hip extension: This ineffective motion puts excessive strain on the attacker's own lower back and shoulders, while not generating enough force to secure the submission and increasing risk of joint injury.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the "honeyhole" grip and bicep-slicer grip placement on a training dummy or your own leg, focusing on hip angles and shoulder placement. (50 reps per side)
    2. Static partner drill: With a compliant partner, transition to the Inside Heel Hook position from a pre-set entanglement (e.g., 50-50). Focus on establishing grips and body positioning without any resistance. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    3. Controlled movement drill: Partner offers minimal resistance (e.g., does not actively defend). Focus on executing the full sequence from grip to finish. (10 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Active defense drill: Partner attempts basic escapes (e.g., turning hips). You focus on maintaining position and adjusting grips. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Submission attempt drill: Partner actively defends the heel hook attempt. You work to secure the submission or transition if they escape. (5 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Implement the Inside Heel Hook as a submission option during rolling, focusing on positional control and timing before committing to the submission attempt. (Start with 1-2 minute rounds, 90-100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have established control in the 50-50 guard, ensuring both your legs are on the same side as the opponent's attacked leg.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard and their leg becomes available for entanglement.
    • When you are in a leg entanglement position and can isolate one of the opponent's legs, creating an opportunity to transition to the Inside Heel Hook.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Escape to 411/Honeyhole: If your opponent attempts an Inside Heel Hook, immediately try to spin your hips towards their head and place your free leg inside their thigh to create a "411" (or "honeyhole") position, breaking the torque on your heel.
    • Leg Drag Escape: If the opponent has a strong grip, attempt to "leg drag" their attacking leg by stepping your hips back and driving your chest onto their thigh, while simultaneously pulling their heel towards your opposite hip.
    • "Sit Out" and Turn In: As the heel hook is being applied, sit up rapidly and turn your hips into the opponent, driving your heel into their hip and attempting to create space to uncurl your leg. This often requires a strong base and a quick reaction.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try to do an Inside Heel Hook?

    Your ankle is likely hurting because you are applying the pressure incorrectly. Instead of isolating the heel and creating a fulcrum with your hips, you might be trying to twist their whole leg. Ensure your "honeyhole" grip is secure on the heel and your hips are driving forward and slightly up to create the correct rotational force at the ankle joint.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paramount. Focus on securing a very tight "honeyhole" grip on their heel and a solid "bicep-slicer" grip on their calf. Your shoulder must be firmly planted on their hip to prevent them from using their weight to simply crush you. Extend your body fully to create tension, and use your hip drive as the primary force, rather than trying to out-muscle them.

    Q: The Inside Heel Hook is best attempted when you have established a dominant leg entanglement, such as the 50-50 guard, or when you have successfully isolated one of your opponent's legs during a scramble or guard pass attempt. Trying to force it without proper control will likely result in you losing position or giving up an advantageous situation.

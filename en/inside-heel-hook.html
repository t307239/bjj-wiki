Mastering the Inside Heel Hook in BJJ: A White Belt Guide

BROWN★★★★★Expert
Brown Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Brown Belt Requirements

The Inside Heel Hook is a submission targeting the opponent's ankle joint, primarily initiated from positions where you can isolate a leg, like the 50-50 guard. It aims to hyperextend the knee and rotate the ankle past its natural range of motion.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of limb mechanics and a tendency to use brute strength instead of leverage. This leads to missed opportunities and potential injury to themselves or their training partners.

The single most crucial mechanical insight is controlling the opponent's hip and knee while creating a fulcrum with your own body to apply torque to the ankle, not just pulling.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Starting Position: Achieve a position where you have secured one of your opponent's legs, ideally with you on your hip and your opponent's leg trapped between your thighs (e.g., transitioning to 50-50 guard).
    2. Secure the Leg: Place your arm closest to the opponent's hip across their thigh, just above the knee, with your hand gripping your own bicep (e.g., "S-grip" or "figure-four grip" on the thigh).
    3. Control the Foot: Reach your other arm around the opponent's heel, securing a "double-grip" on their foot, with your palm on their heel and your fingers curled over their instep, ensuring your thumb is pointing upwards.
    4. Hip Elevation: Elevate your hips off the mat by bridging slightly, driving your hips towards your opponent's knee. This creates space and leverage.
    5. Knee Alignment: Ensure your opponent's knee is bent and pointing towards their own chest. Your body should be positioned so your hip is against their knee, acting as a block.
    6. Torque Application: Begin to rotate your torso away from your opponent while simultaneously driving your hips forward and slightly upward. Your grip on the heel should remain tight.
    7. Ankle Rotation: Apply rotational pressure by drawing your opponent's heel towards your chest while keeping their knee pinned. The ankle will rotate internally as you apply this pressure.
    8. Finishing Pressure: Continue to drive your hips forward and rotate your torso until the opponent taps. The pressure is felt in the ankle and knee.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Hip Drive: Driving hips directly forward without elevating them creates insufficient leverage and can lead to the opponent easily escaping or retaliating. This can cause knee ligament tears (ACL/MCL) in the opponent or hip strain in the attacker.
    • Over-rotation/Pulling: Yanking or excessively rotating your body without controlled hip pressure can lead to a sudden, uncontrolled torque on the ankle. This can result in severe ankle sprains or fractures for the opponent.
    • Lack of Knee Control: Failing to pin the opponent's knee with your own hip allows their leg to straighten, creating a dangerous situation where they can escape or potentially injure your arm with a counter. This can lead to armbar or kimura opportunities for the opponent.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Grip and Body Mechanics: Practice securing the grips and performing the hip elevation and torso rotation with no opponent. 50 repetitions per side.
    2. Partnered Static Position: Partner lies on their back, you secure the leg and practice the mechanics without any resistance. 20 repetitions per side.
    3. Light Resistance (25%): Partner actively resists but doesn't try to escape. Focus on maintaining control and executing the mechanics smoothly. 10 repetitions per side.
    4. Moderate Resistance (50%): Partner begins to actively try and defend or escape. You must adjust your hip and body position to maintain the lock. 10 repetitions per side.
    5. High Resistance (75%): Partner actively attempts to escape and counter. Focus on maintaining tight control and finishing the submission. 5 repetitions per side.
    6. Live Rolling (90-100%): Attempt the Inside Heel Hook when the opportunity arises in sparring, prioritizing safety and control over finishing. 3-5 attempts per round, focusing on technique execution.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Transitioning from a failed guard pass to isolate a leg.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard and their weight is distributed incorrectly.
    • During scrambles or transitions where you can secure a dominant leg entanglement.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Escape to Standing: If the attacker's hip control is weak, immediately drive your hips back and attempt to stand up, pulling your leg free. Maintain a wide base and keep your knee bent.
    • Roll Through: If the attacker secures a strong grip but lacks hip control, attempt to roll in the direction of the attack, forcing them to follow and potentially releasing the pressure. This requires good spatial awareness.
    • Leg Switch/Counter-Heel Hook: If the attacker has a weak grip or poor body positioning, you can sometimes switch your hips and attempt to attack their leg with an Inside Heel Hook of your own, forcing them to defend.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try to do the Inside Heel Hook?

    Your ankle likely hurts because you are applying pressure incorrectly, leading to excessive torque on your own ankle. Ensure you are gripping your partner's heel firmly, not their foot, and that your hips are driving forward to create leverage. The pressure should be on their ankle, not directly on your own.

    Q: How can I Inside Heel Hook a bigger opponent if they are too strong?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise mechanics are paraMount. Focus on isolating their leg completely and securing a tight grip on their heel. Elevate your hips forcefully and use your torso rotation to create the torque, rather than relying on raw strength. Control their knee with your hip to prevent them from straightening their leg.

    Q: When is the best time to go for an Inside Heel Hook in BJJ?

    The best time to attempt an Inside Heel Hook is when you have successfully isolated one of your opponent's legs and can control their hip and knee. This often occurs when they are trying to pass your guard, or when you have established a dominant leg entanglement like the 50-50 guard. Timing is critical to prevent them from escaping or retaliating.

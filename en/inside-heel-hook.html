Mastering the Inside Heel Hook in BJJ: A White Belt's Guide

BROWN★★★★★Expert
Brown Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Brown Belt Requirements

This technique attacks the opponent's heel from a controlling position, aiming to hyperextend the knee and ankle joint for a submission.

White belts often rush the setup, leading to a loss of position or a failed submission attempt due to improper body mechanics.

The key insight is controlling the opponent's hip and knee while isolating the heel, creating a stable lever for the finish.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a controlling position like the 50-50 guard, secure your opponent's leg by placing your forearm across their thigh, just above the knee.
    2. Grip your own bicep with your opposite hand, creating a "figure-four" grip around the opponent's thigh.
    3. Turn your hips to face your opponent's hip, ensuring your chest is tight against their hamstring.
    4. Drive your hips forward and slightly upwards, creating pressure into their knee.
    5. Isolate the opponent's heel by cupping it with your hand, thumb pointing upwards.
    6. Extend your hips and legs away from your opponent's body while simultaneously flexing your foot to trap their heel.
    7. Apply a controlled pulling motion with your arms, drawing the heel towards your body while maintaining hip pressure, creating a rotational force on the knee and ankle.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly applying pressure: Trying to "pull hard" with your arms without proper hip control can strain your own shoulder or wrist.
    • Allowing the opponent to escape: Not securing the opponent's hip means they can often roll out, potentially twisting your own knee or ankle.
    • Over-extending too early: Applying full extension before the leg is properly isolated can lead to a hyperextended knee on yourself if the opponent resists effectively.
    • Neglecting the "cup" on the heel: Without firmly cupping the heel, it can slip out, causing you to lose the submission and potentially fall out of position.
    • Rushing the hip turn: A slow or incomplete hip turn allows the opponent to counter your weight distribution, making the lock ineffective.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip turn and figure-four grip placement on a dummy or training partner's stationary leg (0% resistance).
    2. Isolated movement drill: From 50-50 guard, focus on isolating the heel and cupping it correctly, with your partner offering no resistance (0% resistance).
    3. Controlled rotation drill: With your partner providing minimal resistance (25%), practice the hip drive and the controlled pulling motion to simulate the finish.
    4. Positional flow drill: Start from 50-50 guard and have your partner offer light resistance (50%) as you work to secure the leg and set up the Inside Heel Hook.
    5. Live drilling (controlled): Engage in live rolling with a partner who understands the technique, focusing on executing the setup and finish with 75% resistance.
    6. Live rolling (full): Attempt the Inside Heel Hook during live rolling with a partner who is actively defending, aiming for 90-100% resistance and focusing on timing and execution.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have established solid control in the 50-50 guard.
    • When your opponent attempts to escape a leg entanglement by exposing their heel.
    • When you can isolate one of your opponent's legs with your own leg structure.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The "Sailor Roll": If the opponent attempts the Inside Heel Hook, the defender can roll in the direction of the lock, untwisting the ankle and knee to relieve pressure.
    • "Slipping the Heel": The defender can attempt to quickly pull their foot out of the attacker's "cup" by dorsiflexing their ankle and rotating their foot outwards.
    • "Stacking": The defender can attempt to "stack" their weight onto the attacker, driving their hips and shoulders forward to collapse the attacker's structure and relieve pressure.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do an Inside Heel Hook?

    This is likely due to over-extension or improper hip control. If you're not turning your hips to face your opponent's hip and driving forward, you're relying solely on your leg strength. This can hyperextend your own knee. Ensure you maintain tight hip pressure and only extend fully once the heel is secured and your body is aligned to create the rotational force.

    Q: How do I Inside Heel Hook against a bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paraMount. Focus on controlling their hip to prevent them from using their weight to stack you. Secure your figure-four grip tightly and use your hips to drive into their knee joint, creating the angle for the submission. Don't try to muscle it; use their size against them by creating a stable, powerful lever with your body.

    Q: Is it okay to grab the opponent's foot directly for an Inside Heel Hook as a white belt?

    While some advanced practitioners may use direct foot grabs, as a white belt, it's much safer and more effective to use the figure-four grip on the thigh and cup the heel. Grabbing the foot directly often leads to a loss of control over the opponent's knee and hip, increasing the risk of injury to both parties. Focus on the structured grip first.

Inside Heel Hook: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

The Inside Heel Hook is a submission targeting the knee joint by applying rotational force. It's typically attacked from positions where you can isolate an opponent's leg, such as the 50-50 guard or when they are on their hands and knees.

White belts often struggle with this submission due to a lack of control and understanding of the delicate joint mechanics involved. Rushing the finish or improperly applying pressure can lead to injury for both parties.

The key insight is to create a stable platform with your body while isolating the opponent's foot and ankle, then using your hips to drive into the heel, creating a controlled torque on the knee.

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Starting Position: Opponent's leg is trapped between your legs, ideally in a 50-50 guard configuration or similar leg entanglement where their foot is accessible.
    2. Grip 1 (Primary Control): Secure an "Assisted Grip" on the opponent's foot by placing your forearm across the top of their foot, trapping their toes against your chest/shoulder. Simultaneously, cup your hand around the Achilles tendon area of their heel.
    3. Grip 2 (Stabilization): With your other hand, grip the opponent's ankle or shinbone firmly, ensuring no slippage. This grip prevents them from easily rotating their leg out.
    4. Hip Angle: Drive your hips forward and slightly to the side, aligning your pelvis perpendicular to the opponent's femur (thigh bone). This creates the necessary leverage.
    5. Weight Transfer: Shift your body weight onto your hips, pressing them into the opponent's trapped leg. Your chest should be close to their thigh.
    6. Knee Direction (Your Body): Keep your own knees tucked in and protected, forming a stable base. Your left knee (if attacking their right leg) should be pointing towards the ceiling, and your right knee should be driving into their hip or abdomen for control.
    7. Execution: With your hips forward and weight applied, use your chest and the "Assisted Grip" to pull the opponent's heel towards your chest. Simultaneously, drive your hips forward and slightly upward, creating a rotational force on their knee joint.
    8. Finishing Pressure: The pressure is applied by extending your hips and pulling the heel, creating torque. The opponent will tap when they feel discomfort in their knee.
    9. Disengagement: If the submission is not there or the opponent defends, release the grip cleanly and safely return to a neutral or advantageous position.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Hip Drive: Applying pressure by "pulling hard" with your arms while your hips are not properly aligned (e.g., hips too far back, not perpendicular to the femur). This can lead to tearing of the ACL or MCL in the opponent's knee, as the rotational force is uncontrolled and applied at a bad angle. The correct movement is to drive the hips forward into the opponent's leg, using the hips as the primary source of leverage.
    • Foot Slippage During Grip: Allowing the opponent's foot to slip out of your "Assisted Grip" while attempting to apply pressure. This can result in your own foot or ankle being twisted unnaturally if you are not careful, potentially spraining or fracturing your own ankle. Always maintain a secure "Assisted Grip" on the heel and ankle before initiating the hip drive.
    • Jerking Motion: Applying a sudden, jerky motion to finish the submission instead of a steady, increasing pressure. This sudden impact can cause severe ligamentous damage (ACL, PCL) or even fractures to the tibia or fibula in the opponent's knee. The correct finish is a controlled, gradual increase in torque until the tap.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Grip & Frame Drill: Practice securing the "Assisted Grip" and the ankle grip on a training dummy or your own leg, focusing on hip alignment and chest pressure. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    2. Partnered Isolation Drill (No Submission): With a partner lying on their back, practice isolating one of their legs and achieving the correct "Assisted Grip" and ankle grip, with your hips correctly angled. Partner offers no resistance. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    3. Controlled Leg Trap Drill: Partner is on their hands and knees. You are on your back, trapping one of their legs. Practice entering the 50-50 guard or a similar position and securing the "Assisted Grip" and ankle grip without applying any pressure. Partner remains passive. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    4. Controlled Submission Drill (Verbal Tap): In a controlled position (e.g., 50-50 guard), secure the grips. Apply slow, steady pressure, and have your partner verbally tap the moment they feel any discomfort. Focus on the hip drive and controlled extension. (10 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    5. Live Drilling (Submission Focus): Roll with a partner who understands the submission. Focus on setting up the Inside Heel Hook from a specific position (e.g., transitioning from closed guard to 50-50). Partner defends passively but realistically. (10 minutes, 50% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling (Full Resistance): Integrate the Inside Heel Hook attempt into regular sparring. Focus on recognizing opportunities and executing the submission safely when the chance arises. Partner defends with full intent. (15 minutes, 75-100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard and their leg becomes accessible in a "50-50" or similar entanglement.
    • When you are in your own 50-50 guard and can secure the "Assisted Grip" before your opponent does.
    • When your opponent is on their hands and knees (e.g., after a failed sweep or guard pass) and you can isolate and control one of their legs.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The "Honey Hole" Defense: If your opponent has the Inside Heel Hook grip on your leg, and you feel pressure, immediately try to rotate your hips *away* from the direction of the hook. This reduces the torque on your knee. Simultaneously, try to bring your free leg over their hips to create a frame and push them away, breaking their posture.
    • The "Stomp and Frame" Defense: As the opponent secures the "Assisted Grip," quickly stomp your trapped foot down and away from their body while simultaneously creating a frame with your free leg against their hip. This disrupts their hip angle and breaks their platform for leverage.
    • The "Escape and Turn-In" Defense: If the opponent has control but has not yet applied significant pressure, attempt to "stomp" your heel out of their grip. If successful, immediately turn your hips into your opponent's body and attempt to shrimp or clear your leg, regaining a more neutral or advantageous position.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do an Inside Heel Hook?

    Your knee is likely hurting because you are applying pressure incorrectly. The Inside Heel Hook targets the knee's rotational stability. If you are using a jerky motion or not properly aligning your hips, you are creating shear forces instead of controlled torque. Ensure your hips are driving forward and perpendicular to the opponent's femur, and that the pressure is applied gradually.

    Q: How do I set up an Inside Heel Hook against a bigger opponent?

    Against a bigger opponent, focus on leg entanglements that give you control over their lower body's movement, like the 50-50 guard. The key is to use technique to negate their size advantage. Secure a strong "Assisted Grip" on their heel and ankle, then use your hip drive to create leverage. Don't rely on strength; use precise body mechanics to control their leg.

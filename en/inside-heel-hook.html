Inside Heel Hook Technique Guide for BJJ White Belts

BROWN★★★★★Expert
Brown Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Brown Belt Requirements

The Inside Heel Hook attacks the opponent's ankle joint by applying rotational pressure to the heel. It's typically initiated from a position where you have control of the opponent's leg, such as the 50-50 guard or a specific leg entanglement.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of the specific mechanics and a tendency to apply brute force rather than leverage. This can lead to ineffective attempts and increased injury risk for both parties.

The key mechanical insight is to create a "figure four" grip on the ankle and use your hips to drive *into* the opponent's leg while simultaneously extending your body to create torque on the heel.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From the 50-50 guard, ensure your opponent's heel is trapped between your knees. Your hips should be angled slightly towards your opponent's head.
    2. Secure a "figure four" grip on the opponent's ankle: your arm closest to their heel goes under their ankle, and your other arm goes over the top, gripping your own bicep or wrist.
    3. Position your knee on the same side as your gripping arm against the opponent's calf, perpendicular to their shin.
    4. Drive your hips forward and slightly upward, creating a fulcrum point with your knee on their calf.
    5. Simultaneously extend your body, straightening your legs and lifting your hips away from your opponent's hips.
    6. Apply a slight inward rotation of your grip, turning the opponent's heel towards the mat.
    7. Maintain constant hip pressure and keep your core engaged to generate consistent torque on the heel.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly applying pressure with your knee directly on the opponent's shin, rather than their calf, can hyperextend their knee joint, risking ACL or MCL tears. The correct movement is to place your knee on the meaty part of the calf, acting as a pivot.
    • Gripping the opponent's foot too loosely or attempting to "pull" the heel without hip drive can lead to the opponent escaping and you losing control, increasing the risk of a scramble that could result in a knee injury for you if your leg is trapped improperly.
    • Failing to maintain a tight "figure four" grip and instead grabbing the foot with open palms can allow the opponent to easily pull their foot out, wasting energy and potentially exposing your own limbs to counter-attacks or joint locks.
    • Not angling your hips correctly, keeping them square to the opponent's legs, prevents the necessary torque generation and can lead to you being stacked, risking a spinal compression injury. Your hips should be angled towards their head.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the "figure four" grip on your own leg, focusing on the hip drive and body extension motion without resistance (10 reps each side).
    2. Partnered drilling (no resistance): With a willing partner, isolate the leg control and grip. Focus on achieving the correct body position and grip for 5 reps each side.
    3. Partnered drilling (light resistance): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to secure the position and practice the hip drive and extension. Focus on smooth transitions (5 reps each side).
    4. Static control drilling: Your partner attempts to defend minimally while you practice securing the lock and applying controlled pressure. Focus on feeling the torque (5 reps each side).
    5. Dynamic drilling with controlled escape: Your partner actively defends and attempts to escape, allowing you to practice maintaining control and adjusting your position under light pressure. (5 reps each side).
    6. Live rolling (controlled): Apply the Inside Heel Hook in a controlled rolling scenario, focusing on timing and execution against a resisting opponent. Tap early if you feel pressure and communicate. (10 minutes rolling time).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have established control of the opponent's leg in the 50-50 guard, with both legs on the same side.
    • When you have successfully transitioned to a leg entanglement position where you can isolate and control one of the opponent's legs.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard by stepping over your legs, creating an opportunity to attack their trailing leg.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The "straighten and shrimp" escape: As the opponent attempts to secure the figure-four grip, immediately extend your leg and shrimp your hips away to break their control and create space. Focus on keeping your knee straight to avoid torque.
    • The "turn-in" defense: If the opponent has established the grip, turn your body towards their hips and attempt to drive your own hips into their attacking leg. This can disrupt their leverage and create an opportunity to untangle.
    • The "stomp" counter: If the opponent has the grip but is slow to apply pressure, you can attempt to stomp your free foot down onto their attacking leg or hip, disrupting their structure and potentially forcing them to release the grip.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try to do an Inside Heel Hook?

    Your ankle is likely hurting because you are trying to force the lock with raw strength instead of using proper biomechanics. The Inside Heel Hook relies on creating torque through hip drive and body extension, not pulling or twisting with your hands and arms alone. Ensure your "figure four" grip is tight and your knee is positioned correctly on their calf to act as a stable pivot point. Focus on driving your hips forward and extending your body to create rotational pressure on their heel.

    Q: How can I perform an Inside Heel Hook against a bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are even more critical. Focus on securing a deep "figure four" grip and maintaining a tight control of their heel. Use your hips to drive *under* their weight, creating a strong fulcrum with your knee on their calf. Your hip angle is paraMount; ensure you are positioned to drive into them, not simply pulling them towards you. Extend your body away from them to generate the necessary torque, making their size work against them.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt an Inside Heel Hook in BJJ if I'm a white belt?

    As a white belt, the safest and most effective time to attempt an Inside Heel Hook is when you have achieved a stable 50-50 guard position. This means both you and your opponent have control of each other's legs, and you have a clear path to isolate one leg and secure the "figure four" grip. Avoid attempting it from less controlled positions where your own legs are vulnerable or where your opponent can easily stack you.

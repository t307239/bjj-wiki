Inside Heel Hook: A Biomechanically Precise BJJ Guide for White Belts

This technique is typically initiated from positions where you have control of your opponent's leg, most commonly in a 50-50 guard or a similar leg entanglement. Its goal is to isolate the opponent's heel and apply torque to the knee joint, forcing a submission.

White belts often fail because they focus on brute force rather than precise control and leverage. They may also rush the setup, exposing themselves to counter-attacks or ineffective application.

The single most critical mechanical insight is understanding the "banana split" principle: creating opposing forces that hyperextend the knee and ankle simultaneously, rather than just trying to twist.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip: Secure a "figure-four" grip around the opponent's ankle with your arms. Your top arm's bicep should be against the opponent's Achilles tendon, and your bottom arm's forearm should be across the top of their foot, interlacing your fingers.
    2. Hip Angle: Position your hips so they are perpendicular to the opponent's knee line. This means your hips will be facing their side, not their front.
    3. Weight Distribution: Drive your hips into the opponent's thigh. This creates a fulcrum point and prevents them from easily escaping.
    4. Leg Placement: Your free leg (the one not controlling the opponent's leg) should be placed across the opponent's hip. This acts as a post and prevents them from turning into you.
    5. Knee Drive: Actively drive your knee of the trapped leg (the one you are attacking) towards the opponent's buttock. This creates internal rotation and destabilizes their knee.
    6. Ankle Control: Maintain a tight figure-four grip, ensuring your bicep is firmly against the Achilles tendon. This grip is crucial for controlling the ankle's rotation.
    7. Submission Execution: Simultaneously, pull your top arm towards your chest and push your bottom arm away, while driving your hips forward and keeping your free leg planted. This combination creates the hyperextension.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Improper Gripping: Holding the ankle too loosely or without a proper figure-four grip. This can lead to the opponent escaping or the attacker's hands slipping, potentially causing a hyperextension of the attacker's own fingers or wrist.
    • Knee Rotation Without Control: Attempting to twist the leg without maintaining solid hip and ankle control. This can result in a dangerous, uncontrolled torque on the opponent's knee, risking ACL or MCL tears, or the attacker's knee being twisted awkwardly.
    • Forward Hip Pressure Without Support: Driving hips forward without planting the free leg against the opponent's hip. This allows the opponent to easily turn away, potentially twisting the attacker's knee or ankle in an unsafe manner.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip and Grip Practice: Perform the figure-four grip and hip positioning 20 times per side, focusing on the correct angles and weight distribution without a partner.
    2. Partnered Grip and Hip Control (0% Resistance): With a compliant partner, practice securing the figure-four grip and hip angle. Partner remains still. 10 repetitions per side.
    3. Controlled Leg Pull (25% Resistance): Partner gently resists as you initiate the hip drive and ankle pull. Focus on the initial stages of the submission mechanics. 10 repetitions per side.
    4. Application from 50-50 (50% Resistance): Start in a controlled 50-50 position and execute the Inside Heel Hook. Partner offers mild resistance to escape. 5 repetitions per side.
    5. Simulated Submission (75% Resistance): Execute the technique with increasing pressure. Partner taps immediately upon feeling any significant pressure. 5 repetitions per side.
    6. Live Rolling (90-100% Resistance): Attempt the Inside Heel Hook in controlled rolling situations, emphasizing proper setup and execution. Tapping immediately if the submission is locked in or if you feel compromised.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you secure a strong 50-50 guard entanglement.
    • When your opponent attempts to turn into you from a leg entanglement.
    • When you successfully trap an opponent's leg during a guard pass attempt.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The "Straightening" Defense: The opponent actively straightens their leg and pulls their heel away from the attacker's body, while simultaneously creating space with their free leg to push off the attacker's hip.
    • The "Hip Escape" Defense: The opponent uses their free hip to bridge and escape the entanglement, creating space to turn their leg out of the attacking angle and often transitioning to a scramble.
    • The "Grip Break" Defense: The opponent uses their free hand to peel the attacker's figure-four grip off their ankle, often by attacking the attacker's wrist or forearm.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Inside Heel Hook?

    Wrist pain typically occurs when you're not using a proper figure-four grip, or when you're trying to generate all the force with your hands alone. Ensure your bicep is firmly against the opponent's Achilles tendon and your forearm is across their foot, interlacing your fingers. The primary force should come from driving your hips into their thigh and pulling your top arm towards your chest, not just twisting with your wrists.

    Q: How do I Inside Heel Hook against a bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, precision is paraMount. Focus on achieving a deep, secure figure-four grip and establishing a strong hip connection; this leverage is key. Use your body weight to your advantage by driving your hips forward aggressively into their thigh, creating a solid base. Ensure your free leg is firmly planted on their hip to prevent them from turning away, which is their primary escape method.

    Q: When is the best time to try an Inside Heel Hook in BJJ?

    The Inside Heel Hook is best attempted when you have established a strong leg entanglement, such as the 50-50 guard, or when your opponent's leg is isolated and accessible. It's particularly effective when your opponent tries to turn into you, as this often exposes their heel in a vulnerable position for the hook. Avoid attempting it if you don't have a secure control of their leg and hips, as this can lead to compromised positions.

Clock Choke: A White Belt's Precise BJJ Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Clock Choke is typically applied from Side Control or mount, aiming to secure a submission by compressing the carotid arteries. It requires precise control and understanding of leverage.

White belts often struggle by relying on raw strength or incorrect body positioning, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury. They may not understand the subtle weight shifts needed.

The core principle is to use your body weight and positioning to create a strong fulcrum, allowing a small aMount of pressure to generate a powerful choke.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From side control, establish a cross-face grip with your left arm, placing your forearm across the opponent's face, pushing their head away from your body. Your right hand controls their far-side hip.
    2. Shift your weight forward, driving your chest into the opponent's chest, making their hips heavy.
    3. Your left knee should be tucked close to their head, creating a strong base and preventing them from turning into you.
    4. Your right leg extends back, creating a stable tripod base with your right foot on the mat.
    5. Reach your right arm under their head, aiming to secure a grip on your own left bicep, forming a Gable grip or a palm-to-palm grip.
    6. Your left forearm, currently across their face, now moves to cup the back of their head, applying downward pressure.
    7. To finish, drive your left shoulder into their head and simultaneously pull your right elbow down and back, creating a powerful squeezing action on their neck.
    8. Maintain constant chest-to-chest pressure and keep your hips low to prevent them from bridging or shrimping out.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly framing with the left arm: Pushing the opponent's head with an open palm instead of cupping the back of the head can allow their head to slip out or lead to a wrist sprain if they resist forcefully.
    • Overextending the right arm to grip: Reaching too far and losing connection with your own bicep can create a gap, allowing them to escape. This can also strain the shoulder joint if they pull away.
    • Lifting the hips too high: Trying to 'sit up' to apply the choke instead of driving forward with the chest and maintaining a low base. This compromises your base, allowing the opponent to shrimp or bridge effectively, and can strain the lower back.
    • Applying pressure with the forearm on the windpipe: This is a painful, ineffective choke that can damage the trachea. The choke should be applied to the sides of the neck, compressing the carotid arteries.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Drilling (0% resistance): Practice the grip transitions and body positioning from side control. Focus on the weight shift and hip angle. 20 repetitions.
    2. Partner Drilling (25% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice establishing the initial cross-face and hip control. Work on the leg and arm placement. 15 repetitions per side.
    3. Controlled Execution (50% resistance): Execute the full technique, focusing on the grip and the finishing squeeze. Partner offers minimal resistance to escape. 10 repetitions per side.
    4. Flow Drilling (75% resistance): Practice the Clock Choke from side control, allowing the partner to attempt to defend. Work on maintaining the position and adjusting as they defend. 5 repetitions per side.
    5. Positional Sparring (90% resistance): Start from side control and attempt the Clock Choke. Partner actively defends but does not attempt to submit you. 3 rounds, 2 minutes each.
    6. Live Rolling (100% resistance): Attempt the Clock Choke during live sparring, focusing on applying the mechanics learned. Do not force the submission; learn from the attempts. 2 rounds, 3 minutes each.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have established a strong cross-face from side control and the opponent is turtled or trying to frame away.
    • When you are in mount and the opponent is defending by turning their head to one side, exposing the neck.
    • When the opponent attempts to shrimp or bridge and you can maintain head control and transition to the choke.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The "Head and Arm Trap": If the opponent tries to frame with their arm, you can trap it with your chest and transition to a north-south choke or armbar. Instead of pushing their head, keep it pinned.
    • The "Shrimp and Re-guard": If you feel pressure on your neck and can't finish, immediately shrimp your hips away and attempt to regain guard. Do not allow yourself to be choked.
    • The "Bridge and Roll": If the opponent is slow to finish, a well-timed bridge can create space to escape or even reverse the position. This requires good core strength and timing.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Clock Choke from side control?

    Wrist pain often occurs from gripping your own bicep with a bent wrist or from the opponent actively pulling their arm away. To fix this, ensure your grip is strong and your wrist is neutral or slightly extended when you grab your bicep. Focus on driving your shoulder into their head and pulling your elbow down, rather than just yanking with your wrist.

    Q: How can I apply the Clock Choke against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise body positioning are paramount. Do not try to muscle the choke. Focus on driving your chest into their chest to make their hips heavy and ensure your left knee is tight to their head to limit their movement. Use your body weight to create the compression, not just arm strength.

    Q: The optimal time to transition to the Clock Choke from mount is when the opponent is actively defending by turning their head towards your chest or trying to create space by pushing your hips away. This exposes their neck and allows you to secure the cross-face and finish the choke before they can fully recover their defense.

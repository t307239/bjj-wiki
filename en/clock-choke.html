Clock Choke BJJ: Your First Submission

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Clock Choke is typically applied from Side Control, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain via the carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength instead of precise leverage and body positioning.

The key is to create a tight "figure-four" grip and use your body weight to compress the neck, not just pull with your arms.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish side control, ensuring your chest is heavy on the opponent's torso and your hips are lower than theirs.
    2. Secure your opponent's collar with your right hand, reaching deep into the lapel with your thumb inside the collar.
    3. Your left arm will wrap around the opponent's head, with your left hand gripping your own right wrist (a "figure-four" grip).
    4. Drive your right shoulder into the opponent's neck and upper chest, creating a wedge.
    5. Rotate your hips slightly counter-clockwise, angling your body away from the opponent's head.
    6. Use your left forearm to apply upward pressure on the back of the opponent's head, while your right forearm presses down on their collarbone area.
    7. Squeeze your elbows together and slightly drive your chest forward, compressing the carotid artery on the side of their neck facing you.
    8. Maintain a stable base by keeping your knees bent and feet flat on the mat.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to crank the neck by twisting your torso excessively: This can lead to cervical spine strain or disc injury. The correct movement is a controlled squeeze, not a violent twist.
    • Gripping too loosely on the collar or your own wrist: This allows the opponent space to escape and reduces the choking pressure, potentially leading to a failed submission and lost position. Always ensure a firm, deep grip.
    • Allowing your hips to rise too high off the mat: This removes your base and weight, making it impossible to generate choking pressure and increasing the risk of the opponent escaping or reversing your position. Keep your hips low and anchored.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the grip setup and body rotation without a partner. Focus on the feel of the figure-four grip and hip angle. (10 reps)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the full sequence from side control to choke completion. (15 reps)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the pressure points. Focus on maintaining the tight grips. (10 reps each side)
    4. Controlled sparring (75% resistance): Spar with a partner, specifically looking for opportunities to set up and apply the Clock Choke. Your partner will focus on defending. (5 minutes)
    5. Controlled sparring (90% resistance): Increase the intensity of sparring. Your partner will actively defend. Focus on finishing the choke if you secure the position. (5 minutes)
    6. Live rolling (100% resistance): Apply the Clock Choke in a live roll situation. Understand that successful submissions will be less frequent but the learning is invaluable. (10 minutes)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have established solid side control and the opponent is trying to bridge or shrimp away.
    • As a follow-up if your opponent defends a head-and-arm choke attempt from side control.
    • When the opponent turns their head away from you, exposing the side of their neck.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • "The Turtle": If the opponent tucks their chin and curls into a fetal position, your choke is ineffective. You must then transition to a different submission or continue to improve your control.
    • "The Escape": The opponent can try to create space by framing with their arms and shrimping their hips out. You must maintain chest-to-chest pressure and adjust your hips to prevent this.
    • "The Grip Release": The opponent can attempt to peel your hands off their collar or wrist. You must maintain a strong, tight grip and be ready to re-establish if it slips.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Clock Choke from side control?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you are gripping your own wrist too tightly or at an awkward angle, trying to force the choke. The correct grip is a firm, but not rigid, figure-four. Ensure your left forearm is pressing on the back of the opponent's head, and your right hand is gripping your left wrist in a neutral position, avoiding excessive flexion or extension of your wrist joint. Focus on using your body weight and rotation, not just your forearm strength.

    Q: How can I apply the Clock Choke against a much larger opponent from side control?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise body positioning become even more critical. Ensure your hips are lower than theirs and your chest is firmly on their torso to prevent them from using their size to create space. Focus on driving your shoulder deep into their neck to create the initial compression, and use a strong figure-four grip. Rotate your hips counter-clockwise to generate the choking torque, rather than trying to overpower them with arm strength. Make sure your base is wide and stable.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to a Clock Choke from side control if my opponent is defending other chokes?

    The Clock Choke is an excellent follow-up submission when an opponent defends a standard arm-triangle or head-and-arm choke. If they turn their head away to create space or prevent the initial choke, they often expose the side of their neck. This is your cue to immediately adjust your grips to the Clock Choke setup, ensuring your shoulder is deep in their neck and initiating the figure-four grip. The key is to recognize the defensive movement as an offensive opportunity.

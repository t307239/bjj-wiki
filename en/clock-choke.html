Clock Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Clock Choke is typically initiated from the Closed Guard, aiming to secure a submission by constricting blood flow to the brain. It's a fundamental choke that teaches control and leverage. White belts often struggle with the precise body positioning required, leading to ineffective attempts or missed opportunities. The key mechanical insight is understanding how to use your hips and shoulders to create a fulcrum, generating leverage through angular force rather than brute strength.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From closed guard, secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand on the opponent's left lapel, thumb inside the collar. Your left hand secures their right collar, thumb inside.
    2. Simultaneously, begin to open your guard by posting your right foot on their hip, pushing their body away slightly.
    3. As you push with your right foot, pivot your hips to the left, creating an angle where your left hip is closer to their head.
    4. Drive your left shoulder into the space between their chin and chest, applying pressure.
    5. Extend your right leg, sliding it up their back towards their opposite shoulder, creating a 'clock' like structure with your legs and torso.
    6. Tighten the choke by pulling both lapels towards you, simultaneously driving your left shoulder forward and slightly down.
    7. Ensure your right knee is tucked behind their head, preventing them from posturing up and relieving pressure.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly extending your neck: This can strain your cervical spine, leading to neck pain or even a sprain if you arch backward excessively.
    • Trying to lift the opponent with your arms: This strains the shoulder and bicep tendons, often resulting in rotator cuff or bicep tendonitis, and is ineffective.
    • Allowing the opponent to bridge or stack: If your hips are not angled correctly and your right leg is not secured behind their head, they can create space and cause you to lose position, potentially injuring your lower back or knees from the impact.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling of the grip setup and hip pivot motion without a partner (50 reps).
    2. Partner drill: Practice the entire sequence with a compliant partner, focusing on precise body positioning and grip transitions (20 reps per side).
    3. Introduce light resistance: Partner attempts to defend by posturing up or pulling their head out, you maintain control and adjust (30 reps, 25% resistance).
    4. Increase resistance: Partner actively defends, but allows the choke to be applied if done correctly. Focus on maintaining pressure and finishing (30 reps, 50% resistance).
    5. Live rolling simulation: Apply the technique from closed guard during rolling, focusing on timing and recognizing opportunities (5 rounds, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the Clock Choke during full sparring, prioritizing technique over outcome (10 rounds, 100% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is postured up in your closed guard, creating space between your chests.
    • Opponent attempts to stack you from closed guard, pushing your hips down.
    • You have secured a strong cross-collar grip and are ready to initiate the body rotation.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The Escape Posture: If the opponent feels the choke coming, they can quickly post their foot on your hip and push away, breaking your base and relieving pressure.
    • The Head Pull: The opponent can pull their head down and towards your chest, creating space and making the choke ineffective.
    • The Grip Break: A strong opponent can grab your choking arm's wrist and break your grip, or simply pull their collar free.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Clock Choke from BJJ closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs if you are gripping the lapel with your fingers fully extended and pulling directly backward. Instead, ensure your thumb is tucked inside the collar and use the heel of your palm to drive into their neck. This engages your forearm and shoulder more effectively, reducing strain on the wrist joint.

    Q: How do I apply the Clock Choke in BJJ if my opponent is much bigger than me?

    Leverage is your best friend against a larger opponent. Focus on the hip angle and shoulder drive. As you pivot your hips, drive your shoulder deep into their neck to create a fulcrum. Your legs then act as a lever, using your body weight to constrict blood flow, rather than relying on pure grip strength.

    Q: When should I start to open my guard and move my hips for the Clock Choke?

    You should initiate the guard opening and hip movement precisely as you feel the opponent posture up and create a small gap, or when you secure the cross-collar grip with confidence. This timing is crucial; opening too early allows them to pass, while opening too late means they are already too tight and can defend easily.

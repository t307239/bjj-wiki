    More Questions

    What is the primary goal when setting up the Twister in BJJ?

    The primary goal is to isolate and control one of your opponent's legs while simultaneously creating a dominant spinal lock. This control is crucial for preventing escapes and setting up the submission itself.

    What are the common mistakes beginners make when attempting a Twister setup?

    Common mistakes include not securing a strong grip on the opponent's leg, allowing them to bridge or roll out, and not properly controlling the hips. Overextending or rushing the setup can also lead to losing position.

    How can I transition from a guard pass to a Twister setup?

    From certain guard passes, like a knee slice or toreando, you can look to secure a leg as your opponent tries to shrimp or re-guard. The key is to anticipate their movement and quickly secure the leg before they can establish a strong defensive base.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: I'm struggling to get my hips low enough to effectively apply the BJJ Twister Setup from guard, what am I doing wrong biomechanically?

    To achieve a proper hip height for the Twister setup from guard, focus on driving your hips towards your opponent's shoulder while simultaneously extending your legs to create space. This action compresses their torso and allows you to isolate their arm and spine more effectively, preventing them from posturing up.

    Q: How can I prevent my opponent from defending the BJJ Twister Setup by bridging or rolling when I'm trying to isolate their arm?

    To counter bridging and rolling, maintain a strong connection by keeping your chest tight to their hip and using your free arm to control their far shoulder, preventing them from creating upward momentum. Simultaneously, use your legs to pin their hips to the mat, limiting their ability to generate rotational force for a bridge.

    Q: When attempting the BJJ Twister Setup, my opponent keeps turning into me, making it hard to get the spinal lock. What are the key biomechanical adjustments to counter this?

    When your opponent turns into you, immediately adjust by driving your shoulder into their hip to prevent them from completing the turn, and use your legs to 'hug' their hips, creating a strong base. This positional control limits their ability to face you and allows you to re-establish the proper angle for the spinal lock.

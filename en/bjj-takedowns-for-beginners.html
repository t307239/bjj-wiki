BJJ Takedowns for Beginners: Your First 5 to Master

Contents

    1. Double Leg Takedown

    Why learn it: The most universal takedown in all of grappling. Setup: level change (bend knees, drop hips), drive into opponent with both arms around their legs, head on the outside, drive through. Key detail: keep your back flat (not hunched). After: control to side control or mount. See: Double Leg Guide

    2. Single Leg Takedown

    Why learn it: Easier entry than double leg, works against many defenses. Setup: shoot for one leg (lead leg), elevate leg while driving forward and turning corner. Variations: run the pipe, trip, high crotch lift. Key detail: keep level on entry, don't grab too low. See: Single Leg Guide

    3. Hip Throw (O-goshi)

    Why learn it: Excellent from a wrestling clinch, very high percentage for gi BJJ. Setup: establish underhook and far shoulder control, step in front of opponent, load their weight on your hip, rotate and throw. Key detail: get your hip truly in front — partial entries fail. See: Hip Throw Guide

    4. Collar Drag

    Why learn it: Works against aggressive opponents, requires minimal athleticism. Setup: grip collar with one hand, pull sharply across your body, turn and take the back or enter top position. Key detail: the drag is explosive — it stops working if you slow it down. Great entry into back takes.

    5. Snap Down

    Why learn it: No penetration step needed — great for BJJ beginners uncomfortable with shots. Setup: two-on-one or neck grip, sharp pull downward while circling to create a level change, follow to back or turtle position. Key detail: combine the snap with footwork — don't just pull straight down.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: When I try to do a double leg takedown, my partner always seems to just step back and I can't get them down, what am I doing wrong with my BJJ Takedowns for Beginners: Your First 5 to Master?

    You're likely not closing the distance effectively before initiating the shot. Focus on driving forward with your hips and chest, maintaining a low center of gravity, and ensuring your head is up and to the side of your opponent's center of mass as you penetrate.

    Q: My arm gets really tired and sore after trying the single leg takedown, how can I improve my BJJ Takedowns for Beginners: Your First 5 to Master without hurting myself?

    The soreness is probably from gripping too tightly and not using your legs and core to drive. Once you secure the single leg, drive forward with your legs and hips, keeping your grip firm but relaxed, allowing your body's momentum to do the work.

    Q: How can I use the hip bump sweep effectively as one of my BJJ Takedowns for Beginners: Your First 5 to Master when my opponent is much heavier than me?

    Against a heavier opponent, leverage is key. From your guard, create a strong angle by shifting your hips to one side, then explosively bump your hips upwards and towards their side, using your arms to push their shoulder and create the imbalance for the sweep.

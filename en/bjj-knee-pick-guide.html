    Frequently Asked Questions

    How do I grip for the knee pick takedown?

    A common grip is to secure the opponent's collar with one hand and their opposite arm (wrist or bicep) with the other. This allows for control and leverage to initiate the takedown.

    What's the most important part of the knee pick?

    The most crucial element is the level change and forward pressure. You need to drop your hips and drive forward powerfully to break their base and secure the takedown.

    What if my opponent defends the knee pick?

    If they sprawl, you can transition to a single leg or try to circle around their hips. Maintaining connection and adapting your attack is key to overcoming their defense.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why do I keep losing my balance and falling over when I try to execute the BJJ Knee Pick Takedown?

    You're likely not driving your hips forward and down with sufficient force. Ensure your lead leg drives off the mat while your hips remain low and anchored, creating a stable base and pulling your opponent's weight over their supporting leg.

    Q: How can I effectively finish the BJJ Knee Pick Takedown against a much larger opponent who is resisting?

    Focus on using your body weight to drive into their chest and control their posture by keeping your head up and chest tight against theirs. As you pick their knee, simultaneously pull their collar or shoulder to break their balance and guide them down.

    Q: What is the most common mistake white belts make when attempting the BJJ Knee Pick Takedown that leads to them getting swept?

    The most common error is reaching too far with the arm to grab the knee, which exposes your own balance. Instead, step in close and use your shoulder to bump their hip as you secure the knee with your arm, keeping your body connected.

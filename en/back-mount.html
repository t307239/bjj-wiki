Back Mount: The Ultimate BJJ Position Guide for White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Blue Belt Guide

Back Mount is a dominant control position in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu where you secure your opponent's back, controlling their posture and limiting their movement.

Many white belts struggle with Back Mount because they focus on the arms and legs, forgetting the core principle: controlling the hips and preventing escape.

The key mechanical insight is to establish a strong "seatbelt grip" and "hook" with your legs, keeping your hips lower than their hips to prevent them from turning into you.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a "Seatbelt Grip": With your opponent on their stomach, reach your right arm over their left shoulder, gripping your own left bicep with your right hand. Your left arm goes under their right armpit to grip your own right bicep. Ensure your knuckles are pointed towards their spine.
    2. Secure the "Hooks": Immediately insert your left leg hook between their legs, with your shin across their hip bone and your foot pointing towards the ceiling. Repeat with your right leg hook on the other side, ensuring both knees are bent at approximately 90 degrees.
    3. "Climb" your Hooks: If your hooks are not deep enough, actively drive your knees forward while maintaining the seatbelt grip. This allows you to "climb" your hooks higher on their hips, closer to their ribs.
    4. Lower Your Base: Drop your hips down so they are lower than your opponent's hips. This creates a downward pressure and prevents them from posturing up or turning.
    5. Maintain Spinal Alignment: Keep your chest pressed against their upper back, aligning your spine with theirs. This limits their ability to create space and turn.
    6. Weight Distribution: Distribute your weight evenly over their upper back and shoulders. Avoid leaning too far forward or backward, which creates openings.
    7. Control Their Head: Use your chin to lightly press against their head, preventing them from turning their head to create escape angles.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect "Hook" Placement: Driving your knee directly into your opponent's hip bone with your shin horizontal can cause hyperextension of your own knee or damage their hip socket. The correct method is to insert your shin across the hip bone, with your foot pointing towards the ceiling, maintaining a bent knee.
    • "Seatbelt Grip" Slippage: Allowing your arms to become too wide or loose on the seatbelt grip, especially when your opponent turns, can lead to your arms being trapped or your opponent escaping. Always maintain a tight grip on your own biceps, keeping your knuckles close to their spine.
    • Excessive Forward Lean: Leaning too far forward with your chest while attempting to maintain the seatbelt grip can cause your own base to become unstable, allowing your opponent to shrimp or roll you over. Keep your hips lower than theirs and maintain a balanced posture.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo "Hook" Insertion and "Seatbelt Grip" Practice: From a kneeling position, practice reaching for the seatbelt grip and inserting your hooks. Repeat 20 times per side.
    2. Partner Drill: "Seatbelt Grip" and "Hook" Hold: Your partner lies on their stomach. You practice securing the seatbelt grip and hooks, holding for 15 seconds. Repeat 10 times.
    3. Partner Drill: "Climbing the Hooks": Your partner starts with you on their back but with loose hooks. You practice climbing your hooks higher on their hips while maintaining the seatbelt grip. Repeat 10 reps.
    4. Partner Drill: "Back Mount Maintenance" (0% Resistance): Your partner attempts to escape from Back Mount, and you focus solely on maintaining the position without attempting submissions. Repeat 5 minutes.
    5. Partner Drill: "Back Mount with Light Submission Attempts" (25% Resistance): Your partner attempts light escapes. You maintain Back Mount and practice setting up a basic submission like a rear-naked choke, but without applying full pressure. Repeat 5 minutes.
    6. Live Rolling (50% Resistance): During live rolling, prioritize securing and maintaining Back Mount. Focus on the mechanics learned, and don't worry about winning or losing.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • After a successful takedown where you land on your opponent's back.
    • During a scramble where you manage to get behind your opponent.
    • When your opponent attempts a submission from bottom and you can turn their escape into a Back Mount.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • "Shrimping" and "Bridging": When the attacker has hooks in, you can "shrimp" your hips away to create space and then bridge to disrupt their base, potentially allowing you to roll them or escape.
    • "Elbow-Knee Connection": If the attacker has a loose seatbelt grip, you can bring your elbows to your knees and then attempt to turn into them, breaking their control.
    • "Arm Trap and Turn": If one of the attacker's arms is exposed, you can trap it with your own arm and then use that leverage to initiate a turn and escape.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why do my shoulders hurt when I try to get Back Mount on a bigger opponent?

    Your shoulders are likely hurting because you are using your arms to try and "pull" your opponent into position, creating excessive tension and strain. Instead, focus on using your legs to "climb" your hooks and your hips to "lower" your base. This shifts the weight distribution to your core and legs, reducing shoulder load and increasing control.

    Q: How can I prevent my opponent from turning into me when I have Back Mount?

    The most common reason for an opponent turning into you is insufficient hip control. Ensure your hooks are deep, with your shins across their hip bones and your feet pointing towards the ceiling. Crucially, keep your hips lower than their hips. This downward pressure makes it biomechanically difficult for them to generate the rotational force needed to turn.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from a failed takedown to attempting Back Mount?

    The ideal moment is during the "scramble" phase immediately after a failed takedown, when both you and your opponent are off-balance. If you find yourself behind your opponent's hips as they are falling or trying to regain their base, quickly insert your hooks and establish the seatbelt grip. This is a high-percentage opportunity to secure the dominant position.

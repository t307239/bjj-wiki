Leandro Lo

BJJ Athlete8x World ChampionBlack Belt

Leandro Lo (Leandro Lo Pereira, 1991–2022) was a Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu black belt and one of the most decorated competitors in the sport's history, winning 8 World Championship titles across multiple weight classes and at absolute. A student of Roberto Satoshi at NS Brotherhood, Lo was celebrated for his masterful guard passing, creative half guard play, and dominant open guard — a complete game that made him virtually unstoppable at the highest levels of gi competition.

Biography

Born in São Paulo, Brazil in 1991, Leandro Lo began training BJJ under Roberto Satoshi Souza at NS Brotherhood (Não Sou Novo Brotherhood). He earned his black belt and rapidly rose through the competitive ranks, becoming one of the most dominant forces in gi BJJ during the 2010s.

Lo competed across multiple weight categories throughout his career — from lightweight to heavyweight and the absolute — winning world titles at multiple weights, a feat that underscores both his technical mastery and physical adaptability. He was awarded the IBJJF World Champion title in 2012, 2013, 2014, 2015, 2016, 2017, 2019, and 2020.

Leandro Lo passed away in July 2022 at the age of 33. His legacy endures through his competition footage, instructionals, and the many students and fans he inspired worldwide.

Competitive Style

Leandro Lo's game was characterized by athleticism, creativity, and a guard passing system that was both aggressive and systematic. Key elements of his style:

  • Dynamic guard passing: Lo was famous for his ability to pass the guard of the world's best players with speed and positional awareness. His passes were often initiated from distance, using footwork to create angles before committing.
  • Half guard mastery: Both from top and bottom, Lo's half guard understanding was exceptional. He could create sweeps and back-takes from underneath, and had precise pressure passing from on top.
  • Open guard versatility: Lo played effective spider guard, De La Riva, and X-guard, creating transitions and sweeps seamlessly.
  • Submission hunting: While Lo was a points-based competitor at the elite level, he possessed dangerous submission skills including arm triangles, back takes to rear naked choke, and chokes from guard.

Guard Passing System

Leandro Lo's guard passing is considered one of the most sophisticated in BJJ history. Core principles:

  • Footwork and angle: Lo rarely attacked guard passes straight on. He used lateral movement and circling to create passing angles before committing, making it difficult for guard players to set up their best attacks.
  • Torreando (Bullfighter) pass: One of Lo's signatures — redirecting guard player's legs to create a passing lane, then moving decisively to side control or north-south.
  • Knee cut / knee slice: Lo's knee slice was precise and well-timed, using body weight and angle rather than brute strength.
  • Back-step sequences: Lo frequently used back-steps and leg drags to bypass complex guard entanglements.
  • Pressure from half guard top: His ability to slowly and methodically pass through half guard from on top was nearly unbeatable — using hip pressure, underhooks, and patient positional control.

Guard Game

Though most famous as a passer, Lo was a complete player from guard as well. He played:

  • Half guard: Used Z-guard and underhook-based half guard to create sweeps and back-takes.
  • Spider guard and lasso: Especially at lighter weights where longer limb leverage is an advantage.
  • X-guard and single leg X: Transitional guard to create off-balancing and sweeps.

Championship Titles

Leandro Lo won 8 IBJJF World Championship titles: 2012 (lightweight), 2013 (middleweight), 2014 (medium-heavyweight), 2015 (lightweight), 2016 (medium-heavyweight), 2017 (lightweight), 2019 (absolute), and 2020 (lightweight). He also won multiple Brasileiros titles and Pan-American Championships, establishing himself as a multi-class champion across more than a decade.

Legacy

Leandro Lo is remembered as one of the greatest BJJ competitors of all time — a rare combination of technical brilliance, athletic ability, and relentless competitive drive. His instructional materials (particularly the "Lo Guard" and guard passing systems) continue to influence students and competitors worldwide. He is frequently cited alongside Roger Gracie, Marcus Almeida "Buchecha," and Gordon Ryan when discussing the greatest competitors in the sport's history.

⚡ Learning from Leandro Lo

  • Study Lo's torreando pass — it works at all levels and his execution is textbook.
  • Watch his footwork before committing to a pass — the angle is everything.
  • His half guard top game is one of the best ever recorded: study how he uses hip pressure and underhook control methodically.
  • Lo's competition footage is freely available online — watching full matches reveals his pressure-building approach over time, not just individual techniques.
Q: How many World Championships did Leandro Lo win?

Leandro Lo won 8 IBJJF World Championships across his career (2012–2020), competing at multiple weight classes. This places him among the most decorated competitive BJJ practitioners of all time.

Q: What was Leandro Lo known for technically?

Lo was best known for his guard passing system — particularly his footwork-based approach to creating passing angles, his torreando/bullfighter pass, and his half guard top control. He was a complete player with a strong guard game as well, but his passing is considered his defining technical legacy.

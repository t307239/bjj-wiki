Ippon Seoi Nage: The One-Armed Shoulder Throw for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

This is a dynamic throwing technique originating from a standing position, aiming to off-balance and take your opponent to the ground.

White belts often struggle by trying to muscle the throw rather than understanding the kinematic chain and leverage.

The key is to disrupt their base and use your body's rotation and hip drive to create the throwing motion.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a strong Collar Grip with your right hand, digging your knuckles into the opponent's gi collar, approximately at the sternal notch.
    2. Simultaneously secure a Sleeve Grip with your left hand on the opponent's right sleeve, just above the elbow, fingers pointing upwards.
    3. Step your left foot forward, placing it slightly wider than shoulder-width apart, creating a stable base.
    4. Rotate your hips to your right, angling your pelvis approximately 45 degrees towards your opponent.
    5. Duck your head and right shoulder underneath the opponent's right arm, bringing your head to their chest level.
    6. Drive your right hip forward and upwards, making contact with the opponent's right hip or lower abdomen.
    7. Execute a powerful hip rotation and slight forward lean, pulling the sleeve grip and pushing with your right shoulder to initiate the throw.
    8. As the opponent is airborne, continue the hip rotation and guide them to the mat with control, maintaining your base.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly rounding your back during the duck under: This can strain your lumbar spine and lead to a disc herniation by compressing the intervertebral discs excessively.

    The correct movement involves hinging at the hips and maintaining a neutral spine while bending your knees.

    • Attempting to pull the opponent over your shoulder with arm strength alone: This puts excessive stress on your shoulder joint, potentially causing a rotator cuff tear or labral tear due to hyperextension or uncontrolled rotation.

    The correct motion involves driving your hip and rotating your torso, using your arm only for control and guiding the throw.

    • Stepping too close or too wide with your feet: Stepping too close compromises your base, making you vulnerable to sweeps or throws, while stepping too wide can lead to knee strain or ankle sprains if your base is unstable.

    Maintain a stable, shoulder-width base with slightly bent knees, allowing for effective weight transfer and balance.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling: Practice the steps 1-8 without a partner, focusing on hip rotation and footwork. (10 reps, 0% resistance).
    2. Partner Static Grip & Duck Under: With a compliant partner, practice establishing grips and performing the duck under and hip drive. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance).
    3. Partner Controlled Throw: With a compliant partner, execute the full Ippon Seoi Nage, focusing on control and technique. (5 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Resistance Drill 1: Partner offers light resistance to the duck under and sleeve pull. Focus on driving through their resistance. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Resistance Drill 2: Partner attempts to post and resist the hip drive. Focus on maintaining connection and hip contact. (10 reps per side, 75% resistance).
    6. Live Takedown Situational Sparring: Attempt Ippon Seoi Nage from a standing clinch, with the partner actively defending. (5 attempts per round, 90% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is posturing up, creating an opening at their chest and neck.
    • Opponent is leaning heavily forward, making their base unstable.
    • When you have secured a strong collar and sleeve grip, allowing you to control their posture.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Posting the far leg: As you duck under, the opponent can step their far leg back and to the side, creating a wider base and preventing the throw.
    • Sprawling: If the opponent commits to the throw without proper connection, the defender can sprawl their hips back and down, breaking the hip connection.
    • Reversing the throw: A skilled opponent might use your committed forward momentum to reverse the throw by rotating their hips and stepping to the opposite side.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try Ippon Seoi Nage?

    Your shoulder likely hurts because you are trying to use your arm to lift and throw your opponent, rather than using your hips and core. When you duck under, ensure your head is low and your shoulder is driving into their chest, not their armpit. The throw is generated by hip rotation and a forward drive, with your arm acting as a lever and guide, not the primary lifting force. Focus on a powerful hip hinge and rotation to take the strain off your shoulder.

    Q: How can I use Ippon Seoi Nage against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing become paraMount. Focus on disrupting their base by pulling their sleeve and collar inward and slightly downward as you duck under. Your hip contact needs to be firm and driven by your leg power, not just your torso. Look for opportunities when they are slightly off-balance or reaching. A strong grip fight to break their posture down first is essential before committing to the throw.

    Q: When should I stop trying Ippon Seoi Nage and switch to a different technique?

    You should stop trying Ippon Seoi Nage if your opponent successfully sprawls, breaking your hip connection and base, or if they manage to step their far leg back to create a very stable, wide base. Another indicator is if you've committed to the duck under but cannot achieve a strong hip drive or rotation; continuing to force it will likely lead to you being off-balance. In these scenarios, transition to a Guard Pass or a different takedown attempt.

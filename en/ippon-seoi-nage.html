Ippon Seoi Nage: The Essential BJJ Takedown Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

Ippon Seoi Nage is a dynamic judo throw, often utilized in BJJ from a standing position or during transitions. It aims to unbalance your opponent and throw them over your shoulder.

White belts often struggle by trying to muscle the throw, neglecting the crucial hip rotation and weight transfer. This leads to wasted energy and potential injury.

The core mechanical insight is transforming your opponent's forward momentum into their own downfall through precise body alignment and leverage.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a strong left collar grip with your right hand, reaching deep to the opponent's lapel near the sternum. Your left hand secures a right sleeve grip, palm facing inward, at the opponent's wrist joint.
    2. Take a step forward with your left foot, placing it slightly ahead and to the outside of the opponent's right foot. Your feet should be approximately shoulder-width apart for a stable base.
    3. Simultaneously, lower your hips by bending your knees, creating a slight backward lean of your torso. Your center of gravity should be low.
    4. Drive your right shoulder forward and slightly downward, aiming to insert it between the opponent's right arm and torso. Your right hip should be positioned near the opponent's left hip.
    5. Rotate your hips counter-clockwise, pivoting on your left foot while keeping your right foot planted. This hip rotation is critical for generating torque.
    6. As your hips rotate, lift your opponent by driving your right shoulder into their chest and using your left arm to pull their right sleeve forward and across their body.
    7. Continue the hip rotation and torso movement, bringing your opponent over your right shoulder. Extend your left leg backward for balance as they ascend.
    8. Finish by guiding your opponent to the mat, maintaining control of the grips, and settling into a dominant position like side-control or mount.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to lift the opponent solely with arm strength without hip rotation: This can strain the rotator cuff and biceps, leading to tears. The correct action is to use the powerful hip rotation to lift.
    • Stepping too close or too far with the lead foot: Stepping too close can cause your knee to collide with theirs, risking a knee ligament sprain. Stepping too far makes you unstable. Maintain a stable, slightly wider than shoulder-width stance.
    • Dropping your hips too low or too high: Dropping too low can strain your lower back and make it difficult to generate power. Dropping too high negates the leverage. Find a hip height that allows for effective hip rotation and opponent contact.
    • Not securing a deep collar grip: A shallow collar grip allows the opponent to easily posture up, negating the throw. Reach deep to the opponent's lapel for maximum control.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling (0% resistance): Practice the footwork, hip rotation, and arm movements without a partner. Focus on the sequence and body mechanics. (20 repetitions)
    2. Partnered Grip Fighting and Setup (25% resistance): Stand with a partner and practice establishing the grips and performing the initial step and hip turn. Partner offers minimal resistance, focusing on grip breaks and maintaining posture. (15 repetitions)
    3. Controlled Throwing Drills (50% resistance): With a compliant partner, execute the Ippon Seoi Nage. Partner offers controlled resistance and helps you complete the throw safely. Focus on hip and shoulder connection. (10 repetitions per side)
    4. Standing Entry Drills (75% resistance): Practice entering the throw from a more resistant stance. Partner actively defends but allows you to secure grips and begin the entry. Focus on breaking posture and creating the angle. (8 repetitions per side)
    5. Takedown Situational Drills (90% resistance): Start from a neutral standing position or after a simulated guard pull. Partner actively defends the entry and tries to counter. Focus on timing and commitment to the throw. (5 repetitions per side)
    6. Live Rolling (100% resistance): Integrate the Ippon Seoi Nage into sparring. Look for opportunities to set up and execute the throw. Focus on successful entries and finishes, accepting that not every attempt will be successful. (5-10 attempts per roll)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up and leaning slightly forward, creating an opening for a forward throw.
    • During grip fighting exchanges when you can secure a strong collar and sleeve grip and break their posture.
    • When your opponent attempts a forward movement or a weak takedown, which you can redirect into your Ippon Seoi Nage.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • The 'Swim' Defense: As the thrower enters, the defender quickly swims their arm (the one connected to the sleeve grip) across their body and under the thrower's armpit, creating a frame and preventing the throw. This requires immediate reaction and arm extension.
    • The 'Posture Up and Drive': The defender immediately fights to regain upright posture, driving their hips back and away from the thrower's center of gravity. Simultaneously, they can grip-fight to break the collar grip.
    • The 'Forward Roll/Breakfall': For advanced practitioners, a controlled forward roll can absorb the momentum of the throw. However, for white belts, this is a high-risk counter and should be practiced extensively before attempting under pressure.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try Ippon Seoi Nage?

    Shoulder pain during Ippon Seoi Nage often occurs when you try to force the throw with arm strength alone, or if your entry is incorrect. If you don't properly insert your shoulder between your opponent's torso and arm, and instead try to 'hug' them, you can strain your rotator cuff. Ensure your shoulder is driving into their chest and your hip is rotating to create the lever, not just your arms pulling.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, you must rely on technique over strength. Focus on their balance by using strong grips to pull them forward and break their posture. Your hip rotation needs to be explosive, and you must get your shoulder deep under their armpit. Lowering your hips significantly to get underneath their center of gravity is crucial. Timing is everything; exploit moments of instability.

    Q: Ippon Seoi Nage is primarily a standing takedown and is rarely attempted directly from the guard. However, you might transition to it if your opponent attempts to stand up from your guard or pass your guard in a way that presents an opening. For instance, if they are leaning heavily forward while trying to pass, you could use an arm drag to get to their back or transition to a standing position to attempt the throw.

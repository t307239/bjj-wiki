Pendulum Sweep: A White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

The Pendulum Sweep originates from Closed Guard and aims to invert your opponent, allowing you to take top position. It leverages your hips and legs to create leverage.

White belts often struggle because they rely on brute strength or uncontrolled movements, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key insight is using your hip as a pivot point to swing your opponent's weight over your body, creating an unbalanced tipping point.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish closed guard with both hooks in. Secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (e.g., left sleeve) with your left hand.
    2. Begin to "open" your guard slightly by straightening your legs, creating space at the hips. Your hips should be slightly elevated off the mat.
    3. With your left hand on their sleeve, pull their arm across their body towards your right hip. Simultaneously, use your right hand on the collar to "shimmy" their upper body to your right.
    4. Shift your weight onto your left hip, creating a fulcrum. Your right leg remains extended, acting as a lever.
    5. As you pull their arm and push their upper body, your right leg begins to swing in a pendulum motion, passing over their shoulder.
    6. Your hips rotate towards your opponent's left side as your right leg arcs over their shoulder, driving them down.
    7. As their weight transfers over your swinging leg, immediately drive your hips forward and up, and transition to your knees or feet to establish top position.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to "kick" your leg over their shoulder with a straight leg: This can hyperextend your knee or cause a ligament tear (e.g., ACL) if their weight is not properly controlled. Instead, maintain a slight bend in your knee and use your hip as the primary driver.
    • Pulling your opponent's arm with excessive force without hip rotation: This can strain your shoulder or bicep. Focus on initiating the movement with your hip rotation and hip elevation.
    • Allowing your opponent to flatten you out completely: This compromises your base and can lead to spinal compression or neck strain. Maintain a slight angle and keep your hips active.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip rotation and leg swing motion without a partner. Focus on the pendulum arc. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): From closed guard, practice the grip setup and the hip swing and leg arc. Partner remains passive. (20 reps)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers slight resistance to the pull and push. Focus on generating the sweep with your hip and leg. (25% resistance, 15 reps)
    4. Partner drill (controlled resistance): Partner reacts slightly to the sweep attempt, allowing you to feel the timing and leverage. (50% resistance, 10 reps)
    5. Live rolling (controlled): Attempt the sweep during positional sparring, focusing on execution and timing. Partner may defend but not actively counter aggressively. (75% resistance, 5 attempts)
    6. Live rolling (full): Integrate the Pendulum Sweep into regular sparring, aiming to utilize it when the opportunity arises. (100% resistance, ongoing)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is postured up high in your closed guard, creating an angle.
    • When your opponent attempts to stack you from closed guard, creating an opening for inversion.
    • When your opponent is reaching for your legs to pass, leaving their upper body exposed to your control.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • "Knee Shield" Defense: If your opponent attempts the sweep, quickly bring your opposite knee (the one not being used for the sweep) to their chest, creating a barrier and stopping the forward momentum. Re-establish guard or transition.
    • "Base and Drive": As they initiate the swing, the opponent can quickly widen their base and drive their weight forward into you, preventing the tipping point. This requires awareness and good base control.
    • "Straighten and Posture": If you feel the sweep starting, the defender can quickly straighten their legs and posture up, breaking the leverage and stabilizing their position. This requires quick reaction and regaining posture.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to do the Pendulum Sweep?

    Shoulder pain often occurs when you're pulling your opponent's arm with your arm alone, without using your hips and body to generate the force. Ensure your grip is secure on their collar and sleeve, and focus on initiating the movement by rotating your hips and allowing your shoulder to follow through in a controlled arc, rather than yanking.

    Q: Against a heavier opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on controlling their posture by pulling their collar tightly and using your sleeve grip to off-balance their upper body towards your hip. Elevate your hips significantly to create the space needed for your leg to swing, and ensure you are rotating your hips to drive their weight over. Don't try to force it; wait for them to create an angle or posture up.

    Q: Ending up on your back means you're not successfully creating a tipping point or you're not following through with the sweep to secure top position. Ensure your hips are actively lifting off the mat and rotating. As your leg swings over, drive your hips *forward* and *up* to complete the inversion and land in a dominant position, rather than just letting your leg go over.

Pendulum Sweep: A White Belt's Guide to BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Pendulum Sweep is initiated from Closed Guard, aiming to reverse position and achieve top control. It involves using leg leverage to unbalance and rotate your opponent over.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of hip mechanics and insufficient framing, leading to stalled attempts or injury.

The key is to create a strong base with your hips and use your legs as levers to generate rotational force, not just brute strength.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip: Establish cross-collar grip with your right hand and right sleeve grip with your left hand.
    2. Leg Position: Place your left foot on your opponent's right hip bone, knee pointing towards the ceiling.
    3. Right Leg Placement: Hook your right foot behind your opponent's left knee joint, ankle facing inwards.
    4. Hip Elevation: Elevate your hips off the mat, creating space and angling your pelvis slightly to your right.
    5. Weight Transfer (Initial): Shift your weight onto your left leg, which is anchored on their hip.
    6. Rotation Initiation: Simultaneously, pull down on the collar grip and push with your right foot behind their knee, initiating a rotation to their left.
    7. Body Movement: As they rotate, drive your right hip towards the mat and extend your left leg to push their hip away, creating an angle.
    8. Completion: Drive forward with your hips as they fall, transitioning to mount or side control. Keep your left foot on their hip to control distance.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Knee Hyperextension: Grasping the opponent's pant leg too low and pulling with straight legs can cause your opponent's knee to buckle or hyperextend, damaging the ACL or PCL. Instead, grip behind the knee and use your leg as a lever, keeping a slight bend in your supporting leg.
    • Ankle Sprains: Attempting to 'kick' the opponent over with a stiff, straight leg can lead to your own ankle twisting inwards or outwards if their weight is too substantial. Maintain a slight bend in your ankle and use a scooping motion, focusing on hip rotation.
    • Lower Back Strain: Trying to 'lift' the opponent with your back instead of using your hips and legs will put immense strain on your lumbar spine, risking disc injury. Ensure your hips are actively engaged and driving the movement, not your back muscles.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Movement (0%): Practice hip elevation and the leg hooking/pushing motion without a partner. Focus on the feeling of creating space and the angle. (20 reps)
    2. Partnered Framing (25%): With a compliant partner lying on their back, practice establishing grips and leg positions. Focus on the initial hip elevation and leg placement. (15 reps per side)
    3. Controlled Sweep Attempt (50%): Your partner offers minimal resistance. Focus on executing the sweep motion, emphasizing the hip rotation and leg drive. (10 reps per side)
    4. Adding Light Resistance (75%): Your partner actively tries to prevent the sweep but doesn't resist fully. Focus on maintaining your grips and adjusting your body angle. (8 reps per side)
    5. Simulated Sweep (90%): Your partner tries to defend the sweep effectively, but you are not actively trying to pass after. Focus on executing the sweep itself. (5 reps per side)
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Attempt the Pendulum Sweep during rolling. Focus on recognizing opportunities and applying the learned mechanics under pressure. (Until successful or opponent defends)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When opponent is posturing up in your closed guard.
    • When opponent has a loose grip or is leaning too far forward.
    • When you feel an opening to create an angle with your hips.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass Defense: If the opponent attempts to stack pass, immediately widen your base and drive your hips into their chest, preventing them from lowering their weight. Then, re-establish your guard.
    • Leg Post/Base: If the opponent posts their leg to prevent rotation, use your free leg to sweep their posted leg away or transition to a different sweep. Maintain your grips.
    • Guard Retention: If the sweep fails and you end up in a compromised position, focus on quickly re-establishing your guard by bringing your knees together and creating frames with your forearms.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do the Pendulum Sweep?

    Your knee might hurt because you are likely hyperextending it when pushing off your opponent's leg, or the angle of your hook behind their knee is incorrect. Ensure you maintain a slight bend in your hooking leg and that your foot is placed just behind the knee joint, not on the shin. Focus on using your hip to drive the rotation rather than straightening your leg.

    Q: How can I Pendulum Sweep a much bigger and heavier opponent?

    Against a heavier opponent, focus on creating a significant angle with your hips and using leverage. Elevate your hips higher than usual and ensure your foot is securely hooked behind their knee. The key is to generate momentum through rotation. Pull down on the collar to break their posture and use your leg to drive their hip away as you rotate your own body.

    Q: You are likely focusing too much on the pushing motion and not enough on the rotational aspect and breaking their posture. The 'pendulum' action requires a strong pull on the collar to bring their upper body towards you as you rotate your hips. This combined action creates the imbalance needed for the sweep, not just a push. Ensure your hips are driving the rotation.

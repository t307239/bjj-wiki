This sweep initiates from Closed Guard, aiming to invert your opponent and secure a dominant position. It's a fundamental sweep to learn for controlling the match.

White belts often struggle with the Pendulum Sweep due to insufficient hip elevation and a lack of understanding of weight transfer. They focus too much on pulling rather than using their body's mechanics.

The key insight is creating a lever with your legs and hips, using your opponent's weight against them to initiate rotation and positional dominance.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish closed guard with both feet locked around the opponent's waist. Secure double-sleeve grips (right hand on opponent's left sleeve, left hand on opponent's right sleeve).
    2. Shift your hips slightly to your right, creating a small space under your left hip. Your left knee should point towards the ceiling.
    3. Begin to elevate your hips off the mat, driving your left hip upwards and slightly forward, towards your opponent's chest.
    4. Simultaneously, pull down sharply on the opponent's right sleeve with your left hand, and pull up on their left sleeve with your right hand. This breaks their posture.
    5. As your hips are elevated, swing your right leg (the one closest to the mat) in an arc, aiming to place your right foot on the opponent's left hip bone.
    6. Continue to drive your hips up and pivot your body clockwise. Your right leg acts as a fulcrum on their hip, while your left leg continues to pull their upper body down.
    7. As your opponent's weight shifts to their left side due to the pull and your hip elevation, drive your hips forward and to the left. Your right leg pushes off their hip to propel you upwards.
    8. Immediately transition to a top position, ideally knee-on-belly or side control, as your opponent falls to their side.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect hip elevation: Driving hips forward instead of upward can strain the lower back (lumbar spine) and fail to create the necessary leverage. Always focus on lifting your hips off the mat first.
    • Knee hyperextension: Pushing off the opponent's hip with a locked-out leg can cause hyperextension of the knee joint, potentially damaging the ACL. Maintain a slight bend in your knee when pushing off.
    • Arm/Shoulder strain: Gripping too tightly on sleeves without using hip and leg drive can lead to shoulder impingement or elbow tendonitis. Focus on using your entire body's momentum, not just arm strength.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Elevation and Leg Swing: Practice lifting hips off the mat and swinging the leg in an arc without a partner. (10 reps x 3 sets, 0% resistance).
    2. Solo Guard Retention and Hip Lift: Practice closing the guard and lifting hips while maintaining the locked position. (10 reps x 3 sets, 0% resistance).
    3. Partner Assisted Hip Elevation: Have a partner lie on their back. Practice closing guard and lifting hips, feeling their weight. (5 reps x 3 sets, 25% resistance).
    4. Partner Assisted Sweep (No Resistance): Partner lies on their back, you practice the full sweep motion with no active defense from them. (5 reps x 3 sets, 50% resistance).
    5. Light Resistance Sweep Drill: Partner actively tries to prevent the sweep but offers no significant weight or power. Focus on timing and hip drive. (5 reps x 3 sets, 75% resistance).
    6. Controlled Rolling Sweep Attempt: Attempt the sweep against a resisting partner who allows the sweep to be completed if executed correctly. Focus on smooth transitions. (5 successful sweeps per partner, 90% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When opponent is postured up and you have good control of their sleeves and posture.
    • When opponent is attempting to pass your guard and you can secure their posture.
    • When opponent is on their knees and you can effectively lock their legs.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee-Shield Defense: If opponent attempts the sweep, quickly bring your knee between their hips and your own, creating a strong frame to prevent hip elevation and rotation. Your knee should be angled towards their chest.
    • Posture Up and Base Out: If opponent starts to elevate their hips, immediately sit up straight, widening your base and creating pressure forward. This disrupts their lever and prevents them from swinging their leg.
    • Grip Break and Base Reset: If they have sleeve grips, focus on breaking those grips by driving your elbows into your ribs. Simultaneously, widen your base and push your opponent away to reset your guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try the Pendulum Sweep?

    Your lower back pain likely stems from trying to force the sweep by pushing your hips forward instead of lifting them upwards. The Pendulum Sweep relies on creating an upward lever with your hips to break your opponent's base. Focus on elevating your glutes off the mat first, then initiating the hip rotation and leg swing.

    Q: Against a heavier opponent, the Pendulum Sweep requires even more precise hip elevation and a sharper, more decisive leg swing. Focus on getting your hips significantly higher than theirs to create the leverage. Use your legs to create a tight frame and pull their upper body down aggressively to off-balance them before initiating the sweep.

    Q: The Pendulum Sweep is ideal when your opponent is postured up and you have control of their sleeves and posture. It's also effective when they're on their knees. Avoid attempting it if your opponent has already established a strong base and is actively pressuring forward, as this will make it difficult to create the necessary leverage and can leave you vulnerable to passes.

Pendulum Sweep: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

This sweep originates from Closed Guard, aiming to invert your opponent and secure a dominant position. It's a fundamental transition for beginners.

Many white belts fail due to a lack of understanding of weight transfer and hip action, leading to uncontrolled movements. They often rely on brute strength instead of leverage.

The core mechanic is using your hips and leg to create a fulcrum, lifting and rotating your opponent over your shoulder.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Secure collar and sleeve grips on your opponent's gi, pulling them tightly towards your chest.
    2. Position your hips at a 90-degree angle to your opponent's body, creating a tight seal.
    3. Place your left foot (if sweeping to your right) on the mat, heel close to your glute, knee pointing upwards.
    4. Hook your right foot around your opponent's left hip, specifically targeting the iliac crest area for maximum leverage.
    5. Initiate the sweep by hip escaping to your left, driving your hips forward and upwards, creating a lifting motion.
    6. Simultaneously, pull strongly on the collar grip and push with your right foot on their hip, assisting in their rotation.
    7. As your opponent begins to fall, drive your left shoulder into their torso, continuing the rotational momentum.
    8. As they land, immediately transition your right foot to their hip and your left foot to the mat, establishing base.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Hip Extension: Attempting to lift your opponent solely by extending your hips upwards without a proper hip escape. This can strain your lower back ligaments, leading to lumbar hyperextension.
    • Knee Valgus Stress: Allowing your planted knee (e.g., left knee) to collapse inwards towards your body during the hip escape. This places excessive stress on the medial collateral ligament (MCL) of your knee.
    • Arm Overextension: Grabbing the opponent's sleeve too tightly and pulling with your arm isolated, rather than using your body's rotation. This can lead to elbow hyperextension or shoulder impingement.
    • Lack of Hip Angle: Failing to create a sufficient 90-degree hip angle, resulting in your hips being parallel to your opponent. This limits your ability to generate leverage and can lead to your own hips being swept.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Drill (0%): Practice the hip escape and leg hook motion without a partner. Focus on the feeling of your hips moving perpendicular to your opponent. (20 reps)
    2. Partner Drill - Static (25%): With a compliant partner lying on their back, practice the grips and the initial hip escape and leg hook. Partner offers minimal resistance. (15 reps per side)
    3. Partner Drill - Controlled Sweep (50%): Execute the full sweep motion against a partner who is actively trying to prevent it but allows the sweep to happen. Focus on the timing of the pull and push. (10 reps per side)
    4. Partner Drill - Light Resistance (75%): Perform the sweep against a partner who is actively defending and trying to maintain their base. Focus on adapting to their defense. (8 reps per side)
    5. Partner Drill - Dynamic (90%): Drill the Pendulum Sweep with a partner who is actively resisting and trying to counter. Focus on maintaining your position and re-establishing grips if lost. (5 reps per side)
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Attempt the Pendulum Sweep during rolling, focusing on correct execution and recognizing opportunities. Do not force the sweep if the setup isn't there. (5 minutes of rolling focused on guard retention and sweeps)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent closes their guard tightly, creating a solid frame.
    • Opponent attempts to posture up, creating space and a potential lifting point.
    • Opponent is leaning heavily forward, making their weight easier to manipulate.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: If the opponent initiates the sweep, they should immediately drive their hips back and stack their weight onto your chest, flattening you out. This prevents the rotation and can lead to a guard pass.
    • Technical Stand-up: If the opponent's leg hook is weak or their hip escape is insufficient, you can quickly technical stand up, breaking the connection and establishing your base on the mat.
    • Guard Break: If you feel the opponent's hip escape is weak, you can use their momentum against them by stepping your hips out in the opposite direction and driving your knee between their legs to break the guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do the Pendulum Sweep from closed guard?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are allowing your planted leg's knee to collapse inwards (valgus stress) during the Hip Escape. To fix this, actively keep your planted knee tracking over your foot and think about driving your hip *away* from your opponent, not just lifting. Ensure your foot is close to your glutes for stability.

    Q: Against a heavier opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on getting your hips *underneath* them as you hip escape. The key is to create a strong fulcrum with your hip and use your leg hook to lift their hips *off* the mat before initiating the rotation. Don't try to muscle them; use their weight against them by angling your body.

    Q: You're likely not generating enough rotational force. Ensure your collar grip is tight and you're pulling them *into* your shoulder as you initiate the sweep. The push from your foot on their hip is crucial for driving their rotation. Also, make sure your hip escape is sharp and creates a dynamic lift, not just a passive movement.

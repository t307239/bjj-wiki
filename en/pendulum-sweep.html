The Pendulum Sweep originates from Closed Guard and aims to invert the opponent's base, allowing you to sweep them and establish a dominant top position.

White belts often struggle because they rely on brute strength or jerky movements, failing to utilize leverage and timing effectively.

The key insight is to use your hips and a controlled pendulum motion to unbalance their base, creating an opening for the sweep.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish closed guard. Secure one sleeve grip with your left hand and the opposite collar grip with your right hand.
    2. Place your right foot's sole on their left hip crease, keeping your knee pointed towards their chest.
    3. Shift your weight slightly to your left, creating a small gap between your hips and theirs.
    4. Begin to drive your right hip upwards and towards your right, simultaneously pulling their left arm across your body with your right hand.
    5. As your right hip rises, extend your left leg slightly, creating a lever against their left thigh or knee.
    6. Continue the hip drive and arm pull, making your hips perform a controlled pendulum motion to the right.
    7. As their weight shifts uncontrollably to their left, bring your right leg over their shoulder, pivoting on your left foot to land in side control.
    8. Immediately secure your grip on their collar and sleeve to prevent them from re-establishing their base.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying excessive upward force on the collar grip without hip rotation: This can strain the rotator cuff and shoulder ligaments in the opponent's arm and your own shoulder joint, leading to tears.
    • Kicking your leg out stiffly to create space: This can hyperextend your knee or ankle, risking ligament damage (ACL, MCL, ankle sprains).
    • Allowing your hips to drop flat after the sweep attempt: This leaves your base unstable and can result in your own back being exposed, potentially leading to spinal compression or injury if they fall heavily.
    • Not clearing the opponent's hip with your own hip: This can lead to your hips getting stuck and your legs becoming tangled, increasing the risk of knee or ankle injuries from awkward twisting.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip motion and leg extension without a partner, focusing on the pendulum swing. 20 reps.
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): Focus on the grip exchange, hip movement, and leg placement with no resistance. 10 reps per side.
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Opponent offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the hip lift and arm pull. 10 reps per side.
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Opponent begins to resist slightly, forcing you to adjust timing and hip drive. 10 reps per side.
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Opponent actively tries to defend, requiring more commitment to the sweep mechanics. 5 reps per side.
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Execute the sweep during controlled rolling, focusing on commitment and finishing the sweep. 3 attempts per round.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured up slightly, creating an opening for hip ingress.
    • When the opponent's weight is not fully anchored to the mat, making them susceptible to unbalancing.
    • After a failed guard pass attempt by the opponent where they are momentarily off-balance.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape and Base Reinforcement: If you feel the sweep initiate, immediately drive your hips away from the sweeping leg and widen your base by planting your free foot firmly.
    • Guard Recovery (Tightening Guard): If the opponent's leg comes over, focus on bringing your knees back together and securing your guard tightly to prevent them from passing.
    • Framing and Stacking: Create a strong frame with your hands on their hips or shoulders and attempt to stack your weight forward, neutralizing their hip drive.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to do the Pendulum Sweep?

    Your shoulder pain likely stems from an improper collar grip or an excessive pulling motion. Instead of yanking, focus on a controlled rotation of your torso and a slight upward pull on the collar. Ensure your elbow stays relatively close to your body and avoid extending your arm fully. The primary force should come from your hips, not your arm strength.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paramount. Focus on creating a significant gap with your hip lift before initiating the pendulum motion. Use your sleeve grip to break their posture and control their arm's angle. A well-timed hip drive combined with a strong leg extension against their thigh can still unbalance even a larger opponent.

    Q: The Pendulum Sweep is most effective when your opponent is postured up, either by leaning forward to create pressure or when they are attempting to break your guard. Look for moments where their weight is not perfectly centered or when they are slightly off-balance from their own movements. Avoid attempting it when they have a very tight, low base.

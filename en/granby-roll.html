Granby Roll: Your First BJJ Transition

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Granby Roll is a dynamic transition used primarily from bottom positions like Closed Guard or half guard to escape unfavorable situations or set up sweeps. It's a forward rolling motion that re-establishes a better base or creates space for offense.

White belts often struggle because they initiate the roll with their head or shoulders, leading to uncontrolled movement and potential injury. They lack the understanding of using their hips and legs to generate momentum.

The key mechanical insight is to drive your hips UP and FORWARD, using your legs to propel your body into a controlled forward rotation, rather than 'falling' into the roll.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From bottom closed guard, establish a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip on your opponent's left sleeve with your left hand.
    2. Arch your back, bringing your hips OFF the mat and UPWARDS, creating space.
    3. Simultaneously, drive your left knee UP and ACROSS your body, towards your opponent's right hip.
    4. Begin to rotate your torso FORWARD, leading with your chest, aiming for a forward somersault motion.
    5. As your hips clear your opponent's base, extend your legs to create separation and land on your feet or knees.
    6. Maintain the collar and sleeve grip throughout the roll to control your opponent's posture.
    7. Focus on driving through your hips and legs to initiate the roll, not just your upper body.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect head/neck drive: Driving your head directly into the mat can cause cervical spine hyperextension or compression, leading to neck injury. The correct movement uses your hips as the primary driver.
    • Knee impact: Rolling without sufficient hip elevation can cause your knees to slam directly into the mat, potentially damaging the patella or anterior cruciate ligament (ACL). Ensure your hips are high before initiating the roll.
    • Unsupported torso rotation: Initiating the roll by simply throwing your shoulders forward without hip drive can lead to uncontrolled spinal flexion and potential back strain. Use your legs to power the hip rotation.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Drive Drill: On your back, practice arching your back and lifting your hips UP and FORWARD, simulating the initial hip drive, 10 reps.
    2. Solo Roll Drill (No Partner): On a mat, practice the full Granby Roll motion without a partner, focusing on a smooth, controlled forward rotation, 5 reps.
    3. Assisted Granby Roll (No Resistance): With a partner lying on their back, practice the Granby Roll escape, focusing on hip drive and rotation, 5 reps per side.
    4. Light Resistance Granby Roll: Partner offers minimal resistance, perhaps a loose grip, as you execute the Granby Roll, 5 reps per side (25% resistance).
    5. Controlled Granby Roll: Partner actively tries to prevent the roll with light pressure, focusing on maintaining hip drive and overcoming slight resistance, 5 reps per side (50% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling Granby Roll: Attempt the Granby Roll during live rolling when an opportunity arises, focusing on timing and proper execution, 3-5 attempts per round (75-90% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent has established strong control from closed guard and you are struggling to create space.
    • As a counter to certain guard pass attempts, like a knee-slice where you can use the momentum to roll.
    • To escape a bad position in half guard where your opponent is stacking you.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stacking Counter: If your opponent attempts a Granby Roll, immediately stack your weight onto their hips and shoulders, preventing them from rotating. This can lead to a sweep or submission.
    • Posting and Bridging: If you see the roll coming, post your hands on the mat and bridge your hips UP and AWAY, disrupting their rotational momentum and potentially trapping their arm.
    • Leg Grapevine: As they begin to roll, secure a leg grapevine on their legs and use your hips to drive them sideways, preventing forward momentum and potentially transitioning to a sweep.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck hurt after attempting the Granby Roll?

    Neck pain from the Granby Roll usually indicates you're leading the roll with your head or shoulders instead of your hips. This causes your cervical spine to hyperextend or compress. To fix this, focus on driving your hips UP and FORWARD first, using your legs to initiate the forward rotation. Your head should follow the natural curve of your body's roll, not be the primary point of contact or force.

    Q: Against a bigger opponent, the Granby Roll requires even more emphasis on hip elevation and creating space. Use your legs to aggressively push their hips away as you begin to arch. The initial drive must be powerful and upward to create enough clearance. Think of it as 'lifting' yourself over their base rather than 'rolling' under them. Maintaining your grip is crucial to prevent them from simply falling with you.

    Q: The Granby Roll is best used when you are stuck in a position where your opponent has good control and you need to change the angle of engagement drastically. Common scenarios include being trapped in their closed guard with them posturing up, or in half guard when they are pressuring heavily and you need to create immediate space to re-guard or escape. It's a dynamic escape and transition tool, not a primary offensive move.

The Granby Roll: A White Belt's Guide to BJJ Transitions

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Granby Roll is a dynamic transition used to escape inferior positions like the bottom of Side Control or mount, aiming to achieve a more dominant or neutral position.

White belts often struggle by attempting to muscle through the movement, leading to uncontrolled rolls and potential injury rather than a fluid, controlled escape.

The key mechanical insight is to use the opponent's pressure to initiate momentum, creating a rotational force through your hips and shoulders.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From bottom side control, establish a "frame" with your lead forearm (near opponent's head) pressing into their neck/shoulder, and your trail arm gripping their far hip or pant leg at the knee.
    2. Shift your hips towards your lead arm, creating a slight angle of approximately 30 degrees away from your opponent's chest.
    3. Contract your core and drive your lead shoulder downwards and slightly forwards, initiating a forward-rolling motion.
    4. As your lead shoulder contacts the mat, simultaneously drive your trail hip up and over your body, tucking your chin to your chest.
    5. Maintain a tight "fetal position" tuck, minimizing exposed limbs to prevent the opponent from establishing grips.
    6. Continue the rotation, allowing your back to curve, aiming to bring your hips past your head.
    7. As you complete the roll, extend your legs and push off the mat with your hands to create space and stand or transition to a guard.
    8. If escaping mount, the principle is similar, but you'll likely initiate the roll from your lead shoulder, driving your hips over your opponent's legs.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly tucking the chin: Leads to whiplash or cervical spine injury by hyperextending the neck backward during the roll.

    Correct: Keep the chin tucked firmly to the chest throughout the entire roll.

    • Over-extending the limbs during the roll: Exposes knees and ankles to potential hyperextension or ligament tears as they cannot absorb the rotational force.

    Correct: Maintain a tight, "fetal position" tuck, keeping elbows and knees close to the body.

    • Rolling directly into the opponent's weight: Puts excessive pressure on the spine and hips, risking compression fractures or hip joint damage.

    Correct: Use the opponent's pressure as a lever, rolling perpendicular to their weight distribution, not into it.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo: Practice the tuck and hip rotation motion on a soft mat without any movement. (50 reps)
    2. Solo: Practice the full rolling motion on a soft mat, focusing on tucking the chin and rotating from the hips. (50 reps)
    3. Partnered (0% resistance): Lie on your back with your partner in side control. Practice the frames and initiate the roll, allowing them to follow passively. (20 reps each side)
    4. Partnered (25% resistance): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to initiate the roll and escape to your feet or guard. Focus on maintaining your frame. (20 reps each side)
    5. Partnered (50% resistance): Your partner actively tries to prevent the roll by maintaining some pressure, but you can still initiate and complete the escape. (20 reps each side)
    6. Live Rolling (75% resistance): Execute the Granby Roll during live rolling when the opportunity arises, focusing on safe execution and achieving a better position. (5-10 attempts)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent has established a tight side control or mount, with their weight significantly on your chest.
    • You feel your base compromised and are struggling to shrimp or create space.
    • You are about to be submitted from a dominant position and need a quick, drastic positional change.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • "Stacking": When the opponent initiates the roll, the defender can drive their weight forward and "stack" on top of the roller, preventing the rotation and potentially securing a pin.
    • "Posting and Bridging": The defender can quickly post an arm on the mat to stop the roll and then bridge their hips up, creating space and disrupting the roller's momentum.
    • "Grip Fighting and Re-securing": If the roller attempts to use the Granby to escape, the defender can immediately fight for grips on the roller's arms or legs, preventing them from completing the rotation and re-establishing control.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck hurt after attempting a Granby Roll?

    Neck pain typically occurs from not tucking your chin sufficiently. When you roll, your head can snap backward, leading to strain on the cervical spine. Always maintain a tight chin tuck, as if holding an apple between your chin and chest, throughout the entire rolling motion.

    Q: How do I Granby Roll against a much bigger and heavier opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paraMount. Instead of trying to force the roll, use their weight against them. When they apply pressure, create a frame and angle your hips to use their forward momentum to initiate your rotation. Focus on a tight tuck and rolling perpendicular to their weight, not directly into it, to avoid being crushed.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Granby Roll, and what if I mess it up?

    The Granby Roll is best attempted when you feel trapped and are about to be submitted or lose significant positional advantage. If you mess it up, the most common consequence is ending up in a worse position or getting stacked. Prioritize safety: if you feel uncontrolled, tuck your chin and protect yourself, even if it means ending up in a less-than-ideal spot. Recovering to a safer position is better than injury.

    More Questions

    When is the best time to switch from De La Riva to Combat Base?

    The optimal time to transition is when your opponent attempts to pass your guard by driving forward or stepping over your DLR hook. This is your cue to initiate the switch before they solidify their position.

    What are the main advantages of using Combat Base against a DLR opponent?

    Combat Base offers superior stability and frames, allowing you to better defend sweeps and prevent your opponent from advancing their hips. It also creates better angles for potential sweeps or transitions to other guards.

    How do I prevent my opponent from countering my switch from DLR to Combat Base?

    Maintain a strong grip on your opponent's leg and use your free leg to push their hip away as you transition. Keeping your hips low and your base wide will make it harder for them to exploit the movement.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: When I try to transition from De La Riva guard to Combat Base, why does my opponent often end up on top of me instead of me sweeping them?

    This commonly happens when you prematurely lift your hips and extend your legs to initiate the sweep before securing your opponent's base. To avoid this, ensure your DLR hook is deep and your free leg is actively pushing their hip away, creating leverage before you attempt to hip escape and drive through.

    Q: How can I effectively use Combat Base to create space and prevent my opponent from passing my guard when they are much heavier than me?

    Against a heavier opponent, focus on using the shin of your top leg in Combat Base to actively wedge between their hips and torso, preventing them from collapsing your guard. Simultaneously, drive your bottom knee into their hip to maintain distance and create angles for sweeps or to establish a better guard.

    Q: What is the main difference in hip movement between setting up a sweep from De La Riva guard and transitioning to Combat Base?

    In De La Riva, the primary hip movement for sweeping involves a powerful hip escape and extension of your legs to drive your opponent over. For Combat Base, the hip movement is more about a controlled hip lift and pivot, using your knees and shins to create a stable platform and transition to a more dominant position.

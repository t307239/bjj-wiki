The Pressure Pass is initiated from various guard positions, aiming to break down the opponent's base and secure a dominant side-control or Mount position.

White belts often fail by trying to force the pass with raw strength rather than understanding leverage and body mechanics, leading to stall and potential injury.

The core principle is to use your body weight and structure to create constant, overwhelming pressure, collapsing their guard and making escapes impossible.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From closed guard, establish double-on-the-hip grips, with your forearms pressing into the opponent's iliac crests, thumbs pointing upwards.
    2. Lower your hips towards the mat, creating a strong base with your knees bent at 90 degrees and feet flat on the floor, spaced shoulder-width apart.
    3. Drive your chest into the opponent's sternum, ensuring your shoulders are slightly higher than their hips, initiating a downward and forward force.
    4. Simultaneously, begin to 'smash' your hips forward and slightly to one side (e.g., your left hip smashing towards their right hip).
    5. As you drive forward, begin to uncoil your legs, extending your right leg (assuming you are smashing to the left) to clear their leg while maintaining hip contact.
    6. Keep your head up and connected to their chest; do not let your head drop below their hips, which compromises your base and vision.
    7. Once their legs are separated, maintain chest-to-chest pressure and drive your hips through to achieve side-control, securing an underhook on their far arm and a cross-face grip.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Forcing the pass with straight legs: This can lead to knee hyperextension or ligament tears (ACL/MCL) if the opponent suddenly shifts their weight or creates a frame. The correct movement is to maintain bent knees and drive with the hips and core.
    • Arching the back excessively: This puts immense strain on the lumbar spine and can cause disc compression or muscle strains. Maintain a neutral spine and drive pressure from the hips and chest.
    • Releasing head control: Allowing your head to come off their chest breaks your connection and base, making you vulnerable to sweeps and submissions. Keep your head firmly planted as a point of connection and pressure.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Drive Drill: From your knees, practice driving your hips forward and to the side, mimicking the smashing motion, 20 reps per side.
    2. Mat Pressure Drill: On your knees, place your forearms on a mat representing the opponent's hips. Drive your hips forward and downward, simulating the pressure transfer, 20 reps.
    3. Partnered Grip and Drive Drill (0% resistance): With a partner on their back, establish double-on-the-hip grips and practice the initial hip smash and chest drive for 30 seconds.
    4. Partnered Guard Break Drill (25% resistance): Partner attempts to maintain closed guard. You focus on breaking the guard using the hip smash and chest drive. Reps: 5 passes per side.
    5. Controlled Pass Drill (50% resistance): Partner uses light resistance to maintain guard. You focus on executing the full pressure pass sequence. Reps: 5 passes per side.
    6. Live Rolling (75% resistance): Focus on implementing the pressure pass in a rolling scenario, understanding when to commit and when to disengage. Rounds: 3 x 3 minutes.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent has their knees too far apart in closed guard, creating a natural opening.
    • When the opponent attempts a sweep but fails to establish a strong base, leaving them vulnerable.
    • When you have established a strong chest-to-chest connection and they are unable to create frames.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape and Frame: As the pressure passer drives forward, the defender immediately performs a hip escape to the opposite side of the pressure and uses their forearm to create a frame between their hips and the passer's chest.
    • Guard Retention with Knee Shield: The defender actively brings their knee up to create a barrier between their hips and the passer's chest, using their foot to hook the passer's hip or bicep.
    • Leg Pummeling and Re-guarding: The defender actively pummels their legs to re-establish guard, often by bringing one leg inside the passer's hips and using it as a lever to create space and re-close the guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my back hurt when I try to Pressure Pass from closed guard?

    Your back is likely hurting because you are arching your spine to generate force, rather than using your hips and core. When establishing the double-on-the-hip grips, ensure your hips are low and your chest is driving forward. Focus on a powerful hip smash and forward drive, keeping your spine neutral. Imagine driving through the opponent's hips with your own.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, raw pressure alone might not be enough. Focus on precise grip fighting to prevent them from establishing frames. Use your hip angle to 'smash' to the side, making it difficult for them to maintain their base. Once you get past a leg, immediately drive your hips through to secure the position before they can recover their hips.

    Q: You should consider switching passes if the opponent successfully establishes strong frames with their arms and legs, preventing your chest from making contact or your hips from driving through. If they begin to shrimp effectively to create space, it's a sign the pressure pass is stalled, and you might transition to a knee-slice or leg-drag if you have a favorable grip.

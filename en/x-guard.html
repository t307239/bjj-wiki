The X-Guard is an open guard position where one leg is hooked inside the opponent's thigh and the other is placed on their hip, creating an X-shaped structure. It's designed to off-balance, attack, and sweep opponents from the bottom.

White belts often struggle with X-Guard by trying to force the position rather than creating angles and leverage. They also tend to keep their hips too flat, negating the power of the guard.

The core of X-Guard lies in creating a strong base with your hooked leg and using your free leg to control the opponent's hip angle, driving them forward and off-balance.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Starting from an open guard, establish a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a left sleeve grip with your left hand.
    2. Drive your left knee towards your opponent's left hip, creating space to insert your right foot.
    3. Hook your right foot's sole on the inside of your opponent's left thigh, just above the knee, ensuring your shin is perpendicular to their leg.
    4. Place the sole of your left foot firmly on your opponent's right hip bone, creating a strong frame.
    5. Simultaneously, begin to sit up, elevating your hips off the mat and tilting your pelvis forward, creating a 45-degree angle.
    6. Shift your weight onto your right hooked leg, driving your hips upwards and forward, forcing your opponent to step their left leg back.
    7. Maintain the cross-collar grip and pull down, while using your left foot on the hip to push and maintain the angle, guiding their weight forward and off-balance.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to 'pull' the opponent over with arm strength alone: This can lead to shoulder dislocation or rotator cuff tears due to excessive torque on the glenohumeral joint.
    • Keeping hips flat on the mat and not driving upwards: This results in a weak base and makes it easy for the opponent to stack, potentially causing lumbar spine compression injuries.
    • Hooking the opponent's knee joint directly with the foot: This puts extreme valgus/varus stress on the knee ligaments (ACL/MCL), leading to tears or sprains.
    • Overextending the leg on the hip frame: This can strain the hip flexors or cause adductor pulls if the opponent resists with significant force.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg hook and hip frame placement, focusing on hip elevation and pelvic tilt. (50 reps per side)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): From a seated position, have a partner stand in front of you. Practice inserting your legs and establishing the X-Guard structure. (25 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance - 25%): Partner stands, you establish X-Guard. They offer minimal forward pressure. Focus on maintaining frames and hip angle. (30 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (light resistance - 50%): Partner applies light forward pressure and attempts to step around. You practice driving hips and controlling angles. (20 reps per side)
    5. Partner drill (medium resistance - 75%): Partner applies moderate pressure and attempts to break your structure. Focus on maintaining your base and driving their weight. (15 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling (90-100%): Attempt to implement X-Guard transitions and sweeps against a resisting opponent. Focus on understanding timing and leverage. (5 minutes rounds, 3 rounds)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When an opponent is posturing up from your closed guard, creating space.
    • When your opponent is attempting to pass your guard and is within range to establish leg hooks.
    • When you have successfully transitioned to your back from a failed sweep or submission attempt.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stacking: The opponent drives their weight directly down onto your hips, attempting to crush your base. Maintain your frames and drive your hips up to create space.
    • Knee Slice Pass: The opponent attempts to drive a knee across your body. Use your free leg to push their hip away and your hooked leg to pull their leg across.
    • Toreando Pass: The opponent attempts to circle their hips around your legs. Use your foot on the hip to push their hip away and your leg hook to pull their leg closer, forcing them to face you.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my hip flexor hurt when I try to set up X-Guard?

    This pain often occurs because you're trying to force the leg hook into position without creating sufficient hip separation. Ensure you're actively driving your knee towards their hip to create the necessary space first. Also, check that your pelvis is tilted forward; a flat pelvis restricts hip mobility and strains the hip flexors.

    Q: How can I use X-Guard effectively against a much bigger and heavier opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paraMount. Focus on precise foot placement on the inside thigh and hip bone. Instead of trying to lift them, focus on driving their weight forward and off-balance by elevating your hips and creating a strong angle. Use your collar grip to pull their upper body down as you drive their hips forward.

    Q: When should I transition from X-Guard to a sweep or submission?

    The ideal moment to transition is when you feel the opponent's weight shift significantly forward and their base destabilize. This is your window to execute a sweep, like the X-Guard sweep, by driving their hips further and using your leg hook to lift their leg. If they react defensively by posting a hand or leg, that can be an opportunity for a submission like an Armbar if the angle is right.

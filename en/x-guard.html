X-Guard: Your First Steps in BJJ

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Purple Belt Requirements

The X-Guard is an open guard position where one leg is hooked inside the opponent's thigh and the other is between their legs, creating an 'X' shape. It aims to unbalance and attack the opponent's base.

White belts often struggle with X-Guard due to a lack of core stability and understanding of weight distribution, leading to them being easily passed.

The fundamental principle of X-Guard is creating an angle of attack by lifting and sweeping the opponent using your hips and legs as levers.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a seated open guard, establish a standard collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (e.g., right sleeve) with your left hand.
    2. Swing your right leg over the opponent's right hip, hooking your foot on the outside of their thigh, just above the knee.
    3. Bring your left leg through the opponent's legs, placing your left foot on their left hip bone for a strong frame.
    4. Simultaneously, lift your hips off the mat, creating a 45-degree angle relative to the opponent's torso.
    5. Secure your right arm's grip on the opponent's right collar, pulling their upper body towards you to break their posture.
    6. Use your left foot on their hip to push and create space, while your right leg acts as a lever by flexing your hamstring and extending your knee slightly.
    7. Initiate the sweep by driving your hips upwards and forwards, scooping your opponent's leg with your hooked right leg and pushing them over your base.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly extending your knee under the opponent's weight: This can cause hyperextension of the knee joint, leading to ACL or MCL tears.
    • Correct alternative: Maintain a slight bend in your knee and use your hip and core to absorb and redirect weight.
    • Grabbing the opponent's pants too low and pulling directly: This can strain your biceps and shoulders, and your opponent can easily posture up.
    • Correct alternative: Grip the opponent's collar and sleeve to control their upper body, and use your legs to control their lower body.
    • Allowing your hips to sag to the mat: This removes your base and makes you vulnerable to passes and submissions.
    • Correct alternative: Actively lift your hips off the mat, creating a strong base with your core engaged.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice transitioning into the X-Guard position, focusing on leg placement and hip elevation without any resistance. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drilling (static): Your partner stands or kneels, allowing you to practice establishing grips and leg hooks. Focus on creating the 'X' and lifting your hips. (25 reps per side)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to practice the sweep motion. Focus on the timing of hip lift and push/pull. (25 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner drilling (dynamic): Your partner tries to resist your sweep attempts by posturing up or shifting weight. Focus on maintaining your structure and adjusting your grips. (25 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Situational drilling: Start from a specific guard retention scenario and transition into X-Guard, attempting a sweep. Partner provides moderate resistance. (15 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Integrate X-Guard into your rolling. Aim to get to X-Guard and attempt a sweep or transition to a submission. (5 rounds, 90-100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up from your closed guard, creating an opening to transition to open guard.
    • When you have successfully defended a guard pass attempt and are in a transitional open guard position.
    • When your opponent establishes a knee-slide pass, allowing you to trap a leg and enter X-Guard.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: The opponent drives their weight directly down onto your hips, attempting to crush your legs. To counter, maintain a strong frame with your feet on their hips and use your arms to push their head away while bridging your hips to create space.
    • Toreando Pass: The opponent tries to circle around your legs. To counter, use your foot on their hip to push them away and re-establish your guard, or transition your leg hooks to prevent them from circling.
    • Knee-on-Belly Transition: The opponent attempts to transition to knee-on-belly. To counter, use your outside leg hook to pull their leg across your body and shrimp your hips away to create space and regain guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to enter X-Guard?

    Your knee is likely hurting because you are allowing your knee to be the primary point of contact and pressure, rather than using your shin and foot hooks. When entering, ensure your hooked leg's knee stays slightly bent and protected by your opponent's thigh. The force should be distributed through your hamstring and hip flexor, not directly on the knee joint itself.

    Q: How can I use X-Guard effectively against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paraMount. Focus on controlling their posture with your collar and sleeve grips, pulling them forward to break their base before initiating the sweep. Use your hips to lift and create an angle, rather than trying to overpower them. Your leg hooks should be tight, preventing them from easily stabilizing.

    Q: Is X-Guard a good starting point for white belts, or should I learn other guards first?

    X-Guard can be introduced to white belts, but it requires a strong emphasis on fundamental biomechanics and safety. Prioritizing core stability, hip elevation, and precise limb placement is crucial to avoid injury. Understanding basic open guard principles and Hip Escapes first can build a better foundation for more complex guards like X-Guard.

The X-Guard is an open guard position used to attack the legs and sweep opponents, initiated from a flat, supine position with one leg between the opponent's legs.

White belts often struggle because they lack the understanding of leverage and frame control, leading to them being easily passed or injured.

The core principle is using your hips to create off-balancing angles and your legs to control the opponent's base, allowing for powerful sweeps and submissions.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a supine position, secure a Double Sleeve Grip on the opponent's gi sleeves, palms facing up, fingers spread.
    2. Simultaneously, place your right foot (as the attacker) on the opponent's left hip bone, sole of your foot perpendicular to their hip.
    3. Bring your left foot to hook behind the opponent's right knee, creating a "figure four" with your legs around their lower body.
    4. Arch your back, lifting your hips off the mat, creating a strong upward pressure against the opponent's hip.
    5. Rotate your hips 45 degrees to your left, pulling your opponent's right arm across their body with your grips.
    6. Extend your right leg forcefully, pushing off their hip to create distance and initiate the sweep.
    7. As the opponent falls, transition your left leg from behind their knee to secure a hook for a sweep or transition to a submission.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly placing your foot on the opponent's knee instead of behind it: This can lead to hyperextension of your own knee joint or ankle sprains due to improper leverage.
    • Trying to "pull" the opponent over with your arms alone: This strains your shoulders and elbows, risking rotator cuff tears or bicep tendonitis, instead of driving your hips up and out.
    • Allowing your hips to remain flat on the mat: This removes your base and leverage, making you susceptible to a stack pass and risking lower back strain from the opponent's weight.
    • Gripping too tightly on the opponent's gi without hip engagement: This leads to hand and wrist fatigue, increasing the risk of wrist sprains and failing to generate the necessary off-balancing force.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice leg placement and hip elevation with no resistance (10 reps each side).
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Opponent stands, you practice entering X-Guard and hip lift, opponent maintains balance (10 reps each side, 0% resistance).
    3. Partner drill (light pressure): Opponent applies minimal weight, you practice the sweep initiation, focusing on hip drive (10 reps each side, 25% resistance).
    4. Controlled drill: Opponent offers mild resistance to the sweep, you focus on maintaining control and completing the sweep (10 reps each side, 50% resistance).
    5. Flow drill: Practice entering X-Guard and executing sweeps with a partner who understands the technique and provides varied resistance (10 reps each side, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Integrate X-Guard into sparring, focusing on timing and proper execution against an uncooperative opponent (5 minutes, 90-100% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured up and has one leg forward, creating an opening.
    • When the opponent attempts a knee-slice pass or pressure pass, you can invert into X-Guard.
    • When you are in a closed guard and want to transition to a more dynamic attacking position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: The opponent drives their weight directly down on your hips, attempting to crush your base. Counter by driving your hips up and away, and potentially transitioning to a single-leg X-Guard.
    • Hip Switch: The opponent attempts to hip-switch to face away from your attacking leg. Counter by maintaining your grip on the sleeve and using your free leg to block their hip movement.
    • Base Destruction: The opponent spreads their legs wide and lowers their base to prevent your sweep. Counter by adjusting your leg hooks to target their knee and ankle more aggressively.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to enter X-Guard?

    Your wrist pain likely stems from gripping the opponent's gi sleeves too tightly and pulling directly with your arms. Instead, focus on using your forearm and bicep to create tension, and prioritize driving your hips up to create the off-balancing force. The grips should be firm but not locked with your fingers alone; engage your entire arm chain.

    Q: How do I use X-Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paraMount. Ensure your hips are significantly elevated off the mat, creating a strong base. Use your feet on their hip and knee to create angles and push them away, rather than trying to pull them directly. Focus on sweeping them by driving them sideways, not straight up, to utilize their weight against them.

    Q: When should I transition from X-Guard to a submission instead of sweeping?

    You should consider transitioning to a submission when the opponent over-commits to defending your sweep, leaving a limb exposed. For example, if they plant their weight heavily on their leg to resist a sweep, you might have an opportunity to isolate that leg for a heel hook. Or, if they posture up aggressively to avoid being swept, you can look for a Triangle Choke setup.

    More Questions

    What's the main difference between Reverse De La Riva and regular De La Riva sweeps?

    In Reverse De La Riva, you're attacking from the opposite side of your opponent's leg compared to standard De La Riva. This means your hooking leg is typically on the outside, and you're looking to off-balance them in a different direction.

    How do I prevent my opponent from passing when I initiate a Reverse De La Riva sweep?

    Maintaining tight control of your opponent's leg and hip is crucial. Use your free arm to control their upper body or collar, and be ready to adjust your base to prevent them from collapsing your guard.

    What are common mistakes beginners make with Reverse De La Riva?

    A common mistake is not committing to the sweep, leading to a weak attack and an easy pass for the opponent. Another is overextending and losing balance, so focus on keeping your hips active and your base solid.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: When I try to do a Reverse De La Riva sweep, my opponent just stands up easily, what am I doing wrong with my body positioning?

    Ensure your hips are actively driving forward into your opponent's base, creating a fulcrum. Simultaneously, use your free leg's foot to hook their shin and pull it towards your body, preventing them from straightening their leg and establishing an upright base.

    Q: How can I prevent my opponent from passing my guard when I'm setting up a Reverse De La Riva sweep, especially if they are much heavier than me?

    Maintain a tight grip on your opponent's ankle and hip, using your shoulder to pressure their chest and prevent them from creating space. Your hooking leg should be actively pushing their knee outwards to disrupt their balance and maintain your guard integrity.

    Q: My Reverse De La Riva sweep attempt feels awkward and I can't generate enough power to sweep them, what should I be focusing on with my leg and arm mechanics?

    Focus on simultaneously extending your hooking leg to elevate their leg while driving your hips forward and upward with a scooping motion. Your free arm should be extended to control their hip or shoulder, providing leverage and preventing them from posting their hand to defend.

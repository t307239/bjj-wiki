Loop Choke: The Essential BJJ Technique for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Loop Choke is typically initiated from the Closed Guard, aiming to secure a submission by constricting the carotid arteries. It's a powerful choke that can catch opponents off guard.

White belts often struggle by over-relying on raw strength or incorrect body positioning, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury. Focus on the mechanics, not brute force.

The key insight is creating a tight "loop" around the neck and shoulder with your arm and the opponent's lapel, using your body to generate leverage and pressure.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From closed guard, establish a cross-collar grip with your right hand on the opponent's left lapel, thumb inside the fabric. Your left hand grips the same lapel near the opponent's shoulder.
    2. Begin to open your guard and shrimp your hips to the opponent's right side, creating an angle. Your right hip should be slightly higher than your left.
    3. With your right arm, reach across the opponent's neck and under their chin, establishing a "loop" grip on their gi collar on the opposite side (their right lapel).
    4. Simultaneously, pull the opponent's head and upper body towards you with your left hand gripping their left lapel. This motion helps to secure the lapel in the loop.
    5. Begin to close your guard again, ensuring your right forearm is snug against the opponent's carotid artery and your grip on their right lapel is firm.
    6. Drive your hips forward and slightly upward, creating upward pressure on the opponent's shoulder and downward pressure on their head, tightening the choke.
    7. Maintain a strong connection with your legs around the opponent's waist, preventing them from posturing up or escaping the guard. Keep your back flat on the mat.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Grabbing the opponent's gi collar too high or too loosely: This can lead to the lapel slipping, causing a failed choke and potential strain on your shoulder joint as you overreach.
    • Forcing the head down with excessive neck flexion: This can injure your own cervical spine or the opponent's neck; instead, use hip pressure to guide the head down.
    • Squeezing with arm strength alone without hip drive: This is inefficient and risks elbow hyperextension if the opponent resists; the primary force comes from hip elevation and forward movement.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling of grip acquisition and arm positioning without a partner (50 reps).
    2. Partner drilling of grip acquisition and body angling with zero resistance (20 reps each side).
    3. Partner drilling of full Loop Choke mechanics with light resistance, focusing on hip movement (30 reps).
    4. Partner drilling with 50% resistance, focusing on maintaining the loop and hip pressure.
    5. Partner drilling with 75% resistance, simulating a live attempt with escape opportunities.
    6. Live rolling (rolling) with a focus on attempting the Loop Choke when the opportunity arises (3 x 5-minute rounds).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured up slightly in your closed guard, creating a pocket for your arm.
    • When the opponent is reaching in to break your guard, exposing their neck.
    • When you have successfully isolated an arm and are looking for a secondary submission threat.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Posture Up and Grip Release: The opponent can immediately posture up, widening their base and creating space to break your grip on the lapel or release the loop.
    • Shoulder Walk Escape: The opponent can "shoulder walk" their hips away, creating space and allowing them to untangle their neck from the loop.
    • Stack Pass Defense: The opponent can attempt to "stack" you by driving their weight forward, collapsing your guard and relieving pressure.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my right wrist hurt when I try the Loop Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often stems from gripping the lapel too tightly with your fingers extended, creating excessive torque. Ensure your grip is firm but not rigid, and try to keep your wrist in a neutral position. The pain might also indicate you're trying to "pull" the choke with your wrist instead of using your hips and core to drive the pressure.

    Q: How can I effectively set up the Loop Choke against a much larger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paraMount. Focus on creating a significant hip angle by shrimping aggressively to their side. Use your legs to control their posture and create a tight seal on your guard. The loop itself should be made with the opponent's own gi fabric, not by trying to force your arm around their neck through sheer strength.

    Q: When is the best moment to transition to a Loop Choke from closed guard if my opponent is defending well?

    The ideal moment is when the opponent attempts to break your guard by reaching inside or posturing up. This creates an opening. If they are maintaining a tight posture, look for opportunities when they drive forward or lean to one side, giving you the necessary angle to initiate the loop and secure the lapel.

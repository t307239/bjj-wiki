Loop Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Loop Choke is a powerful strangulation technique typically initiated from Closed Guard, aiming to secure a submission by constricting the carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle because they rely on brute strength rather than precise leverage and body positioning, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key mechanical insight is creating a stable base and using your hips to drive the choking arm across the opponent's neck, generating a strong, consistent pressure.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From closed guard, establish a standard cross-collar grip with your right hand deep on the opponent's left lapel, thumb inside the collar.
    2. Your left hand secures the opponent's right sleeve, gripping the fabric near the wrist.
    3. Shimmy your hips to your right, creating a slight angle and freeing your right arm.
    4. Thread your right arm *under* the opponent's chin and across their neck, ensuring your bicep is pressed against their carotid artery.
    5. Your right hand grips the far side of your own gi jacket, just below your left shoulder, creating a "loop" with your arm.
    6. Drive your hips forward and upward, simultaneously pulling your right elbow back and slightly down, creating a fulcrum point at your own shoulder.
    7. Tighten the choke by drawing your right elbow towards your left shoulder, compressing the carotid artery with your forearm and bicep.
    8. Maintain hip pressure and a stable base throughout the submission to prevent the opponent from posturing up.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying the choke by simply "pulling" your own collar with excessive upper body tension can hyperextend the cervical spine, risking a neck injury. The correct movement involves driving your hips forward to create leverage.
    • Gripping the opponent's head instead of their collar allows them to escape and can strain your own wrist and elbow joints. Always target the carotid artery with your forearm and bicep.
    • Trying to force the choke by arching your back without hip drive leads to inefficient pressure and can cause lower back strain. Focus on generating power from your hips and core.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice the grip setup and arm-thread motion on your own body or a dummy (5 reps per side).
    2. Solo drill: Practice the hip shimmy and arm positioning without a partner, focusing on hip angle (10 reps).
    3. Partner drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner in closed guard, practice the full motion of threading the arm and establishing the grip. Partner offers no resistance (10 reps).
    4. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner maintains a loose guard. Focus on achieving the correct grip and arm placement, allowing them to resist slightly by framing with their arms (15 reps).
    5. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner attempts to defend by posturing slightly. Practice driving your hips and maintaining the choke grip. Partner actively tries to prevent the choke (20 reps).
    6. Live rolling (75% resistance): Attempt the Loop Choke from closed guard in a rolling scenario. Focus on timing and proper mechanics, not necessarily finishing (3 attempts per round).

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is postured up slightly within your closed guard, creating space to thread your arm.
    • When the opponent attempts to stack you from closed guard, their head is lower, making it easier to reach their neck.
    • After a failed sweep or guard pass attempt by the opponent, when they are momentarily out of position.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Posture Up and Frame: If the opponent attempts to thread their arm, immediately posture up, widen your base, and frame with your forearms against their choking arm and shoulder, creating space.
    • Grip Release and Hip Escape: If the choke is being applied, focus on breaking the collar grip by pulling their hand away and simultaneously escaping your hips to the side to relieve pressure.
    • Head Control and Sweep: If the opponent's arm is around your neck, establish strong head control with your hands and use your free arm to create an escape route, often leading to a sweep or transition.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Loop Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs if you are gripping your own gi too tightly with your hand, or if you are trying to pull your own collar across with your wrist instead of using your forearm and bicep. Ensure your right hand grips your gi jacket firmly but without excessive wrist flexion, and focus on driving your hips to create the choke pressure, not just pulling your arm.

    Q: How can I effectively apply the Loop Choke against a much bigger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paraMount. Focus on creating a significant hip angle by shimming your hips to the side, which makes their head lower and more accessible. Drive your hips forward and upward forcefully to create the choking pressure, rather than relying on arm strength. A deep cross-collar grip is essential for control.

    Q: When is the best time to go for the Loop Choke, and what if my opponent just grabs my arm?

    The Loop Choke is most effective when the opponent postures slightly or attempts to stack you, as this creates the necessary space to thread your arm. If they grab your arm, do not panic. Maintain your collar grip, use your hips to create space to continue the threading motion, or, if the grip is too strong, transition to a sweep or a different submission like an Armbar by breaking their posture.

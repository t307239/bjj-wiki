The Loop Choke is a powerful strangulation from Closed Guard, targeting the carotid arteries. It requires precise grip placement and body mechanics to be effective.

White belts often struggle with the hip movement and grip integrity, leading to ineffective attempts or even self-choking. Understand the mechanics, not just the motion.

The key is to create a fulcrum with your forearm and use your hips to drive the choking arm across the neck, tightening the choke with your opposite arm.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From closed guard, establish a cross-collar grip with your right hand, thumb inside the lapel, fingers gripping the fabric deep near the opponent's shoulder.
    2. Place your left hand on the opponent's right hip, palm flat, fingers pointing towards their knee.
    3. Shift your hips to your left by lowering your left hip to the mat, creating a 45-degree angle relative to your opponent's torso.
    4. While maintaining the cross-collar grip, thread your right forearm *under* the opponent's chin, ensuring the bicep is flush against their neck.
    5. Immediately secure your right bicep with your left hand, forming a "loop" grip around the opponent's neck and your own arm.
    6. Drive your left hip forward and slightly up, while simultaneously arching your back and extending your hips towards your opponent.
    7. Your right elbow should point slightly upwards, and your left hand acts as a brace, preventing them from posturing up. Apply pressure by squeezing your arms and driving your hips.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect grip placement: Grabbing too high on the collar or not deep enough allows the opponent to easily escape or prevents the choke from forming.
    • Risk: Damage to the shoulder capsule or elbow joint from awkward pulling or joint flexion.
    • Correct: Secure the lapel deep, near the shoulder, with thumb inside for maximum control.
    • Forcing the choke with arm strength alone: This is inefficient and can lead to self-injury.
    • Risk: Ulnar nerve damage or bicep strain from excessive, uncoordinated pulling.
    • Correct: Utilize hip drive and body mechanics to create the strangulation force.
    • Not establishing a proper angle: Staying square to the opponent prevents the choking arm from moving across the neck.
    • Risk: Knee or hip hyperextension if you forcefully try to break posture without the correct angle.
    • Correct: Shift hips to a 45-degree angle to create space for the arm to traverse.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo grip and frame practice: Perform the grip setup and forearm loop without a partner. 20 reps.
    2. Partnered grip and angle drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice establishing grips and hip angle. 10 reps per side.
    3. Partnered forearm loop drill (0% resistance): Practice threading the forearm and securing the loop grip, focusing on hip movement. 10 reps per side.
    4. Full technique execution with compliant partner (25% resistance): Execute the entire Loop Choke sequence, allowing the partner to resist slightly on the initial setup. 5 successful attempts per side.
    5. Controlled submission drill (50% resistance): Apply the choke with moderate pressure, allowing the partner to tap early. Focus on feeling the choke. 10 successful attempts per side.
    6. Live rolling application (75-90% resistance): Attempt the Loop Choke during rolling, focusing on timing and integration into your guard game. Aim for 3 successful attempts.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured up and their head is within reach from closed guard.
    • When the opponent tries to stack you from closed guard, creating an opportunity to trap an arm.
    • When you have established a strong cross-collar grip and have control of their posture.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape and Frame: If the opponent attempts to thread their arm, immediately hip escape to create space and frame with your opposite arm against their choking arm's bicep.
    • Grip Break and Posture Up: The opponent can break your collar grip by reaching across their own body and pulling your hand off. They can then posture up to relieve pressure.
    • Stalling/Defensive Posture: If the opponent defends by tucking their chin and keeping their posture upright, the choke will not be effective. Focus on breaking their posture before attempting.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Loop Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you're trying to force the choke with your hand and wrist strength, rather than using your forearm and hip drive. Ensure your forearm is deep against their neck and your bicep is flush. The pressure should come from your body weight and hip movement, not a wrist bend or pull.

    Q: How can I effectively apply the Loop Choke against a much bigger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, your hip movement and angle become even more critical. Focus on getting that 45-degree angle by driving your hip to the mat. Use your legs to help create space if needed to thread your forearm, and then use your entire body weight to drive the choke, not just arm strength.

    Q: I keep accidentally choking myself with the Loop Choke, what am I doing wrong?

    Self-choking happens when you don't properly thread your forearm under their chin and across your own neck. Your choking arm (e.g., right arm) needs to go *under* their chin and then be secured by your other hand (e.g., left hand on right bicep). Ensure your choking arm's bicep is tight against their neck, not your own.

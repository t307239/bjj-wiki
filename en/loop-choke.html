Loop Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

The Loop Choke is typically applied from Closed Guard, aiming to secure a submission by constricting the carotid arteries. White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of proper leverage and framing. The key to the Loop Choke is creating a tight "loop" with the gi lapel and using your body mechanics to drive it across the opponent's neck, combined with precise hip movement.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From closed guard, establish a standard cross-collar grip with your right hand on the opponent's left lapel, thumb inside the collar.
    2. With your left hand, reach across the opponent's body and grip their right lapel, thumb outside the collar, creating a "loop" around their neck.
    3. Arch your back and bring your hips slightly up, while simultaneously pulling the right lapel towards your chest.
    4. Drive your right elbow forward and slightly down, creating tension on the lapel across the opponent's neck.
    5. As you drive your right elbow, simultaneously shift your hips to your left, creating a rotational force.
    6. Tighten the choke by pulling the right lapel further across and driving your hips into the opponent's chest.
    7. Maintain a strong closed guard throughout, preventing the opponent from posturing up or relieving pressure.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly reaching for the far lapel: This can lead to the opponent easily defending by pulling their arm out, potentially causing a shoulder strain or dislocation if the grip is forced.
    • The correct action is to maintain control of your own side before reaching, ensuring a secure grip.
    • Overextending the neck: Trying to force the choke by craning your neck forward strains the cervical spine and makes the choke less effective.
    • Maintain a neutral neck position and focus on hip and arm mechanics for pressure.
    • Letting the opponent's head escape the loop: If the opponent's head is not fully contained within the lapel loop, the choke will not be effective and can lead to a loss of position.
    • Ensure the lapel is snug around the neck before applying pressure.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the lapel grip and the motion of driving the elbow and shifting hips without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice establishing the grips and executing the hip shift and elbow drive. (20 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to posture or movement, allowing you to feel the mechanics. (20 reps per side)
    4. Controlled execution: Practice the Loop Choke from closed guard with a partner who understands the technique and offers controlled defense. (10 reps per side)
    5. Scenario drilling: Practice setting up the Loop Choke after specific guard breaks or transitions. (10 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the Loop Choke during rolling with the intention of executing the technique, understanding that successful completion may vary. (5 attempts per roll)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured up slightly, creating space to establish lapel grips.
    • When the opponent is pressing their weight into your closed guard, making it difficult for them to defend easily.
    • Immediately after a failed submission attempt by the opponent, when they might be out of position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Squeezing the lapel grip: The opponent can try to squeeze the lapel grip from the outside, making it harder for you to slide it across the neck.
    • To counter, maintain a tight grip on your own lapel and ensure your thumb is tucked inside the collar for stability.
    • Defending the neck by tucking the chin: The opponent can tuck their chin to their chest to create space and prevent the lapel from tightening.
    • To counter, use your free hand to pull their head slightly to the side, exposing the carotid artery more effectively.
    • Escaping the guard: The opponent can try to break your closed guard by posturing up or stacking you.
    • To counter, maintain a strong closed guard and use your legs to control their posture and hip movement.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Loop Choke?

    Wrist pain often occurs if you are gripping the lapel too tightly with your fingers extended, or if you are trying to force the choke with brute wrist strength. Ensure your thumb is tucked inside the collar for a stable grip and focus on driving your elbow and shifting your hips for power, rather than just flexing your wrist.

    Q: How can I effectively use the Loop Choke against a much bigger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paraMount. Focus on creating a tight lapel loop by controlling their collar with your right hand (thumb inside). Then, reach for their opposite lapel with your left hand, ensuring your arm is snug. Drive your right elbow forward and simultaneously shrimp your hips to your left to create the necessary torque and leverage.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to a Loop Choke from closed guard?

    The optimal time is when the opponent is postured up slightly, or when they are pressing their weight into your guard, creating a "wedge" effect. This posture provides the necessary space to establish your grips and begin the rotational movement. Avoid attempting it when the opponent is completely flat on their back or has their posture broken too severely.

Mikey Musumeci

"Darth Rigatoni"
🌍 US 🥋 Black Belt ⚖️ Flyweight / Strawweight 🏫 New Wave Jiu-Jitsu
  • ADCC 57kg Champion 2022
  • IBJJF World Champion 5×
  • ONE Championship MMA debut winner
Mikey Musumeci, nicknamed 'Darth Rigatoni', is one of the most accomplished Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu athletes from American. Known for exceptional skill in triangle choke, omoplata, lasso guard, rubber guard, Mikey has earned a reputation as one of the sport's most technically refined competitors. Training out of New Wave Jiu-Jitsu, they have amassed an impressive collection of world titles and competition victories across gi and no-gi formats.

Biography

Mikey Musumeci began training Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu and quickly demonstrated natural talent for the sport. Born in 1996, they progressed through the belt ranks at an accelerated pace, earning their black belt and immediately competing at the highest levels. Their affiliation with New Wave Jiu-Jitsu provided world-class training partners and coaching that accelerated their development.



On the competition circuit, Mikey Musumeci accumulated title after title: ADCC 57kg Champion 2022; IBJJF World Champion 5×; ONE Championship MMA debut winner. Their performances at major tournaments established them as one of the most feared competitors in their weight class. Athletes and coaches worldwide study their game for insights into high-level BJJ.



Beyond competition, Mikey Musumeci has contributed to the BJJ community through teaching, instructionals, and demonstrating that technical mastery can prevail over physical advantages. Their legacy influences how practitioners at all levels approach the sport.



Today, Mikey Musumeci continues to train, teach, and compete, passing on the lessons of their championship career to the next generation of BJJ athletes.

Fighting Style

Mikey Musumeci's game is defined by excellence in triangle choke, omoplata, Lasso Guard, rubber guard. Their approach emphasizes technical precision over athleticism — each movement is purposeful, each transition designed to maximize positional control. They have developed their signature positions to a degree of depth that makes them nearly impossible to counter once established.

What makes Mikey's style particularly effective is the seamless connection between offense and defense. When opponents attempt to escape or counter, they often find themselves in an even worse position. This quality — turning defense into offense — is the hallmark of elite BJJ.

Signature Technique

Mikey Musumeci's most iconic technique is the Triangle Choke. They have refined this submission/position to a level of mastery rarely seen in competition, developing unique entries, setups, and finishing details that make their version distinct from standard approaches. Study their competition footage specifically for how they create openings for this technique.

In-Depth: Mikey Musumeci

Biomechanics & Physics

Mikey Musumeci's technical mastery, particularly his guard retention and submission attacks, is deeply rooted in a sophisticated understanding of biomechanics and physics. His lasso guard, for instance, leverages the principles of rotational inertia and leverage. By controlling his opponent's sleeve with his leg and wrapping the opposite arm around their neck, he creates a fulcrum. The subtle hip movements and torque he applies generate significant rotational force, disrupting the opponent's base and posture. This rotational dynamic makes it difficult for them to establish a solid base, opening opportunities for sweeps and submissions. Similarly, in his triangle chokes, Musumeci emphasizes precise angles. He manipulates the opponent's center of gravity, ensuring their weight is aligned over the apex of the triangle formed by his legs. This creates a powerful crushing force by restricting blood flow through the carotid arteries and an extreme stretch on the shoulder joint. The extension of his hips, coupled with the flexion of his hamstrings and glutes, maximizes the pressure, making the submission incredibly difficult to escape once locked in correctly. His movements are efficient, minimizing wasted energy and maximizing the application of force vectors for optimal control and submission.

Common Mistakes (Specific to This Technique)

  • Prematurely Tightening the Triangle: Instead of establishing the proper hip angle and control of the opponent's head, practitioners often try to lock the triangle legs immediately, allowing the opponent to post their shoulder or uncross the legs.
  • Lack of Hip Extension in the Triangle: Failing to drive the hips forward to create the tightening pressure, leading to a loose triangle that is easily defended or escaped.
  • Poor Lasso Grip Control: In the lasso guard, allowing the opponent to strip the sleeve grip or pass the leg over the head without proper triangulation, resulting in loss of control and potential pass.
  • Giving Up Posture in Lasso Guard: Allowing the opponent to establish strong upper body control and posture within the lasso entanglement, negating the rotational advantage.

Variations & Counters

Musumeci's game is a rich tapestry of interconnected techniques, where one position seamlessly flows into another. His lasso guard, for instance, isn't just a static guard but a dynamic platform. From the lasso, he can initiate a variety of sweeps, such as the 'tripod sweep' by using the lasso hook to control the opponent's arm and leg, then sweeping their base. More crucially, the lasso provides excellent control for transitioning into omoplatas and triangles. If an opponent attempts to posture up out of the lasso, Musumeci can use the leg control to fall back, secure the arm for the omoplata, or adjust his hips to secure a triangle. Conversely, when facing opponents attempting to pass his guard, particularly from a top half guard or knee slice position, his ability to quickly establish a lasso or an overhook and then transition to a triangle choke is a hallmark. He can also use the lasso to defend against leg entanglements, preventing his opponent from securing a stable position by keeping them off-balance and controlling their base.

Drilling Recommendations

To emulate Musumeci's technical proficiency, dedicate specific drilling sessions. For lasso guard, practice the entry and control phase for 5 sets of 30 seconds with a resisting partner attempting to pass. Focus on maintaining the sleeve grip and leg over the shoulder without allowing the opponent to pass. For triangle chokes, drill the setup and tightening sequence repeatedly. Start with a compliant partner: 5 sets of 5 repetitions for entry, hip extension, and tightening, ensuring the opponent cannot defend. Then, introduce light resistance, focusing on achieving a solid lock before the partner can escape. Incorporate drills where you deliberately isolate an arm and practice transitioning into either an omoplata or a triangle, performing 3 sets of 5 repetitions per side. The key is deliberate repetition focusing on clean mechanics and positional control, not just movement.

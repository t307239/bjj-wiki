The D'Arce Choke is a powerful chokehold applied from various positions, most commonly when the opponent is on their hands and knees or when you have established Side Control.

White belts often struggle by trying to muscle the choke, failing to understand the leverage points and the necessary body mechanics for a clean submission.

The key to the D'Arce Choke is creating a fulcrum with your forearm and using your body weight and hip rotation to constrict blood flow, not brute strength.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From side control, establish a cross-face with your left arm (if your left arm is under their neck). Your right arm snakes through the crook of their neck, aiming for their far shoulder.
    2. Secure a Gable grip with your right hand, interlacing your fingers around your own right wrist. Your left forearm should be pressing into their carotid artery on the right side.
    3. Hip Angle: Rotate your hips ninety degrees towards your opponent's head. Your chest should be pressing into their upper chest.
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight forward, driving your chest and shoulder into their neck and shoulder area.
    5. Elbow Position: Keep your right elbow tucked in, close to your body, preventing them from posturing up.
    6. Choking Action: Begin to sit up, bringing your hips back slightly while simultaneously driving your chest forward. This creates a powerful squeezing action.
    7. Head Position: Use your head to block their head from escaping, keeping it tight to their shoulder.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly trying to 'pull' the choke with your arms: This can lead to shoulder impingement or rotator cuff tears due to excessive external rotation and strain on the glenohumeral joint.
    • Allowing the opponent's head to 'pop' out by not maintaining head control: This can result in a hyperextended neck or cervical strain if you continue to apply pressure without proper alignment.
    • Over-extending your own back when trying to sit up: This can cause lumbar spine strain or disc compression by creating excessive lordosis without core engagement.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the grip and body positioning without a partner. Focus on the hip rotation and chest pressure. (50 reps each side).
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice entering the D'Arce and securing the grip. Focus on the setup and initial body mechanics. (20 reps each side).
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Your partner gently resists, allowing you to feel the pressure and make adjustments. Focus on hip movement and chest connection. (15 reps each side).
    4. Partner drilling (moderate resistance): Partner offers more resistance, allowing you to work on finishing the choke. Focus on the sit-up and squeezing mechanics. (10 reps each side).
    5. Live rolling (controlled): Apply the D'Arce when the opportunity arises, but focus on safe execution and recognizing when to disengage. (5 minutes, 50% resistance).
    6. Live rolling (full intensity): Attempt the D'Arce choke in a full-speed roll, prioritizing correct technique over speed. (10 minutes, 100% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent turtles up and you have side control or are behind them.
    • When passing to side control and the opponent tries to frame with their arm across their face.
    • When an opponent attempts a guillotine choke and you can secure an underhook and trap their arm.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Posture Up and Drive Forward: If the choke is being applied from the front, the defender can attempt to break the grip by driving their forehead into the attacker's chest and pushing upwards, while simultaneously tucking their chin.
    • Escape by Turning In: The defender can attempt to turn their body *into* the choke, towards the attacker's chest. This relieves pressure on the carotid artery and allows for a potential escape.
    • Grip Break with Underhook: If the attacker has a gable grip, the defender can try to secure an underhook on the attacking arm and use their free arm to pry the attacker's hands apart, often by targeting the thumbs.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try to do the D'Arce Choke?

    Neck pain during the D'Arce Choke often stems from not isolating the carotid artery correctly. Instead of driving your forearm into the side of their neck, you might be pressing into their trachea or the front of their neck, causing discomfort. Ensure your forearm is high on the neck, pressing the artery against the shoulder, and that your head is used to block their head, not to apply direct neck pressure.

    Q: How can I effectively D'Arce Choke a much larger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise mechanics are paraMount. Focus on getting your forearm deep into their neck and securing a tight gable grip. Instead of muscling, use your hip rotation to create the squeeze. Sit up by bringing your hips back slightly while driving your chest into their shoulder, using their size against them by constricting blood flow rather than trying to overpower their frame.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to a D'Arce Choke if my opponent is defending my guard pass?

    The D'Arce Choke is an excellent opportunistic submission during Guard Passes. If your opponent frames with an arm across their face to prevent a knee-slice pass, or if they turtle up as you attempt a pressure pass, these are prime moments. Quickly snake your arm through, secure the grip, and use your body weight to follow them as they try to escape, turning their defensive movement into your offensive opportunity.

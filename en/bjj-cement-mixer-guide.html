    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck feel strained or hurt when I try to finish the Cement Mixer submission in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu?

    Neck pain often arises from using your neck muscles to generate torque instead of your hips and core. To fix this, drive your hips forward into your opponent while keeping your head up and your spine neutral, allowing your body's rotation to apply the pressure.

    Q: How can I effectively apply the Cement Mixer submission against a much larger and stronger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on secure grip placement and leverage rather than brute strength. Ensure your shoulder is deeply wedged under their chin and use your hips to create a fulcrum, driving them over your shoulder to initiate the rotation.

    Q: What are the common mistakes white belts make when attempting the Cement Mixer and how do I avoid them?

    A common mistake is not getting the shoulder deep enough under the opponent's chin, leading to a weak grip and ineffective pressure. Ensure your shoulder is firmly planted in their neck pocket and that your elbow is higher than their head, creating a strong lever for the submission.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is the main goal of the Cement Mixer?

    The primary goal of the Cement Mixer is to break down your opponent's posture and create an angle for a submission, often leading to a choke or armbar. It's about controlling their head and upper body to disrupt their base and mobility.

    How do I prevent my opponent from escaping the Cement Mixer?

    To prevent escapes, maintain tight control of your opponent's head and arm. Keep your hips close and use your legs to control their hips and base, preventing them from posturing up or turning into you.

    What are common mistakes when applying the Cement Mixer?

    Common mistakes include not getting deep enough on the head and arm control, allowing the opponent to maintain posture, and not using your legs effectively to control their hips. This can lead to the opponent escaping or even reversing your position.

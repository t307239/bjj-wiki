    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my opponent's arm slip out when I try to secure the armbar from side control?

    Your opponent's arm is likely slipping because you are not properly isolating their elbow joint. Ensure your chest is pressing down on their bicep, preventing them from straightening their arm, and that your hips are low and tight to their shoulder, creating a strong base that limits their ability to shrimp away.

    Q: How can I prevent my opponent from stacking me when I attempt an armbar from side control?

    To prevent stacking, keep your hips low and your knees tucked towards your chest, creating a stable base that resists their upward pressure. Simultaneously, use your free arm to grip their hip or gi, anchoring yourself and preventing them from driving their weight into you.

    Q: What's the best way to finish an armbar from side control when my opponent is much larger than me?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on creating a strong fulcrum with your hips and legs by lifting them high off the mat. Secure your grip on their wrist tightly, and extend your hips upwards while keeping their elbow bent and trapped between your legs to generate the necessary leverage for the submission.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What's the most common mistake beginners make when trying an armbar from side control?

    The most common mistake is not controlling the opponent's hips and allowing them to shrimp away. Another frequent error is not securing a strong grip on the arm, giving them an easy escape.

    How do I prevent my opponent from bridging or rolling out when I go for the armbar?

    Maintain tight hip pressure and use your chest to keep their hips down. If they try to roll, adjust your body to follow their movement and maintain control of the arm.

    What if my opponent tucks their elbow really tight, making it hard to get the armbar?

    Try to use your forearm to pry their elbow open, or switch to a different submission like a kimura or americana. Sometimes, you might need to adjust your position to get a better angle on the arm.

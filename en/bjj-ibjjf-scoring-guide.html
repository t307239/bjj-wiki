    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: As a beginner in BJJ, how do I ensure I'm actually scoring points in a match according to the IBJJF rules and not just holding my opponent?

    To score points, you need to achieve a dominant position and maintain it for at least three seconds. For example, a 'Takedown' is scored when you bring your opponent to the mat and establish control, meaning your hips are lower than theirs and you have significant upper body pressure. A 'Sweep' is scored when you reverse position from the bottom to the top, again maintaining control for three seconds.

    Q: I'm struggling to get points when I'm on my back, what's the key to scoring a sweep in IBJJF competition?

    The key to a successful sweep is using your opponent's weight and momentum against them. From your guard, you need to create an angle by shifting your hips to the side, typically using your legs to hook their hips or legs and your arms to break their posture and control their upper body. Then, explosively drive your hips up and away while simultaneously pulling them forward to generate the rotation and end up on top in a controlled position.

    Q: When is the right time to transition to a dominant position to score points in IBJJF, and how do I avoid losing the points if my opponent tries to escape immediately?

    You score points by establishing and *maintaining* control for three seconds. Immediately after achieving a position like side control or mount, you must solidify your base by pinning their hips and shoulders with your weight, ensuring your hips are heavy and connected to their body. Simultaneously, use your grips to prevent them from creating space or re-guarding, effectively smothering their escape attempts.

    More Questions

    What are the most common ways to score points in BJJ?

    The most frequent scoring actions are takedowns (2 points), sweeps (2 points), knee-on-belly (2 points), and passes (3 points). Submissions, if successful, end the match immediately and don't award points directly.

    How do I know if my sweep was successful for IBJJF points?

    For a sweep to be scored, you must establish a dominant position for at least three seconds, with you in top position and your opponent having been on their back or side. You need to maintain control and prevent them from returning to guard or regaining their base.

    What is the difference between a pass and a sweep in IBJJF rules?

    A sweep is when you are on the bottom and reverse the position to end up on top. A pass is when you are already on top and move from your opponent's guard to a side control or mount position, securing a dominant top control.

50/50 Guard: Your First Step into Open Guard Control

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Blue Belt Guide

The 50/50 Guard is an open guard position where both grapplers' legs are locked around one of the opponent's legs, creating a stalemate for sweeps and submissions.

White belts often struggle by exposing their own leg to sweeps or failing to create leverage for attacks due to poor hip positioning.

The key is to control the opponent's hip and maintain a strong base, preventing them from easily passing or escaping the leg entanglement.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a seated or knee-on-ground position, secure a "hook grip" on the opponent's ankle with your foot, ensuring your shin is across their Achilles tendon.
    2. Simultaneously, wrap your other leg around the opponent's thigh, trapping their knee joint between your thigh and calf.
    3. Your hips should be angled away from the opponent, creating a "shelf" with your shin to control their thigh's movement.
    4. Secure a "belt grip" on the opponent's pants just above the knee or a "double sleeve grip" on their arms to prevent them from posturing up.
    5. Drive your hips forward, pinning their trapped leg against your torso, creating pressure and limiting their mobility.
    6. Use your free leg to "push" off their hip or mat, creating space to adjust your angle and maintain the 50/50 entanglement.
    7. Apply pressure through your hips and torso to control their base, making it difficult for them to drive forward or stack you.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly "locking" your own ankles together: This can lead to knee hyperextension or ligament tears (ACL, MCL) if the opponent drives forward or spins out.
    • Correct: Maintain a "figure-four" grip with your legs around their leg, not your own ankles crossed.
    • Over-extending your hips outwards: This exposes your lower back and can cause lumbar strain or disc compression if the opponent stacks forcefully.
    • Correct: Keep your hips angled, but controlled, using your shin as a "shelf" to support their thigh.
    • Releasing your "belt grip" or "double sleeve grip" prematurely: This allows the opponent to posture up, creating a "stack pass" and potentially injuring your neck or shoulders if they drive down with force.
    • Correct: Maintain strong grips until you are ready to transition to a sweep or submission, or have established a dominant control.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice establishing the 50/50 entanglement on a dummy or willing partner, focusing on grip placement and hip angle (20 reps).
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Partner stands, you establish 50/50 from guard, focusing on hip and grip control (10 reps per side).
    3. Partner drill (static, 25% resistance): Partner attempts to stand or posture; you maintain the 50/50 position and hip control. Focus on maintaining the entanglement against light pressure (1 minute rounds, 5 rounds).
    4. Partner drill (light movement, 50% resistance): Partner attempts to pass or create space; you focus on maintaining the 50/50 and initiating a sweep attempt (e.g., scissor sweep) or transition (1 minute rounds, 5 rounds).
    5. Sparring (75% resistance): Focus on entering the 50/50 guard from open guard or scrambles, and attempting to maintain control and initiate attacks.
    6. Live rolling (100% resistance): Integrate 50/50 guard into your rolling, focusing on maintaining the position, creating opportunities, and defending counters.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your open guard by stepping their legs inside your guard.
    • When you have successfully "clinch-gripped" their ankle or shin and can trap their leg.
    • As a defensive measure when you feel your guard is about to be passed and need to stabilize.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • "Stack Pass Defense": If the opponent attempts to stack you, drive your hips forward into their chest while simultaneously pulling their trapped leg tighter to your body, creating a "frame" with your free leg.
    • "Leg Release": If the opponent manages to free their leg, immediately transition to a "closed guard" or "half guard" by bringing your knees to your chest and securing a double-leg grip.
    • "Hip Escape to Guard Recovery": If they begin to spin out or pass, use your free leg to "push" off their hip and "hip escape" to create space and re-establish your guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get into 50/50 Guard?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are forcing your leg into an unnatural position or not properly trapping the opponent's knee. Ensure your shin is across their Achilles and their knee is firmly between your thigh and calf. Avoid locking your own ankles together; focus on controlling their leg with your entire leg structure.

    Q: How do I use 50/50 Guard against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is crucial. Focus on maintaining a tight "belt grip" on their pants above the knee to control their base and prevent them from driving forward. Use your hips to create a "shelf" and angle away, making it difficult for their weight to crush you. Prioritize controlling their hip angle over trying to lift them.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from 50/50 Guard to an attack?

    The best time to transition is when you feel you have established solid control of their leg and hip, and they are struggling to maintain their base or posture. Look for moments when they overcommit to a pass or when they momentarily lose balance. This is your opportunity to initiate a sweep like the "inside Heel Hook" or transition to a "straight ankle lock."

The Torreando Pass is an open guard pass designed to move around your opponent's legs, aiming for Side Control. It's initiated from a standing or kneeling position when your opponent has established a form of open guard.

White belts often struggle because they lack the coordinated hip movement and understand the concept of "passing" as simply pushing past, rather than circling.

The key is to use your hips and base to create an angle and force your opponent to commit their weight, allowing you to circle to their open side.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grips: Secure a "collar and sleeve" grip on the same side of the opponent's body you intend to pass. For example, left hand on opponent's right collar, right hand on opponent's right sleeve.
    2. Base: Establish a wide, stable base with knees bent, hips low. Your chest should be angled towards the opponent's hips, not their face.
    3. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your lead leg (the one on the same side as your sleeve grip), lifting your trail leg slightly.
    4. Hip Rotation: Rotate your hips 45 degrees away from the opponent, creating an angular distance.
    5. Leg Drive: Drive your lead leg forward and slightly across the opponent's body, aiming to place your knee on the mat as you circle.
    6. Head Position: Keep your head up and looking towards the opponent's hips, maintaining awareness.
    7. Circle to Side Control: Continue circling your hips and body, keeping your base low, until you are past their legs and can establish side control.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Knee Placement: Driving your knee directly into the opponent's hip socket with excessive force can cause a hip impingement injury, straining the labrum or cartilage.
    • Hyperextended Knee: When lifting your trail leg, if you allow your knee to lock out and strike the opponent's leg, you risk hyperextending your own knee, potentially damaging the ACL or meniscus.
    • Twisting Torso Without Hip Movement: Attempting to circle by only twisting your upper body without engaging your hips can lead to lumbar spine strain or disc injury.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Footwork Drill: Practice the circling footwork and hip rotation without a partner. Focus on maintaining a low base and 45-degree hip angle. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance).
    2. Partnered Grip and Step Drill: With a compliant partner, practice securing grips and taking the initial step and hip rotation, stopping before the knee drive. (10 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    3. Controlled Knee Drive: With a compliant partner, execute the knee drive and hip circle, focusing on controlling your body position. Partner remains passive. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    4. Partnered Pass with Passive Resistance: Execute the Torreando Pass with a partner offering light, non-resisting pressure. Focus on maintaining your base and circle. (5 reps per side, 75% resistance).
    5. Partnered Pass with Active Resistance: Execute the Torreando Pass against a partner who actively tries to keep their legs in place but doesn't actively fight the pass. (5 reps per side, 90% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling Application: Attempt the Torreando Pass during live rolling, prioritizing technique over outcome. (5 minutes, 100% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent has established a loose open guard and is not actively controlling your legs.
    • When your opponent attempts to create distance by pushing on your hips or knees.
    • When your opponent's hips are angled away from you, creating an opening on their side.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee Shield: Opponent extends their leg and places their knee directly into your hip or thigh, preventing your forward drive and creating a frame.
    • Inside Hook: Opponent uses their foot to hook your ankle or shin on the side you are circling towards, preventing you from advancing.
    • Hip Escape and Re-Guard: Opponent quickly hip escapes away, creating enough space to bring their legs back into a guard position.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do the Torreando Pass?

    Your knee might hurt if you are not properly angling your body and driving your knee towards the mat. Instead of a direct impact, think of it as a pivot point. Ensure your hip is rotating and your base is wide, allowing your knee to glide towards the mat on the outside of their leg, not directly into it. This protects the joint from hyperextension or direct trauma.

    Q: How can I effectively Torreando Pass against a much bigger opponent who has strong grips?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on creating an angle and using their weight against them. Instead of trying to push through their mass, use your grips to off-balance them slightly as you begin to circle. Your goal is to get to their side where their weight is less distributed. Prioritize getting your hips low and driving your knee *across* their body, not just around it, to disrupt their base.

    Q: When should I actually try to Torreando Pass, and not just keep trying to push their legs away?

    The Torreando Pass is most effective when your opponent's legs are not actively framing against your hips or knees. If they have their feet on your hips, you're likely in a situation better suited for a knee-slice or pressure pass. Look for opportunities when their guard is open, perhaps after they've attempted a sweep or when they are trying to create space. It's about exploiting an opening, not forcing it.

Torreando Pass: A Biomechanically Precise Guide for BJJ White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Blue Belt Guide

The Torreando Pass is initiated from a standing or kneeling position against an opponent's open guard, aiming to secure Side Control.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of weight distribution and frame creation, leading to stalling or ineffective attempts.

The key insight is using your opponent's leg as a lever against their own body to create space and move around them.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip opponent's right pant leg at the ankle with your left hand, maintaining a thumb-in grip. Your right hand grips opponent's right knee with an over-under grip, palm facing down.
    2. Position your hips low, slightly behind your opponent's right hip, creating a 45-degree angle between your torso and their legs.
    3. Step your left foot forward, placing it outside and slightly ahead of your opponent's right knee, ensuring your knee is not directly on the mat.
    4. Drive your right shoulder into your opponent's right thigh, applying downward pressure to flatten their leg.
    5. Simultaneously, pull the right pant leg towards your left hip with your left hand, creating a lever.
    6. Shift your weight onto your right foot, driving your hips forward and to your left, circling your body counter-clockwise.
    7. As your opponent's right leg is pulled past their body, step your right foot to your left, bringing your hips to the mat to secure side control.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to step over the opponent's legs without proper hip angle: This can lead to knee hyperextension or ligament tears (ACL/MCL) in your own knee as you force it into an unnatural position.
    • Gripping too high on the pants (e.g., above the knee) and pulling directly: This can cause your opponent to hip escape, and if you maintain a rigid posture, you risk straining your lower back from the twisting motion.
    • Allowing your base to become too narrow when stepping: This compromises your stability, making you susceptible to sweeps and risking ankle sprains as your foot can easily roll.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip angles, leg placement, and torso rotation motion without a partner. 20 reps each side.
    2. Partner drilling (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, execute the pass, focusing on grip integrity and hip movement. 10 reps each side.
    3. Partner drilling (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the leg lever and hip movement. 10 reps each side.
    4. Partner drilling (50% resistance): Partner attempts to frame or resist slightly, forcing you to adjust your pressure and hip angle. 8 reps each side.
    5. Partner drilling (75% resistance): Partner actively tries to maintain their guard or frame, requiring more precise timing and weight transfer. 6 reps each side.
    6. Live rolling (90% intensity): Apply the pass in a controlled sparring environment, focusing on applying the mechanics learned. 3 attempts per round.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent has their legs extended in an open guard, creating a clear target.
    • Opponent attempts a leg entanglement (e.g., spider guard) but leaves a leg exposed.
    • Opponent is posturing up, creating an opportunity to break their base and pass.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Pummeling: As the passer attempts to grip your pant leg, quickly pummel your leg to the inside, creating a strong frame with your shin against their hip.
    • Hip Escape and Re-guard: If the passer gains a strong grip and begins to circle, immediately hip escape to create space and attempt to re-establish guard.
    • Knee Shield and Base: If the passer drives their shoulder into your thigh, bring your opposite knee up to create a knee shield and use your free leg to establish a strong base, preventing them from circling.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my elbow hurt when I try to Torreando Pass?

    Elbow pain typically occurs when you're gripping too tightly or in an awkward angle, often when your opponent is actively resisting. Ensure your grip on the pant leg is firm but not tense, and that your wrist remains neutral. The primary force should come from your hips and torso rotation, not from muscling the leg with your arm.

    Q: How can I effectively Torreando Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on using their size against them. Employ a lower base and ensure your hips are well behind theirs to avoid being crushed. Use your shoulder to drive into their thigh with leverage, and prioritize circling your hips around their leg rather than trying to pull their leg directly towards you. A well-timed hip circle is more effective than raw strength.

    Q: When is the best time to use the Torreando Pass instead of a knee-slice?

    The Torreando Pass is ideal when your opponent's legs are extended or they are actively using their feet to control distance, creating an open guard structure. If their knees are tucked close to their chest, or they are actively trying to shut down passing lanes with their thighs, a knee-slice pass might be more appropriate as it aims to break through that tighter guard structure.

Torreando Pass: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

The Torreando Pass is initiated from a standing or kneeling position against an opponent's Closed Guard, aiming to transition to side control or mount.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of controlled movement, leading to predictable attacks and compromised stability.

The core principle is maintaining a strong, upright posture while using your hips and legs to create a stable base as you circle around the opponent's legs.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip: Secure a strong collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip on the opponent's left sleeve with your left hand. Maintain a neutral hip angle, facing your opponent.
    2. Posture: Drive your chest towards your opponent's chest, creating a strong upright posture. Your base should be shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent.
    3. Break Guard (if closed): With your collar grip, lift the opponent's head slightly while using your left hand to push down on their left knee, breaking the closed guard connection.
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight slightly onto your left leg, creating a subtle pressure that encourages the opponent to react.
    5. Circular Movement: Begin to circle your hips to your right, moving your body in a semi-circular path around the opponent's left leg.
    6. Leg Placement: As you circle, step your right foot out to the side, away from the opponent's legs, maintaining a wide base.
    7. Control and Transition: Keep your chest connected to the opponent's chest. As you complete the circle, drive your right knee down to the mat and immediately transition to control their hips, establishing side control or mount.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Hip Angle: Dropping your hips too low or angling your hips directly towards the opponent's feet exposes your knees and ankles to potential attacks like ankle locks or knee bars. The correct movement involves maintaining an upright posture and a perpendicular hip angle to the opponent's legs.
    • Over-Reliance on Arm Strength: Pulling excessively with your arms to break the guard or circle can lead to shoulder strain and loss of balance. Focus on using your entire body's weight and hip rotation. The correct action is to use your torso and hips to drive the movement, with arm grips serving to control posture and direction.
    • Foot Placement During Circle: Stepping directly forward or backward instead of in a circular arc around the opponent's legs can lead to them easily re-guarding or sweeping. Maintain a consistent distance and a sweeping motion with your feet. The correct footwork involves a controlled pivot and step that creates space for your body to pass.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Posture and Grip Practice: Stand in front of a mat, practicing your upright posture and securing collar and sleeve grips. Perform 50 repetitions.
    2. Solo Guard Break and Hip Circle: Practice breaking an imaginary closed guard and then performing the hip circle motion, maintaining posture. 50 repetitions per side.
    3. Partnered Guard Break and Hip Circle (No Resistance): With a willing partner, practice breaking their closed guard and executing the hip circle without them resisting. 25 repetitions per side.
    4. Partnered Torreando Pass (Light Resistance): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to practice the full movement and transition to side control. 25 repetitions per side.
    5. Partnered Torreando Pass (Medium Resistance): Your partner actively tries to defend and re-guard, requiring you to adjust your pressure and speed. 25 repetitions per side.
    6. Live Rolling (Sparring): Attempt the Torreando Pass during live sparring sessions, focusing on timing and execution. Aim for 5 successful attempts per round.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent's closed guard is tight, and you are struggling to create space for other passes.
    • Opponent is posturing up, creating a slight gap between their knees and your hips.
    • You have successfully broken the opponent's guard and are in a stable, upright position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Sweep/Re-Guard: As you circle, the opponent can attempt to sweep your base leg or bring their legs back to re-establish guard. To counter, maintain a wide base and keep your chest connected, driving your weight forward to prevent them from getting under you.
    • Guard Retention: The opponent can try to keep their guard closed or use their feet to push you away. To counter, focus on a strong, consistent pressure and a deliberate circular motion, ensuring your hips clear their legs before they can reset.
    • Submission Attempts (e.g., Triangle Choke): If you are too slow or predictable, the opponent might attempt a submission. To counter, maintain your posture, keep your head up, and ensure your hips are not squared up to their legs. If a submission is imminent, disengage your hips and retreat your head.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to do the Torreando Pass?

    Shoulder pain often arises from over-relying on arm strength to pull the opponent's guard open or to drive your body around. This puts excessive strain on the shoulder joint. Instead, focus on using your hips and torso to generate the circular motion, with your grips serving to control posture and direction. Keep your elbows tucked and use your core for power.

    Q: How can I Torreando Pass against a much bigger opponent with strong hips?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on a more dynamic and explosive hip circle. Instead of a slow, grinding pass, time your movement for when they momentarily relax their hips. Secure a very tight collar grip to control their head and posture, and use a powerful drive with your hips to circle quickly. Ensure your base is wide to prevent them from using their weight to stifle your movement.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate the Torreando Pass, and how do I avoid getting swept?

    The Torreando Pass is best attempted when the opponent's guard is somewhat open, or when you've just broken their closed guard and have a stable upright posture. To avoid sweeps, always maintain a wide base, keep your hips low but not on the mat, and continuously drive your chest into their chest. As you circle, think about stepping your passing leg *around* their legs, not directly *past* them, to maintain structural integrity.

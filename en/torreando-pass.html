The Torreando Pass is a dynamic guard pass initiated from the standing or kneeling position against an opponent's Closed Guard. Its goal is to bypass the opponent's legs and establish side control.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of hip mobility and an overreliance on upper body strength, leading to inefficient movement and potential injury.

The key is to utilize your hips and core to create a sweeping motion, disconnecting your hips from your opponent's base, allowing you to circle around.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip: Establish a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (either standard or pistol grip) on your opponent's left sleeve with your left hand.
    2. Hip Angle: Immediately angle your hips to your right, creating a 45-degree angle relative to your opponent's hips.
    3. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your left foot, lifting your right knee slightly off the mat.
    4. Hip Sweep: Drive your right hip towards the mat, sweeping your right leg outward and backward in a circular motion.
    5. Base Adjustment: As your right leg sweeps, your left foot pivots, aligning your body more directly with your opponent's side.
    6. Head and Shoulder Pressure: Lower your head and right shoulder towards your opponent's left hip, applying downward pressure.
    7. Circle and Secure: Continue circling your hips and body around your opponent's legs, maintaining pressure, and aim to secure side control with your right arm under their neck and your left arm controlling their far hip.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to 'pull' the legs apart with only arm strength: This can lead to biceps tendon tears or shoulder impingement due to excessive rotational force on the glenohumeral joint.
    • Standing straight up to break the guard: This puts immense pressure on the lumbar spine, risking disc herniation, and allows the opponent to easily sweep or take your back.
    • Dropping your weight too vertically onto the opponent's hips: This can cause knee ligament sprains (MCL/LCL) in your own knees from unexpected twisting forces, and doesn't effectively break their base.
    • Not angling hips sufficiently: This leads to getting stuck in the guard and can cause hip flexor strains from trying to force a straight-line pass.
    • Gripping too high on the collar: This can lead to wrist sprains if the opponent turns their wrist sharply or causes excessive strain on your own wrist joint.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Sweep Drill: Practice the hip sweeping motion without a partner, focusing on the circular movement of your hips and legs. (10 reps per side)
    2. Partnered Grip and Hip Sweep Drill (0% Resistance): With a partner lying on their back, practice establishing grips and performing the hip sweep motion, with the partner offering no resistance. (5 reps per side)
    3. Partnered Guard Break and Hip Sweep Drill (25% Resistance): Partner applies a loose closed guard. You grip and execute the hip sweep, then circle. Partner offers minimal resistance to the leg separation. (5 reps per side)
    4. Torreando Pass Entry Drill (50% Resistance): Partner maintains a closed guard. You execute the grips, hip sweep, and begin circling. Partner attempts to maintain guard connection but does not actively resist the pass. (5 reps per side)
    5. Controlled Torreando Pass (75% Resistance): Partner actively tries to keep their guard closed and might offer slight hip pressure. You focus on executing the pass smoothly. (3 reps per side)
    6. Live Rolling Application (90-100% Resistance): Attempt the Torreando Pass during live rolling, focusing on the biomechanics and timing. (3-5 attempts per round, or as opportunities arise)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent has a tight closed guard and you are in a kneeling or semi-kneeling position.
    • When your opponent attempts to open your guard with their feet, creating a slight separation.
    • When your opponent's hips are slightly elevated, making it harder for them to control your base.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Pummel and Hip Escape: As the Torreando passer circles, the defender can pummel their legs inward, simultaneously hip escaping away to re-establish guard or create space.
    • Inside Heel Hook/Ankle Lock Setup: If the passer overcommits to the hip sweep and leaves their leg exposed, the defender can attack the exposed leg with an inside heel hook or ankle lock.
    • Scissor Sweep/Hip Bump Sweep Setup: If the passer's weight distribution is too vertical and their hips are close, the defender can use a scissor sweep by extending one leg and pulling the passer's head down, or a hip bump sweep by bridging their hips up and to the side.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to Torreando Pass?

    Wrist pain during the Torreando Pass often stems from gripping the opponent's collar too high or with an unstable hand. When you grip too high, the opponent can twist their wrist, causing a painful torque on your own wrist joint. Ensure your collar grip is firm and controlled, and avoid letting your wrist bend at an extreme angle during the pass.

    Q: How can I Torreando Pass a bigger opponent if they have strong legs?

    Against a bigger opponent, focus on maximizing your hip mechanics and minimizing reliance on pure grip strength. Use your weight and hip angle to initiate the sweep, rather than trying to muscle their legs apart. The key is to create a moment of separation by driving your hips, allowing you to circle around their frame, not through it.

    Q: When should I stop attempting the Torreando Pass and switch to another pass?

    You should consider switching if the opponent consistently shuts down your hip sweep with strong leg control or by maintaining a very tight, unyielding closed guard. If you find yourself repeatedly stuck in the same position without progress after 2-3 attempts, it's a sign to transition to a different passing strategy like a knee-slice or Leg Drag to avoid expending unnecessary energy.

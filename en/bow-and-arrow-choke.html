Bow and Arrow Choke: The Ultimate BJJ Technique Guide for White Belts

The Bow and Arrow Choke is a powerful lapel choke typically initiated from Side Control or mount, aiming to constrict the carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of foundational body mechanics and a tendency to rely on brute force rather than leverage.

The key to this choke is isolating an arm and using your body as a fulcrum to create a precise, crushing pressure on the neck.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From side control, establish a deep cross-collar grip with your right hand (thumb inside lapel), reaching across the opponent's chest.
    2. Secure your left arm's elbow on the opponent's hip, creating a strong frame to prevent them from shrimping away.
    3. Shift your weight forward, driving your chest into the opponent's sternum, ensuring your hips are lower than theirs.
    4. With your right hand, pull the lapel taut, creating tension across their neck.
    5. Simultaneously, use your left hand to grip the opponent's pants near the knee on the same side as the collar grip.
    6. Begin to drive your right shoulder forward and down, applying pressure directly into the lapel grip.
    7. As you drive forward, extend your left leg, pushing your hips back and away from the opponent's hips, creating a bowing action.
    8. The combination of the lapel pull and the hip extension generates the choking force.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to crank the neck with your arm instead of using the lapel grip: This can strain the cervical spine ligaments and cause whiplash-like injuries.

    The correct action is to maintain a static collar grip and use your body's hip movement to create the choke.

    • Gripping the gi too loosely or too high on the collar: This allows the opponent to easily relieve pressure and can lead to shoulder impingement for the attacker.

    Grip deep and firmly on the opposite side lapel, ensuring your knuckles are near their shoulder.

    • Overextending your back or hyperextending your neck: This puts excessive stress on the lumbar and cervical spine, increasing the risk of disc injury or nerve impingement.

    Maintain a neutral spine and use controlled hip movement to generate power.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling of the grip sequence and body positioning: 10 repetitions per side, 0% resistance.
    2. Partner drilling with no resistance, focusing on grip placement and hip movement: 5 repetitions per side, 25% resistance.
    3. Partner drilling with light resistance, focusing on maintaining frame and hip control during the choke setup: 5 repetitions per side, 50% resistance.
    4. Drilling the full Bow and Arrow Choke sequence against a compliant partner who offers minimal defense: 3 repetitions per side, 75% resistance.
    5. Drilling with a partner who actively defends but doesn't resist the choke itself: 3 repetitions per side, 90% resistance.
    6. Live rolling with conscious attempts to implement the Bow and Arrow Choke from appropriate positions: 5 minutes, 100% resistance (focus on application, not success).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have secured side control and the opponent is turtled or exposing their neck.
    • When you have established mount and the opponent is attempting to bridge or escape by turning away.
    • When transitioning from guard passes and you can isolate an arm and control the opponent's posture.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Defense against the grip: If the opponent has a deep cross-collar grip, immediately peel their hand off or create space by shrimping your hips away.

    The counter involves breaking the grip by using your free hand to lift their knuckles or by creating a tight frame with your forearm.

    • Defense against the body lock: If the opponent tries to establish the lapel and pants grip, immediately tuck your chin and bring your knees towards your chest to create a compact defensive posture.

    The counter is to minimize your exposed surface area and prevent the choking mechanics from being established.

    • Defense against the hip extension: If the opponent begins to extend their hips, drive your hips into them and attempt to re-establish a strong base or turn into them.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Bow and Arrow Choke?

    Wrist pain often occurs if you are gripping the lapel too far down or with your fingers extended straight, creating a sharp angle at your wrist. You might also be trying to pull the lapel with your forearm muscles rather than using your body's leverage. To fix this, ensure your grip is deep on the lapel, with your knuckles pointing towards their shoulder, and keep your wrist in a neutral, slightly flexed position. Use your entire body's rotation to pull the lapel, not just your wrist.

    Q: How can I effectively apply the Bow and Arrow Choke against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, brute force is your enemy. Focus on precise biomechanics and timing. Ensure your cross-collar grip is extremely deep, almost to their shoulder. Use your hip frame on their hip to prevent them from crushing you. The key is to generate maximum leverage through your hip extension, driving your hips back and creating that 'bowing' motion, rather than trying to pull them down with your arms.

    Q: When is the best moment to transition to a Bow and Arrow Choke during a roll?

    The ideal moment is when your opponent is trying to escape or re-establish their guard by turning away from you, or when they Turtle up. These moments expose their neck and create an opening for the lapel grip. If you are in side control and they are flat on their back, it's harder to set up. Look for opportunities where their posture is broken or they are actively trying to move away from your control.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: As a complete beginner in BJJ, what are the absolute fundamental techniques I should focus on learning first in the curriculum to build a solid foundation?

    Focus on mastering the fundamental positional controls like the mount and side control, emphasizing hip pressure and shoulder-to-hip connection to maintain dominant positions. Simultaneously, learn basic escapes from these positions, such as the shrimp escape (hip escape) and bridging, utilizing leverage from your hips and core to create space and regain guard.

    Q: I'm struggling to understand the progression of techniques in BJJ. How should the curriculum be structured so I'm not just learning random moves but building a logical understanding of offense and defense?

    A well-designed curriculum should progress from foundational positional control and escapes to basic submissions from those dominant positions, and then introduce defensive concepts and counters. This builds a layered understanding, where you first learn to secure and maintain positions, then how to attack from them, and finally how to prevent your opponent from doing the same.

    Q: When learning new BJJ techniques, how can the curriculum be designed to help me understand the 'why' behind each movement, not just the 'how,' so I can adapt them better on the mats?

    The curriculum should explain the biomechanical principles driving each technique, such as leverage, weight distribution, and joint mechanics, rather than just rote memorization. Understanding how your body's structure and your opponent's structure interact allows for more intuitive adaptation and problem-solving when the exact prescribed movement isn't possible.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How do I structure a BJJ curriculum for a new academy?

    Start with fundamental positions and movements, gradually introducing more complex techniques and transitions. Ensure a logical progression that builds on prior knowledge, focusing on safe and effective execution at each stage.

    What are the key components of a good BJJ curriculum?

    A comprehensive curriculum includes a balance of striking defense, takedowns, positional control, submissions, and escapes. It should also incorporate drills, live rolling, and theoretical instruction to solidify understanding and application.

    How often should I update or revise my BJJ curriculum?

    Regular review is crucial, perhaps annually or bi-annually, to incorporate new trends, refine existing techniques, and address feedback from students and instructors. Stay current with the evolution of BJJ to maintain relevance and effectiveness.

The Balloon Sweep is initiated from Closed Guard, aiming to invert the opponent and take top position. White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of leverage and weight distribution.

The key to the Balloon Sweep is creating a dynamic imbalance by using your hips and legs as a lever, not just brute strength.

This sweep leverages your opponent's forward momentum against them, creating a powerful rotation to your advantage.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From closed guard, secure both of your opponent's wrists with a gable grip (interlocking fingers). Maintain a tight hip connection.
    2. Angle your hips slightly to your right, creating a small space between your hips and their lower back. Keep your knees tight to your chest.
    3. Shift your weight onto your left hip, keeping your right leg tight around their left leg. Your right knee should point towards the ceiling.
    4. Simultaneously, begin to drive your hips upwards and to your left, using your left leg as the fulcrum. Your opponent's weight should start to shift forward.
    5. As their weight commits forward, extend your right leg forcefully upwards and to your left, scooping under their left leg. This action initiates the "balloon" effect.
    6. Continue the hip drive, rotating your torso to your left. Your right leg acts as a lever, lifting and sweeping their leg out.
    7. As their leg clears the ground, immediately drive your hips forward and to your left, transitioning to a knee-on-belly or side control position. Maintain your grip on their wrists to control their base.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to "pull" your opponent over with arm strength alone: This can strain your shoulder joint (rotator cuff tears) by hyperextending the humerus. Instead, drive your hips and use your legs as levers.
    • Extending your legs too early or too wide: This can expose your knee joint to hyperextension or twisting (ACL/MCL tears) if the opponent resists. Keep your legs tight and use them in a controlled, sweeping motion.
    • Losing your closed guard by opening your knees prematurely: This can lead to your opponent posturing up or passing your guard, risking knee-on-belly pressure or stack passes that can injure your lower back from compression. Maintain hip connection until the sweep is initiated.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Drive Drill (0% resistance): Practice the hip elevation and rotation motion without a partner, focusing on the feeling of lifting your hips. 20 reps.
    2. Closed Guard Hip Angle and Grip Practice (0% resistance): Sit in closed guard, practice the hip angle and gable grip on wrists. Hold for 30 seconds, repeat 5 times.
    3. Balloon Sweep Motion with Partner (No Resistance) (25% resistance): Partner lies on their back. You practice the entire sweep motion, focusing on the hip drive and leg scoop. Partner offers minimal resistance. 10 reps per side.
    4. Balloon Sweep with Light Resistance (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to prevent the sweep by posturing slightly. Focus on timing the hip drive with their forward lean. 15 reps per side.
    5. Balloon Sweep Against Posture (75% resistance): Partner attempts to posture up and create frames. You must break their posture and execute the sweep. 20 reps per side.
    6. Live Rolling Application (90% resistance): Attempt the Balloon Sweep during rolling. Focus on correct execution and timing, accepting that it won't always work. 5 attempts per round.

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up and leaning forward, creating a weight advantage for you.
    • When your opponent is attempting to stack pass from closed guard, their weight is already forward.
    • When you have established strong wrist control and can prevent them from framing effectively.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Posture Up and Frame: As the sweep is initiated, immediately drive your hips back down to the mat and create frames with your forearms against your opponent's hips. This stops the hip elevation.
    • Spin Under: If the sweep is weak, quickly spin your hips out to your side and try to come up to a standing position or transition to a leg drag. This avoids being swept.
    • Knee Shield and Base: If your leg is being swept, aggressively drive your knee into your opponent's hip and widen your base to prevent the rotation. This can sometimes lead to a guard pass for you.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try the Balloon Sweep from closed guard?

    Your shoulder likely hurts because you are relying too much on pulling with your arms instead of driving with your hips and legs. This causes hyperextension of the humerus. Focus on the upward and outward drive of your hips and the scooping motion of your leg, using your core and glutes for power, not just your arm strength.

    Q: How can I perform the Balloon Sweep against a much bigger and heavier opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, the key is to emphasize the *timing* and *angle* of your hip drive. You need to wait for them to lean forward significantly, creating a moment of imbalance. Use their forward momentum against them by initiating the hip lift and leg sweep simultaneously as they commit their weight. Focus on getting under their center of gravity.

    Q: Is the Balloon Sweep effective if my opponent keeps their hips very low in closed guard?

    The Balloon Sweep is less effective when your opponent keeps their hips extremely low and tight, as it limits your ability to get your leg underneath them and create leverage. In this scenario, it's better to focus on breaking their posture first, perhaps by using a "sit-up sweep" or by transitioning to an open guard to create space and attack their legs or arms.

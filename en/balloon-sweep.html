The Balloon Sweep is initiated from Closed Guard, aiming to off-balance and invert your opponent to sweep them.

White belts often struggle by simply trying to muscle the sweep, neglecting the crucial body mechanics.

The key is creating a dynamic fulcrum with your hips and using your opponent's weight against them.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish an overhook with your right arm on your opponent's left arm, gripping their gi lapel near the shoulder. Your left hand grips their right sleeve at the wrist.
    2. Sit up slightly, bringing your hips forward and tilting your pelvis upwards. Your knees should be tucked towards your chest, creating a strong base.
    3. Drive your right knee *under* your opponent's right armpit, creating a powerful lever point. This knee acts as the anchor for the sweep.
    4. Simultaneously, extend your left leg forcefully upwards, aiming to kick their right hip upwards and outwards. Your left foot should be flexed, pushing into their hip.
    5. As your left leg extends, drive your hips further forward and upward, creating a "balloon" effect where their weight is lifted.
    6. Immediately transition your hips to the side, rolling your opponent over your right shoulder as you fall to your left side.
    7. Secure a strong top position, ideally side control or mount, as they land.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly lifting your hips: This can hyperextend the lumbar spine, causing lower back strain or disc injury. The correct movement involves tilting the pelvis upward, not just arching the back.
    • Over-extending the kicking leg without hip drive: This can lead to a hamstring strain or a weak sweep attempt. Focus on the hip-up and leg-kick combination, not just the leg.
    • Gripping too high on the sleeve: This allows the opponent to easily pull their arm out, negating your control and potentially causing a wrist sprain for you if they yank forcefully. Grip firmly at the wrist with a strong elbow hook.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice hip elevation and leg kick motion from the closed guard position on the mat. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a willing partner, practice the grip setup and hip elevation, focusing on the knee-under-armpit and leg-kick motion. (20 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the mechanics of lifting their hip. Focus on the sweep execution. (15 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled rolling: Attempt the Balloon Sweep against a partner who understands the technique and offers controlled resistance, focusing on timing and hip drive. (5 successful sweeps, 50% resistance)
    5. Moderate resistance rolling: Attempt the sweep in a live roll, but don't force it. Focus on identifying opportunities and executing the mechanics correctly. (3 successful sweeps, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the Balloon Sweep into your regular rolling, aiming for successful executions against fully resisting opponents. (Aim for 1-2 successful sweeps per roll, 100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • Opponent establishes a strong, high closed guard, making other sweeps difficult.
    • Opponent is leaning forward, creating an opportunity to use their weight.
    • You have secure grips on their collar and sleeve, providing good control.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stacking Pass: If the opponent initiates the sweep, they can immediately stack their weight forward onto your hips and shoulders. To counter, keep your base wide and drive your hips down, negating their leverage.
    • Hip Escape and Base Out: As the sweep begins, the defender can quickly hip escape to their right (away from the sweeping leg) and drive their left knee to the mat, creating a stable base. This prevents the opponent from getting their knee under the armpit.
    • Grip Break and Posture Up: The defender can attempt to break the collar and sleeve grips by pulling their arms apart or by posturing up forcefully, creating space and disrupting the sweep setup.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my back hurt when I try the Balloon Sweep?

    Your back likely hurts because you are over-arching your lumbar spine instead of using your hips to create leverage. The correct movement involves tilting your pelvis upwards from the hips, not just bending your back. Focus on driving your hips forward and upward, keeping your core engaged, to generate the sweep power.

    Q: How can I do the Balloon Sweep against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, the Balloon Sweep relies even more heavily on timing and precise mechanics rather than brute strength. Ensure your grips are tight, especially the collar grip, to control their posture. Focus on driving your knee *deep* under their armpit and using your leg kick to lift their hip, rather than trying to lift their entire body.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Balloon Sweep in BJJ?

    The Balloon Sweep is most effective when your opponent has a relatively tight closed guard and is leaning forward slightly, creating an imbalance. It's also a good option if you've been struggling to break their guard or execute other sweeps due to their strong posture. Look for moments of slight forward pressure from them to initiate.

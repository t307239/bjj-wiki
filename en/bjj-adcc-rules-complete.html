    More Questions

    What are the key differences between ADCC rules and IBJJF rules regarding submissions?

    ADCC rules are known for allowing more submission types, particularly heel hooks and leg locks, which are often restricted or disallowed in IBJJF competition. The 'no-time-limit' aspect of ADCC also significantly impacts strategy and submission hunting.

    How does the ADCC scoring system differ from other rule sets, and how does it influence strategy?

    ADCC uses a system that heavily rewards takedowns and control, with points for sweeps and passes. This encourages aggressive offensive grappling and can lead to strategic decisions focused on securing dominant positions rather than solely pursuing submissions, especially when time is a factor.

    What are the most common submission threats to be aware of when competing under ADCC rules?

    Given the allowance of leg locks, heel hooks, toe holds, and ankle locks are extremely prevalent and dangerous. Additionally, the emphasis on positional control means submissions from dominant positions like side control, mount, and back control remain critical threats.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: I'm a white belt and keep getting my arm popped in ADCC Rules Complete Guide, what specific body mechanics should I focus on to prevent this?

    To prevent arm locks, always keep your elbow tucked towards your centerline, creating a strong frame with your forearm against your opponent's shoulder. Actively engage your core by slightly arching your back and pulling your hips in, which makes it much harder for them to isolate and extend your arm.

    Q: When I'm in ADCC Rules Complete Guide and someone is trying to pass my guard, how can I use my hips and legs biomechanically to maintain space and prevent them from getting past?

    To maintain guard retention, focus on hip bridging and shrimp movements to create space. Use your shins to block their hips and knees, and actively push their weight away with your feet on their hips, maintaining a strong base and preventing them from collapsing your structure.

    Q: As a beginner in ADCC Rules Complete Guide, I struggle with maintaining a stable base when someone is trying to sweep me. What specific leg and hip positioning can I use to stay grounded?

    To maintain a stable base against sweeps, keep your hips low and your knees bent, creating a wide base of support. Actively drive your feet into the mat and use your core muscles to connect your upper and lower body, making it difficult for them to leverage your limbs and unbalance you.

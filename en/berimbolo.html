Your First Berimbolo: A White Belt's Guide to BJJ

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Purple Belt Requirements

The Berimbolo is a dynamic guard transition, typically initiated from open guard, aiming to secure a back-Mount position. It involves a rolling movement to secure a dominant position.

White belts often struggle because they lack a foundational understanding of hip mobility and weight transfer, leading to predictable and easily countered movements.

The core principle is using your hips to 'throw' your opponent's leg over your shoulder while simultaneously initiating a backward roll, creating momentum and leverage.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a De La Riva hook with your right leg, hooking behind the opponent's left thigh from your open guard base (hips off the mat, knees bent).
    2. Grip opponent's left ankle with your right hand, ensuring your palm faces inward and fingers grip the fabric of the sock or ankle bone.
    3. Grip opponent's left pants cuff (just above the knee) with your left hand, creating a strong pulling anchor point.
    4. Initiate the roll by driving your left hip downwards and slightly forward, creating an angle where your left knee points towards your opponent's right armpit.
    5. As your left hip descends, simultaneously pull the opponent's ankle towards your chest with your right hand and pull the pants cuff towards your left hip with your left hand.
    6. Tuck your head and drive your right shoulder into the mat, initiating a forward rolling motion, using the opponent's leg as a lever.
    7. As you roll, your right leg should swing over the opponent's hips, and your left leg should chase to secure a body triangle or seatbelt grip on their back.
    8. Maintain a strong grip on the ankle and pants throughout the roll, ensuring the opponent's leg stays connected to your body.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to 'muscle' the roll without proper hip engagement: This can lead to hyperextension or tears in the knee ligaments (ACL/MCL) as the opponent's leg forces the knee into an unnatural position.
    • Releasing grips prematurely during the roll: This causes a loss of control, potentially leading to the opponent escaping or you landing awkwardly on your back, risking spinal injury.
    • Not tucking the head and neck properly: This can result in a direct impact on the cervical spine, leading to whiplash or more severe neck injuries upon landing.
    • Overextending the rolling leg before hip engagement: This strains the hip flexors and can lead to groin pulls or labral tears.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip movement and leg swing without a partner. Focus on hip angles and the trajectory of your right leg. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Your partner stands passively. Execute the Berimbolo, focusing on grip integrity and the rolling motion. (25 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to the leg pull and hip rotation. Focus on maintaining control and completing the roll. (20 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (controlled resistance): Partner actively tries to post their leg or hip away, but without actively trying to pass. Work on adapting your grips and hip drive. (15 reps per side)
    5. Flow rolling (90% resistance): Execute the Berimbolo when the opportunity arises. Your partner will defend but not actively try to submit or pass aggressively. (10 attempts)
    6. Live rolling (100% resistance): Look for opportunities to execute the Berimbolo in a live roll. Focus on timing and reacting to your partner's movements. (As opportunities arise)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When opponent extends their leg away from your closed guard, creating space.
    • When opponent is posturing up and their weight is forward, making it harder for them to react.
    • When you have established a strong De La Riva hook and can control their ankle.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Post: If the opponent begins the roll, immediately post your free leg (the one not hooked) flat on the mat to stop your backward momentum. This prevents the roll and allows you to regain base.
    • Hip Escape/Shrimping: As the opponent rolls, if you feel their leg swinging over, hip escape aggressively to create space and avoid being caught in the entanglement.
    • Standing Up: If the opponent attempts the Berimbolo from a distance, immediately stand up straight and drive your weight forward, breaking their base and preventing the roll.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Berimbolo?

    Knee pain during the Berimbolo usually stems from insufficient hip engagement and improper leg mechanics. Instead of using your hips to 'throw' the opponent's leg, you might be forcing your own knee into a compromising position. Ensure your hips are driving down and forward, creating a pivot point for your rolling leg, and that you're pulling the opponent's ankle towards your chest, not letting their leg dictate your knee angle.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paramount. Focus on securing a deep De La Riva hook and a tight ankle grip. Initiate the hip drive with maximum explosiveness, using their size against them by creating a significant lever with their leg. Don't try to overpower them; instead, use their weight and forward momentum during the roll to your advantage. A strong pants grip is crucial for controlling their base.

    Q: The Berimbolo is rarely initiated directly from closed guard. It's typically a transition *from* an open guard, like De La Riva or Reverse De La Riva. You would first need to break their posture, open your guard, and establish the necessary grips (De La Riva hook, ankle grip) to transition into the Berimbolo mechanics. Trying to force it from a tight closed guard often leads to being crushed or losing position.

The Berimbolo Technique: A White Belt's First Steps

The Berimbolo starts from Closed Guard, aiming to invert and secure a back-take position. It bypasses traditional guard passes by using leg entanglement.

White belts often struggle with a lack of body awareness and fear of the inverted position, leading to uncontrolled movements. This can cause them to fall flat or expose their back instead.

The core mechanical insight is generating rotational momentum through hip movement and controlled leg scooping, creating an angle for the sweep and Back Take.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a strong collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip on your opponent's left sleeve with your left hand. Keep your elbows tight to your body.
    2. Initiate a slight hip lift, bringing your hips slightly off the mat while maintaining the closed guard connection.
    3. Simultaneously, begin to bring your right knee towards your opponent's left armpit, initiating a subtle hip rotation to your right.
    4. As your right knee enters the armpit space, begin to release your opponent's left sleeve grip and reach under their left thigh with your right hand, securing a deep grip on their pants or thigh.
    5. Scoop your left leg, extending it as you simultaneously drive your hips forward and rotate your torso further to the right, creating a lever action with your legs.
    6. Your goal is to bring your right foot to the mat behind your opponent's left hip, while your left leg hooks behind their right thigh.
    7. Drive off your right foot and push your hips up and over, transitioning to your right hip and then your back, securing a body triangle as you land.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly attempting to 'kick' your leg over your opponent's head: This can hyperextend the knee or ankle, leading to ligament tears (ACL, MCL, ankle sprain) because the joint is forced beyond its natural range of motion.
    • Falling flat on your back during the inversion: This puts immense pressure on the lumbar spine and can cause facet joint compression or disc injury if the landing is uncontrolled and the spine is not properly protected.
    • Releasing grips too early and losing control of your opponent's base: This allows your opponent to easily post and stand up, potentially leading to uncontrolled falls for you, risking shoulder or neck injuries if you land awkwardly.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip and Leg Scooping Drill: Practice the hip lift, knee drive, and leg scoop motion without a partner. Focus on the feeling of hip rotation. (10 reps per side)
    2. Partnered Grip and Hip Lift: With a partner in your closed guard, practice establishing grips and performing the hip lift and slight hip rotation. Partner remains passive. (5 reps per side)
    3. Controlled Inversion Drill: With a partner providing minimal resistance, practice the full Berimbolo motion, focusing on getting your foot behind their hip and securing the hook. Partner offers no resistance to the sweep. (3 reps per side)
    4. Guard Pass Counter Drill: Have a partner attempt a basic guard pass (e.g., knee slice). Practice initiating the Berimbolo as they begin to pass. Partner offers 25% resistance to the sweep. (5 reps per side)
    5. Berimbolo Setup Drill: From closed guard, practice setting up the Berimbolo by controlling your opponent's posture and initiating the leg entanglement. Partner offers 50% resistance to your control and sweep attempt. (5 reps per side)
    6. Live Rolling Integration: Attempt the Berimbolo during live rolling with a partner of similar size and skill level, focusing on proper execution rather than success. Partner offers 75% resistance to the sweep. (3 attempts per roll)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent attempts to pass your guard by posting their weight forward.
    • Opponent's hips are close to yours in closed guard, allowing for leg entanglement.
    • Opponent is posturing up, creating an opening for inversion.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Post and Stand: If the attacker attempts to invert, immediately post your foot on their hip and push them away, then stand up. This prevents them from getting their hook.
    • Hip Escape and Re-guard: As the attacker attempts to scoop, hip escape away to create space and re-establish your guard or a different guard. This disrupts their angle.
    • Grip Break and S-Mount: If the attacker successfully initiates the sweep, break their leg grips and immediately transition to an S-mount to regain positional advantage.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to Berimbolo?

    Knee pain during the Berimbolo often stems from forcing the inversion without proper hip rotation. Instead of driving your knee towards the armpit and scooping with your hip, you might be trying to 'kick' your leg over, causing hyperextension or valgus stress on the knee. Focus on initiating the movement from your hips and allowing your leg to follow naturally, ensuring your knee stays aligned with your foot.

    Q: How do I Berimbolo against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are crucial. Focus on controlling their posture and creating a strong connection before initiating the Berimbolo. Use your grips to pull their weight forward and off-balance them. When inverting, emphasize driving your hips into them to create the necessary angle, rather than trying to lift them. Use your legs to scoop and entangle their base, making their size a disadvantage for them.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Berimbolo from closed guard?

    The ideal time to attempt a Berimbolo from closed guard is when your opponent is actively trying to pass by posting their weight forward and their hips are close to yours. This forward pressure makes them vulnerable to being swept and inverted upon. Avoid attempting it when they are postured high and far away, as this creates too much space and reduces your ability to entangle their legs effectively.

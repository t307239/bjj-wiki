The Berimbolo is a dynamic BJJ guard transition originating from open guard, aiming to secure a back-take. It requires precise hip movement and weight transfer to initiate the roll.

White belts often struggle with the Berimbolo due to a lack of body awareness and a tendency to 'muscle' the movement instead of using leverage.

The core mechanical insight is using your hips to 'load' your opponent's weight, creating an angle for your body to rotate underneath them.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip: Establish a collar grip (one hand on the opponent's gi collar, opposite side of the leg you will swing) and a sleeve grip (on the opponent's gi sleeve, same side as the leg you will swing).
    2. Hip Angle: Sit back, bringing your hips towards your heels, creating a 90-degree angle between your torso and thighs. Your base should be wide.
    3. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your gripping arm's shoulder, creating a fulcrum.
    4. Leg Action: Swing the leg opposite your gripping arm *under* the opponent's leg, aiming to hook their thigh or shin with your heel.
    5. Hip Rotation: Simultaneously, rotate your hips *towards* the opponent's leg you are hooking, initiating a roll.
    6. Body Rotation: As your hips rotate, drive your torso forward and around, tucking your head to avoid impact.
    7. Securing Position: As you complete the roll, your goal is to end up behind your opponent, ideally in back-mount, securing your hooks.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Hip Rotation: Swinging your hips outwards instead of rotating them inward can lead to a knee hyperextension injury, as the knee joint is subjected to shear forces it's not designed to withstand. The correct movement involves a controlled inward hip rotation, using your core and glutes.
    • Head First Roll: Launching your head forward without proper body alignment can result in neck injuries (strains, sprains) or concussions. Always tuck your chin and lead with your shoulder and hips as you rotate.
    • Overextending the Leg Hook: Trying to force the leg hook without the correct hip rotation can strain the hamstring or groin muscles. Focus on using your hips to 'pull' their leg into position, not just extending your leg.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Mobility Drill: Practice the hip rotation and tucking motion without any opponent. Focus on generating power from the hips. (50 reps)
    2. Shadow Berimbolo: Perform the entire Berimbolo motion, visualizing an opponent and focusing on grip placement and weight transfer. (20 reps per side)
    3. Partner Assisted Roll (0% Resistance): With a partner lying on their back, practice the rolling motion, focusing on initiating the roll with your hips and securing the imaginary back-mount. Partner provides zero resistance. (10 reps per side)
    4. Partner Assisted Roll (25% Resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance by slightly pushing back, forcing you to generate more hip power. Focus on the initial leg hook and hip turn. (15 reps per side)
    5. Controlled Berimbolo (50% Resistance): Partner actively defends the initial leg hook but allows the roll to happen. Focus on completing the rotation and ending in a stable position. (10 reps per side)
    6. Live Rolling (75-90% Resistance): Attempt the Berimbolo during sparring, focusing on timing and commitment. Your partner will actively resist. (Attempt 5 times per round, focus on learning)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is posturing up from your closed guard, creating space.
    • When you have established a strong De La Riva or Reverse De La Riva guard and they are pressuring forward.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard and their weight is forward and over your legs.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • The "Sit Out" Counter: If the opponent attempts to Berimbolo, as they initiate the roll, sit your hips out to your non-gripping side, creating a base. This prevents them from getting behind you and allows you to recompose guard or pass.
    • The "Stand Up" Counter: As they begin to swing their leg, quickly drive your hips down and stand up, creating distance and breaking their connection. This often leads to a guard pass opportunity for you.
    • The "Spin Under" Counter: If they commit to the Berimbolo, you can anticipate their movement and spin under their rolling motion, often ending up in a superior position like side control or even attempting your own guard pass.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to Berimbolo?

    Shoulder pain during the Berimbolo is usually caused by an improper weight transfer and a lack of hip drive. Instead of using your shoulder as the primary fulcrum, you're likely leaning all your weight onto it without generating the necessary hip rotation. Focus on driving your hips forward and around, using your shoulder as a pivot point, not a weight-bearing platform.

    Q: How can I Berimbolo against a much bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paraMount. You must create a significant angle with your hips before initiating the roll. Use their forward pressure against them by sitting back and creating that initial hip disconnect. Focus on securing a deep leg hook and using a strong collar grip to pull their weight over your shoulder as you roll.

    Q: When is the right time to transition from guard to Berimbolo?

    The ideal time to attempt a Berimbolo is when your opponent is actively pressuring forward into your guard or attempting a pass. Look for moments when their weight is committed forward and their base is slightly unstable. Avoid attempting it when they are postured up and have a strong base, as this will make the roll much harder and riskier.

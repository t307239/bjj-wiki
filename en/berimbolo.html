Berimbolo: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

The Berimbolo is a dynamic guard retention and sweeping technique that originates from open guard positions, aiming to invert and secure a back-take or sweep. White belts often struggle due to a lack of fundamental body awareness and uncontrolled movements, leading to compromised positions. Success hinges on controlling your opponent's base with your legs while using your hips to generate rotational momentum.

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a De La Riva hook with your left leg around the opponent's right leg, securing a pants grip (e.g., right hand on opponent's right ankle).
    2. Place your right hand on the opponent's left knee to prevent them from posturing up or passing.
    3. Initiate a hip escape to your left, creating space to bring your right leg underneath the opponent's hips.
    4. As your right leg slides under, your hips should angle towards your left, creating a strong base.
    5. Drive your right knee towards the opponent's hip pocket, anchoring your right foot on the mat for leverage.
    6. Simultaneously, pull the opponent's right ankle towards you with your left hand while pushing their left knee away with your right hand.
    7. Rotate your hips and torso to your left, driving your right shoulder towards the mat and bringing your left leg over the opponent's back.
    8. Secure your hooks by placing your feet on the opponent's hips, maintaining pressure and control.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to roll your hips too high or too early can hyperextend your lumbar spine, risking a lower back injury.
    • The correct movement involves a controlled hip escape followed by a rotation, not an uncontrolled arch.
    • Letting go of grips prematurely to "reach" for the back can cause your shoulder to dislocate if the opponent posts or resists.
    • Maintain a firm connection and initiate the rotation with hip and leg drive, not arm-only reach.
    • Driving your knee directly into the opponent's groin or thigh instead of their hip pocket can lead to knee joint strain or ligament damage for you.
    • Focus on tucking your knee into the crease of their hip, using your foot for a stable base.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice the leg movements and hip rotation without resistance. Focus on smooth transitions and body positioning. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill: With a compliant partner, practice establishing the De La Riva hook and performing the hip escape and leg underhook. (25 reps)
    3. Partner drill: Add the grip changes and the initial hip rotation to the previous step. Partner offers minimal resistance. (50 reps)
    4. Partner drill: Focus on the full inversion and securing the hooks, with the partner offering light resistance to your movements. (75 reps)
    5. Live drilling: Attempt the Berimbolo against a partner who is actively defending but not aggressively passing. (90% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the Berimbolo into rolling scenarios, aiming to use it when the opportunity arises against a resisting opponent. (100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent extends their leg to establish a De La Riva guard or similar open guard.
    • Opponent attempts to pass your guard by stepping their leg across your body.
    • Opponent is in a seated or kneeling position, offering a clear target for your leg entry.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Opponent spins away: If the opponent spins out of your Berimbolo attempt, immediately transition to a leg drag or knee-on-belly pass by maintaining pressure and connecting to their hip.
    • Opponent posts their foot: If the opponent posts their foot to prevent your inversion, use that foot to drive and elevate them into a sweep, or switch to a sweep attempt like a tripod sweep.
    • Opponent stacks you: If the opponent attempts to stack you, use your hooks and hips to create space and drive them over, securing a sweep or transitioning to mount.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do the Berimbolo?

    Knee pain during the Berimbolo often stems from driving your knee directly into the opponent's tibia or femur instead of their hip pocket. This misalignment puts excessive shear force on your knee joint. Ensure you're tucking your knee into the crease of their hip and using your foot as a stable base on the mat for leverage, not as a direct impact point.

    Q: How can I successfully Berimbolo against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paraMount. Focus on establishing a strong De La Riva hook and securing a deep pants grip on their ankle to control their base. Use your hip escape and leg underhook to create a small window of opportunity, then drive with your hips and legs to initiate the rotation. Don't try to muscle it; use their weight against them by creating off-balancing angles.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate the Berimbolo in a BJJ match?

    The Berimbolo is most effective when your opponent is actively trying to pass your guard or is in a static, somewhat upright position. If they overcommit to a pass or extend a leg, that's your cue to initiate the inversion. Avoid attempting it when they are already in a dominant, flattened-out passing position, as this will make inversion much more difficult and dangerous.

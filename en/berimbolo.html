The Berimbolo: A Precise BJJ Technique Guide for White Belts

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Purple Belt Requirements

The Berimbolo is a dynamic guard retention and sweep system, typically initiated from open guard positions like De La Riva or Reverse De La Riva.

It aims to invert your body and secure a back-taking position or sweep by attacking the opponent's base.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body awareness and a tendency to rely on brute strength instead of leverage.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip: Establish a sleeve grip on the opponent's far leg (e.g., right sleeve if attacking opponent's left leg) and a pant grip on the opponent's near leg (e.g., left pant cuff).
    2. Hip Angle: Rotate your hips 45 degrees away from the opponent, creating a wedge with your near leg's shin against their hip joint.
    3. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your hips, allowing your anchored leg (near leg) to act as a pivot point.
    4. Inversion: Drive your far leg (the one with the sleeve grip) under the opponent's leg, sweeping it upwards and across your body.
    5. Hook Placement: As your far leg comes over, place your foot (instep or shin) onto the opponent's hip or lower back to maintain leverage.
    6. Rotation: Continue rotating your body, bringing your chest towards the opponent's back and your head to the outside.
    7. Finish: Secure a seatbelt grip (one arm over the shoulder, one under the armpit) and drive your hips forward to complete the sweep or transition to a back take.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Turtling without a Hook: Attempting to invert without securing a strong hook on the opponent's hip or back allows them to easily escape your base, potentially leading to a knee hyperextension if your leg gets trapped.
    • Over-Rotating the Hips: Forcing hip rotation without proper weight transfer can strain the lumbar spine and sacrum, especially if the opponent resists with a strong base.
    • Head-First Dive: Diving head-first into the Berimbolo without maintaining head control and shoulder alignment can lead to neck injuries, particularly if the opponent sprawls or pivots away suddenly.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Rotation Drill: Practice the hip-turning and leg-sweeping motion without a partner. Focus on smooth transitions and maintaining a low center of gravity. (50 reps per side)
    2. Dummy Berimbolo: Use a training dummy to practice the entire Berimbolo sequence, focusing on grip acquisition and body positioning. (25 reps per side)
    3. Static Partner Drill (0% Resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the initial grips and hip angle. Partner remains completely still. (20 reps per side)
    4. Controlled Partner Drill (25% Resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to practice the inversion and hook placement. Focus on maintaining control. (15 reps per side)
    5. Semi-Live Partner Drill (50% Resistance): Partner begins to react to your movements, but not actively defending. Focus on adapting your technique. (10 reps per side)
    6. Live Rolling (75-90% Resistance): Attempt the Berimbolo during rolling with a training partner who is actively trying to defend. Focus on timing and execution. (5-10 attempts per round)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent extends their leg away from you, creating space for your initial hook.
    • When the opponent posts their hand on the mat to defend your initial guard retention attempts.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard by stepping over your legs.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • The "Stack" Counter: If the opponent attempts to stack you during the Berimbolo, immediately drop your hips to the mat and drive your knees towards your chest, creating a strong base to prevent the stack.
    • The "Knee-Block" Defense: As the opponent begins to invert, use your free leg (the one not hooked) to block their advancing hip and prevent them from completing the rotation.
    • The "Return to Guard": If the Berimbolo attempt is failing, quickly pull your legs back to a stable closed or open guard position, maintaining your base and preventing the opponent from capitalizing on the failed attempt.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to Berimbolo?

    Lower back pain during the Berimbolo often stems from excessive lumbar hyperextension or twisting without proper hip engagement. Ensure your hips are rotating as a unit, not just your spine. Focus on driving your hips forward and using your core muscles to support the movement, rather than relying solely on spinal flexion.

    Q: How can I successfully Berimbolo against a much larger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paraMount. Focus on securing deep grips and using their weight against them. Instead of trying to force the rotation, wait for them to shift their weight or attempt a pass. Use your legs to create angles and control their hips, then execute the inversion explosively when you feel a slight opening.

    Q: When is the right time to initiate the Berimbolo from De La Riva guard?

    The optimal time to initiate the Berimbolo from De La Riva Guard is when the opponent's weight is forward, and they are attempting to pressure into you. Look for moments when they step their outside leg across your body to create a passing lane. This provides the perfect opportunity to use their forward momentum to invert and secure your position.

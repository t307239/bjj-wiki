Your First Berimbolo: A White Belt's Guide

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Purple Belt Requirements

The Berimbolo is a dynamic guard transition, typically initiated from open guard positions like De La Riva or Spider Guard, aiming to secure a back-take.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of fundamental body control and a tendency to rely on brute force, leading to ineffective or dangerous attempts.

Success hinges on understanding the hip rotation and weight transfer, using your body's levers to off-balance and secure the position efficiently.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From De La Riva Guard, establish a collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (left sleeve) with your left hand.
    2. Swing your right leg (the one hooked around the opponent's leg) over their hip, aiming to place your right foot on their hip crease, establishing a "hook."
    3. Simultaneously, release your left sleeve grip and reach your left arm under their armpit, securing a grip on their far hip or waist.
    4. Initiate a hip-heist: drive your hips to the left while keeping your hooked leg tight.
    5. As your hips move, pivot your body clockwise, bringing your right knee towards the mat and your left leg extended to create space.
    6. Drive your right foot (currently on their hip) off their hip to generate forward momentum, scooping your opponent towards your right side.
    7. Continue the hip rotation, bringing your right leg to the mat and your left leg to hook behind their knee, transitioning to a back-mount position.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to "pull" yourself into the Berimbolo by yanking the collar and sleeve without hip movement: This can strain the rotator cuff and biceps tendons in your arms and shoulders due to excessive tensile load.
    • Letting your hips "collapse" or remain static during the sweep: This exposes your lumbar spine to hyperextension and potential disc injury as you try to force the rotation.
    • Allowing your leg to "drop" or become loose when hooking the opponent's leg: This can lead to knee hyperextension or ligamentous tears (ACL/MCL) if the opponent applies pressure in the wrong direction.
    • Forcing the rotation with your upper body alone: This can cause neck strain and shoulder impingement as your spine twists unnaturally.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Rotation Drill: Practice the hip-heist and body pivot motion without any partner. Focus on a 90-degree rotation. (50 reps)
    2. Shadow Berimbolo with Resistance Band: Use a resistance band anchored to a stable object, simulating the opponent's leg. Practice the leg swing and hip rotation. (25 reps)
    3. Partner Drills - Static Grips: With a compliant partner, practice establishing grips and performing the hip-heist and leg swing motion without any resistance. (50% resistance, 20 reps)
    4. Partner Drills - Controlled Sweep: Partner provides minimal resistance, allowing you to execute the full Berimbolo sweep. Focus on landing in side control or mount initially. (75% resistance, 15 reps)
    5. Flow Rolling - Berimbolo Focus: Execute Berimbolos from De La Riva or Spider Guard during flow rolling, aiming for the back take. Focus on technique over completion. (90% resistance, 5 minutes)
    6. Live Rolling - Berimbolo Attempts: Actively seek opportunities to initiate the Berimbolo during live rolling. Accept failed attempts as learning opportunities. (100% resistance, 2 rounds)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is posturing up significantly from De La Riva or Spider Guard, creating a space to enter.
    • When your opponent attempts a guard pass that leaves their legs exposed and within reach of your hooks.
    • When you have established a strong De La Riva hook and have good control of their pants or knee.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • "Stacking" Pass: When the Berimbolo attempt begins, the defender can drive their weight forward and down, "stacking" the attacker's hips and preventing the rotation. This requires precise hip pressure and base.
    • "Bait and Switch" Guard Pass: The defender can pretend to be off-balanced, encouraging the Berimbolo, then quickly switch their base and drive their hips to the opposite side, turning into the attacker.
    • "Tuck and Roll" Defense: As the attacker swings their leg over, the defender can tuck their own leg in and roll with the attacker's momentum, ending up in a more dominant position or escaping.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my hip hurt when I try to Berimbolo from De La Riva guard?

    Hip pain during the Berimbolo often stems from trying to force the rotation with your upper body or legs instead of using a proper hip-heist. Ensure you are driving your hips to the side *before* swinging your leg over, and that your hooked leg is actively pulling your opponent's leg towards you. This creates a more efficient pivot and reduces strain on your hip flexors and labrum.

    Q: How can I successfully Berimbolo against a much bigger and heavier opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, the Berimbolo becomes about leverage and timing, not strength. Focus on using your legs to off-balance their weight *before* committing to the rotation. Secure a deep De La Riva hook and control their pants to prevent them from easily posturing up. Your hip-heist must be sharp and decisive, using their weight against them to initiate the spin.

    Q: When is the right time to initiate a Berimbolo, and what if my opponent anticipates it?

    The Berimbolo is best initiated when your opponent is actively posturing up or attempting to pass your guard, creating an opening. If they anticipate it, don't commit fully. Instead, use the momentum to transition to another sweep or guard. For example, if they stack, you can often use that pressure to transition to a Butterfly Guard sweep or even attempt a leg lock if they overcommit.

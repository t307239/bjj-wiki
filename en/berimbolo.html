Mastering the Berimbolo in BJJ: A White Belt Guide

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Purple Belt Requirements

The Berimbolo is a dynamic guard transition originating from open guard positions, aiming to secure a back-Mount position.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body awareness and attempting the move with excessive force rather than leverage.

Success hinges on understanding the hip's rotational mechanics and using your legs to control your opponent's base.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a De La Riva hook: Your right leg hooks behind your opponent's left leg, just above the knee. Your left leg is free.
    2. Grip opponent's right ankle with your right hand, and their left hip or belt with your left hand.
    3. Shift your weight onto your left hip, angling your torso towards your right.
    4. Drive your right knee towards the mat, under your opponent's right thigh, creating a "seatbelt" for your leg.
    5. Simultaneously, swing your hips forward and to your right, rotating your body.
    6. Use your left hand to pull your opponent's hip forward, aiding your rotation and preventing them from posturing up.
    7. As your hips clear, extend your right leg to secure the back, driving your right knee into their hip crease.
    8. Secure the "seatbelt" grip on their torso with your right arm and your far arm under their armpit.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly extending your knee while rotating: This can cause a valgus stress on your knee joint, potentially tearing the ACL or MCL.

    The correct movement involves bending your knee and tucking it under their thigh, not hyperextending.

    • Swinging your leg over the opponent's head without proper hip rotation: This can lead to your ankle or shin being trapped and twisted, risking ligament damage.

    Always ensure your hips are leading the rotation before your leg clears their head.

    • Relying on arm strength to pull opponent during rotation: This can strain your shoulder and elbow joints, especially if the opponent resists forcefully.

    Focus on using your hips and the opponent's momentum, using your arms for control and grip rather than brute force.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Rotation Drill: Practice rotating your hips on the mat, mimicking the Berimbolo hip movement. 10 reps per side.
    2. Static Grip and Leg Placement Drill: With a partner lying on their back, practice establishing the De La Riva hook and ankle grip, then execute the hip rotation without moving the partner. 5 reps per pair.
    3. Controlled Transition Drill (0% Resistance): Partner gently resists. Focus on the exact grip and hip movement, not on taking the back. 5 reps per side.
    4. Light Resistance Drill (25% Resistance): Partner offers light resistance. Focus on maintaining grips and executing the rotation with controlled power. 5 reps per side.
    5. Dynamic Transition Drill (50% Resistance): Partner actively tries to defend. Focus on the timing of your hip drive and grip changes. 5 reps per side.
    6. Live Rolling Application (75-100% Resistance): Attempt the Berimbolo in live rolling situations, prioritizing correct mechanics over immediate success. 3-5 attempts per roll.

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent has a strong De La Riva guard and you are trying to pass.
    • When your opponent is posturing up from your open guard, creating space.
    • When you have established a strong leg entanglement and are looking for a dominant transition.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Leg Posture: As the Berimbolo attacker initiates the rotation, immediately straighten your leg on the side of the hook, creating a strong frame and preventing the hook from being established.
    • Hip Escape and Base: If the Berimbolo attacker gets their hook, immediately shrimp your hips away and drive your knee to the mat on the side of the hook, creating a strong base and breaking the entanglement.
    • Grip Break and Framing: If the attacker secures the ankle grip, use your free hand to break their grip while simultaneously driving your elbow into their hip to create space and prevent them from completing the rotation.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my back hurt when I try the Berimbolo?

    Back pain during the Berimbolo often stems from initiating the rotation with your upper body or a 'hunching' motion instead of a fluid hip-driven movement. This puts excessive strain on your lumbar spine. Ensure you are driving your hips forward and rotating your torso as a unit, using your legs to scoop your opponent's leg and guide your body's rotation.

    Q: How can I use the Berimbolo against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on securing a deep De La Riva hook and a tight ankle grip. Use your hip rotation to create angles and disrupt their base, rather than trying to muscle them. Your goal is to get your leg *under* their base, using their weight against them to initiate the sweep and transition to their back.

    Q: The Berimbolo is not typically initiated directly from closed guard. It's a transition *from* an open guard. You would first need to break your opponent's posture and open your guard, then establish a De La Riva or similar open guard position before attempting the Berimbolo. Trying to force it from closed guard usually results in a loss of position.

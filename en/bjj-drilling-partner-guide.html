    Frequently Asked Questions

    How long does it take to learn Drilling Partner Guide?

    Most practitioners develop functional competency with Drilling Partner Guide within 3–6 months of consistent drilling. Mastery — the ability to execute reliably in live rolling against resisting opponents — typically takes 1–2 years.

    Is Drilling Partner Guide effective for beginners?

    Yes. Drilling Partner Guide is part of the core BJJ curriculum and taught at all belt levels. Beginners should focus on the fundamental mechanics and concepts before refining advanced entries.

    How often should I drill Drilling Partner Guide?

    3–5 times per week is ideal for rapid skill acquisition. Even 10 focused repetitions per session compounds over time — consistency matters more than volume.

    What positions connect to Drilling Partner Guide?

    BJJ is a linked system. Drilling Partner Guide flows naturally to and from related positions. Study transitions in both directions to build a complete positional game.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: I'm a brand new white belt and I'm struggling to find someone to drill BJJ techniques with outside of class, what's the best way to approach people without being awkward?

    After class, approach people you've rolled with and express your interest in practicing specific techniques you learned. Ask if they'd be open to a quick 10-15 minute session focusing on those movements before or after the next class. Frame it as a mutual learning opportunity to solidify techniques for both of you.

    Q: When I try to drill guard passing techniques with a partner, they seem to be able to just sprawl and push me off easily, how can I maintain pressure and avoid getting sprawled on when drilling?

    To maintain pressure during guard passing drills, keep your base low and your hips driving forward, using your chest and shoulders to apply consistent weight. When your partner sprawls, immediately drive your hips into their thighs and use your forearm to create a wedge between their hips and yours, preventing them from fully extending their legs and maintaining your forward momentum.

    Q: My drilling partner keeps resetting to the starting position too quickly when I make a mistake, how can I encourage them to let me work through the mistake and learn from it during drilling?

    Communicate before drilling that you'd like to explore the consequences of mistakes. Ask your partner to allow you to experience the resulting position for a few seconds, even if it's not ideal, so you can feel the breakdown in your mechanics and understand where the error occurred. This allows for a more organic learning process rather than an immediate reset.

    More Questions

    How do I find a good drilling partner in BJJ?

    Look for someone who is consistent, has a similar skill level or slightly above, and communicates well. Attend classes regularly and observe who is focused and respectful during drills.

    What if I don't have anyone my size to drill with?

    Focus on drilling techniques that don't rely heavily on size, like fundamental escapes or positional control. Communicate with your partner about adjusting pressure and intensity to make the drill effective for both of you.

    Is it okay to drill with people from other gyms?

    Yes, it can be very beneficial to drill with practitioners from different gyms to gain new perspectives and experience different styles. Always be respectful, ask permission, and be mindful of the host gym's rules.

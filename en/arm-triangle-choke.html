The Arm Triangle Choke is typically applied from dominant positions like side control or mount, aiming to constrict blood flow to the opponent's brain.

White belts often struggle by not understanding the proper pressure application and body positioning, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key mechanical insight is to use your body weight and hip pressure to drive your shoulder into the carotid artery while trapping their arm to prevent escape.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From Side Control: Establish a standard cross-face grip with your right hand on their left collar, thumb inside.
    2. Secure your opponent's left arm with your left arm, hooking their elbow with your forearm and gripping your own right bicep.
    3. Shift your hips to your left, creating a perpendicular angle to your opponent's body, ensuring your left shoulder is directly under their chin.
    4. Drive your chest into their neck, compressing the right carotid artery with your shoulder and their own arm.
    5. Walk your left foot up towards their head, increasing the tightness of the choke by isolating their head and arm.
    6. Squeeze your knees together and arch your back slightly, driving your hips forward to increase pressure.
    7. Maintain head control by tucking your chin and keeping your head tight against their shoulder, preventing them from creating space.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying the choke with your arm in front of their head: This allows them to easily pull their head out, leaving your shoulder vulnerable to their elbow and potentially straining your rotator cuff.
    • Trying to 'squeeze' with arm strength alone: This is inefficient and can lead to bicep or shoulder strain. Instead, focus on driving your hips forward and using your body weight.
    • Not securing their arm properly: If their arm is loose, they can post it on your face or push your head away, compromising the choke and risking neck injury.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice the body positioning and hip drive without a partner, focusing on the shoulder placement and hip angle (10 reps).
    2. Static partner drill: With a compliant partner in side control, practice the grip setup and body shift without any resistance (20 reps).
    3. Light resistance drill: Your partner offers minimal resistance as you execute the choke, focusing on maintaining control and position (15 reps).
    4. Moderate resistance drill: Your partner actively tries to defend, allowing you to practice applying pressure and adjusting your body mechanics (10 reps).
    5. Technical sparring (limited): Attempt the Arm Triangle Choke from side control with 50% resistance, focusing on proper execution and not forcing the submission.
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the Arm Triangle Choke into your rolling sessions with 75-90% resistance, aiming to secure the position and apply the choke effectively.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have established dominant side control and have a strong cross-face grip.
    • When your opponent attempts to bridge or shrimp away from your side control, creating an opening.
    • When your opponent tries to stack you from guard, allowing you to transition to side control and attack.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • "The Turtle" Defense: If they are in turtle position, you can often transition to a S-mount and apply the Arm Triangle from there.
    • "The Escape": If the choke is not tight, the opponent can create space by shrimping their hips away and bringing their free arm to defend.
    • "The Counter-Stack": If the attacker stacks too aggressively, the defender can attempt to belly-down and create space for an escape.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try the Arm Triangle Choke from side control?

    Your shoulder likely hurts because you are not properly trapping your opponent's arm. The correct technique involves hooking their elbow with your forearm and gripping your own bicep, creating a stable frame. This prevents their arm from pushing into your shoulder and allows your body weight to do the work, rather than relying on arm strength which can strain the joint.

    Q: How can I effectively apply the Arm Triangle Choke against a much larger and stronger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise body mechanics are crucial. Focus on getting your shoulder deep under their chin and driving your hips forward to compress their carotid artery. Ensure you have a tight grip on their arm by securing your own bicep; this prevents them from using their size to muscle out of the choke. Don't try to 'squeeze' them into submission; use your entire body weight to create the pressure.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from side control to attempt an Arm Triangle Choke?

    The optimal time is when your opponent attempts to create space or escape by bridging or shrimping. As they move, their arm will often naturally come across their body, creating the perfect opportunity to trap it. Simultaneously, shift your hips to perpendicular and drive your shoulder into their neck. This timing capitalizes on their defensive movement to set up your offensive attack.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try the Bow and Arrow choke in BJJ and how can I avoid it?

    Neck pain during the Bow and Arrow choke often stems from overextending your spine and using excessive neck pressure. To avoid this, focus on driving your hips into your opponent's back while simultaneously pulling their collar and sleeve towards your hip, creating a fulcrum with your torso to leverage their body and restrict blood flow through the carotid arteries, not directly crushing the trachea.

    Q: How can I effectively set up the Bow and Arrow choke against a much bigger and stronger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, prioritize isolating their arm and creating a strong grip on their collar with your choking arm. Then, drive your hips into their back and use your legs to hook their far hip, creating a stable base and pulling their body into your chest; this leverage allows you to control their weight distribution and apply the choke without needing brute strength.

    Q: What are the key body positioning adjustments I need to make to finish the Bow and Arrow choke with more power as a smaller BJJ practitioner?

    To maximize power as a smaller practitioner, ensure your choking shoulder is deep and tight against their neck, and your forearm is driving into their carotid artery. Simultaneously, extend your legs and drive your hips forward into their back, using your torso as a lever to create a strong pulling motion with your opposite arm on their collar, thereby maximizing the torque and pressure on their neck.

    More Questions

    How do I prevent my opponent from escaping the Bow and Arrow choke?

    To prevent escapes, maintain tight control of your opponent's collar and sleeve. Ensure your hips are heavy and your body is angled to prevent them from turning into you or creating space.

    What is the most common mistake people make with the Bow and Arrow choke?

    A common mistake is not getting the collar grip deep enough or not securing the sleeve grip properly. This allows the opponent to create space and relieve pressure, making the choke ineffective.

    Can the Bow and Arrow choke be applied from other positions besides the back?

    While primarily a back-taking submission, variations can be adapted from side control or mount with significant adjustments. However, the most effective and standard application is from the back control position.

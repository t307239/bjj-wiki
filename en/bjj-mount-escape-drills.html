    More Questions

    What is the main goal of mount escape drills?

    The primary objective is to teach students how to regain guard or get to a safer position when their opponent has achieved the dominant mount position. These drills emphasize fundamental movements and body positioning to break free.

    How do I prevent my opponent from getting the mount in the first place?

    While mount escapes are crucial, prevention is key. Focus on maintaining a strong guard, framing effectively, and understanding common sweep and submission setups that can disrupt your opponent's attempt to establish mount.

    What are the most common mistakes beginners make during mount escapes?

    A frequent error is panicking and flailing, which often leads to giving up more space or even getting submitted. Another common mistake is not using their hips effectively to create space and shrimp out of the dominant position.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: When I try to shrimp out of mount, my hips feel stuck and I can't create space, what am I doing wrong biomechanically?

    You're likely not creating enough space by driving your knees towards your chest and simultaneously pushing your hips away. Focus on actively flexing your hips to bring your knees in, then use your heels to drive your hips back, creating a wedge between you and your opponent.

    Q: How can I use my arms to help escape mount more effectively, instead of just my legs?

    Your arms should be used to frame against your opponent's hips or thighs, creating leverage to push them away. When you shrimp, actively push off their hips with your forearms while simultaneously driving your hips back, preventing them from collapsing your base.

    Q: I feel like my opponent's weight is crushing me when I try to do the Upa mount escape, what biomechanical adjustments can I make?

    The Upa escape requires you to drive your hips up and over your opponent's body. Ensure you are bridging by extending your spine and driving your hips vertically, not just pushing with your legs, and aim to get your hips underneath their center of gravity to initiate the roll.

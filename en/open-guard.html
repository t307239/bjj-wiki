Open Guard Fundamentals for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

Open Guard is a dynamic position where you break grips and create space with your legs, preventing your opponent from establishing a dominant top position like Side Control or mount.

Many white belts struggle by keeping their legs too close, creating easy openings for passes, or by being too passive and allowing pressure.

The core principle is using your legs as adjustable frames and levers to control distance and angle, creating opportunities for sweeps or submissions.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Start from a seated or supine position, opponent inside your guard.
    2. Secure grips: Left collar grip with your right hand, left sleeve grip with your left hand.
    3. Use your legs to push opponent's hips away, creating 6-12 inches of space.
    4. Immediately insert your right foot onto opponent's left hip crease, maintaining a strong frame.
    5. Insert your left foot onto opponent's right hip crease, creating a similar frame.
    6. Keep your knees slightly bent and your feet flexed, toes pointing towards the ceiling.
    7. Maintain a slight inward pressure with your knees and ankles to actively control their base.
    8. Your pelvis should be slightly tucked, creating a stable base on the mat.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Extending legs rigidly without control: This can lead to knee hyperextension, damaging the ACL or PCL ligaments, or causing patellar tendonitis. Always maintain a slight bend in the knee.
    • Allowing opponent to close the distance and pin legs: This compresses the hips and knees, risking meniscus tears or groin strains. Actively use your feet on the hips to create space.
    • Gripping opponent's gi too tightly without leg control: This makes you vulnerable to passes and can strain your own shoulder or elbow joints. Prioritize leg frames before committing to deep gi grips.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice opening your guard and placing feet on hips 20 times, focusing on smooth leg extension and retraction.
    2. Solo drill: Practice transitioning between open guard positions (e.g., feet on hips to spider guard grips) 20 times.
    3. Partner drill: With a compliant partner, practice establishing open guard from a static position 10 times, focusing on grip fighting and frame placement (0% resistance).
    4. Partner drill: Practice maintaining open guard against a partner trying to pass with minimal pressure, focusing on frame strength and space creation 10 times (25% resistance).
    5. Partner drill: Introduce light pressure passing attempts, focusing on controlling distance and switching frames 10 times (50% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Practice using open guard for defense and sweep opportunities for 5 minutes, focusing on applying learned principles (75-90% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When opponent attempts to close the distance and establish a strong base inside your guard.
    • When opponent is postured up, creating an opportunity to break their posture and control their base.
    • When you feel your closed guard is being effectively controlled or passed.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Spider Guard: Grip opponent's sleeves with your feet, controlling their arms and creating angles for sweeps or submissions. Maintain strong frames on the hips.
    • Lasso Guard: Wrap one leg around opponent's arm and grip their opposite sleeve, creating a strong control point for sweeps and attacks. Keep your base wide and hips mobile.
    • De La Riva Guard: Hook opponent's leg with your foot, allowing you to off-balance them and attack their base or legs. Maintain active grips on their upper body to control posture.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do Open Guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you're gripping the opponent's gi too tightly or for too long without sufficient leg control. The force of their posture or movement transfers to your wrists. Ensure your legs are actively creating frames on their hips to absorb and redirect force, and practice breaking your grips when not actively attacking or defending.

    Q: How do I use Open Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, emphasize creating maximum space with your legs. Use your feet on their hips to constantly push away and disrupt their base. Focus on controlling their posture with strong frames and collar grips, preventing them from driving forward and crushing you. Mobility is key; be ready to shrimp and reposition your hips.

    Q: When should I transition from Closed Guard to Open Guard in BJJ?

    You should transition to Open Guard when your opponent is successfully 'stacking' you in Closed Guard (driving their weight forward and upward, making it difficult to maintain control) or when they are posturing up effectively, giving you an opportunity to break their posture. It's also a good option if you feel your Closed Guard is about to be passed.

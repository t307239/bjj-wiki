    Frequently Asked Questions

    How do I prevent my opponent from passing my guard when they shoot for a takedown?

    The sprawl is your primary defense. As your opponent shoots, explosively extend your legs back and sprawl your hips down to create space and prevent them from getting their hips close to yours.

    What is the purpose of the front headlock after a sprawl?

    The front headlock allows you to control your opponent's upper body and head, limiting their ability to posture up or attempt to recover guard. It's a transitional position that sets up further control or submission opportunities.

    What are common mistakes beginners make when sprawling?

    A common mistake is not committing to the sprawl, leaving your hips too high or your legs too close, allowing the opponent to get underneath. Another mistake is not following up with control, leaving the opponent in a dangerous position.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why do I feel a sharp pain in my neck when I try to transition from a sprawl into a front headlock?

    This pain often stems from not driving your hips down and forward into your opponent's hips during the sprawl; instead, you're likely crunching your neck to try and secure the position. To avoid this, focus on sinking your hips low and wide, creating a stable base, and then using your shoulder and bicep to secure the head, not your neck.

    Q: How can I effectively execute the Sprawl to Front Headlock sequence when facing an opponent who is significantly larger and heavier than me?

    Against a larger opponent, prioritize using their weight against them by sprawling wide and low, forcing them to carry their mass over your hips. Once sprawled, drive your chest into their chest and secure the headlock by cupping their ear with your bicep and driving your shoulder into their jawline, creating leverage rather than relying on brute strength.

    Q: What is the correct body positioning to prevent my opponent from escaping the front headlock after I've successfully sprawled?

    After sprawling, maintain a strong base by keeping your hips heavy and your chest glued to your opponent's chest. Secure the headlock by actively driving your shoulder into their jaw and using your bicep to control their head, preventing them from posturing up or turning into you; this creates a crushing pressure that limits their mobility.

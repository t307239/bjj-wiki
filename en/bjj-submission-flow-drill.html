    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is submission flow drilling in BJJ?

    Submission flow drilling involves practicing a series of connected submissions in a fluid, continuous manner. Instead of drilling one submission repeatedly, you transition smoothly from one submission attempt to the next as your opponent defends, creating a chain of offensive options.

    How do I transition between submissions during flow drilling?

    Transitions are the key. Focus on maintaining positional control while your opponent defends a submission. Use their defensive movements to create openings for your next submission, always looking to improve your position or set up a different angle of attack.

    What are the benefits of submission flow drilling for a blue belt?

    For blue belts, submission flow drilling helps develop a better understanding of submission chains and how to react to defenses. It builds muscle memory for transitions and improves your ability to attack from multiple angles, making your submission game more dynamic and effective.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: When I'm practicing Submission Flow Drilling Method, why do I feel like I'm just going through the motions and not actually learning the submissions?

    This often happens when you're not actively engaging your core and hips to generate power and control. Ensure your hips are driving into your partner on submissions like armbars and triangles, and that your shoulders are driving forward to create leverage, rather than just passively moving your limbs.

    Q: How can I make Submission Flow Drilling Method effective for learning to defend against submissions, not just apply them?

    To focus on defense, reverse the drill's intention: your partner applies the submission, and you focus on creating space by driving your hips away and framing with your forearm into their neck or shoulder to break their posture. Then, use that created space to shrimp out or transition to a defensive position.

    Q: When performing Submission Flow Drilling Method from guard, how do I prevent my training partner from posturing up and making the submissions impossible?

    To prevent posturing, maintain a tight guard by squeezing your knees together and keeping your heels close to your glutes, actively pulling your partner's hips towards you. Simultaneously, use your arms to control their biceps or collar, preventing them from creating upward leverage.

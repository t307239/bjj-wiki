    More Questions

    What's the difference between an Anaconda choke and a D'Arce choke?

    While both are variations of the arm-triangle choke, the Anaconda choke typically starts from a position where you have one arm wrapped around the opponent's neck and the other arm securing your own bicep. The D'Arce choke usually involves securing the opponent's arm and neck simultaneously with both of your arms in a more connected grip.

    How do I prevent my opponent from defending the Anaconda choke?

    The key is to maintain tight control of their head and arm, preventing them from posturing up or creating space. Driving your shoulder into their neck and using your hips to maintain pressure are crucial for stifling their escape attempts.

    What are common mistakes beginners make with the Anaconda choke?

    A common mistake is not getting the shoulder deep enough into the opponent's neck, leading to a weak choke. Another is relying too much on squeezing with the arms rather than using body weight and hip pressure to finish the submission.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck sometimes feel strained or uncomfortable after attempting the Anaconda Choke in BJJ, and what specific body movements can I adjust to prevent this?

    Neck strain often occurs from over-extending your neck backward or allowing your opponent to create space by driving their head forward. To prevent this, keep your head tucked firmly into your bicep and use your chest and shoulders to drive forward, creating a tight seal and controlling their head's movement.

    Q: How can I effectively finish the Anaconda Choke against a significantly larger opponent who seems to shrug off my initial attempts to apply pressure?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on securing a deep grip and driving your shoulder into their carotid artery, not just their neck. Then, use your hips to bridge and elevate your body, creating leverage by pulling your opponent's trapped arm across their own body and sinking your chest deeper into their neck.

    Q: What are the most common mistakes white belts make when trying to set up the Anaconda Choke from guard, and how can I improve my hip positioning to make it more effective?

    A common mistake is not getting your arm deep enough under their chin and shoulder, leading to a loose grip. To improve, when you shoot for the choke, actively drive your shoulder and chest forward to create a tight seal, and then use your hips to shrimp out slightly and create the angle needed to drive your body into their neck for the finish.

The Biomechanically Accurate Scissor Sweep for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Scissor Sweep is initiated from Closed Guard, aiming to unbalance your opponent and transition to a superior position. It's a fundamental sweep for beginners.

Many white belts struggle due to a lack of understanding of leverage and weight distribution, often relying on brute strength which is ineffective and risky.

The key insight is using your legs as levers to create an angle and lift, combined with hip movement to break their base.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a "figure-four" grip on the opponent's upper back or shoulders, with your arms fully extended to create frames. Your left hand grips their right shoulder/collar, right hand grips their left shoulder/collar.
    2. Simultaneously, secure a "pant grip" on the outside of their opponent's right pant leg, just above the knee, with your left hand. This prevents them from posturing up.
    3. Bring your right knee across the opponent's chest, aiming to place it just below their left armpit. This creates a wedge and initiates the unbalancing.
    4. Extend your left leg, pushing the opponent's right knee towards the mat. This creates a fulcrum point.
    5. As your left leg extends, use your right leg (the one across their chest) to lift and drive their hips away from you. Your hip angle shifts to approximately 45 degrees relative to their body.
    6. Simultaneously, pull down on the pant grip with your left hand and drive your right knee forward into their chest/shoulder area. This directs their weight over their left shoulder.
    7. As their weight shifts, use your hips to shrimp (move your hips to your left) and elevate your opponent over your extended left leg, driving them to the mat.
    8. Immediately transition your legs from the sweep position to control their hips and begin to establish mount or side control.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Knee/Ankle Twist: Applying the sweep by "kicking" with the bottom leg and twisting the knee outwards can damage the ACL and MCL. The correct movement is a controlled extension and push, not a violent kick.
    • Hip/Lower Back Strain: Trying to lift the opponent solely with upper body pulling and an unaligned hip angle can strain the lumbar spine. Maintain a stable base with your hips tucked and aligned with your shoulders.
    • Wrist/Elbow Injury: Gripping too tightly with bent wrists or attempting to "muscle" the sweep with straight, unsupported arms can lead to wrist sprains or elbow hyperextension. Use full arm extensions and controlled leverage, not raw power.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Movement (0%): Practice the leg movements and hip shrimping motion without a partner. Focus on the sequence of bringing the knee across, extending the bottom leg, and hip elevation. (50 reps)
    2. Partnered Isolation (25%): With a compliant partner, practice establishing the grips and executing the leg and hip movements. Partner remains passive. (20 reps per side)
    3. Controlled Sweep (50%): Partner offers light resistance to posturing and hip movement. Focus on breaking their base and completing the sweep motion. (15 reps per side)
    4. Flow Roll - Sweep Focus (75%): In a controlled roll, focus exclusively on achieving closed guard and attempting the scissor sweep when the opportunity arises. Partner defends passively but intentionally. (5 attempts)
    5. Integrated Sweep (90%): Attempt the scissor sweep as part of a regular roll, but with a clear intention to execute it. Partner defends actively. (3 successful sweeps)
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Attempt the scissor sweep organically during a full sparring session. Focus on timing, grip fighting, and follow-up positions. (As opportunities arise)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up and has weight on their feet, creating a stable base.
    • When your opponent is attempting to pass your guard from the top, giving you an opportunity to counter.
    • When your opponent is controlling your legs too tightly, allowing you to use your grip fighting to create space.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass Defense: If the opponent attempts a stack pass, maintain your frame with extended arms and immediately bring your knees together, creating a solid base to resist the pressure. Then, use your hips to push their weight back.
    • Knee Slice Defense: If they try to knee slice, use your foot on their hip to create distance and bring your other knee between their legs. This resets your guard and prevents the pass.
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping) to Guard Recovery: If they successfully break your guard and flatten you out, immediately shrimp your hips away and work to bring your knees back between your legs to re-establish guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Scissor Sweep from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs because you're gripping too tightly with bent wrists, or trying to pull your opponent down with straight, unsupported arms. Instead, use full arm extensions to create a frame, and focus on driving your elbow towards your hip on the pant grip side. This utilizes your shoulder and core for leverage, not just your wrist.

    Q: How can I do the Scissor Sweep against a bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing become even more critical. Focus on breaking their posture by using your arms as levers against their shoulders. When you initiate the sweep, ensure your hip angle is sharp (around 45 degrees) and use your leg across their chest to create a strong lifting wedge. Don't try to overpower them; instead, use their weight against them by driving their hips over your elevated leg.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Scissor Sweep and what if they don't fall?

    The ideal time is when they are postured up and their weight is on their feet, creating a stable base you can disrupt. If they don't fall, do not persist with excessive force. Instead, immediately use the leg that was across their chest to create space and bring it back to your guard, or transition to a Hip Bump Sweep by pushing their knee. The key is to have a follow-up plan.

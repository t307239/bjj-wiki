The Biomechanically Precise Scissor Sweep for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Scissor Sweep is a fundamental sweep initiated from Closed Guard, aiming to invert your opponent's base and create an opportunity to move to a dominant position.

Many white belts struggle because they lack control of their opponent's posture and focus on leg power over precise body mechanics.

The key insight is using your hips to create leverage and your legs to control distance and angle, not brute force.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a strong closed guard. Secure both of your opponent's arms with your legs, hips slightly elevated off the mat.
    2. Grip your opponent's left collar with your right hand and their right sleeve (near the wrist) with your left hand.
    3. Pull your opponent's left arm across their body towards your right hip, creating a slight angle.
    4. Simultaneously, drive your right knee (same side as the collar grip) towards your opponent's left armpit, creating a frame.
    5. Untuck your left leg from under your opponent's right leg, keeping it slightly bent at the knee.
    6. Extend your right leg forcefully, pushing your opponent's left arm and shoulder away from your body, while simultaneously curling your left leg up towards your opponent's left hip.
    7. As your opponent falls, pivot your hips to the right, bringing your left leg over their torso to secure side control or mount.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Rushing the sweep by lifting your hips too high without proper grip control can lead to your opponent posturing up and potentially injuring your cervical spine by hyperextension if they stack you.
    • Forcing the sweep with straight legs instead of using hip drive can strain your knee ligaments (MCL/LCL) as your legs act as levers against your opponent's weight.
    • Grabbing the opponent's gi too loosely and attempting to pull them over without angling your body can result in your opponent escaping, leaving your ankles exposed to potential leg locks and causing wrist strain from improper gripping.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice the leg movements and hip escapes without a partner. Focus on the untucking and sweeping motion. (10 reps)
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the grips and the initial leg movement. Focus on establishing the angle. (10 reps)
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to the collar and sleeve grips. Focus on the hip drive and leg extension. (15 reps)
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner lightly resists the pull and tries to maintain posture. Focus on using your body to create the angle and sweep. (20 reps)
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner actively tries to defend the sweep by posturing up and resisting the leg movement. Focus on maintaining control and adjusting your body. (25 reps)
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Apply the Scissor Sweep during rolling. Focus on timing and execution against a resisting opponent. (5 minutes)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up in your closed guard, leaning forward.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard by stacking you or driving their weight forward.
    • When you have established control of both of your opponent's sleeves and have a strong collar grip.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Posture Up: If the sweep is initiated, immediately drive your hips down and post your feet on the mat, creating a strong base to prevent being swept.
    • Leg Escape: As your opponent attempts to swing their leg over, peel their sweeping leg off with your hands and step your leg back to create space.
    • Hip Escape & Re-Guard: If the sweep is failing, quickly hip escape to the side and re-establish your guard or transition to a different position.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Scissor Sweep from closed guard?

    Wrist pain during the Scissor Sweep often stems from a weak or incorrect grip. Instead of gripping the sleeve near the wrist with your entire hand, ensure you are using a deep sleeve grip, wrapping your fingers around the fabric and thumb on the outside. This transfers the pulling force to your forearm and shoulder, not just your wrist joint, preventing hyperextension or twisting.

    Q: How can I use the Scissor Sweep against a much bigger and stronger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing become paraMount. Focus on using your hips to create a strong angle, pulling their upper body down and to the side. Instead of trying to 'lift' them, think of 'tipping' them over by driving your knee into their armpit and extending your opposite leg to push their hip away. This allows you to use their size against them.

    Q: The Scissor Sweep is most effective when your opponent is leaning forward, trying to establish a strong posture or pass your guard. If they anticipate it, they will likely try to straighten their legs and base out. In this case, don't force the sweep. Instead, use the angle you've created to transition into another sweep, like a pendulum sweep, or secure a submission like a triangle choke.

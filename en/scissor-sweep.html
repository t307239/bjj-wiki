The Scissor Sweep is initiated from Closed Guard, aiming to invert your opponent and gain top position. It leverages leg mechanics to create an off-balancing moment.

White belts often struggle by trying to force the sweep with upper body strength, neglecting the crucial leg action. This leads to being stuck or even swept themselves.

The core principle is using your hip rotation and the precise angle of your legs to unbalance their base, creating an opening for you to transition to Mount.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grips: Establish a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (left opponent sleeve) with your left hand.
    2. Hip Angle: Dig your right hip into your opponent's left hip, creating a slight angle away from them.
    3. Leg Positioning: Bring your left shin across the front of their right thigh, ensuring your knee is pointing slightly towards their left hip.
    4. Right Leg Action: Swing your right leg over their right hip, placing your right foot on the mat behind their right shoulder.
    5. Hip Lift & Rotation: Simultaneously lift your hips off the mat and rotate your torso to your left, pulling their collar towards your left shoulder.
    6. Leg Drive: As you rotate, drive your left shin forward and upward, pushing their right thigh away from your body.
    7. Weight Transfer: Continue the hip rotation and leg drive to unbalance them over their right side, transferring your weight to their side as they fall.
    8. Transition: Immediately post your right hand on the mat for base and drive your hips forward to secure mount.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Knee Hyperextension: Attempting to push the opponent's leg away with a straight, locked knee can lead to hyperextension of your own knee joint, potentially tearing the ACL or MCL.
    • Correct Movement: Maintain a slight bend in your left knee as you drive it forward, using your quadriceps and hip flexors for power.
    • Ankle Sprain: When posting your right foot on the mat, if your ankle is not properly aligned or you land awkwardly, you can roll your ankle, causing a severe sprain.
    • Correct Movement: Land with your foot flat and toes pointing slightly forward, using the ball of your foot and heel for a stable base.
    • Lower Back Strain: Trying to lift your hips with a rounded back and excessive upper body pulling can strain your lumbar spine.
    • Correct Movement: Engage your core and use your glutes and hamstrings to lift your hips, keeping your back relatively flat.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Mobility Drill (0%): Practice the leg movements and hip rotation without a partner. Focus on the timing of bringing your shin across and swinging your leg over. 50 reps.
    2. Static Partner Drill (25%): Partner lies on their back in closed guard. You practice securing grips and initiating the hip angle. Partner offers minimal resistance. 20 reps each side.
    3. Controlled Leg Sweep (50%): Partner allows you to get the grips and hip angle. You execute the leg sweep motion, and they gently allow themselves to be swept. Focus on the leg drive and hip lift. 30 reps.
    4. Assisted Sweep (75%): Partner actively tries to maintain base but doesn't resist the sweep mechanics. You focus on the full execution, from grips to unbalancing. 40 reps.
    5. Live Rolling - Guard Retention Focus (90%): Practice the Scissor Sweep during live rolling, but your primary goal is to *attempt* the sweep. If it fails, immediately focus on regaining guard. 5 minutes.
    6. Full Live Rolling (100%): Integrate the Scissor Sweep into your regular rolling. Attempt it when the opportunity arises, focusing on clean execution and follow-through. 10 minutes.

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is posturing up, creating space between your hips and theirs.
    • When their weight is primarily on their heels, making them susceptible to off-balancing.
    • When you have established strong collar and sleeve grips, allowing you to control their posture.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass: If the opponent attempts the sweep, they will drive their hips into you. Instead of letting them sweep, drive your weight forward and down, stacking them by placing your chest on their head and shoulders. This neutralizes their hip movement.
    • Shrimping Away: As the opponent initiates the leg motion, quickly shrimp your hips away from the direction of the sweep. This creates space for you to potentially stand up or transition to a pass.
    • Leg Pummeling: If they get their shin across your thigh, actively pummel your leg back to create space and break their control. This can involve driving your knee forward and using your foot to push their shin away.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt after attempting the Scissor Sweep?

    Wrist pain often stems from gripping too tightly with your fingers only, or from an improper sleeve grip. When you pull on the sleeve, ensure your thumb is wrapped around it and your forearm is engaged, not just your wrist. Also, avoid 'death-gripping' the collar; use your whole hand and forearm to control their posture.

    Q: How can I effectively Scissor Sweep a much larger and heavier opponent in BJJ?

    Against a heavier opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on the precise angle of your hips to create a fulcrum, and use the rotation of your torso to generate the unbalancing force. Ensure your shin is firmly across their thigh, creating a strong wedge. Don't try to overpower them with strength; use their weight against them by timing your sweep perfectly when they are slightly off-balance.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Scissor Sweep, and what if my opponent anticipates it?

    The ideal time is when your opponent is posturing up and their weight is shifting forward, creating a moment of instability. If they anticipate it, they might try to stack you. In this case, be ready to transition your hips to a more defensive position, or use the stacking pressure to your advantage by driving your hips forward and attempting to pass or transition to mount as they commit to the stack.

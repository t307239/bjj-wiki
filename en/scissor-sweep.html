The Essential Scissor Sweep for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Scissor Sweep is initiated from Closed Guard, aiming to invert your opponent's base and transition to a superior position. It's a fundamental sweep for controlling your opponent's posture.

White belts often struggle by relying on upper body strength or an incorrect understanding of leverage, leading to wasted energy and ineffective attempts.

The core principle is creating a strong base with your legs and using your hips to generate the sweeping motion, not just pulling with your arms.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish 'Spider Guard' grips: Right hand grips opponent's left sleeve, left hand grips opponent's left collar. Maintain a firm, controlled grip.
    2. Secure opponent's left leg: Place your right foot on their left hip, maintaining knee-to-hip distance. Your left leg remains inside their guard.
    3. Hip angle and base: Angle your hips 45 degrees to your left. Your base is formed by your right foot on their hip and your left foot on the mat, keeping your knees slightly bent.
    4. Initiate the pull and push: Pull their left sleeve towards your right shoulder while simultaneously pushing their left hip away with your right foot.
    5. Leg action: As you pull and push, extend your left leg across their body, aiming to place your left foot on their right hip or thigh.
    6. The scissor motion: Simultaneously, bring your right leg (which was on their hip) across their body, aiming to hook their left ankle or shin. This creates the 'scissor' action.
    7. Hip elevation and rotation: Elevate your hips off the mat and rotate them to your right, using the scissor action to unbalance and lift their left leg.
    8. Transition: As they fall, drive your hips forward and bring your right leg over their body to establish side control, maintaining grips on their sleeve and collar.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect hip extension: Trying to kick the opponent's leg out with your hips can hyperextend your own lumbar spine, leading to lower back strain.
    • Correct movement: Focus on a controlled hip lift and rotation, using your legs to create the lever.
    • Over-reliance on arm pulls: Yanking the opponent's arm with excessive force without hip engagement can strain your shoulder joint (glenohumeral joint) or elbow (ulnar collateral ligament).
    • Correct movement: Use your grips to control posture and create angles, but the sweep power comes from your hips and legs.
    • Knee valgus stress: If your supporting leg's knee collapses inward (valgus) while sweeping, you risk tearing the medial collateral ligament (MCL) of your knee.
    • Correct movement: Keep your supporting knee tracking over your toes, maintaining a stable base.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling (0%): Practice the grip setup, hip angle, and leg extension/hooking motions without a partner. Focus on the feeling of the scissor action. (20 reps)
    2. Partner drills - no resistance (25%): With a compliant partner, execute the entire technique slowly, focusing on grip accuracy and hip movement. Partner offers minimal resistance. (10 reps)
    3. Partner drills - light resistance (50%): Partner begins to resist slightly by trying to posture up or keep their base. Focus on maintaining control and executing the sweep. (15 reps)
    4. Partner drills - moderate resistance (75%): Partner actively defends by trying to prevent your grips or stack you. Work on adapting your grips and hip angle. (20 reps)
    5. Live rolling - controlled (90%): Attempt the Scissor Sweep during positional sparring. Focus on timing and recognizing opportunities, even if the sweep isn't successful. (5 attempts per round)
    6. Live rolling - full intensity (100%): Integrate the Scissor Sweep naturally into your rolling. Focus on fluid execution and follow-through to a dominant position. (As opportunities arise)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • Opponent posts their foot on your hip, trying to break your closed guard.
    • Opponent is posturing up with their chest close to your face, creating a tall base.
    • Opponent's weight is distributed unevenly, with one side lower than the other.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass Defense: If the opponent attempts to stack pass, immediately drive your hips forward and to the side, creating space to re-guard or transition to a technical stand-up.
    • Hip Escape to Open Guard: If they defend the sweep by flattening their hips, hip escape to create space and transition to an open guard like spider or de la riva guard to reset.
    • Leg Pummeling: If they try to 'pummel' their leg inside yours to prevent the sweep, use your free leg to pummel back in, maintaining control and blocking their escape.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Scissor Sweep from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often stems from gripping too tightly with your fingers only, or pulling with excessive ulnar deviation. Ensure you are gripping with your entire hand and forearm, and that the pulling motion originates from your shoulder and torso, not just your wrist. Keep your wrist in a neutral, slightly extended position for better joint health.

    Q: How can I effectively do the Scissor Sweep against a much bigger and heavier opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on precision and leverage over brute strength. Secure deep collar and sleeve grips to control their posture. Use your hips to create a strong base and angle, then drive your legs to create the scissor action. Elevating your hips and rotating them is key to unbalancing their superior weight.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Scissor Sweep, and what if they just flatten me out?

    Attempt the Scissor Sweep when the opponent is posturing up or has an uneven base. If they flatten you out, don't force the sweep. Instead, use the opportunity to Hip Escape and re-establish your guard or transition to a different sweep. Maintaining your guard is the priority before attempting to sweep.

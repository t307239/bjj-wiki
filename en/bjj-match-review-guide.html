    Muscling through setups creates bad habits and fails against stronger or more skilled opponents. Focus on leverage and angles.

    More Questions

    How often should I review my BJJ matches?

    Ideally, review matches shortly after they happen while the details are fresh. Aim for at least once a week, especially if you're competing or have significant training sessions. Consistent review is key to identifying patterns and areas for improvement.

    What are the most important things to look for during match analysis?

    Focus on your successes and failures. Analyze your transitions, submission attempts, defensive strategies, and reaction to your opponent's actions. Pay close attention to moments where you felt lost or were outplayed, as these are prime learning opportunities.

    How can I use match analysis to improve my BJJ game?

    Identify recurring mistakes and develop specific drills to address them. Recognize successful strategies and consciously try to implement them more often. Discuss your findings with your coach or training partners for additional insights and guidance.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: When reviewing my BJJ matches, I notice I always get stuck in side control and can't escape, what specific body mechanics should I focus on to improve my defense?

    To escape side control, focus on creating frames with your forearms, driving your elbow into your opponent's hip and your wrist into their shoulder, to create space. Then, bridge by driving your hips into the mat and simultaneously shrimping your hips away to create an angle, allowing you to bring your knee inside to establish guard.

    Q: During BJJ match review, I see myself giving up my back easily when trying to scramble. What biomechanical adjustments can I make to better protect my back?

    When scrambling, prioritize keeping your hips low and tight to the mat, creating a 'seatbelt' grip with your arms around your opponent's waist or hips to prevent them from getting hooks. Actively drive your chest into their chest and use your legs to push off their hips, creating a barrier and maintaining your base.

    Q: In my BJJ match analysis, I'm consistently swept from guard without understanding how. What specific hip and leg mechanics should I focus on to prevent being swept?

    To prevent sweeps from guard, keep your knees tight to your chest and your feet on your opponent's hips, maintaining a strong 'L' shape with your legs. Actively use your hips to 'shuck' or push your opponent's weight away, and be prepared to hip escape laterally to break their balance and re-establish your guard.

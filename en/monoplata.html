Monoplata BJJ: A White Belt's Biomechanically Precise Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Monoplata is an arm entanglement submission, typically initiated from the Closed Guard or open guard, aiming to isolate and hyperextend the opponent's shoulder.

White belts often struggle by trying to force the submission or neglecting base and hip control, leading to escapes or injury.

The key is using your hip and leg to create a fulcrum, forcing the opponent's arm into a compromising angle for the lock.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From closed guard, secure a collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip on the opponent's left arm with your left hand.
    2. Simultaneously, break the opponent's posture by pulling them down and forward, driving your right knee towards their left armpit.
    3. As you drive your right knee, extend your left leg, placing the outside of your left shin across the opponent's upper back, just below their neck.
    4. Your right leg then swings around, hooking behind the opponent's left elbow, with your right shin parallel to the mat and your right foot pointing towards their head.
    5. Your left shin should now be across their upper back, and your right shin behind their elbow, forming a figure-four grip with your legs.
    6. Rotate your hips counter-clockwise, creating a strong base and driving your hips forward and slightly up, away from the opponent's head.
    7. Maintain your collar grip and pull down, while simultaneously using your right leg to pull the opponent's arm across their body, towards your opposite hip.
    8. Your left hand now releases the sleeve and secures your right shin, creating a solid frame. Drive your hips forward to finish the shoulder lock.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Arm hyperextension risk: A white belt might try to 'crank' the arm by forcefully rotating their hips without proper leg positioning. This can cause anterior capsule tears or rotator cuff injuries in the opponent's shoulder.
    • Correct: Ensure your right shin is behind the opponent's elbow and your hips are driving forward, using your body weight to create the angle, not raw force.
    • Knee/Ankle Strain: A white belt might 'fall' into the Monoplata, letting their own legs collapse or twist awkwardly. This can lead to medial collateral ligament (MCL) sprains in the knee or ankle sprains.
    • Correct: Maintain a strong base, keep your core engaged, and ensure your shins are firmly planted in the trapping positions before committing your hips.
    • Neck/Spine Injury: Attempting to force the Monoplata by pulling the opponent's head down while their arm is trapped can lead to awkward neck strain for both parties.
    • Correct: Focus on controlling the opponent's arm and upper body with your legs and hips; the head control is secondary and should be a natural consequence of hip movement, not a primary action.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Leg Drill (0% resistance): Practice the leg entanglement sequence (steps 2-5) repeatedly without a partner. Focus on smooth transitions and correct shin placement. 20 reps.
    2. Partnered Isolation (25% resistance): With a compliant partner lying on their back, practice steps 2-6. The partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to focus on hip and leg mechanics. 10 reps per side.
    3. Controlled Setup (50% resistance): Start from closed guard. Your partner allows you to break their posture and begin the Monoplata setup. They will resist the initial leg over but not the submission attempt. 10 reps per side.
    4. Full Setup, No Submission (75% resistance): Your partner allows the full Monoplata entanglement. They will defend the submission by posturing up or tucking their chin, but you do not apply submission pressure. Focus on maintaining the entanglement. 10 reps per side.
    5. Partial Submission (90% resistance): Your partner attempts to defend the submission minimally. Apply gentle submission pressure, and they will tap. This builds awareness of the submission mechanics. 5 reps per side.
    6. Live Rolling (100% resistance): Attempt the Monoplata during live rolling. Prioritize setting it up safely and understanding when to commit, rather than forcing it. Focus on one successful attempt per rolling session.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured down in your closed guard, presenting their arm across your body.
    • When the opponent attempts a guard pass that leaves an arm exposed and accessible to your legs.
    • When you have successfully controlled one of the opponent's arms and can isolate it from their base.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The "Stand Up" Defense: If the opponent initiates the Monoplata, they must commit their hips. The defender can immediately stand up, driving their weight forward and lifting their trapped arm out by straightening it and shrugging their shoulder upwards.
    • The "Roll Through" Defense: As the opponent swings their leg over, the defender can roll their hips in the same direction as the trapped arm, effectively bringing their body to the top of the opponent's trapped arm and potentially escaping or transitioning to a sweep.
    • The "Stack Pass" Defense: The defender can attempt to "stack" their weight onto the opponent by driving their chest into the opponent's shoulders and hips, flattening the opponent out. This disrupts the opponent's hip angle and can break the entanglement.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Monoplata?

    Your wrist is likely hurting because you are trying to finish the Monoplata submission by pulling the opponent's arm with your hands alone, rather than using your hips and body weight. The Monoplata is a shoulder lock, not a Wrist Lock. Ensure your right shin is securely behind the opponent's elbow and drive your hips forward and up. Your hands should be securing your shin and controlling the opponent's upper body to maintain the angle, not forcing the rotation.

    Q: How do I Monoplata against a much bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are even more critical. Focus on breaking their posture down effectively to create the angle for your legs. Use your hips to create a strong fulcrum; don't try to muscle the arm. Ensure your shin is deeply hooked behind their elbow. You might need to adjust your hip angle slightly more towards their head to create a tighter lock, emphasizing precision over brute force.

    Q: The ideal time to transition to a Monoplata from closed guard is when the opponent lowers their posture significantly, bringing their arm across your body in an attempt to pass or control your guard. This often happens when they are trying to "stack" you or drive their weight down. Recognizing this opportunity to trap their arm and begin the hip rotation is key to a successful setup.

Monoplata Technique: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide to BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Monoplata is a shoulder lock initiated from guard, typically Closed Guard. It achieves a submission by isolating and hyperextending the opponent's arm. White belts often struggle due to improper weight distribution and grip fighting.

The key insight is using your hips to create leverage and isolate the arm, not just pulling. Think of it as a controlled rotation of your body.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From closed guard, establish a cross-collar grip with your left hand on the opponent's right collar. Your right hand secures their right wrist.
    2. Slice your left leg (your right leg if attacking the other side) between their arms, aiming to get your shin across their upper chest, perpendicular to their spine.
    3. Simultaneously, extend your hips forward and slightly to your left, creating space and preventing them from posturing up.
    4. Release the wrist grip and secure your own left shin with your left hand (forming a "figure four" grip around their arm).
    5. Drive your hips forward and down, lowering your base. Your left knee should point towards the ceiling and your right knee towards the floor.
    6. Rotate your torso counter-clockwise, keeping your hips driving forward. This isolates their shoulder joint.
    7. Extend your hips further and slightly lift your chest to apply pressure to their shoulder. Maintain the figure-four grip.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly lifting your hips while the opponent is postured up can strain your lower back ligaments by hyperextension. Always drive hips forward and down first.
    • Attempting to "pull" their arm into the lock without proper hip and body rotation can sprain the opponent's elbow or shoulder ligaments. Maintain control of their upper body with your shins and hips.
    • Letting your own leg fall to the side instead of maintaining the shin-across-chest angle allows the opponent to escape easily and can twist your own knee if they bridge improperly. Keep your shin perpendicular to their spine.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg slice, hip drive, and figure-four grip without a partner. Focus on smooth transitions. (10 reps each side)
    2. Static drilling with a compliant partner: Partner lies on their back, you perform the Monoplata setup and finish without resistance. Focus on grip and body positioning. (5 reps each side)
    3. Resisted grip fighting drill: Partner resists your initial collar and wrist grips, but does not actively defend the lock. (20 reps)
    4. Controlled submission drill: Partner allows you to achieve the Monoplata position and taps immediately upon your hip drive and chest extension. (30 reps)
    5. Live rolling - light resistance: Practice the Monoplata setup from closed guard against a partner who offers mild resistance to your entry. (3 rounds x 2 minutes)
    6. Live rolling - full resistance: Attempt the Monoplata during live rolling with full intention and resistance from your opponent. (5 rounds x 3 minutes)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is postured up in your closed guard, creating space between your hips and theirs.
    • Opponent is posturing down, attempting to pass your guard from a close distance.
    • During transitions where you have isolated one of their arms while in guard.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • "The Stack": If the attacker's hips are not driving forward effectively, the defender can stack forward, driving their hips into the attacker's chest, relieving pressure and potentially escaping.
    • "The Leg Slice Escape": If the attacker fails to secure their figure-four grip, the defender can drive their trapped leg forward and attempt to slice it out, often transitioning to a guard pass.
    • "The Roll-Under": If the attacker is slow to rotate, the defender can attempt to roll their trapped arm towards the attacker's hips and roll their body underneath the attacker's weight to escape.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to do the Monoplata from closed guard?

    Your shoulder likely hurts because you are trying to force the lock using only arm strength. The Monoplata is a hip-driven technique. When you fail to drive your hips forward and create the correct angle, you end up pulling their arm into an unnatural position, stressing your own shoulder joint. Ensure your hips are actively pushing forward and down to isolate their shoulder, not just yanking their arm.

    Q: How can I successfully set up a Monoplata against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, hip mobility and precise timing are crucial for the Monoplata. Focus on using your legs to create the initial space by extending your hips, preventing them from crushing you. Secure your figure-four grip *before* attempting to drive your hips forward. The larger opponent's weight can actually work for you if you use their mass to help rotate their arm into the lock, rather than fighting their strength directly.

    Q: When should I abandon a Monoplata attempt and what are the signs it's not working?

    You should abandon a Monoplata if the opponent successfully stacks their weight into your chest, making it impossible to drive your hips forward. Another sign is if they manage to pull their arm out of your figure-four grip. If you find yourself off-balancing and unable to maintain control of their arm and upper body, it's better to disengage and reset to avoid giving up a dominant position or getting swept.

    Common Mistakes in Pressure Vs Movement

    Rushing the Setup

    Attempting to finish before proper mechanics are in place results in failed attempts and positional loss. Prioritize position before submission.

    Using Strength Over Technique

    Muscling through setups creates bad habits and fails against stronger or more skilled opponents. Focus on leverage and angles.

    Skipping Drilling

    Techniques only become available in live rolling after extensive drilling. Regular repetition builds the muscle memory needed for execution under pressure.

    Ignoring Defensive Reactions

    Every technique has common counters. Learn the most frequent defensive reactions and have follow-up attacks ready.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: When I'm trying to apply pressure in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, why does my opponent often just move away easily and how can I stop them from escaping my control?

    Your opponent escapes because your pressure isn't anchored. To counter this, ensure your base is wide and your hips are heavy, driving your weight directly into their center of mass through your shoulders and chest, not just pushing with your arms. Maintain contact points, like your shoulder on their neck or your knee on their hip, to prevent them from creating space to move.

    Q: How can I use movement effectively in BJJ to create openings for submissions or sweeps when my opponent is just smothering me with pressure?

    To overcome static pressure, use hip escapes and shrimping to create space and change your angle of attack. Shift your weight explosively to one side while simultaneously extending the opposite hip, forcing your opponent to readjust their base and potentially overcommit, creating an opening for you to transition or attack.

    Q: What's the best way to combine pressure and movement in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu to control a bigger, stronger opponent who seems to just absorb my pressure?

    Against larger opponents, use sharp, explosive movements to break their posture and create momentary imbalances, then immediately apply heavy pressure to capitalize on that opening. For example, a quick hip bump sweep followed by driving your shoulder into their chest as they try to recover will utilize their size against them by making their recovery more difficult.

Harai Goshi: A White Belt's Guide to the Sweeping Hip Throw

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

Harai Goshi is a forward-moving hip throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to sweep your opponent's leg and off-balance them to the mat.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength or poor body positioning, leading to missed opportunities and potential injury.

The key mechanical insight is creating a stable base and using your hips as a fulcrum, rotating your body to generate the sweeping force.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a right-side Kuzushi grip: Right hand grips opponent's left lapel (near collar bone), left hand grips opponent's right sleeve (near elbow).
    2. Step your left foot forward, slightly diagonal to opponent's right foot, creating a base wider than shoulder-width.
    3. Turn your hips to face opponent's right side, placing your right hip firmly against their solar plexus area.
    4. Bend your knees deeply, sinking your weight and lowering your center of gravity below opponent's.
    5. Bring your right leg across opponent's right leg, positioning your right thigh behind their right knee joint.
    6. Initiate the sweep by lifting your right leg forcefully upwards and outwards, simultaneously pulling with your lapel grip and pushing with your sleeve grip.
    7. Pivot on your left foot, rotating your body 180 degrees counter-clockwise while maintaining hip contact and pulling opponent forward and over your sweeping leg.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to lift opponent with your arms alone: This can strain your biceps and triceps, potentially causing muscle tears, as your arms are not designed for lifting significant body weight.

    Instead, focus on hip contact and leg sweep to generate momentum.

    • Stepping directly in front of opponent without turning hips: This exposes your knees to hyperextension if opponent resists, leading to ACL or MCL tears.

    Always turn your hips to create a stable base and proper throwing angle.

    • Rushing the entry without proper Kuzushi (off-balancing): This results in a weak throw and leaves you vulnerable to counters, potentially causing ankle sprains if you stumble.

    Focus on breaking opponent's posture and balance before committing to the throw.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo practice of hip rotation and leg sweep motion (no partner): 20 repetitions.
    2. Mirror drilling with a compliant partner, focusing on grip and body turn (0% resistance):
    • Step 1-7 execution, 10 repetitions per side.
    1. Soft entry drill with partner, focusing on Kuzushi and hip contact (25% resistance):
    • Attempt Harai Goshi, partner offers minimal resistance to the throw.
    • 5 repetitions per side.
    1. Controlled entry drill with partner, focusing on sweep and rotation (50% resistance):
    • Partner attempts to resist the sweep slightly.
    • 5 repetitions per side.
    1. Live grappling with focus on takedown attempts (75% resistance):
    • Harai Goshi attempts are allowed, partner can defend.
    • 3 minutes, focus on recognizing opportunities.
    1. Live grappling (100% resistance):
    • Harai Goshi is a valid option, full resistance from opponent.
    • 5 minutes, attempt to integrate into rolling.

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • Opponent has a wide, stable stance and is leaning forward slightly.
    • During a clinch where you have established control of their sleeves and collar.
    • When opponent attempts a forward-moving attack that you can redirect.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Uchi Mata (Inner Thigh Throw): If opponent attempts Harai Goshi, step your left foot slightly back and pivot your hips to the outside, using your right leg to block their sweeping leg and drive them forward.
    • Osoto Gari (Major Outer Reaping): As opponent commits to their hip turn, step your left foot back and drive your right leg across their body, reaping their right leg outwards and backwards.
    • Evasion and Counter-Grip: Immediately absorb the pressure into your hips and step your right foot back, breaking your hip contact and creating space to transition to a guard position or escape.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my back hurt when I try to do Harai Goshi?

    Your back pain likely stems from rounding your lumbar spine during the hip turn. Instead of bending your knees and rotating your hips from a stable, neutral spine, you're likely arching or rounding excessively. Focus on keeping your core engaged and initiating the hip rotation by bending your knees and driving your hips forward, rather than hinging at the waist.

    Q: How do I perform Harai Goshi effectively against a much larger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, you must prioritize Kuzushi (off-balancing) and leverage over brute strength. Focus on breaking their posture down and creating an angle with your grip fighting. When you enter, sink your hips much lower, making your base wide and stable, and use your entire body's rotation to sweep their leg, rather than trying to lift them.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt Harai Goshi from the clinch in BJJ?

    The optimal time to attempt Harai Goshi from the clinch is when your opponent has a slightly forward lean and a stable, but not overly rigid, base. Look for moments where they are moving forward or have just committed to a step, as this creates an opening to disrupt their balance. Avoid attempting it when they are completely static and rooted, or when they are actively resisting with a strong base.

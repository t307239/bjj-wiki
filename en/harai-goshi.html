Harai Goshi is a dynamic hip throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to unbalance your opponent and bring them to the mat.

White belts often struggle by attempting to muscle the throw instead of using proper leverage and body rotation.

The key mechanical insight is to use your hip as a fulcrum and your body's rotation to sweep their leg out, not lift them.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a strong right collar grip (thumb-in) and a left sleeve grip (forearm control).
    2. Step your right foot forward, slightly offset to your opponent's left side, creating a 45-degree angle between your bodies.
    3. Turn your hips inward to face your opponent's left side, bringing your right hip into contact with their lower abdomen.
    4. Simultaneously, step your left foot back and slightly to the side, widening your base for stability.
    5. Drive your right hip upwards into their torso while pulling their left sleeve towards your right hip.
    6. Rotate your torso counter-clockwise, using your opponent's sleeve to guide their movement across your hip.
    7. As you complete the rotation, sweep their left leg outwards and upwards with your right leg, extending it behind their ankle.
    8. Maintain downward pressure with your arms and body as they fall, ensuring they land safely on their back.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly attempting to lift your opponent with your arms can strain your lower back ligaments by hyperextension.

    The correct movement involves driving your hip into their core and using rotation, not pure lifting.

    • Swinging your leg too high without proper hip contact can lead to knee hyperextension or ligament tears.

    Ensure your hip is securely in place and sweep their leg with controlled extension from the hip.

    • Gripping too tightly with your hands and not engaging your core for rotation can cause wrist and elbow joint strain.

    Focus on using your body's rotational power and a secure sleeve grip, not just finger strength.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the footwork, hip turn, and arm movement without a partner (10 repetitions each side).
    2. Partner drilling (static): With a compliant partner, practice the grips and initial body positioning (20 repetitions each side, 10% resistance).
    3. Partner drilling (controlled movement): Partner takes a few steps while you execute the throw with minimal resistance (30 repetitions each side, 25% resistance).
    4. Partner drilling (dynamic): Partner attempts to resist slightly, focusing on the sweep and rotation (40 repetitions each side, 50% resistance).
    5. Live rolling (low intensity): Attempt Harai Goshi during sparring, focusing on timing and execution without immediate counter-attempts (5 minutes, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling (full intensity): Integrate Harai Goshi into regular sparring, accepting counters and learning from the experience (10 minutes, 90% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is leaning forward slightly, creating an opening for your hip.
    • When you have a secure collar and sleeve grip, allowing you to control their posture.
    • When you have established a strong connection and are able to turn your hips into them.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Leg Catch: If your opponent attempts Harai Goshi, immediately drop your weight and catch their sweeping leg with your hands on their ankle or calf, preventing the sweep.
    • Forward Roll (Ukemi): If you feel the sweep initiating, execute a forward roll to escape the throw and land on your feet or knees.
    • Posture Up and Grip Break: While they turn, forcefully straighten your posture and attempt to break their sleeve grip to disrupt the throwing motion.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do Harai Goshi in BJJ?

    Wrist pain during Harai Goshi often stems from gripping too tightly with only your fingers and not engaging your forearm and upper body rotation. When you pull the sleeve, your wrist is absorbing all the force. Instead, focus on a secure grip with your thumb inside the sleeve, and use your entire body's rotation to drive the throw, allowing your larger muscle groups to manage the force, not just your wrist.

    Q: How can I effectively use Harai Goshi against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paraMount. Focus on getting your hip *underneath* their center of gravity by stepping in deep and turning your hips sharply. Use their forward momentum against them by initiating the throw as they step forward. Your goal is to sweep their leg with your leg, not to lift their weight, using your hip as the fulcrum.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt Harai Goshi from a standing clinch in BJJ?

    The optimal time to attempt Harai Goshi is when your opponent is off-balance and leaning slightly forward, or when they are attempting their own entry. Look for moments when their weight is shifting onto one leg, making it easier to sweep. A strong, committed grip and a decisive hip engagement are crucial for success, rather than trying to force it when they are stable.

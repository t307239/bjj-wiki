Harai Goshi: The Sweeping Hip Throw for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

Harai Goshi is a dynamic hip throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to off-balance and sweep your opponent to the mat.

White belts often struggle by trying to muscle the throw, ignoring the crucial hip and footwork connection.

The key is to use your hips as a pivot point and your sweeping leg to unbalance their base, not brute force.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand, knuckles facing upwards, and a belt grip with your left hand at their right hip.
    2. Step your right foot forward, angling your body 45 degrees to your opponent's left, bringing your feet to a shoulder-width stance.
    3. Drive your hips forward and slightly to the right, creating a stable fulcrum point against their torso.
    4. Simultaneously, pivot on the ball of your right foot, turning your hips inward and bringing your left leg up and across their right thigh.
    5. Sweep your left leg in a wide arc from back to front, aiming to make contact with the back of their thigh or calf.
    6. As your left leg sweeps, pull their collar down and across your body with your right hand, while your left hand guides their hip, driving their weight onto your hips.
    7. Continue the hip pivot and leg sweep, transferring your weight forward and down, completing the throw as they fall over your hips.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Trying to lift the opponent with your arms instead of using hip rotation: This can lead to lower back hyperextension, damaging the lumbar spine ligaments.
    • Stepping too close or too far with the initial right foot: This compromises your base and hip contact, leading to awkward leverage and potential knee valgus (knees collapsing inward) when attempting the sweep, risking ACL tears.
    • Straightening the supporting (right) leg during the hip pivot: This can cause knee hyperextension and strain on the meniscus, especially if the opponent resists strongly.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling: Perform the entire Harai Goshi motion without a partner, focusing on hip rotation and leg sweep arc. (50 reps, 0% resistance).
    2. Static Partner Mirroring: Stand in front of a compliant partner, mirroring their stance. Practice the grip, stance, hip drive, and leg sweep motion, with the partner offering minimal resistance. (30 reps per side, 10% resistance).
    3. Controlled Entry and Sweep: With a partner offering light resistance, practice the entry, hip contact, and leg sweep. The partner stands firm but allows the sweep to happen. (20 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Partial Resistance Sweep: Partner offers slight resistance to the hip drive and sweep. Focus on maintaining your base and driving through the motion. (20 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Full Resistance Entry and Sweep: Partner actively resists the entry and sweep. Focus on timing and power generation from the hips. (15 reps per side, 75% resistance).
    6. Live Takedown Attempts: In a controlled rolling scenario, attempt Harai Goshi when the opportunity arises, focusing on proper execution and awareness of partner's reactions. (5-10 attempts per round, 90% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up in your closed guard, creating space.
    • During a standing clinch when you have strong collar and belt grips and can control their upper body.
    • When your opponent attempts a forward-moving attack, such as a snap-down or a forward step, creating an opportunity to counter.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape and Base: If the opponent initiates the Harai Goshi entry, immediately hip escape to the side and widen your base to break their hip connection. This prevents their hips from becoming the pivot point.
    • Foot Block and Push: As they bring their leg up to sweep, use your corresponding leg to block their sweeping leg and simultaneously push their hip away with your hands to disrupt their balance.
    • Inside Leg Trip Counter: As they attempt the sweep, if you can maintain balance, step your inside leg slightly back and around their sweeping leg, then use your hips to drive forward, potentially leading to an Osoto Gari or a similar counter-throw.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do Harai Goshi in BJJ?

    Wrist pain in Harai Goshi often stems from an improper collar grip. If you're gripping too tightly on the fabric itself rather than using your knuckles to dig into the collarbone area, you're essentially fighting the opponent's strength with your wrist's weak point. Ensure your grip is firm but focused on leverage from your forearm and shoulder, not just finger strength.

    Q: How can I use Harai Goshi effectively against a much bigger and stronger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, brute force is your enemy. The key is to leverage their own weight and momentum against them. Focus intensely on the hip-to-hip connection; your hips must be firmly planted against their torso to act as a pivot. Your leg sweep must be precise and timed to their forward weight shift, effectively unbalancing them rather than trying to lift them.

    Q: I'm getting stuck trying to sweep with my leg in Harai Goshi, what am I doing wrong?

    The sweeping leg in Harai Goshi isn't meant to lift the opponent. It's a lever to unbalance them. If you're struggling, you're likely not generating enough hip rotation or forward drive. Ensure your supporting leg is stable and your hips are driving forward and inward *before* you initiate the leg sweep. The leg sweep should feel like an extension of your hip's pivot.

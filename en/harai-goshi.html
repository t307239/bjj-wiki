Harai Goshi: The Sweeping Hip Throw for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

Harai Goshi is a dynamic hip throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to unbalance your opponent and sweep their leg out.

Many white belts attempt this by simply muscling the throw, leading to inefficiency and potential injury.

The core insight is using your hips as a fulcrum and generating rotational force through your core, not just arm strength.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grips: Establish a standard right-hand Kuzushi grip (collar grip, knuckles pointing towards opponent's neck) and a left-hand Hikite grip (sleeve grip at the elbow or forearm).
    2. Stance and Entry: Adopt a staggered stance, left foot slightly forward. Step your right foot across your opponent's center line, aiming to place your right hip level with their left hip.
    3. Hip Placement: Rotate your hips inward (counter-clockwise for a right-sided throw) so your right hip contacts their left hip bone, creating a strong pivot point.
    4. Kuzushi (Unbalancing): Simultaneously pull their collar grip upwards and towards your right shoulder while pushing their sleeve grip down and slightly forward, breaking their posture.
    5. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight from your back leg (left) onto your right hip as you initiate the throw.
    6. Sweeping Action: As your hips make contact, drive your right leg up and across the front of your opponent's left leg, sweeping it outwards and upwards.
    7. Completion: Continue the rotation of your torso and hips, driving your opponent over your right hip and landing in a stable control position (e.g., side control or mount).

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Hip Rotation: Trying to throw by lifting with arms instead of rotating hips leads to shoulder impingement or rotator cuff tears as the humerus gets jammed.
    • Knee Valgus Stress: Stepping too wide or not aligning your knee over your foot during the hip rotation can cause medial collateral ligament (MCL) sprain in your right knee.
    • Over-Reliance on Arms: Pushing with arms instead of pulling with your hikite and driving with your hips strains your lower back and biceps tendon.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Rotation Drill (0% Resistance): Practice stepping across and rotating your hips while holding a phantom opponent. 10 repetitions each side.
    2. Shadow Harai Goshi (0% Resistance): Perform the full technique without an opponent, focusing on the footwork and hip mechanics. 10 repetitions each side.
    3. Grip Fighting and Entry (25% Resistance): With a partner standing, practice establishing grips and stepping in for the hip entry, allowing them to resist lightly. 5 repetitions each side.
    4. Controlled Entry and Hip Bump (50% Resistance): Your partner stands passively. You establish grips, step in, and practice the hip rotation and upward bump motion without the sweep. 5 repetitions each side.
    5. Assisted Harai Goshi (75% Resistance): Your partner cooperates by allowing you to execute the sweep after the hip entry, providing minimal upward resistance. 5 repetitions each side.
    6. Live Rolling (90-100% Resistance): Attempt Harai Goshi during live sparring rounds, focusing on timing and commitment to the technique. 3-5 attempts per round.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is leaning forward, breaking their posture.
    • Opponent is posturing up, creating a slight gap at the hips.
    • During grip fighting exchanges when space opens up for entry.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Osoto Gari Counter: If opponent steps their left leg back to defend, immediately transition to an Osoto Gari by stepping your right leg back and sweeping their rear leg.
    • Snap Down and Follow: If the opponent resists the pull and pushes into you, use a snap down motion with the collar grip and follow them to the ground, securing a dominant position.
    • Guard Pull and Sweep: If the Harai Goshi entry fails and you are off-balance, quickly pull guard or transition to a guard retention position and look for a sweep.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my back hurt when I try to do Harai Goshi as a white belt?

    Your back pain likely stems from trying to generate power with your lumbar spine instead of your hips and core. When you step in for Harai Goshi, focus on rotating your pelvis and driving with your glutes and obliques. Imagine your hips are a strong hinge; the power comes from the rotation of this hinge, not from bending your spine.

    Q: How can I use Harai Goshi against a much bigger and heavier opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paraMount. Focus on getting your hips *deeply* under their center of gravity by stepping across and slightly behind them. Use their weight against them by pulling them forward and down with your hikite as you initiate the sweep. A strong Kuzushi (unbalancing) is key to making the throw work.

    Q: I keep getting stuck in my Harai Goshi attempt and my partner just defends. What am I doing wrong?

    You're likely not committing fully to the hip rotation and sweep. Once you establish your hip contact, you must drive *through* your opponent with your hips and simultaneously sweep their leg. Hesitation allows them to re-establish their base. The sweep action is a dynamic extension and rotation, not a gentle push.

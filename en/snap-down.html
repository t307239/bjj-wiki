Snap Down (Neck Snap)

The snap down (also called neck snap or head snap) is one of the highest-percentage wrestling setups in BJJ and MMA. By snapping the opponent's head and posture forward, you create a scramble that exposes their back, sets up the double leg, or creates a front headlock. It requires minimal strength and works against any size opponent.

Execution

1
Grip the head — Collar tie: cup the back of their head with one hand, the other grips their same-side elbow.
2
Snap timing — When they posture up or push into you, use that energy — snap their head sharply downward and forward with your collar tie hand.
3
Follow the snap — As their head goes down, they expose their back: step to the side and take the back, or circle and shoot the double leg.

Key Setups

1
Against the pushout — When they try to push you back to create space, redirect their energy: snap the head as they push.
2
From collar-and-elbow — Standard wrestling collar tie — snap when they go flat-footed or lift their head.
3
Repeated snaps — Multiple snaps tire the opponent's neck and make them defensive, opening up level changes and shots.

What to Do After the Snap

1
Back take — Step behind them, get the seatbelt grip, take back.
2
Double leg — Snap sends their head down, shoot the double leg on the exposed legs.
3
Front headlock — Catch their head in a front headlock and transition to a guillotine or go to the mat.

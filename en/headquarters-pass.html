Headquarters Pass: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

The Headquarters Pass is a fundamental guard passing technique initiated from a standing or kneeling position against a Closed Guard.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of weight distribution and frame creation, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The core principle is to break the opponent's hip connection by establishing a strong base and driving forward with controlled pressure.

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip opponent's pant legs just above the knee with both hands, thumbs inside the material.
    2. Establish a wide, stable base with your knees slightly bent, directly over your ankles.
    3. Lean your torso forward, driving your chest towards the opponent's sternum, creating a forward pressure.
    4. Use your hips to initiate a slight backward movement, drawing the opponent's hips slightly off the mat.
    5. Simultaneously, drive your knees forward and down, aiming to break the closed guard connection.
    6. As the guard opens, drive your right knee towards the mat on the outside of the opponent's left leg.
    7. Extend your left leg straight back, creating a strong post and driving your left shoulder into the opponent's hip.
    8. Maintain forward pressure with your chest and hips, driving your right knee across their body to secure side control.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to muscle the legs open by pulling upwards: This can strain the lower back and shoulders due to improper leverage and can lead to a knee injury if the opponent forcefully extends their legs.
    • Leaning too far back and losing base: This compromises your stability, making you susceptible to sweeps and potentially hyperextending your own knees if the opponent drives into you.
    • Not establishing a firm chest-to-chest connection: This allows the opponent to create space and frame effectively, leading to your arms being trapped or your pass being easily reset.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip drive and knee-to-mat motion with no opponent. 20 reps.
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Partner lies on their back, you execute the pass. 10 reps per side.
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Partner offers slight resistance to guard opening. 10 reps per side.
    4. Controlled sparring (0% resistance): Focus solely on executing the Headquarters Pass when the opportunity arises. 5 minutes.
    5. Controlled sparring (25% resistance): Partner actively defends, but allows the pass to be completed if executed correctly. 5 minutes.
    6. Live rolling (50% resistance): Focus on implementing the Headquarters Pass against a resisting opponent. 5 minutes.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent attempts to close guard from a distance.
    • Opponent's knees are not tightly locked around your hips.
    • You have established a solid base and forward pressure.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • When opponent tries to open guard: Immediately drive your knees forward and down, maintaining a strong chest-to-chest connection. Focus on breaking the hip connection rather than pulling.
    • When opponent attempts to shrimp away: Keep your base wide and drive your hips forward, preventing them from creating space. Use your arms to control their hips and prevent them from moving.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my back hurt when I try to do the Headquarters Pass?

    Your back likely hurts because you are trying to use your back muscles to pull the legs open instead of using your hips and legs. The Headquarters Pass relies on driving forward with your chest and using your hips to break the guard. Ensure your base is wide and your knees are bent, allowing your legs to do the work.

    Q: How do I do the Headquarters Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a bigger opponent, leverage is even more crucial. Focus on establishing a solid, wide base to prevent them from using their weight to crush you. Instead of trying to muscle through, use your hips to create small, controlled movements that disrupt their base. Drive your chest into their sternum to maintain pressure and limit their ability to post.

    Q: When is the best time to switch to a different pass if the Headquarters Pass isn't working?

    If the opponent successfully defends the initial hip break and maintains a tight guard, it's time to switch. Look for an opening to transition to a knee-slice or leg-drag if you can get your knee inside their leg. If they are posturing up significantly, a stack pass might become viable.

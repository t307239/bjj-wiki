The Headquarters Pass is a powerful guard pass initiated from a standing or kneeling position against an opponent's Closed Guard. It aims to break the opponent's posture and establish side control.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of precise body control and understanding of leverage, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key mechanical insight is to use your opponent's own structure against them by creating a strong base and driving forward with controlled hip pressure.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip: Secure a strong collar grip with your right hand on the opponent's left lapel, and a cross-collar grip with your left hand on their right lapel. Ensure your thumbs are inside the collar.
    2. Posture Control: Pull both collars down and slightly forward, breaking their posture by bending their neck. Your elbows should be tucked in, creating a stable frame.
    3. Base and Weight Transfer: Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, creating a wide, stable base. Your hips should be low, almost touching your knees.
    4. Head Position: Drive your head into the opponent's shoulder or chest area, creating a strong focal point for forward pressure. This prevents them from creating space.
    5. Leg Drive: Plant your left foot firmly on the mat, slightly behind your right foot. Your left knee will drive forward towards the opponent's hip.
    6. Hip Drive: As you drive your head and shoulders forward, simultaneously drive your hips forward and down. Imagine pushing your pelvis into their hips.
    7. Guard Break: Use your driving force to push the opponent's legs apart, breaking their closed guard. Your left knee should aim to get inside their left thigh.
    8. Transition to Side Control: Once the guard is broken, immediately drive your left shoulder into their chest and your right arm under their armpit to secure side control. Maintain constant forward pressure.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Grip and Pulling: A white belt might grab the collar loosely and pull upwards with their arms. This strains the rotator cuff and biceps tendons, leading to tears, as the opponent can easily resist and use the momentum against you.
    • Arching the Back: Trying to break posture by excessively arching the lower back instead of using hip drive. This can cause lumbar disc compression and strain, leading to back pain and potential herniation. The correct method is driving the hips forward.
    • Knee Impact: Driving the knee directly into the opponent's hip socket without proper hip alignment. This can lead to painful knee joint irritation and potential ligament strain. Your knee should move in conjunction with your hip drive, aiming to get inside their thigh.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Grip and Posture Break Drill: Practice securing collar grips and pulling down to break posture without a partner. Focus on elbow tuck and head position. (10 reps, 0% resistance).
    2. Partnered Grip and Posture Break (Static): With a partner lying on their back, practice securing grips and pulling them into a broken posture position. Hold for 5 seconds. (10 reps, 10% resistance).
    3. Partnered Headquarters Pass Entry (No Guard Break): Partner lies on their back, you enter the Headquarters Pass position, focusing on head drive and hip pressure. Partner resists slightly by keeping legs closed. (15 reps, 25% resistance).
    4. Partnered Guard Break Initiation: You initiate the Headquarters Pass, and your partner offers minimal resistance to the guard break. Focus on the knee and hip drive to create space. (20 reps, 50% resistance).
    5. Controlled Headquarters Pass: Execute the full Headquarters Pass against a partner who offers moderate resistance by trying to frame or shrimp. Focus on completing the pass to side control. (25 reps, 75% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling Application: Attempt the Headquarters Pass during live rolling with a partner who is actively defending. Focus on applying the learned mechanics and adapting to their defense. (3 rounds, 90% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is in a tight closed guard with good posture.
    • Opponent is trying to create space by pushing your hips away.
    • You have successfully secured strong, deep collar grips.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Shrimping Away: If the opponent drives their hips forward and their knee enters your thigh, immediately shrimp your hips away and create space to re-establish guard.
    • Posting on Head/Shoulder: If the opponent drives their head into your chest or shoulder, push against their head and shoulder with your hands to create space and prevent them from breaking your posture.
    • Leg Pummeling: If the opponent's knee comes inside your thigh, pummel your leg out and try to regain your guard or transition to a different guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try the Headquarters Pass?

    Your neck is likely hurting because you are using your neck muscles to try and break your opponent's posture, instead of using your body's leverage. Ensure you are driving your hips forward and down, and using your arms to pull the collars. Your head should be a stable point of pressure, not the primary force generator.

    Q: How can I perform the Headquarters Pass against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on precision and leverage over raw strength. Secure your grips deeply and break their posture as much as possible before initiating the drive. Use your head and shoulder to create a strong frame, and ensure your hips are driving forward and low to disrupt their base. Constant forward pressure is key.

    Q: Should I attempt the Headquarters Pass from standing or kneeling?

    The Headquarters Pass can be initiated from both standing and kneeling. As a white belt, starting from a kneeling position with a wide base provides more stability and reduces the risk of falling. Once comfortable, you can progress to initiating it from a standing posture, but always maintain a low center of gravity.

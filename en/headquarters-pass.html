Mastering the Headquarters Pass in BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Headquarters Pass is a dominant guard pass initiated from a standing or kneeling position against a Closed Guard or open guard. It aims to secure side control by breaking the opponent's posture and clearing their legs.

White belts often struggle by trying to force the pass with brute strength, neglecting the critical postural and structural elements. This leads to predictable attempts and allows the opponent to maintain their guard.

The key mechanical insight is using your opponent's own weight and structure against them, creating an angle that collapses their base and allows you to isolate and pass a leg. It’s about creating leverage, not just applying force.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip: Secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a cross-sleeve grip with your left hand. Alternatively, use two cross-collar grips.
    2. Posture: Establish strong upright posture, keeping your head up and back straight. Your hips should be slightly forward, creating a stable base.
    3. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your left leg, creating a slight angle. Your left knee should point towards the mat, and your right foot should be planted firmly.
    4. Break Posture: Drive your right shoulder into the opponent's chest, simultaneously pulling their collar grips down and towards your body. This action collapses their upper body.
    5. Clearing the Leg: As their posture breaks, step your right foot outside their left hip. Your left knee should remain on the mat, acting as a pivot.
    6. Hip Angle: Rotate your hips so they are perpendicular to your opponent's hips. Your left hip should be tight against their torso.
    7. Drive and Secure: Drive your chest into their hip, using your right arm to control their leg. Push their left leg to the side as you drive your hips forward to establish side control.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Knee Pressure: Driving your knee directly into the opponent's hip socket with excessive force can cause a hip labral tear or acetabular fracture. Always ensure your knee is angled slightly and pressure is distributed through your hip, not just your kneecap.
    • Neck Strain from Poor Posture: Arching your back excessively while trying to break posture can lead to lumbar disc herniation. Maintain a neutral spine and use your shoulder and arm leverage to break their posture, not your back.
    • Wrist Injury from Grip Fighting: Gripping too tightly and without proper wrist alignment when the opponent resists can cause a sprained wrist or carpal tunnel irritation. Keep your wrists straight and use your forearm and biceps to control grips, not just finger strength.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Mobility Drill: Practice hip rotation and weight transfer from a kneeling position without a partner. Focus on angling your hips and stepping outside. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    2. Solo Posture Break Drill: From a kneeling position, practice reaching for collar grips and driving your shoulder into an imaginary opponent's chest, simulating the posture break. (10 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Partnered Posture Break and Hip Angle (No Resistance): With a partner lying on their back, practice securing grips, breaking their posture, and achieving the correct hip angle. Partner remains passive. (10 reps, 0% resistance)
    4. Partnered Leg Clear and Hip Turn (Light Resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance. Practice driving your shoulder, clearing their leg, and rotating your hips to the perpendicular position. (15 reps, 25% resistance)
    5. Partnered Pass Execution (Moderate Resistance): Partner actively tries to recompose guard. Focus on executing the full Headquarters Pass sequence, aiming for side control. (20 reps, 50% resistance)
    6. Controlled Sparring (Positional): Practice the Headquarters Pass from closed guard or open guard in a controlled sparring scenario. Focus on timing and pressure. (5 minutes, 75% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent has you in closed guard and you have established strong posture.
    • When the opponent opens their guard and you have control of their ankles or hips.
    • When attempting to pass from a kneeling position and the opponent is reaching for your legs.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Re-guarding: If the passer fails to achieve a strong hip angle, the defender can use their free leg to hook the passer's hip and shrimp away, re-establishing guard.
    • Shrimp and Underhook: If the passer's shoulder pressure is too high, the defender can create space by shrimping and simultaneously securing an underhook to disrupt the passer's base.
    • Knee Shield/Push Away: If the passer drives too deep with their shoulder, the defender can place their knee between their chest and the passer's shoulder and push away to create space and reset.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to do the Headquarters Pass?

    Your shoulder pain likely stems from trying to force the posture break with your arm strength alone, rather than using your body's leverage. Ensure you are driving your shoulder into their chest while simultaneously pulling down on the collar grips. This combined action collapses their structure, making the pass easier and safer for your shoulder.

    Q: How can I do the Headquarters Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on the angle and leverage. Instead of driving straight in, create a sharper angle with your hips. Use your head to control their head's direction, and prioritize breaking their posture *before* attempting to clear their legs. Secure strong collar grips to help initiate the break.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from a knee-slice to a Headquarters Pass?

    The switch is most effective when your opponent's hips are high, preventing a clean knee-slice, or when they are actively trying to use their legs to push your knee away. At this point, you can abandon the knee-slice attempt, secure collar grips, and transition to the Headquarters Pass by breaking their posture and angling your hips.

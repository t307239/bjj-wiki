Headquarters Pass: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Headquarters Pass is a fundamental guard passing technique initiated from a standing or kneeling position against a Closed Guard. It aims to break the opponent's guard and establish side control or mount.

White belts often struggle by attempting to force the pass with brute strength, neglecting the proper weight distribution and hip mechanics required.

The core principle is using your hips to create a base and drive forward, forcing the opponent's legs to open by attacking their knee line, not their feet.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Opponent is in closed guard. Assume a knee-on-the-mat, seated position with your left knee down and right foot forward, right shin parallel to the mat.
    2. Grip opponent's left pant leg with your right hand, just above the knee. Grip their right pant leg with your left hand, also above the knee.
    3. Drive your right shoulder into the opponent's left hip, creating a strong frame with your right forearm against their hip bone.
    4. Angle your hips to your right, shifting your weight onto your right foot and forward, creating a diagonal line from your right foot to your left knee.
    5. Simultaneously, begin to drive your left knee forward, aiming to clear the opponent's right leg.
    6. As you drive forward, use your grips to pull the opponent's legs slightly apart and maintain control of their knees.
    7. Continue driving your right shoulder into their hip and stepping your left leg through, clearing their right leg and establishing a side control position by sinking your right knee to the mat and bringing your left leg to parallel.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly driving your head down towards the opponent's legs can lead to neck compression and potential cervical spine injury if the opponent attempts a sweep.
    • Correct: Maintain an upright posture, driving your shoulder into their hip with your head facing forward.
    • Trying to muscle the legs open by pulling upwards on the pant legs can strain your biceps and shoulder joints.
    • Correct: Use your hip drive and forward pressure to create the opening, pulling only to maintain control and guide the legs.
    • Planting your feet too wide in a base can lead to knee valgus collapse and ligamentous knee injuries (e.g., ACL tear) if the opponent bridges or sweeps forcefully.
    • Correct: Maintain a base where your knees are tracking over your toes and your weight is balanced over your hips and forward.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip angle, shoulder drive, and leg clearing motion without a partner. Focus on the weight transfer and body positioning. (10 reps)
    2. Partner drill (static, 0% resistance): Opponent lies on their back in guard. You perform the pass, focusing on grip placement and hip drive. Opponent offers no resistance. (5 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance, 25%): Opponent attempts to maintain closed guard but allows you to initiate the break. Focus on your hip drive and clearing the leg. (5 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (medium resistance, 50%): Opponent actively tries to keep guard closed but does not actively counter. Focus on the timing of your hip drive and leg clear. (5 reps per side)
    5. Partner drill (simulated resistance, 75%): Opponent actively defends the pass, but you are allowed to reset if they successfully counter. Focus on maintaining pressure and adapting. (5 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Apply the Headquarters Pass during sparring. Focus on execution and identifying opportunities. (3 minutes)
    7. Live rolling (100% resistance): Integrate the Headquarters Pass into your rolling with full intent and resistance from your partner. (5 minutes)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent has a tight closed guard and you are struggling to break it with standard methods.
    • Opponent is posturing up slightly, creating a small opening at the hip.
    • You have established a good knee-on-the-mat base and can control their hips.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Trap Sweep: If the opponent drives their knee forward to clear your shoulder, trap their advancing knee with your opposite arm and use your hips to sweep.
    • Hip Bump Sweep: If you have established a strong frame and they are trying to push you away, use your hips to create an angle and bridge into a hip bump sweep.
    • Back Take: If the opponent opens their guard to create space, immediately transition to a back take by securing hooks and controlling their upper body.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try the Headquarters Pass?

    This often happens when you are driving your head directly into your opponent's hip or chest instead of using your shoulder as a fulcrum against their hip bone. This misalignment places excessive pressure on your shoulder joint and can lead to impingement. Ensure your shoulder is pressing into their iliac crest (hip bone) and your head remains neutral, looking forward, allowing your hips to drive the movement.

    Q: How can I do the Headquarters Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is key. Focus on superior hip angle and timing rather than pure strength. Use a strong frame with your forearm against their hip to control their base. Drive your hips forward and to the side at a sharp angle, forcing their legs to open due to the leverage created, rather than trying to pry them apart directly. Maintain a low center of gravity and keep your weight driving forward.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Headquarters Pass, and when should I avoid it?

    Attempt the Headquarters Pass when your opponent's knees are relatively close together, making it easier to drive your shoulder into their hip and clear a leg. Avoid it if your opponent has a very wide open guard or is actively posturing up with their hips away from you, as this creates too much space to effectively apply the leverage needed for this pass. In those situations, consider a different passing strategy like a Knee Slice or leg drag.

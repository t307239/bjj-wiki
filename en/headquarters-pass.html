The Headquarters Pass starts from a dominant side-control or knee-on-belly position, aiming to secure a strong cross-face and control the opponent's hips to advance past their legs.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength rather than leverage, leading to stalled passes and potential injuries. They might also lose the cross-face too early or fail to post their base correctly.

The core mechanical insight is creating a wedge with your shoulder and hip to break their base, then using your far-side arm to control their hip and drive forward.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From side-control, establish a deep cross-face grip with your right hand on their left temple, applying downward pressure. Your left hand secures a strong grip on their left hip or waistband.
    2. Angle your hips to your right, creating a 45-degree angle relative to your opponent's torso. Your right knee should be tucked close to their hip.
    3. Drive your right shoulder into their upper chest, creating a wedge that prevents them from turning into you. Simultaneously, begin to shift your weight forward onto your chest and their upper body.
    4. As you drive forward, extend your left leg back for base, keeping your left knee off the mat. Your left foot should be planted firmly, creating a stable tripod.
    5. With your right shoulder wedged and hip angled, use your left hand to pull their hip towards you, further breaking their posture and creating space.
    6. Begin to walk your right knee forward, sliding it along the mat towards their opposite hip. Your hips remain angled.
    7. Drive your chest down onto their upper chest and continue to walk your right knee forward, aiming to bring your right shin across their body to establish side-control on the other side.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly driving your head forward: This can lead to neck hyperextension or cervical spine injury if the opponent shrimps or bridges effectively. The correct movement is to drive your shoulder into their upper chest, keeping your head neutral.
    • Overextending your base leg: Planting your base leg too far back and straightening it completely can strain the hamstring or knee ligaments. Maintain a slight bend in your base knee and keep it mobile.
    • Releasing the hip grip too early: Letting go of the far-side hip grip allows the opponent to recover guard or shrimp away. The hip grip must be maintained until your shin has passed their hips and you have established a new control position.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling of hip angle and shoulder wedge: Practice angling your hips and driving your shoulder into a padded dummy or imaginary opponent for 20 repetitions per side.
    2. Solo drilling of leg movement: Practice the knee slide and base leg extension from the angled position for 20 repetitions per side.
    3. Partner drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the full Headquarters Pass sequence, focusing on correct grips and weight distribution. 5 repetitions per side, with partner offering no resistance.
    4. Partner drill (25% resistance): The partner offers slight resistance by attempting to frame or shrimp minimally. 5 repetitions per side.
    5. Partner drill (50% resistance): The partner actively tries to frame and recover guard, but does not actively attack. 5 repetitions per side.
    6. Live rolling (90% intensity): Apply the Headquarters Pass in a controlled rolling scenario, focusing on applying the technique when the opportunity arises. 3 attempts per round.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is turtled or trying to recover guard from side-control.
    • Opponent's hips are elevated, creating a slight space to drive your shoulder into their chest.
    • Opponent is attempting a technical stand-up from bottom side-control.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Shrimp and recover guard: As the passer drives forward, shrimp your hips away to create space and bring your knee between your chest and theirs, re-establishing guard.
    • Bridge and roll: If the passer's base is unstable, execute a strong bridge, using their forward momentum against them, and attempt to roll them over.
    • Whizzer counter: If the passer secures a weak cross-face and their base is too high, actively counter their hip grip by securing a whizzer on their arm and using your legs to create space and escape.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to do the Headquarters Pass?

    Shoulder pain often arises from driving your head or shoulder directly into the opponent's face or neck, rather than their upper chest. This misdirected force can lead to impingement. Ensure your shoulder is wedging into their upper pectoral/sternum area, and your head remains neutral or slightly turned away.

    Q: How can I use the Headquarters Pass against a much bigger opponent who is heavier than me?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on leverage and angles. Instead of trying to power through, emphasize the hip-to-hip angle (45 degrees) and the shoulder wedge to break their base. Use your far-side arm to control their hip and pull it towards you, disrupting their stability before driving forward.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from side control to a Headquarters Pass attempt?

    The ideal time is when the opponent is trying to create space by shrimping or bridging, as this often compromises their base and posture. Also, if they are attempting to 'technical stand-up' from bottom, your forward pressure and hip control can prevent them from completing the stand-up and lead to a pass.

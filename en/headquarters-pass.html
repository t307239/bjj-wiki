The Headquarters Pass is initiated from a standing or kneeling position against an opponent's Closed Guard, aiming to achieve side control.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of precise control and an overreliance on brute strength, leading to ineffective attempts and injury.

The core mechanical insight is to use your hips to drive *through* their legs, creating a stable base and breaking their posture.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grips: Secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a cross-sleeve grip (same side as collar grip) with your left hand. Your opponent is in closed guard.
    2. Posture Break: Step your right foot forward, placing it slightly outside your opponent's left hip. Simultaneously, pull their collar down and to your left, breaking their posture forward.
    3. Hip Drive Initiation: Lower your hips by bending your knees, ensuring your pelvis is below their hip line. Your right knee should track towards your opponent's left hip bone.
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your right leg, creating a strong base. Your left leg remains back for balance and to prevent them from regripping.
    5. Leg Separation: As you drive forward, use your right forearm (under their armpit) to push their left shoulder away. This creates space to peel their legs open.
    6. Headquarters Position: Drive your right hip into their left hip. Your right knee should be near their hip bone, and your left leg should be extended backward, creating a powerful lever.
    7. Pass Completion: Continue driving your hips forward and to your left. Your chest should press into their chest. Peel their legs open with your forearm and knee, then step your left leg over their hips to establish side control.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Knee hyperextension: Attempting to force the pass by driving forward without proper hip alignment can cause your lead knee to buckle outwards, potentially tearing the ACL or MCL.
    • Wrist sprains: Gripping too tightly with a flexed wrist while pulling down on the collar can lead to wrist ligament damage or sprains, especially when the opponent resists.
    • Lower back strain: Trying to 'muscle' the pass by arching your back instead of driving with your hips can put excessive shear force on the lumbar spine, leading to muscle strains or disc issues.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Mobility (0%): Practice hip-driving motions and knee-to-hip placements 20 reps per side.
    2. Static Grip and Posture Break (10%): Assume the starting position with a partner lying on their back. Practice breaking posture and securing grips without movement, 15 reps.
    3. Controlled Hip Drive (25%): Partner remains passive. Practice the hip drive and knee-to-hip placement, focusing on body mechanics, 10 reps per side.
    4. Leg Peel and Step (50%): Partner offers slight resistance to leg separation. Practice peeling legs open and stepping over, 10 reps per side.
    5. Simulated Pass (75%): Partner offers light resistance, allowing you to execute the full pass sequence with focus on control, 5 reps per side.
    6. Live Rolling (90-100%): Apply the Headquarters Pass during rolling, focusing on technique over outcome, 3-5 attempts per round.

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent's hips are slightly elevated, creating a small gap.
    • When the opponent's legs are not tightly locked, allowing for initial separation.
    • When you have established a strong collar and sleeve grip, controlling their posture.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Guard Retention (Hip Escape): As the opponent drives their hip, the guard player immediately hips away and re-establishes their guard, preventing the pass.
    • Knee Shield: The opponent uses their knee to create a frame against the attacker's chest or hip, disrupting the hip drive and maintaining distance.
    • Technical Stand-up: If the pass stalls, the guard player can attempt a technical stand-up by bridging and creating space to stand back up.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Headquarters Pass?

    Wrist pain often occurs from gripping the collar too tightly with a flexed wrist while pulling down. To fix this, keep your wrist neutral and strong, like a ramrod, by engaging your forearm muscles. Focus on using your shoulder and back to generate the pulling force, not just your wrist.

    Q: How can I do the Headquarters Pass against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is key. Focus on breaking their posture *before* you attempt to drive your hips. Use your legs to anchor yourself and drive your hips *under* their hip line, not just into them. Your goal is to get your hips lower than theirs to create a mechanical advantage.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Headquarters Pass from a kneeling position?

    The ideal time to attempt the Headquarters Pass from a kneeling position is when the opponent has their guard slightly open or when you can quickly secure dominant grips and break their posture. If they have a very tight closed guard, it's better to work to open it first or transition to another pass.

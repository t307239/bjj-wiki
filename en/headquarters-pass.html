Headquarters Pass: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

The Headquarters Pass is initiated from a standing or kneeling position against a Closed Guard. Its goal is to break the opponent's guard and establish side control.

White belts often struggle by creating a large gap or by not committing their weight properly, leading to the guard being re-established.

The key insight is to use your shoulder and hip to create a powerful, controlled frame that drives through the opponent's legs, not around them.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand, driving your knuckles towards their sternum. Your left hand grips their left sleeve near the wrist.
    2. Lower your base by bending your knees to approximately 90 degrees, keeping your hips low and your back straight.
    3. Step your right foot forward, placing it outside their left hip. Your right knee should be pointing towards their left hip.
    4. Simultaneously, drive your right shoulder into their left hip bone, creating a powerful forward pressure. Your left leg remains planted for base.
    5. Use your left hand to control their left sleeve, preventing them from framing or pulling your arm away.
    6. As you drive forward with your shoulder, begin to shift your weight onto your right foot, lifting your left foot slightly off the mat.
    7. Drive your hips forward and down, forcing their legs to open by the pressure of your shoulder and hip. Your body should be angled slightly, moving towards their left side.
    8. As their guard breaks, continue driving forward, securing your left knee onto the mat inside their legs and your right knee following.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to stand straight up while gripping the collar and sleeve: This places excessive rotational torque on the cervical spine and anterior cruciate ligaments (ACL) of the knees, risking disc herniation or ACL tears.
    • Driving forward with your head instead of your shoulder: This can lead to neck hyperextension or compression, potentially causing whiplash or nerve impingement.
    • Allowing your hips to remain high and not driving them forward: This creates a large gap, allowing the opponent to easily re-establish guard or attempt a sweep. Maintain a low, driving hip pressure.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the footwork, hip angle, and shoulder drive motion without a partner. 10 repetitions per side.
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the exact grips, weight shifts, and hip drive. 5 repetitions per side.
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner provides minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the pressure and movement. 5 repetitions per side.
    4. Partner drill (controlled pressure): Partner offers moderate resistance, focusing on maintaining your frame and driving through. 5 repetitions per side.
    5. Controlled sparring: Engage in live rolling with a focus solely on executing the Headquarters Pass. 3 rounds.
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the Headquarters Pass as opportunities arise during full sparring. 5 rounds.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is actively trying to close their guard from a distance.
    • Opponent has a loose or weak closed guard grip.
    • Opponent is attempting to create space by pushing your hips away.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Open Guard Re-establishment: If the passer drives too linearly, the opponent can use their feet to push the passer's hips away and bring their legs back in.
    • Butterfly Guard Sweep: If the passer's base is too narrow, the opponent can slide their legs underneath the passer's hips and achieve butterfly guard for a sweep.
    • Leg Hook Sweep: If the passer allows their leg to get too close to the opponent's body, the opponent can hook the passer's leg and use it to sweep.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try the Headquarters Pass?

    You are likely driving forward with your head and upper chest instead of your shoulder and hip. This puts direct pressure on your cervical spine. Focus on tucking your chin slightly and using your shoulder to create the initial wedge into the opponent's hip, driving your entire body weight through that point.

    Q: How can I use the Headquarters Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, the biomechanical advantage is even more crucial. You must emphasize a low base and a very strong shoulder-to-hip connection. Instead of trying to muscle through, use their weight against them by driving your shoulder into their hip and pivoting your body to break their posture and open their guard.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate the Headquarters Pass in a match?

    The Headquarters Pass is most effective when the opponent is trying to maintain a tight guard or is attempting to create distance. If they are actively pressing into you or have a very strong, static closed guard, other passes might be more suitable initially. Look for moments where their hips are slightly off-center or they are leaning forward.

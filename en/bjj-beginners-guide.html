BJJ Beginner's Guide: Everything You Need for Your First 6 Months

🥋 Blue Belt ★★☆☆☆ Intermediate

Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu has one of the steepest but most rewarding learning curves of any martial art. This guide gives you the exact roadmap to build a solid foundation in your first 6 months — without the confusion.

The List

#1

Closed Guard

Week 1 priority: Learn to control distance and break grips from closed guard before attempting attacks.

→ #2

Shrimp Escape

The most fundamental movement in BJJ. Master the hip escape drill before your first class ends.

→ #3

Armbar

Month 1: The armbar from guard teaches hip movement, timing, and attack mechanics all at once.

→ #4

Rear Naked Choke

Month 1: The finishing technique from back mount — the most dominant position in BJJ.

→ #5

Triangle Choke

Month 2: Once your hips move naturally, add the triangle as a follow-up to the armbar.

→ #6

Half Guard

Month 2: When you get passed to side control, half guard recovery is your most useful tool.

→ #7

Bridge and Roll

Month 2: Basic escape from mount. Combines with shrimp to give you two options from every bad position.

→ #8

Guard Pass

Month 3: Learn basic pressure passing before getting fancy — toreando and over-under are your friends.

→ #9

Side Control

Month 3: Understand the mechanics of your most common dominant position.

→ #10

Back Mount

Month 4+: Working toward and finishing from back mount — the highest value position in BJJ.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How long does it take to get a blue belt?

On average, 1-3 years of consistent training (3-4 days/week). Belt promotions vary by school, but a blue belt typically represents mastery of fundamental techniques and positions.

Q: Should I train gi or no-gi as a beginner?

Start with gi if possible — it slows the game down, teaches grips and technique, and builds a stronger fundamental base. No-gi is great to add after 3-6 months.

Q: How often should beginners train?

2-3 times per week is ideal for beginners. More frequent training accelerates progress but increases injury risk if recovery isn't managed.

Q: What should I focus on as a white belt?

Survival first. Learn to escape bad positions before worrying about attacks. Submissions will come naturally once you understand positional hierarchy.

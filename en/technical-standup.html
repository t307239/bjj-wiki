Technical Stand-Up: Your First BJJ Transition

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Technical Stand-Up allows you to safely return to your feet from a compromised position, typically when your opponent is in your guard or has just swept you.

White belts often rush this, leading to loss of balance and potential injury, or they fail to create sufficient space to stand.

The core principle is creating a strong base with your hands and knees while simultaneously driving your hips back and up, using your legs to push off the mat.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a seated or kneeling position with opponent in front, establish a wide base with hands planted shoulder-width apart on the mat.
    2. Grip opponent's collar with your right hand and their opposite sleeve (left sleeve) with your left hand, creating a framing structure.
    3. Drive your right knee towards your chest, tucking it under your hips, ensuring your left foot remains flat on the mat for support.
    4. Simultaneously, shift your weight onto your hands and drive your hips backward and upward, creating an angle of approximately 45 degrees between your torso and the mat.
    5. Use your planted left foot to push off the mat, extending your left leg as your hips rise.
    6. Bring your right foot (the one that was tucked) forward and place it flat on the mat next to your left foot, creating a stable staggered stance.
    7. Release grips and stand fully upright, maintaining balance and awareness of opponent's position.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to stand by flinging your legs forward without hip drive: This can hyperextend the lumbar spine, leading to lower back injury.
    • Planting hands too close together or too far apart: This compromises your base, making you unstable and prone to wrist sprains or fractures if you fall.
    • Standing up directly with both knees off the ground simultaneously: This removes your base and can lead to a loss of balance and potential knee ligament tears upon impact with the mat.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip drive and leg extension without grips or an opponent. Focus on creating the 45-degree torso angle. (50 reps)
    2. Partner assisted: With a compliant partner lying on their back, practice the grip and frame. They offer minimal resistance. (25 reps)
    3. Static position drilling: Partner holds a light guard. You execute the technical stand-up with their cooperation. (50% resistance, 10 reps)
    4. Slow motion drilling: Partner applies light pressure. Focus on maintaining frame and hip drive. (75% resistance, 5 reps)
    5. Controlled sparring: Attempt the technical stand-up during positional sparring, with partner instructed to offer moderate resistance. (90% resistance, 3 reps)
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the technical stand-up into live rolling when the opportunity arises. (100% resistance, as opportunities present)

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I do the Technical Stand-Up in BJJ?

    Wrist pain during the Technical Stand-Up often stems from an unstable base or improper hand placement. When you place your hands too close together or too far apart, your wrists bear excessive load, especially if you fall. Ensure your hands are planted shoulder-width apart, fingers spread, and that you are driving your hips back and up, transferring weight to your legs and core rather than solely relying on your wrists for support.

    Q: How do I do the Technical Stand-Up against a much bigger BJJ opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, the key is creating leverage and space. Use your grips (collar and sleeve) not just for framing, but to actively pull their head down and away as you drive your hips back. This disrupts their balance and creates the necessary angle. Focus on a strong hip drive and a wide base with your feet planted firmly, using your leg strength to push off the mat. Don't try to muscle it; use technique and angles.

    Q: When is the best time to use the Technical Stand-Up in BJJ sparring?

    The Technical Stand-Up is most effective when your opponent is momentarily off-balance or has just committed to a sweep or attack that leaves them vulnerable. Look for moments where they are pressing forward with their weight, or where you have just created space by pushing their hips away. It's less about forcing it and more about recognizing the opportune moment to transition to your feet safely and efficiently.

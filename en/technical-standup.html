Your First Steps: The Technical Stand-Up in BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Technical Stand-Up is a fundamental BJJ transition from a prone or seated position to a standing one, maintaining control and safety.

White belts often rush this, losing structure and creating openings for sweeps or submissions.

The core principle is creating a stable base and controlled ascent using opposing forces.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Starting from bottom guard (e.g., closed-guard), secure a Collar grip with your right hand and a Sleeve grip (left arm) with your left hand.
    2. Sit up, bringing your hips forward and creating a tight frame with your forearms against your opponent's biceps.
    3. Step your left foot flat on the mat, knee bent to 90 degrees, directly under your hip.
    4. Drive your right knee to the mat, placing it just behind your left foot, creating a stable tripod base.
    5. Simultaneously, drive your hips forward and up, away from your opponent's base.
    6. Use your Collar and Sleeve grips to pull your opponent forward as you push off your left foot and right knee, generating upward momentum.
    7. As you stand, keep your base wide, your hips low, and maintain grips for balance and control, transitioning to a dominant stand-up position.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Lunging upwards without a solid base: This can strain your lower back by hyperextending the spine, especially if your hips are too far back.
    • Correct: Drive hips forward and up, creating a stable tripod with your foot and knee.
    • Releasing grips too early: This leads to loss of balance and can put your shoulder joint in a vulnerable position if you fall awkwardly.
    • Correct: Maintain grips until you achieve a stable standing base with your weight centered.
    • Straightening your legs prematurely: This removes your base and makes you susceptible to sweeps, potentially twisting your knee ligaments.
    • Correct: Keep knees bent until your standing base is secure and balanced.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Drill: Practice the footwork and hip movement without a partner. Focus on the tripod base and hip drive. (50 reps)
    2. Assisted Drill (0% Resistance): Partner lies prone; you practice the stand-up motion, focusing on grip engagement and frame creation.
    3. Assisted Drill (25% Resistance): Partner maintains a very light posture; you execute the stand-up, feeling their minimal pushback.
    4. Assisted Drill (50% Resistance): Partner applies moderate pressure to resist your stand-up; focus on driving through their resistance.
    5. Assisted Drill (75% Resistance): Partner actively tries to prevent your stand-up; practice maintaining your base and driving upwards.
    6. Live Rolling (90% Resistance): Attempt the Technical Stand-Up in a controlled roll, focusing on timing and execution against active defense.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is in your guard and you want to create space to stand up and pass.
    • As a transition from a sweep attempt that stalls or fails, allowing you to regain a dominant position.
    • When you are on your back and need to disengage safely to avoid a submission or transition to a better position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape & Base Reinforcement: If your opponent tries to drive into you as you stand, immediately widen your base, drop your hips lower, and use your hands on the mat for support to prevent them from collapsing your structure.
    • Grip Break & Re-Guard: If they manage to break your grips, quickly bring your knees to your chest and re-establish guard (e.g., closed guard or open guard) to reset.
    • Knee-Tap Defense: If they attempt to knee-tap you as you rise, drop your left knee firmly to the mat and use your right foot to hook their leg or hip, preventing the tap and stabilizing your base.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my left wrist hurt when I do the Technical Stand-Up in BJJ?

    Your left wrist pain likely stems from placing excessive weight on it during the stand-up. When you step your left foot, ensure it's flat and directly under your hip, not angled outwards. Your right knee should also provide significant support, acting as a tripod leg. Shift your weight distribution so the pressure is primarily on your foot and knee, not solely on your wrist.

    Q: How do I Technical Stand-Up against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on timing and leverage. Use their forward momentum against them by pulling them slightly forward with your grips as you initiate the stand-up. Ensure your left foot is planted securely under your hip for a strong base, and drive your hips up and forward aggressively. The key is to create a moment of imbalance in their weight distribution that you can exploit.

    Q: When is the best time to use the Technical Stand-Up in BJJ, and when should I avoid it?

    The Technical Stand-Up is ideal when your opponent is relatively still or pushing forward, creating an opportunity for you to rise. Avoid it if your opponent has very strong control over your hips or legs, or if they are actively posturing up and creating a strong base, as this will make it much harder to generate the necessary upward force and could lead to you being swept.

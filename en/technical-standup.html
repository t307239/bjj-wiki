The Technical Stand-Up is a fundamental BJJ transition, allowing you to safely rise from bottom positions like guard or Half Guard to a standing base.

White belts often rush this, losing their base and exposing themselves to sweeps or passes before initiating their next action.

The key is to create a stable, forward-moving base by driving your hips and creating a tripod with your hands and feet, not just a frantic scramble.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From bottom guard, secure an Inside Collar Grip with your non-posting hand and an Opposite Sleeve Grip with your posting hand.
    2. Drive your hips forward and slightly to the side of your posting arm, creating a 45-degree angle between your torso and the mat.
    3. Post your posting hand flat on the mat, fingers pointing slightly forward, shoulder directly over your wrist.
    4. Bring your posting leg's knee towards your chest, tucking it beneath your hips.
    5. Drive your hips up and forward, using your posting hand and foot as a stable base.
    6. Simultaneously, bring your non-posting leg forward, landing your foot flat on the mat as your hips rise.
    7. Extend your posting leg and lift your posting hand, driving forward into a standing base with feet shoulder-width apart.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Posting hand placed too far forward or too narrow: This hyperextends the wrist and elbow, creating a high risk of ligamentous tears (e.g., UCL tear in elbow, carpal ligament sprain) due to sudden, uncontrolled weight transfer.
    • Scrambling to feet without hip drive: This puts immense shear force on the lumbar spine, potentially leading to disc herniation or facet joint injury as the torso twists and extends without a stable base.
    • Lifting hips without leg tuck: This can cause the knee of the tucked leg to buckle inwards (valgus stress), risking an ACL or MCL tear by forcing the knee joint beyond its safe range of motion.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo: Practice the hip drive and foot placement on the mat, focusing on creating the tripod with hands and feet. (50 reps)
    2. Solo: Add the leg tuck and forward hip movement without standing. (50 reps)
    3. Solo: Combine all movements to a standing base, focusing on balance. (50 reps)
    4. Partner Assisted (0% resistance): Partner lies supine, you perform the Technical Stand-Up from guard, partner offers no resistance. (25 reps)
    5. Partner Assisted (25% resistance): Partner attempts to maintain a light grip on your sleeves/collar, offering minimal pressure. (25 reps)
    6. Partner Assisted (50% resistance): Partner attempts a light grip and slight pressure, simulating a passive opponent. (50 reps)
    7. Live Rolling (75% resistance): Attempt the Technical Stand-Up during rolling, focusing on proper mechanics over speed. (10 attempts)
    8. Live Rolling (90% resistance): Integrate the Technical Stand-Up into your rolling as a primary escape or transition option. (15 attempts)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent attempts a sweep from your closed guard and you want to reset to a neutral or advantageous position.
    • When you are in open guard and want to create space to stand up and transition to a takedown or escape.
    • When you are in half guard and want to achieve a better base or transition to a different guard.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Hook Sweep Counter: If opponent attempts a leg hook sweep, maintain a strong grip on their collar and sleeve, drive your hips forward, and step your non-posting leg *over* their attempted hook before fully standing.
    • Guard Pass Attempt Counter: If opponent attempts to pass guard as you stand, use your posting hand to create a strong frame on their hip or shoulder, driving your hips into them to maintain your base and prevent them from advancing.
    • Guillotine Choke Defense: If opponent grabs a guillotine as you stand, immediately tuck your chin to your chest, drive your hips forward and down, and use your free hand to peel their grip off your neck while continuing to stand.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I do the Technical Stand-Up in BJJ?

    Wrist pain during the Technical Stand-Up often stems from improper hand placement. If your posting hand is too far forward, your wrist is hyperextended, bearing the full weight of your body without proper support from your shoulder. Ensure your posting hand is directly under your shoulder, creating a stable column of support, and that your fingers are spread wide to distribute pressure.

    Q: How do I do the Technical Stand-Up against a bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, emphasize hip drive and leverage over brute strength. Focus on creating a strong tripod with your posting hand and foot, and drive your hips forward and up aggressively to unweight their base. Use your grips on their collar and sleeve to control their posture and prevent them from easily smothering you as you rise.

    Q: When is the best time to use the Technical Stand-Up in BJJ?

    The Technical Stand-Up is most effective when your opponent is slightly off-balance or when you need to create space to escape a compromising position. It's ideal for resetting from guard when a sweep is initiated, or when you want to transition from a bottom position to a standing engagement, such as setting up a takedown or creating distance to re-establish guard.

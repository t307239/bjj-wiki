    More Questions

    How do I prevent my opponent from stacking me during a pendulum sweep?

    Maintain a strong base and keep your hips low. Use your free leg to push off their hip or thigh, creating space and preventing them from collapsing on you.

    What's the most common mistake people make when attempting a pendulum sweep?

    Often, practitioners don't commit to the hip movement or fail to get their hips high enough. This leads to a weak sweep attempt and makes it easy for the opponent to defend.

    How can I transition to a submission after a successful pendulum sweep?

    Once you've swept them, look to immediately establish side control or mount. From there, you can attack with common submissions like an armbar or kimura, depending on their reaction.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my opponent's hip seem to escape my leg during the BJJ Pendulum Sweep and how can I prevent this?

    Ensure your hooking leg's foot is actively pressing into their hip crease, not just passively resting, and simultaneously use your free leg to drive your knee forward, creating a solid frame that prevents their hip from disengaging.

    Q: How can I generate enough hip elevation to effectively execute the BJJ Pendulum Sweep when my opponent is much larger and heavier than me?

    Focus on driving your hips up by contracting your glutes and lower back, while simultaneously extending your hooking leg forcefully against their hip to create a lever, lifting their weight off the mat.

    Q: What is the correct angle to swing my leg during a BJJ Pendulum Sweep to maximize my chances of sweeping and not just ending up in a bad position?

    Your swinging leg should arc in a wide, horizontal plane, aiming to clear their far leg and drive their hip across your body, rather than a vertical upward motion which often stalls the sweep.

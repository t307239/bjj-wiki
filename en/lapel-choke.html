Lapel Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

This choke is typically initiated from the Closed Guard position, aiming to restrict blood flow to the opponent's brain for submission. White belts often struggle by focusing on raw strength rather than leverage and precise joint angles. The key mechanical insight is using the opponent's own structure against them, creating a tight vise with your forearms and the lapel.

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish closed guard: cross your ankles behind the opponent's lower back, hips tight.
    2. Grip opponent's left lapel with your right hand, thumb inside the collar, palm facing upwards.
    3. Grip opponent's right lapel with your left hand, thumb inside the collar, palm facing upwards.
    4. Arch your upper back, bringing your chest towards the opponent's chest, creating spinal flexion.
    5. Simultaneously, drive your elbows downwards and slightly outwards, pulling the lapels taut across the carotid arteries.
    6. Maintain pressure by squeezing your knees together and pushing your hips forward into the opponent.
    7. Keep your head tight to the opponent's shoulder, preventing them from creating space.
    8. Ensure your forearm on the opponent's neck side is deep and presses against their trachea.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying excessive downward force with your arms without maintaining hip pressure: This can strain your rotator cuffs and shoulder joints, leading to tears. Instead, maintain hip pressure forward and use your arms to create a constricting pressure across the neck.
    • Gripping the lapel too shallowly or with knuckles exposed: This can cause wrist sprains or hyperextension. Grip with your thumb inside the collar, ensuring a secure, deep hold with your knuckles protected.
    • Tensing your neck muscles and looking upwards: This can lead to neck strain or whiplash. Keep your neck relaxed and your head pressed into the opponent's shoulder to maintain a stable base.
    • Arching your back too aggressively without hip engagement: This puts undue stress on your lumbar spine. Focus on driving your hips forward to create the choking angle, not just flexing your upper back.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice lapel gripping and posture breaking on a dummy or a stable object for 20 repetitions.
    2. Solo drill: Practice the full lapel choke sequence on a dummy, focusing on hip drive and arm placement for 20 repetitions.
    3. Partner drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice entering the closed guard and establishing lapel grips for 10 repetitions per side.
    4. Partner drill (25% resistance): Execute the lapel choke from closed guard, allowing the partner to offer minimal resistance to the setup for 10 repetitions.
    5. Partner drill (50% resistance): Practice the lapel choke with a partner who attempts to post their arms or posture up slightly for 10 repetitions.
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Attempt the lapel choke during rolling, focusing on correct mechanics over immediate submission for 5 minutes.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have successfully established closed guard and your opponent has their weight distributed forward.
    • When your opponent attempts to posture up, creating an opportunity to break their posture and establish grips.
    • When you have successfully controlled your opponent's posture and can isolate one side of their neck with your arm.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Posturing Up: The opponent can create space by driving their hips back and extending their arms to break your grip. This requires the attacker to maintain strong hip pressure and use their arms to pull the opponent's posture down.
    • Elbow Escape: The opponent can try to escape the choke by bringing their elbow on the choked side towards their hip, creating space. The attacker must maintain a tight frame with their forearm and prevent this movement.
    • Head and Arm Escape: The opponent can attempt to turn their head away and create space with their free arm. The attacker needs to keep their head tight and use their body to prevent the opponent from turning.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Lapel Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain typically arises from gripping the lapel incorrectly or over-relying on wrist flexion. Ensure you are gripping the lapel with your thumb inside the collar, creating a secure hold that protects your knuckles. The primary force should come from driving your hips forward and squeezing your knees, not just pulling with your wrists.

    Q: How can I effectively apply a Lapel Choke against a much bigger and stronger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paraMount. Focus on breaking their posture by driving your hips forward and pulling their head down with your lapel grips. Instead of trying to muscle the choke, use your body weight and spinal flexion to create a tight seal. Ensure your forearm is deep on their trachea and your knees are squeezed tightly.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to a Lapel Choke if my opponent is defending my closed guard well?

    The ideal time to transition to a Lapel Choke is when your opponent attempts to posture up or create space. As they extend their arms to break your guard, use that moment to secure your lapel grips and immediately drive your hips forward while pulling their head down. This simultaneous action will often catch them off balance and set up the choke effectively.

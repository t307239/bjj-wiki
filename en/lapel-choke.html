The Lapel Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanically Accurate Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

This choke originates from Closed Guard, aiming to submit your opponent by restricting blood flow to the brain.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength instead of precise leverage and body mechanics.

The key is using your hips and the opponent's posture against them, creating a tight choke with minimal effort.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish closed guard by securing your feet on the opponent's hips, knees pointing outwards.
    2. With your left hand, grip the opponent's right lapel approximately 1-2 inches from their collarbone, thumb facing inwards.
    3. With your right hand, grip the opponent's left lapel at a similar depth, thumb facing inwards.
    4. Arch your lower back, lifting your hips slightly off the mat, bringing your knees closer to your chest.
    5. Shift your weight forward, driving your chest into the opponent's sternum, reducing their base.
    6. Begin to tighten the choke by squeezing your elbows inwards towards your ribs, like closing a book.
    7. Simultaneously, extend your legs slightly, pushing your hips forward and up to increase the choking pressure.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Grabbing the opponent's uniform too low on the chest can create slack, preventing choke effectiveness and leading to arm fatigue.
    • This can strain the rotator cuff and biceps by over-extending them laterally.
    • Grip the lapels at the collarbone, ensuring tension is maintained throughout the movement.
    • Trying to pull the opponent's head down with your hands instead of using your hips and chest will strain your neck and shoulders.
    • This puts excessive torque on the cervical spine and trapezius muscles, risking a neck injury.
    • Maintain an upright posture and drive your chest into their chest, using your hips for leverage.
    • Allowing your knees to splay outwards excessively without hip engagement weakens your guard and creates openings.
    • This can lead to knee joint hyperextension or ligamentous strain, especially during sweeps or guard passes.
    • Keep knees tucked in, pointed slightly inwards, and actively squeeze your glutes to maintain a strong guard.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice establishing closed guard and the lapel grip sequence without a partner (50 reps).
    2. Partner assisted drilling (0% resistance): Partner holds still. Focus on grip placement and hip drive (20 reps per side).
    3. Partner assisted drilling (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to grip breaks or posture control. Execute the choke (20 reps per side).
    4. Partner assisted drilling (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to posture up or break grips. Focus on maintaining control and finishing the choke (15 reps per side).
    5. Flow rolling (75% resistance): Apply the choke during positional sparring, but tap early if the submission is not achieved. Focus on timing and setup (5 rounds, 3 min each).
    6. Live rolling (90-100% resistance): Attempt the Lapel Choke during regular sparring. Prioritize technique over outcome (5 rounds, 5 min each).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured low, creating an opportunity to secure tight lapel grips.
    • When the opponent attempts to stack you, and you can use their momentum against them.
    • After successfully defending a guard pass attempt and returning to a stable closed guard.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Grip Break: Opponent uses their free hand to peel your choking hand off the lapel, often by attacking your wrist or fingers.
    • Defense: Maintain tight control with your other hand, use your body to shield the grip, and transition to another submission like an armbar.
    • Posture Up: Opponent drives their hips back and straightens their legs, creating space and relieving pressure.
    • Defense: Immediately squeeze your legs tighter and drive your hips forward, or transition to an armbar as they attempt to escape.
    • Stacking: Opponent drives their weight forward, attempting to crush your chest and break your guard.
    • Defense: Arch your back and push your hips up, using the momentum to create an opening for an armbar or a sweep.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Lapel Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs from gripping the lapel with your palm facing upwards or by over-extending your wrist to compensate for a lack of hip drive. Ensure your thumb is tucked in and your forearm is aligned with the lapel. Focus on driving your hips forward and squeezing your elbows inwards, which uses your larger muscle groups and reduces strain on the wrist.

    Q: How can I do the Lapel Choke from closed guard against a bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, your focus must be on creating a tight seal and using their weight against them. Ensure your closed guard is very tight, preventing them from posturing up. When gripping the lapels, aim for the highest possible point on their collarbone. Drive your hips up aggressively to create the choke, rather than trying to pull them down.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Lapel Choke from closed guard in BJJ?

    The optimal time is when your opponent is slightly off-balance or has committed to a forward movement, such as trying to establish a grip or pass your guard. If they are too upright and stable, it's harder to secure the initial grips and apply effective pressure. Look for moments when they lean in, allowing you to create a strong connection and initiate the choke.

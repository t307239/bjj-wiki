This technique, often initiated from Closed Guard, aims to restrict blood flow to the opponent's brain via their own gi.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength, neglecting the precise angles and pressure needed for a clean submission.

The key is using your hips and shoulders to create a fulcrum, leveraging the opponent's posture against them.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a cross-collar grip with your right hand on the opponent's left lapel, thumb inside the collar.
    2. Simultaneously, secure a standard collar grip with your left hand on the opponent's right lapel, thumb inside.
    3. Bring your knees to your chest, creating a tight closed guard, ensuring your shins are crossed at the ankles.
    4. Arch your lower back, tilting your pelvis upwards towards the ceiling, while keeping your shoulders grounded.
    5. Begin to pull down with your right hand grip and simultaneously push your left elbow towards your own left hip.
    6. Rotate your left shoulder blade towards your right knee, driving your shoulder into the opponent's neck area.
    7. Maintain hip elevation and apply steady pressure with both grips, focusing on the squeeze from your shoulder and triceps.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying the choke by simply yanking the collars: This can strain your rotator cuff ligaments and tendons in the shoulder, leading to tears. Instead, focus on the hip elevation and shoulder drive.
    • Overextending your neck backwards to create space: This puts excessive strain on your cervical spine's intervertebral discs and ligaments, risking disc herniation. Maintain a neutral spine and use your hips to generate power.
    • Gripping too wide on the lapels: This reduces the leverage and can lead to your hands slipping, causing wrist hyperextension and potential ligament damage. Keep your grips snug and deep within the collar.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice the grip setup and hip arch motion 10 times per side, focusing on feel.
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Practice the full sequence from closed guard, 10 repetitions.
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Execute the choke with minimal resistance, 5 successful attempts.
    4. Partner drill (moderate resistance): Focus on applying the choke against a resisting opponent, 5 successful attempts.
    5. Light rolling (focus on position): Attempt the choke from closed guard, aiming for 3 successful submissions.
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the Lapel Choke into your rolling, seeking opportunities and learning from failed attempts.

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is postured up in your closed guard, creating space between your hips and theirs.
    • When the opponent attempts to stack you, driving their weight downwards.
    • When you have secured a strong closed guard and the opponent is reaching inside your guard.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Guard pass attempt (e.g., knee slice pass): If the opponent attempts to pass your guard, you can transition to a sweep or re-guard.
    • Posture breakdown and escape: The opponent can attempt to break your grip and posture up, relieving the pressure and potentially escaping the choke.
    • Framing with forearms: The opponent can frame with their forearms between your chest and their neck to create space and prevent the choke from fully engaging.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Lapel Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often stems from gripping the lapels too loosely or too far out, causing your wrist to bend backwards under pressure. Ensure your grips are deep within the collar, with your thumbs inside, and that you are using your forearm and shoulder to drive the choke, not just your wrist strength. Practicing the correct grip and hip drive without resistance will build the proper muscle memory.

    Q: How can I apply the Lapel Choke against a much bigger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paraMount. Focus on elevating your hips significantly to create a space for your shoulders to drive into their neck. Use your legs to control their posture by keeping your knees tight to your chest, preventing them from posturing up easily. The cross-collar grip is crucial for leverage; ensure it's deep and secure.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Lapel Choke, and when should I avoid it?

    The Lapel Choke is most effective when the opponent's posture is compromised and they are within your guard. Avoid attempting it when the opponent has excellent posture, is actively posturing up, or if you have lost control of your guard and they are posturing outside of your leg control. It's also less effective if they are wearing a very loose gi that allows easy escape from the grips.

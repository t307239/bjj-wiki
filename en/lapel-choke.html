Lapel Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

The Lapel Choke is typically applied from the Closed Guard position. It aims to restrict blood flow to the brain, causing a submission.

White belts often struggle because they rely on brute strength rather than leverage. This leads to inefficient movement and potential injury.

The key is to use your hips and the opponent's own posture against them to create the choking pressure.

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish closed guard: Sit on the opponent's hips, cross your ankles behind their back, ensuring your knees are tight.
    2. Grip 1 (Right Hand): Reach across your opponent's chest and grip their right lapel, thumb inside the collar, approximately 1-2 inches from the center seam.
    3. Grip 2 (Left Hand): Reach across your opponent's chest and grip their left lapel, thumb inside the collar, mirroring the right hand's placement.
    4. Posture Control: Pull your opponent's upper body down towards your chest by using your arms to draw their collars.
    5. Hip Elevation: Simultaneously, slightly lift your hips off the mat, driving your knees forward and into their armpits.
    6. Choke Application: With your opponent pulled down and your hips elevated, drive your elbows down and slightly outwards. This cinches the lapels across their carotid arteries.
    7. Maintain Pressure: Keep your knees tight and your hips elevated, ensuring consistent pressure on the lapels.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect collar grip: Grabbing the lapel with fingers outside the collar can lead to the grip slipping and an ineffective choke. The thumb-inside grip provides a secure anchor.
    • Excessive neck cranking: Pulling directly on the opponent's head or neck instead of cinching the lapels can strain the cervical spine and neck muscles. Focus on driving elbows down and out.
    • Knee pressure too wide: Spreading knees too far apart reduces hip elevation and control, making it difficult to generate choking pressure. Keep knees tight to the opponent's armpits.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling (0% resistance): Practice establishing closed guard, performing the lapel grips, and simulating the hip elevation and elbow drive motion for 10 repetitions per side.
    2. Partner drilling (25% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice establishing closed guard and applying the lapel grips. Partner offers minimal resistance to guard entry and posture.
    3. Partner drilling (50% resistance): Practice the full sequence from guard establishment to choke application. Partner offers light resistance to posture control and attempts to create space.
    4. Partner drilling (75% resistance): Execute the technique against a partner who actively attempts to posture up and defend the grips. Focus on maintaining control and finishing the choke.
    5. Live rolling (90% resistance): Attempt the Lapel Choke during sparring. Focus on recognizing opportunities and executing the mechanics smoothly, prioritizing control over immediate submission.
    6. Live rolling (100% resistance): Apply the technique in full sparring. Work on timing, reaction to defense, and refining the choke for submission.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured up and their head is within reach.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard by standing or kneeling.
    • When you have established strong control of the opponent's upper body and they are struggling to create space.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Escape the choke: If the choke is being applied, attempt to create space by driving your hips up and away, and simultaneously framing with your forearms against the opponent's biceps to break their posture.
    • Grip break: If the opponent has a strong grip, use your free hand to peel their fingers off your lapel, or use your knuckles to push into their knuckles to break the grip.
    • Posture up: If the opponent is struggling to maintain posture, actively try to stand up or drive your hips forward to break their connection and create space to defend.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Lapel Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you grip the lapel too tightly with your fingers extended, putting excessive strain on the carpal bones and ligaments. Instead, ensure your thumb is inside the collar, creating a more natural and powerful grip. Also, focus on driving your elbows down and slightly out to cinch the choke, rather than just pulling with your wrists.

    Q: How can I apply a Lapel Choke effectively against a much bigger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paraMount. Focus on cutting off their posture by pulling their head down with your grips. Simultaneously, elevate your hips aggressively, driving your knees into their armpits to create a tight seal. This combination prevents them from using their size advantage to simply power out and allows the lapels to effectively constrict.

    Q: I can't seem to get the Lapel Choke to work, is there a specific timing issue?

    The most common timing issue is trying to apply the choke before you've fully controlled the opponent's posture. You need to establish a strong, tight closed guard first, then break their posture down. Once they are pulled in and their base is compromised, the hip elevation and elbow drive will generate the choking pressure. Don't rush the choke; focus on the setup.

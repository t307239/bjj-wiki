The Kimura: A White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

The Kimura is a powerful shoulder lock typically initiated from guard or Side Control, aiming to hyperextend the opponent's glenohumeral joint. White belts often struggle due to a lack of control and excessive force, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury. The key is isolating the opponent's arm and using your body to create a strong lever, not just brute strength.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From closed guard, secure your opponent's right arm across their body. Place your left arm over their elbow, gripping your own right wrist (the "figure-four" grip).
    2. Your left forearm should be snug against the outside of their upper arm, perpendicular to it.
    3. Shift your hips slightly to your right, creating a slight angle away from your opponent.
    4. Bring your opponent's trapped arm across their chest towards your own body, keeping your grip firm.
    5. Arch your back slightly, raising your hips off the mat to create upward pressure on their elbow.
    6. Simultaneously, rotate your torso counter-clockwise, driving your left elbow down and back.
    7. Apply continuous, controlled pressure by extending your hips upwards and pulling their elbow towards your sternum, creating a rotational force on the shoulder joint.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying the Kimura by "pulling hard" with your arms: This can lead to biceps tears or elbow hyperextension for both you and your opponent.
    • The correct movement involves using your hips and core to generate leverage, not just arm strength.
    • Not isolating the arm properly, allowing the opponent to turn into the submission: This can result in shoulder dislocations or rotator cuff tears.
    • Ensure your figure-four grip is tight and your body frames the opponent's trapped arm, preventing them from rotating freely.
    • Extending your own elbow during the submission: This risks elbow ligament damage (e.g., UCL sprain).
    • Keep a slight bend in your own elbow throughout the movement, focusing the pressure on the opponent's shoulder joint.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling of the figure-four grip and hip movement from closed guard (10 reps per side).
    2. Partner drilling of the grip setup and hip angle without applying pressure (20 reps per side).
    3. Partner drilling with controlled, slow application of pressure, stopping immediately if resistance is felt (30 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Drilling the Kimura setup and submission from side control, focusing on body positioning (20 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Live rolling, attempting the Kimura from guard when opportunities arise, focusing on setup and timing (5 minutes, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling, actively hunting for the Kimura from various positions, aiming for successful tap-outs (5 minutes, 90% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • From closed guard when the opponent stacks you heavily, creating space to trap an arm.
    • When an opponent attempts to pass your guard and leaves an arm exposed.
    • From side control when you establish a strong cross-face and isolate an arm.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • "Turtling" or "Shelling": If you feel the Kimura being applied, tuck your chin, bring your knees to your chest, and make yourself as compact as possible to prevent arm isolation and leverage.
    • The "Roll": If the opponent is applying the Kimura and you can maintain control of your arm, attempt to roll in the direction of the submission to relieve pressure and potentially escape.
    • "Stacking" the opponent: If you are in guard and the Kimura is being set up, drive your hips forward and stack your weight onto the opponent's shoulders, collapsing their base and disrupting their leverage.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Kimura?

    Wrist pain during a Kimura often occurs when you grip your own wrist too tightly or at an awkward angle. Ensure your figure-four grip is firm but not strained. The primary force should come from your hips and core, not by muscling the lock with your arms and wrists. Focus on rotating your torso and driving your hips up.

    Q: How can I do a Kimura against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paraMount. Focus on isolating their arm completely and ensuring your figure-four grip is secure. Use your hips to create a strong lever; drive them up and slightly away from their body. Your body positioning and hip angle are more important than trying to match their strength directly.

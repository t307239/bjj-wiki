The Kimura: A White Belt's Guide to BJJ Joint Lock Mastery

The Kimura is a powerful shoulder lock typically initiated from guard or Side Control, aiming to hyperextend the opponent's arm at the shoulder joint. White belts often rush the finish, neglecting the setup and control necessary for a clean submission.

The key to a successful Kimura is isolating the opponent's arm and controlling their posture, preventing them from posturing up or rotating out of the submission.

Understanding the rotational mechanics of the shoulder and how to leverage your body to apply torque is crucial for a safe and effective Kimura.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From closed guard, secure an "over-under" grip on the opponent's arm: your right arm wraps under their armpit, and your left arm goes over their bicep, grabbing your own right wrist. Your thumbs should be interlocked or clasped.
    2. Maintain tight hip control, keeping your knees pinched and your hips close to their hips. Your body should be angled slightly towards their trapped arm.
    3. Begin to establish a frame with your free arm (left arm in this example) against their hip or chest to prevent them from posturing up and creating space.
    4. Shift your hips to your left, creating a slight angle. This rotation helps to break their posture and isolate their shoulder.
    5. "Walk" your feet up their back towards their shoulders, moving from the closed guard to a position where your legs are open and your hips are elevated, further controlling their posture.
    6. With your hips still angled to your left, bring the opponent's trapped arm across their body, towards your opposite hip.
    7. Secure the kimura grip: your left hand grabs your right wrist, with your right thumb pointing upwards. Your right elbow should be bent, tucking their wrist towards your chest.
    8. Apply upward pressure with your left hand on their wrist while simultaneously driving your hips forward and slightly upward, creating rotational force on their shoulder.
    9. Maintain pressure on their shoulder by keeping your body tight and aligned with the direction of the lock. Do not pull with your arms; drive with your hips and core.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to "pull" the opponent's arm into the submission without proper body positioning: This can lead to tearing of the rotator cuff ligaments (e.g., supraspinatus, infraspinatus) due to uncontrolled external rotation and abduction.
    • Forcing the lock by cranking the arm upwards without isolating the shoulder: This can cause dislocation of the glenohumeral joint or damage to the labrum.
    • Not controlling the opponent's posture and allowing them to "stack" you: This can result in hyperextension of your own elbow if they drive their weight down on the locked arm, potentially damaging the ulnar collateral ligament.
    • Gripping too loosely on the kimura setup, allowing the opponent to escape their arm early: This can lead to the opponent driving their elbow into your face or ribs, causing facial contusions or rib fractures.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling the grip and body mechanics: Practice the over-under grip transition and the hip rotation motion without a partner. (10 reps each side)
    2. Partner drill - static position: With a compliant partner, practice securing the kimura grip from closed guard and executing the hip shift and arm isolation. Partner offers no resistance. (10 reps each side)
    3. Partner drill - slight resistance: Partner attempts to defend by posturing slightly or shrugging their shoulders. You focus on maintaining control and completing the hip rotation. (20 reps each side, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner drill - guard recovery resistance: Partner tries to break posture and create space. You focus on maintaining the kimura grip and completing the sequence. (20 reps each side, 50% resistance)
    5. Live rolling - controlled attempts: During sparring, actively look for opportunities to initiate the kimura from closed guard. Focus on the setup and control, not necessarily the finish. (3 rounds, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling - full attempts: Apply the kimura technique with full intent, focusing on the biomechanical execution and understanding when the submission is available. (5 rounds, 90% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When an opponent is postured up in your closed guard, leaving their arm accessible.
    • When you have achieved a strong over-under grip and have control of their elbow.
    • When transitioning from a failed sweep or submission attempt and you can secure the arm.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Posture Up and Shrimp: If the opponent attempts the Kimura from closed guard, immediately break your posture by sitting up and shrimping your hips away to create space and relieve pressure.
    • Grip Release and Escape: If the Kimura grip is secured, focus on breaking their grip on your wrist by rotating your wrist outwards and simultaneously driving your elbow towards your body to create space.
    • Roll Through to Top: If the opponent has a strong Kimura grip and you cannot escape, you can attempt to roll them over your head to end up in a top position, potentially reversing the submission.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to do a Kimura?

    Your shoulder likely hurts because you are trying to force the lock by pulling your opponent's arm upwards without isolating their shoulder joint properly. This creates shearing forces on the rotator cuff. Ensure you are controlling their elbow and wrist, driving your hips forward to create a rotational torque at the shoulder, rather than just pulling.

    Q: How can I do a Kimura against a much bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on the biomechanical advantage of leverage. Ensure you are tightly controlling their posture with your legs and hips, preventing them from using their size to simply muscle out. Use your hip rotation to create the angle and isolate their arm, then drive your hips forward to apply the leverage. A tight over-under grip is paraMount.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Kimura from closed guard?

    The ideal time to attempt a Kimura from Closed Guard is when your opponent postures up, creating an opening for you to secure the over-under grip. If they are too low or too far away, the setup is difficult and risky. Look for moments when they are trying to break your guard or pass, as this often leads to them exposing an arm.

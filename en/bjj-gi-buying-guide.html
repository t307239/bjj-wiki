BJJ Gi Buying Guide 2026: How to Choose the Right Kimono

With hundreds of gi options on the market, choosing the right one is overwhelming. This guide cuts through the noise with what actually matters for beginners and experienced grapplers.

Gi Weave: Single vs. Double vs. Pearl

Single weave: Lightest (1.0–1.2 kg), best for hot gyms and travel, lower durability. Double weave: Heaviest, most durable, less comfortable. Pearl weave: The sweet spot — most popular for a reason. Moderate weight (1.4–1.7 kg), excellent durability, comfortable texture. Most competition-legal.

Gi Weight: What Matters

If you compete: Lighter is better — reduces weight before weigh-in. The ideal competition gi is under 1.5 kg. If you just train: Mid-weight (1.5–1.8 kg) balances durability and comfort. A 2.0+ kg gi will wear you down in summer training.

Size Guide

Gis typically run A0–A5 for men, F1–F5 for women. Gis shrink significantly in warm washes — buy for your post-shrink size (check each brand's shrink chart). The jacket should reach mid-thigh, sleeves end 5cm from the wrist. Pants should be 5cm from the ankle.

Brands Worth Considering

Budget ($50–80): Sanabul, Venum, Tatami Estilo. Mid-range ($80–130): Fuji, Atama, Do or Die, Kingz. Premium ($130–200): Shoyoroll, Fuji All-Around, Inverted Gear Panda. For kids: Tatami or Fuji are reliable choices.

IBJJF Legal Requirements

Competition gi must be white, blue, or black. No patches in prohibited areas. Collar must be graspable (2–3 fingers). Ensure the gi is IBJJF approved if you plan to compete.

❓ FAQ

What is the best gi for beginners?

The Tatami Estilo and Fuji All-Around are consistently recommended for beginners: good durability, affordable, IBJJF legal, and available in most sizes.

How much should I spend on my first gi?

Budget $60–100 for a reliable first gi. Anything cheaper often falls apart within months of regular training. Spending $150+ for a first gi is unnecessary.

How do I wash my BJJ gi?

Cold wash (30°C) extends gi life significantly. Hang dry rather than tumble dry to control shrinkage and prevent color fading. Wash after every training session.

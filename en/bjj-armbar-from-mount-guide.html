    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: I'm having trouble keeping my opponent's arm isolated when I try the armbar from mount, what am I doing wrong?

    Ensure your hips are heavy and pressed into their chest, creating a strong base to prevent them from bridging. Crucially, use your opposite arm to actively control their far shoulder by cupping it, preventing them from turning their body into the submission.

    Q: When I go for the armbar from mount, my opponent always seems to be able to pull their arm out by turning their elbow. How can I prevent this?

    As you swing your leg over, your knee should drive *down* on their shoulder blade, not just their head. This action pinches their shoulder joint and restricts the rotation needed to escape the elbow-out position.

    Q: I feel like I'm not getting enough leverage on the armbar from mount, and my opponent can just muscle out. What body mechanics should I focus on?

    Instead of just lifting your hips, focus on bridging *upwards* while simultaneously pulling their wrist towards your opposite hip. This creates a fulcrum with your hips and leverages the strength of your core and back, rather than relying solely on leg strength.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How do I prevent my opponent from stacking me during an armbar from mount?

    To prevent stacking, maintain tight hip pressure and keep your hips low. You can also try to walk your hips to the side, creating an angle that makes it harder for them to drive their weight down.

    What if my opponent tries to roll out of the armbar from mount?

    If they try to roll, control their hips and head to prevent them from completing the roll. You might need to adjust your body position to follow their movement and maintain control of the arm.

    When is the best time to transition to an armbar from mount?

    The best time is when your opponent is actively trying to escape or is posturing up, creating an opening. Look for opportunities when they expose their arm or try to push your hips away.

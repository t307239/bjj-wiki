How to Care for Your BJJ Gi: Washing, Drying & Maintenance

Contents

    Washing Your Gi: The Basics

    Wash your gi inside-out in cold water with mild detergent. Hot water causes excess shrinkage and fading. Use gentle cycle or hand wash for delicate fabrics. Avoid bleach and fabric softeners — they weaken fibers. Wash separately from other clothes to prevent color bleeding. If your academy requires washing after every session, buy 2-3 gis so they have time to dry.

    Stain Removal

    For blood stains, rinse immediately in cold water (hot water sets the stain). For grass or mat stains, use white vinegar or hydrogen peroxide before washing. For sweat stains, soak in cold water with oxygen-based cleaner. Treat stains before washing — detergent won't remove set-in stains. Some stains fade over time with repeated washing.

    Shrinkage Prevention

    Most gis shrink 2-4% after first wash. Cold water reduces shrinkage compared to hot water. Air dry whenever possible — dryer heat causes additional shrinkage. If you must use a dryer, use low heat. Hang-dry gis lay flat or hang from shoulders to prevent stretching. A quality gi properly cared for will fit for years.

    Proper Drying Methods

    Air drying is best — hang your gi in shade or indoors. Direct sunlight can fade colors. Avoid hanging by the collar — use hangers that support the jacket or pants. Ensure your gi is completely dry before storing — moisture promotes mildew and odor. A gi takes 24-48 hours to dry fully depending on humidity.

    Storage Tips

    Store gis in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight. Use breathable storage bags, not plastic bags — plastic traps moisture. Consider cedar blocks or mothballs if storing for extended periods. Keep gis away from pets and high-traffic areas where they can get snagged. Fold neatly or hang to prevent wrinkles and creases.

    Dealing with Gi Odor

    Wash your gi soon after training — sweat and bacteria cause odor. If your gi smells even after washing, soak in white vinegar and water (1:3 ratio) for 30 minutes before washing. Air dry in sunlight if possible — sunlight naturally kills odor-causing bacteria. Never use fabric softener, which traps odors. Baking soda can be sprinkled on gis before washing to help with odor.

    Repairing Common Damage

    Small tears should be repaired immediately to prevent them from spreading. Use needle and thread or a sewing machine for small seams. For large tears, use a fabric patch or take it to a tailor. Loose buttons can be reinforced with extra thread. Weak collar stitching is common — reinforce with extra stitches or take to a tailor.

    Gi Longevity Tips

    Rotate multiple gis to extend the life of each one — washing and drying is hard on fabric. Have at least 2-3 gis so one is always drying while you train. Avoid excessive washing if your academy allows it. Quality gis with proper care last 5-10 years. Treat your gi with respect — it's an investment in your BJJ journey.

    More Questions

    How often should I wash my BJJ gi?

    You should wash your gi after every training session. Sweat and bacteria can build up quickly, leading to odors and potential skin infections if not cleaned regularly.

    Can I put my gi in the dryer?

    It's best to air dry your gi whenever possible to prevent shrinkage and preserve the fabric's integrity. If you must use a dryer, use the lowest heat setting and remove it while slightly damp.

    My gi is shrinking, what am I doing wrong?

    Shrinkage is usually caused by washing in hot water and drying on high heat. Always use cold water for washing and opt for air drying or the lowest heat setting on your dryer to minimize shrinkage.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: I just got my first BJJ gi and I'm worried about shrinking it too much when I wash it, what's the best way to wash a BJJ gi to avoid shrinking?

    To minimize shrinkage, always wash your gi in cold water on a gentle cycle. Avoid using a high-heat dryer; instead, air dry your gi by hanging it on a hanger or laying it flat, which preserves the fabric's original dimensions and prevents excessive heat from causing the fibers to contract.

    Q: My BJJ gi smells really bad after a few training sessions, even after washing it. What's the best way to get rid of the smell and keep it fresh?

    For persistent odors, pre-soak your gi in a solution of one part white vinegar to four parts cold water for 30 minutes before washing. This acidic solution helps break down odor-causing bacteria. Always wash immediately after training and air dry completely to prevent mildew growth, which is a primary source of bad smells.

    Q: I've noticed my BJJ gi is getting a bit stiff and rough after a few months. How can I maintain the softness and flexibility of my gi over time?

    To maintain softness, use a fabric softener sparingly, as excessive use can weaken the gi's fibers and affect its grip. Instead, consider adding half a cup of baking soda to your wash cycle along with your regular detergent; baking soda acts as a natural fabric softener and deodorizer. Always air dry, as high heat from a dryer can make the fabric brittle and stiff.

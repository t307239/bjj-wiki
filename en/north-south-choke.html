North-South Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Purple Belt Requirements

This choke is applied from the North-South position, aiming to cut off blood flow to the brain via the carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength, neglecting proper body mechanics for leverage.

Success hinges on controlling the opponent's head and hips while compressing their neck with your forearm and bicep.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish North-South position: Your chest is on the opponent's chest, head near their head, hips low.
    2. Secure the collar grip: Right hand (if right-handed) grips the opponent's far collar lapel, thumb inside, fingers outside.
    3. Secure the arm-trap grip: Left arm slides under the opponent's head, bicep pressing against their jawline, forearm across their neck.
    4. Drive your hips forward: Lower your hips towards the opponent's hips, creating a tight connection.
    5. Rotate your hips: Angle your hips 45 degrees towards your right side (if right-handed).
    6. Apply shoulder pressure: Drive your left shoulder into the opponent's upper chest/clavicle area.
    7. Extend your hips and squeeze: Simultaneously extend your hips and squeeze your biceps and forearms to apply pressure.
    8. Maintain head control: Keep your head tight to the opponent's head to prevent them from turning into you.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Trying to force the choke by pushing with your hands: This strains the rotator cuff and shoulder capsule, risking tears.

    Correct: Drive your hips forward and rotate your body for leverage.

    • Arching your back excessively to create space: This puts immense stress on the lumbar spine, leading to disc compression or strains.

    Correct: Keep your spine neutral and use hip extension.

    • Gripping the lapel too loosely or with fingers exposed: This allows the opponent to easily peel your grip, wasting energy and creating an opening.

    Correct: Ensure a deep thumb-in grip on the far collar.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo visualization: Mentally rehearse the steps, focusing on hip angles and arm placement. (10 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner positioning drill: Have a partner lie in the North-South position. Practice establishing grips and hip control without pressure. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    3. Light pressure drill: Apply the choke with minimal pressure, focusing on the correct body mechanics and feeling the mechanics engage. (20 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled choke drill: Execute the choke with moderate pressure, allowing your partner to tap early. Focus on maintaining control. (20 reps, 50% resistance)
    5. Resistance drill: Your partner offers light, passive resistance, allowing you to practice finishing the choke against a moving opponent. (15 reps, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the choke during rolling, prioritizing correct technique over immediate submission. (3 x 5-minute rounds, 90% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you achieve a dominant North-South position after a guard pass or sweep.
    • When your opponent attempts to bridge or escape from side control, and you can transition.
    • When you have secured a strong cross-face and are controlling their head.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape and Bridge: The opponent attempts to shrimp their hips away and bridge up, creating space to relieve pressure.
    • Head and Arm Turn-In: The opponent turns their head and shoulders into your choking arm, attempting to relieve the pressure.
    • Guard Recovery from Side Control: If the opponent transitions to side control, the choke is no longer applicable and they can attempt to recover guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the North-South Choke?

    Wrist pain often stems from gripping the lapel incorrectly. Ensure your thumb is inside the collar with your fingers outside, creating a stable grip. Avoid extending your wrist beyond a neutral position. Focus on driving your forearm and bicep into their neck, using your body's rotation for power, not just wrist flexion.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Do not try to muscle the choke. Focus on getting your hips underneath theirs and driving your weight forward. Ensure your choking arm's bicep is firmly against their jaw. Rotate your hips and shoulders to create the compression, rather than trying to pull them down.

    Q: I keep losing the North-South Choke because my opponent escapes their head. What am I doing wrong?

    If your opponent is escaping their head, you are likely not controlling it effectively. Ensure your choking arm's bicep is pressed firmly into their jaw and your forearm is across their neck. Keep your head tight to theirs, preventing them from turning into you. Drive your shoulder into their upper chest to maintain positional control.

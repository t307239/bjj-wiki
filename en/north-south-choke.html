North-South Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

The North-South Choke is a submission typically initiated from the North-South position, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain via the carotid arteries. White belts often struggle due to a lack of positional control and incorrect pressure application, leading to ineffective chokes and potential injury. The key to this choke is establishing a tight shoulder-to-neck seal and using your body weight effectively to create the necessary cranial compression.

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish North-South position with your chest directly over your opponent's chest, head facing their feet.
    2. Secure a deep cross-collar grip with your right hand (or left, depending on your dominant side) on their left lapel, thumb inside.
    3. Place your left hand (or right) on their right lapel, thumb inside, creating a strong grip.
    4. Drive your left shoulder into their neck, specifically targeting the carotid artery on the side of your collar grip.
    5. Bring your right hip down and slightly forward, sinking it towards their hip, creating a 'hip-to-hip' connection.
    6. Extend your legs back, creating a stable base and using your toes to anchor into the mat.
    7. Apply pressure by leaning your upper body weight forward and slightly to the side of your collar grip, compressing their neck.
    8. Squeeze your elbows inward, further tightening the choke by pulling your gripping hands towards each other.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying pressure solely with your arms: This can lead to elbow hyperextension and strain on the shoulder joint, potentially damaging the rotator cuff.
    • Incorrect shoulder placement: If your shoulder is too high or too low, it won't effectively seal the carotid artery, leading to a weak choke and potential neck strain.
    • Allowing opponent to bridge or shrimp: If your base is too narrow or your hips aren't connected, they can create space and escape, risking a knee or ankle injury from awkward pressure.
    • Gripping too shallow on the collar: This reduces the leverage and ability to create cranial compression, potentially leading to wrist strain as you try to compensate.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling of grip acquisition and shoulder placement (10 reps per side).
    2. Partner drilling of positional setup and grip establishment with 0% resistance.
    3. Partner drilling of full choke execution with 25% resistance, focusing on weight distribution.
    4. Partner drilling with 50% resistance, emphasizing tight seals and controlled pressure.
    5. Partner drilling with 75% resistance, focusing on maintaining the choke even with some resistance.
    6. Live rolling (rolling) scenario, attempting the choke when the position is achieved (full resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent turtles up and you can establish North-South control.
    • After successfully passing guard and landing in the North-South position.
    • When your opponent attempts a sweep from bottom mount and you can transition to North-South.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Elbow escape: The opponent tucks their elbows tightly to their body and attempts to bridge their hips, creating space.
    • Shrimp and turn: The opponent shrims their hips away and turns their body to face you, creating an escape angle.
    • Grip break and posture up: The opponent uses their hands to break your collar grips and attempts to sit up and regain guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the North-South Choke?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you are not getting a deep enough collar grip. If your grip is shallow, you compensate by overextending your wrist to try and create pressure. Ensure your hand is deep on the lapel, thumb inside, and focus on driving your shoulder into their neck and using your body weight. This transfers the pressure away from your wrist.

Mastering the North-South Choke in BJJ: A White Belt's Guide

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Purple Belt Requirements

The North-South Choke is a powerful submission applied from the North-South positional control, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain.

White belts often struggle because they focus on brute strength rather than leverage and proper body alignment.

The key is to create a strong frame with your arms and use your hips to drive their head into your forearm, compressing the carotid arteries.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Achieve North-South position: Your chest is on their chest, hips are lower than theirs, and your head is facing their head.
    2. Secure the Cross-Collar Grip: Reach your right hand under their left armpit and grip their left lapel, thumb inside the collar.
    3. Secure the Secondary Grip: Place your left hand on top of your right forearm or grip their right lapel, thumb inside.
    4. Adjust Hip Angle: Lower your hips towards the mat, creating a slight angle where your left hip is closer to their head than your right hip.
    5. Drive and Compress: Drive your left shoulder into their upper chest/neck area, and simultaneously pull your elbows down and slightly back.
    6. Extend and Squeeze: Straighten your back and push your hips forward slightly while squeezing your elbows together to tighten the choke.
    7. Maintain Head Control: Keep your head down and slightly turned towards their head to prevent them from turning into you.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Trying to power the choke by arching your back excessively: This can strain your lower back ligaments and cause muscle tears.

    The correct movement is to drive your hips forward and compress with your arms and upper body, not to hyperextend your spine.

    • Gripping too loosely on the lapel or gripping the fabric instead of the collar: This reduces the effectiveness of the choke and can lead to a failed submission, allowing them to escape.

    Always ensure a deep grip on the lapel fabric itself, thumb inside the collar, for maximum compression.

    • Allowing their head to move freely: If their head is not secured, they can easily relieve the pressure and escape the choke.

    Maintain constant head-to-head contact and use your shoulder to pin their head to the mat.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling of grips and body positioning: 10 repetitions each side, 0% resistance.
    2. Partner drilling of grip setup and hip adjustment: 10 repetitions each side, 10% resistance (partner passively allows setup).
    3. Partner drilling of full choke mechanics without pressure: 10 repetitions each side, 25% resistance (partner offers mild resistance to hip adjustment).
    4. Partner drilling with controlled head and arm pressure: 10 repetitions each side, 50% resistance (partner defends passively).
    5. Controlled submission attempts from North-South: 5 repetitions each side, 75% resistance (partner actively attempts to defend).
    6. Live rolling attempts from North-South position: 5 minutes, 90% resistance (focus on seeking the opportunity).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you achieve North-South position and your opponent is flat on their back.
    • When you have good chest-to-chest pressure and control of their upper body.
    • When they attempt to bridge or shrimp away, creating an opening to establish the choke.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape/Shrimping: As the attacker initiates the choke, the defender should immediately shrimp their hips away, creating space to relieve pressure and potentially escape the position.
    • Framing with Arms: The defender can attempt to create frames with their forearms between their neck and the attacker's forearm, preventing direct carotid compression.
    • Bridging and Turning: The defender can attempt a powerful bridge, turning their hips into the attacker, and then turning their head away to break the grip and escape.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the North-South Choke?

    Wrist pain in the North-South Choke often stems from gripping the lapel incorrectly. If your hand is angled too sharply or you're gripping the very edge of the fabric, you're putting undue stress on your wrist joint. Ensure your thumb is deep inside the collar and your grip is firm on the fabric, allowing your forearm to bear the pressure, not just your wrist.

    Q: How can I apply the North-South Choke against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise body mechanics are paraMount. Focus on establishing a strong chest-to-chest connection to minimize their size advantage. Drive your hips low and angled, using your weight to pin them. Your secondary grip should be on their opposite lapel, creating a tight cross-face pressure that limits their ability to create space.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to the North-South Choke from other positions?

    The most opportune moment to transition to the North-South Choke is when you've successfully passed their guard and landed in a dominant North-South position. It's also effective when an opponent attempts a poor escape from Side Control or mount, inadvertently giving you the angle needed to secure the choke. Look for moments of positional instability from your opponent.

The North-South Choke is applied from the North-South position, aiming to submit an opponent who is on their back. White belts often struggle due to poor weight distribution and insufficient head control.

The key to this choke is isolating the carotid artery by creating a powerful wedge with your shoulder and controlling head and hip separation.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Secure the North-South position: Your chest should be perpendicular to your opponent's chest, with your head near their hip.
    2. Establish the cross-collar grip: Place your right hand deep inside your opponent's left collar, thumb pointing towards their spine.
    3. Secure the secondary grip: With your left hand, grab your right wrist or forearm firmly, creating a strong connection.
    4. Lower your chest: Drive your chest down onto your opponent's upper chest, compressing their diaphragm.
    5. Angle your hips: Rotate your hips 45 degrees towards your opponent's head, creating a wedge with your right shoulder under their chin.
    6. Maintain head pressure: Keep your head tight to their head, preventing them from turning in.
    7. Apply the choke: Pull your right elbow down towards your hip while simultaneously driving your left forearm into their neck, squeezing the carotids.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying the choke by simply pulling your arms apart: This strains your shoulders and elbows, risking rotator cuff tears and bicep tendonitis. The correct action is to drive your chest down and pull your right elbow towards your hip, using your body weight.
    • Not controlling the opponent's head: Failing to keep your head tight to their head allows them to create space and escape, and can lead to neck crank injuries if you apply pressure incorrectly. Always maintain head-to-head contact or pressure.
    • Incorrect hip angle: Having your hips too square or too far away from the opponent's head reduces the effectiveness of the shoulder wedge and makes the choke easier to defend. Your hips should be angled towards their head, creating a tight seal.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling (no resistance): Practice transitioning to North-South and executing the grip and squeeze mechanics with 100 perfect repetitions.
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice securing the grips and applying the choke with 50 perfect repetitions.
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance - 25%): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to focus on precise body mechanics and grip security for 50 repetitions.
    4. Partner drilling (moderate resistance - 50%): Your partner actively tries to defend by creating frames or turning, but without aggressive counter-attacks, for 50 repetitions.
    5. Partner drilling (high resistance - 75%): Your partner defends more actively, forcing you to adjust your weight and leverage for 50 repetitions.
    6. Live rolling (100%): Attempt the North-South Choke when the opportunity arises in sparring, focusing on timing and setup for 5 minutes.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have successfully transitioned to the North-South position after a guard pass or sweep.
    • When your opponent is turtled and you can establish side control, then transition to North-South.
    • When your opponent attempts to recover guard from side control and you can flatten them out to North-South.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Escape to Turtle: If you feel the choke being set up, immediately tuck your chin and roll onto your knees and hands, creating a turtle position to relieve pressure.
    • Hip Escape to Guard Recovery: If the choke is not fully locked in, aggressively hip escape away from the choking arm and attempt to bring your knee between you and the attacker to recover guard.
    • Bridge and Turn: A powerful bridge towards the choking arm, combined with a turn, can create enough space to break the grip or escape the position.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try the North-South Choke?

    Neck pain often occurs when you're not isolating the carotid arteries correctly and instead applying pressure to the trachea (windpipe). Ensure your choking arm's forearm is across the side of their neck, not directly across their throat, and that your secondary grip is tight to control the head. The pressure should come from the squeeze of your arms and the drive of your chest, not a direct ramming motion.

    Q: How do I apply the North-South Choke against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise mechanics are crucial. Focus on driving your chest weight onto their upper body to flatten them and prevent them from bridging. Angle your hips significantly towards their head to create a strong shoulder wedge under their chin. Ensure your cross-collar grip is deep and your secondary grip is tight to prevent them from creating space with their frames.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to North-South Choke from side control?

    The ideal time is when your opponent is trying to recover guard by turning into you or bridging. As they create that movement, you can flatten them out and transition to the North-South position. If they remain flat on their back, you can transition by sliding your hips towards their head and establishing the North-South orientation before attacking the choke.

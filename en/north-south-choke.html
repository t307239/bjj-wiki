North-South Choke: A Beginner's Guide to BJJ

The North-South Choke is applied from the north-south position, aiming to submit an opponent by compressing their carotid arteries. White belts often struggle by relying on raw strength rather than leverage. The key is to use your body weight and hip angle to create a crushing pressure, not just your arms.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From north-south position, secure a Gable grip with your right hand on the opponent's left collar and your left hand on their right collar.
    2. Place your left shoulder blade against the opponent's chin and jawline, ensuring a tight seal.
    3. Drive your hips down and slightly forward, angling your pelvis towards their head.
    4. Simultaneously, pull your elbows down towards your own hips, creating a strong shearing force.
    5. Rotate your hips counter-clockwise, further tightening the choke by pressing your shoulder into their neck.
    6. Maintain constant downward pressure with your chest and hips, preventing them from bridging or creating space.
    7. Keep your head tight to their chest or shoulder to prevent them from turning into you.
    8. As they tap, maintain the pressure for an additional second to ensure the submission is clear.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying the choke by "pulling hard" with only your arms can strain your shoulder joints (rotator cuff impingement) and elbows (epicondylitis). The correct method involves driving your hips down and forward to use your body weight.
    • Trying to force the choke by arching your back excessively puts undue stress on your lumbar spine, risking disc compression or ligament sprains. Instead, focus on a controlled hip drive and torso flexion.
    • Grabbing the collar too high or too low without proper shoulder placement can lead to ineffective pressure and allow the opponent to escape, while also risking wrist hyperextension if you over-grip. Ensure your shoulder is firmly against their neck and your grip is at the sternal notch area.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the grip placement and hip drive motion 20 times per side (0% resistance).
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): From north-south, your partner remains completely still. Execute the choke 10 times, focusing on perfect form (0% resistance).
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the pressure. Execute 8 times (25% resistance).
    4. Partner drill (medium resistance): Partner actively tries to defend by posturing up slightly. Execute 6 times, working through their initial defense (50% resistance).
    5. Partner drill (heavy resistance): Partner actively attempts to escape. Execute 4 times, focusing on maintaining control and pressure (75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the North-South Choke when the opportunity arises during sparring (100% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have established solid north-south control and your opponent is turtled or trying to bridge.
    • When your opponent attempts to shrimp away from your side control, creating an opportunity to transition to north-south.
    • When you've successfully passed their guard and can isolate their head and arm in the north-south orientation.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Bridging: The opponent bridges their hips towards your head, attempting to create space and roll you over. This is countered by driving your hips down and forward, maintaining a wide base with your legs, and keeping your chest tight to their body.
    • Shrimping/Re-guarding: The opponent attempts to create space by shrimping their hips away and re-establishing guard. This is countered by collapsing your base, driving your knee into their hip, and maintaining upper body pressure to prevent them from creating distance.
    • Turning In: The opponent attempts to turn their body into your choking arm. This is countered by using your head and shoulder to block their head movement and maintaining a tight chest-to-chest connection, preventing them from rotating.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try the North-South Choke and they don't tap?

    Your neck likely hurts because you are applying pressure incorrectly, possibly using only arm strength or misaligning your shoulder. The choke works by compressing the carotid arteries, not by a direct neck crank. Ensure your shoulder is firmly against their chin/jaw and drive your hips down to create a crushing pressure, rather than twisting or pulling excessively with your arms.

    Q: How can I successfully apply the North-South Choke against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paraMount. Focus on securing a deep gable grip and driving your shoulder into their neck with maximum force. Your hips must be directly over their head, and you need to drive your weight downwards and slightly forward. Think of it as using your entire body as a wedge, rather than trying to overpower them with arm strength alone.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to the North-South Choke from side control?

    The optimal time to transition to the North-South Choke from Side Control is when your opponent is trying to turn into you or shrimp away, creating a slight opening. As they move, you can adjust your body to face their head, establish the north-south grips, and immediately drive your weight down. The key is to exploit their defensive movements to secure the dominant position before they can fully recover.

North-South Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

This choke is applied from the North-South position, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain via the carotid arteries. White belts often struggle with control, allowing the opponent to escape before applying pressure. The key is maintaining a stable base and isolating one side of the neck with your shoulder.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From North-South position, establish a gable grip with your hands, interlacing fingers, palms facing your own face.
    2. Place your right forearm across the opponent's upper chest, pinning their left shoulder down.
    3. Drive your hips forward and down, creating a strong base with your knees on the mat, hip-width apart.
    4. Rotate your torso slightly to the opponent's left, tucking your chin to your chest.
    5. Use your left shoulder to drive into the opponent's right carotid artery, while your right forearm anchors their chest.
    6. Maintain downward pressure through your hips and shoulders, creating a crushing force.
    7. Keep your head up, looking towards the opponent's feet, to prevent them from bridging.
    8. Squeeze your elbows together to enhance the choke's effectiveness.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying the choke with your chin on the opponent's sternum can strain your cervical spine and fail to apply pressure to the carotid artery; instead, drive your shoulder into the artery with your chin tucked towards your own chest.
    • Grabbing your own biceps instead of a gable grip reduces leverage and can lead to a weak choke, allowing escapes; always secure a firm gable grip with interlaced fingers.
    • Allowing your hips to drift back creates a gap, negating the choking pressure and enabling the opponent to escape the position; continuously drive your hips forward and down.
    • Trying to force the choke by arching your back puts undue stress on your lumbar spine and is biomechanically inefficient; instead, use hip and shoulder pressure.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice the grip and hip drive motion 10 times per side, focusing on body positioning.
    2. Solo drill: Practice the full sequence without a partner 5 times, visualizing the pressure.
    3. Partner drill (0% resistance): Partner lies passively in North-South. Execute the choke for 5 reps per side, focusing on grip and pressure.
    4. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to your hip drive and shoulder placement. Execute for 5 reps per side.
    5. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner attempts to shrimp or bridge slightly. Focus on maintaining control and applying the choke for 5 reps per side.
    6. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner actively defends by trying to create space. Work on maintaining position and finishing the choke for 3 reps per side.
    7. Live rolling (90% intensity): Attempt the choke when the position arises, focusing on proper mechanics over submission. 3 attempts max.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you secure a dominant North-South position after a guard pass.
    • When the opponent attempts to shrimp or bridge out of side control, creating an opening.
    • When you have strong chest and shoulder control on the opponent.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Escape by bridging: The opponent bridges by driving their hips into you, creating space to turn their head away from the choking shoulder.
    • Hip escape to guard: The opponent shrimps their hips towards your head, then turns their body to establish guard.
    • Shoulder shuck: The opponent drives their shoulder into your choking arm, creating space to roll their body away.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the North-South Choke?

    Wrist pain often occurs if you are forcing the gable grip with excessive wrist flexion or extension. Ensure your fingers are interlaced naturally and your wrists remain relatively neutral. The pressure should come from your shoulders and hips, not from wrenching your wrists.

    Q: How can I apply the North-South Choke against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on controlling their head and shoulders to prevent them from posturing up. Drive your hips into their chest to minimize their base. Use your shoulder and chest to apply downward pressure, and ensure your forearm is deep across their neck to isolate the carotid.

    Q: When is the best moment to transition to a North-South Choke from side control?

    The ideal moment is when the opponent attempts to bridge or shrimp out of Side Control, creating a momentary loss of their base and a shift in their body alignment. As they move, you can adjust your position to North-South and capitalize on their momentum to set up the choke.

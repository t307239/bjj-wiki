The North-South Choke is applied when you establish a dominant position with your body perpendicular to your opponent's, facing their head. It achieves a submission by constricting blood flow to the brain.

White belts often struggle because they lack the precise weight distribution and grip refinement, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key mechanical insight is using your shoulder and chest to drive into their carotid artery while your arm creates a fulcrum against their other side.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From North-South position, secure your opponent's far-side collar with your right hand (if you are on their left side). Grip should be deep, fingers inside the lapel.
    2. Place your left hand on their near-side shoulder or upper chest, creating a frame to control their posture.
    3. Drive your left shoulder into their upper chest, directly above their collarbone, to pin their head and shoulders.
    4. Lower your hips towards the mat, aligning your body perpendicular to theirs. Your pelvis should be angled slightly towards their head.
    5. Rotate your torso clockwise, bringing your right shoulder blade towards their throat and your right elbow towards their left armpit.
    6. Simultaneously, extend your left leg straight back and drive your right knee forward, creating a stable base and applying downward pressure.
    7. Squeeze your right elbow towards your left hip, tightening the choke by compressing their carotid artery against your shoulder blade and their own neck.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying excessive upward pressure with your head: This can hyperextend the opponent's neck, risking cervical spine injury. Instead, focus on driving your shoulder into their carotid and rotating your torso.
    • Gripping too loosely on the collar: This allows the opponent to escape the grip easily, rendering the choke ineffective. Always secure a deep, firm grip with your fingers inside the lapel.
    • Allowing your hips to sag: This reduces the pressure and makes it easier for the opponent to bridge or shrimp out. Keep your hips elevated and drive them towards their head.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the grip and body positioning without a partner. Focus on the hip angle and shoulder drive. (20 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Partnered static drill: With a compliant partner in North-South, practice securing the collar and shoulder grips, then driving your shoulder and rotating your torso. (15 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    3. Partnered controlled execution: Execute the full choke sequence with a partner who understands to tap early and not resist the choke itself. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    4. Controlled rolling: Attempt the North-South Choke during live rolling against a partner who is actively defending but not trying to submit you. (5 attempts per roll, 75% resistance)
    5. Live rolling with focus: Make the North-South Choke a specific goal during a rolling session, focusing on creating the correct position and timing. (10 attempts per roll, 90% resistance)
    6. Full live rolling: Attempt the North-South Choke whenever the opportunity arises in regular sparring. (As opportunities arise, 100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have successfully transitioned to the North-South position after a guard pass or sweep.
    • When your opponent is turtled and you can secure a dominant North-South posture.
    • When your opponent attempts to escape from side control by turning into you.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): If the choke is not yet fully secured, the opponent can shrimp their hips away and create space to relieve pressure.
    • Bridging and Turning: A powerful bridge combined with turning towards the side of the choke can break the grip and create an escape angle.
    • Creating Space with the Far Arm: The defender can use their free arm to push against your shoulder or head, creating just enough space to breathe or begin to turn.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the North-South Choke?

    Wrist pain often occurs if you are gripping the collar too far back or with an unnatural angle. Ensure your grip is deep, with your fingers inside the lapel, and your wrist is in a neutral or slightly flexed position. The power should come from your shoulder rotation and torso pressure, not from bending your wrist excessively.

    Q: How do I apply the North-South Choke against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on precise body mechanics rather than brute strength. Drive your shoulder into their carotid artery with maximum chest pressure. Use your hips to create a tight seal, and ensure your body is perpendicular. Control their head by pinning it with your shoulder, preventing them from posturing up to relieve pressure.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the North-South Choke if my opponent is defending well?

    The North-South Choke is most effective when your opponent is slightly flat on their back or has their head exposed. If they are actively defending by tucking their chin or turning, it's often better to transition to another submission or refine your control in the North-South position. Look for moments of stillness or slight disorientation in their defense.

North-South Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Purple Belt Requirements

The North-South Choke is a submission initiated from the North-South position, aiming to constrict blood flow to the brain. It offers a powerful finishing mechanism when properly applied.

White belts often struggle with this choke due to poor weight distribution and improper grip application, leading to ineffective pressure or even self-submission.

The key mechanical insight is creating a strong thoracic compression by bridging your hips and driving your chest into the opponent's throat, while using your arms to create a fulcrum.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Entry: From side control, transition your hips to the North-South position, aligning your sternum perpendicular to the opponent's sternum.
    2. Head Position: Place your head on the mat, slightly to the side of the opponent's head, creating a stable base.
    3. Grip 1 (Collar): Reach your right arm across the opponent's neck and secure a deep grip on their left collar with your thumb inside, fingers outside.
    4. Grip 2 (Underarm): Simultaneously, reach your left arm underneath the opponent's right armpit and grip your own right wrist firmly.
    5. Hip Drive: Drive your hips forward and down, creating a bridge motion. This compresses your chest against the opponent's throat.
    6. Shoulder Pressure: Arch your upper back and drive your left shoulder into the opponent's chest, further tightening the choke.
    7. Finishing: Squeeze your elbows together and continue the hip drive to elicit the tap. Maintain head and upper body control.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Hip Placement: Driving hips too far back or too high allows the opponent to escape or bridge. This can strain the lower back ligaments and cause facet joint impingement.
    • Over-Reaching for the Grip: Extending your arms too far to secure the collar grip compromises your base and creates space for the opponent to posture up or escape. This can lead to shoulder impingement syndrome or rotator cuff tears.
    • Sloppy Head Control: Allowing the opponent to turn their head or create space with their chin negates the choke and can lead to neck strain or hyperextension injuries if they attempt to escape aggressively.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Movement (0% Resistance): Practice transitioning from side control to North-South, focusing on hip and head placement. 10 repetitions.
    2. Grip Focus (0% Resistance): From North-South, practice securing the collar and wrist grips without an opponent. 10 repetitions.
    3. Controlled Transition (25% Resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the transition and grip setup. Partner offers minimal resistance. 5 repetitions per side.
    4. Hip Drive Practice (50% Resistance): With a partner in North-South, practice the hip drive and chest compression. Partner maintains a neutral posture. 5 repetitions per side.
    5. Choke Application (75% Resistance): Apply the choke with a partner who offers mild resistance, focusing on the squeeze and hip drive. Partner simulates tapping early. 5 repetitions per side.
    6. Live Rolling (100% Resistance): Attempt the North-South Choke during live rolling, prioritizing control and biomechanical accuracy over immediate submission. 3-5 minutes.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have successfully transitioned to the North-South position and established solid control.
    • When the opponent attempts to shrimp or bridge out of side control, creating an opportunity for the North-South transition.
    • When you have secured a strong cross-collar grip and can prevent the opponent from defending effectively.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Turtle Escape: If the opponent turtles, immediately transition to a more dominant position like knee-on-belly or attempt a guard pass. Do not commit to the choke if they are in turtle.
    • Bridging and Hip Escape: If the opponent bridges powerfully, maintain your head and shoulder control, and use your hips to follow their movement, preventing them from creating space. Drive your hips down and slightly to their side.
    • Arm Pull and Posture Up: If the opponent pulls your choking arm and attempts to posture up, use your non-choking arm to maintain a tight grip on your wrist and drive your chest forward, keeping your head low.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the North-South Choke?

    Wrist pain often occurs from gripping your own wrist too tightly or with an unnatural angle. Ensure your grip is firm but not strained, and your wrist remains in a neutral, slightly flexed position. The primary force should come from your hips and chest, not a death grip on your own arm.

    Q: How do I apply the North-South Choke effectively against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on maximizing your weight distribution and leverage. Drive your hips forward aggressively, using your sternum to apply pressure to their carotid artery. Ensure your collar grip is deep and your underarm grip is tight to prevent them from creating space. Your head should be firmly planted to prevent them from posturing.

    Q: The North-South Choke is best initiated when you've achieved a stable North-South position and the opponent is unable to effectively defend or escape. Look for moments when they are flat on their back and their neck is exposed. Attempting it too early or when they have good posture can lead to wasted energy and a compromised position.

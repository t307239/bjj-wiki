North-South Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanically Precise Guide

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Purple Belt Requirements

The North-South Choke is a submission applied from the north-south position, aiming to constrict the carotid arteries. It's often initiated after a successful Guard Pass or from a scramble.

White belts frequently struggle because they rely on brute strength rather than leverage and proper body alignment. This leads to inefficient application and increased injury risk.

The key mechanical insight is using your shoulder and upper back to drive into the opponent's neck, while your arms create the constricting pressure, not just pulling.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish North-South position: Your chest is on the opponent's chest, head facing their feet, and your hips are lower than their shoulders. Your knees are wider than shoulder-width for base.
    2. Secure the cross-collar grip: Reach your right arm across the opponent's body and insert your fingers deep into their left lapel, palm facing upwards.
    3. Secure the secondary grip: With your left hand, reach over their shoulder and grip their right lapel, palm facing downwards, fingers deep.
    4. Adjust shoulder pressure: Drive your right shoulder into the side of the opponent's neck and upper chest, pinning their head slightly.
    5. Hip angle and weight shift: Angle your hips slightly to your left, bringing your left knee closer to their head and your right knee towards their hip. This creates a wedge.
    6. Compress and lift: While maintaining shoulder pressure, use your arms to pull both lapels towards your own body, simultaneously lifting your hips slightly off the mat.
    7. Extend and finish: Extend your hips forward and slightly upwards, creating a pulling motion on the lapels. Your head should be pressed into their chest to prevent them from turning into you.
    8. Maintain head control: Keep your head pressed firmly into the opponent's chest to prevent them from creating space or turning.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying excessive neck torque by rotating your head independently: This can cause cervical spine strain or sprain. The correct movement is to keep your head stable and drive your shoulder into their neck.
    • Gripping the lapel too loosely or shallowly: This leads to the grip slipping and requires more strength, increasing the risk of shoulder or elbow hyperextension if you pull incorrectly. Always ensure deep, firm lapel grips.
    • Allowing the opponent to bridge or turn into you: This can lead to the choke failing and potentially injuring your shoulder or elbow if you are pulled off-balance. Maintain a strong base and hip connection.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo grip and body positioning drill: Practice entering the north-south position and securing both lapel grips without an opponent. (5 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    2. Static partner drill: With a compliant partner, assume north-south and practice the grip setup and initial shoulder drive. (5 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    3. Controlled choke application drill: With a partner, apply the choke to the point of feeling resistance without finishing. Focus on correct hip angling and weight transfer. (5 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    4. Full choke drill with tap out: Apply the North-South Choke with full commitment, allowing your partner to tap. Focus on the full sequence from position to finish. (5 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    5. Scramble to North-South Choke: Start from a neutral position and simulate a scramble leading to the north-south position, then execute the choke. (5 reps per side, 90% resistance)
    6. Live rolling application: Attempt the North-South Choke during live rolling when the opportunity arises. Focus on recognizing the setup and executing the mechanics. (10 minutes rolling, 100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have successfully passed the opponent's guard and established a strong north-south position.
    • When the opponent attempts to turn into you from the north-south position, creating an opening.
    • During scrambles where you end up in the north-south position with dominant control.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Opponent turns into you: As the opponent turns into your right side, your left knee should drive forward towards their head, creating a strong base and preventing them from escaping. This transition can lead to other submissions like a kimura or armbar.
    • Opponent bridges upward: If the opponent bridges, maintain your base by widening your stance and keeping your hips low. Simultaneously, drive your chest into their chest to stifle their bridge. This often creates space to transition to side control.
    • Opponent frames with arms: If the opponent frames with their arms, use your shoulder pressure to collapse their frames. Drive your right shoulder into their neck and use your left arm to control their arm to prevent them from creating space.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try the North-South Choke and my opponent doesn't tap?

    Your neck likely hurts because you are trying to generate the choking force by twisting your own head or neck, rather than using your body's leverage. This puts direct strain on your cervical spine. The correct application involves driving your shoulder into their neck to constrict blood flow and using your arms to pull the lapels, with your head providing stable pressure.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, focus intensely on the biomechanics of leverage and weight distribution, not strength. Ensure your shoulder is deeply burrowed into their neck and upper chest, and your hips are angled correctly to create a wedge. Drive your weight through your shoulder and hips, and use your arms for the final tightening, rather than trying to muscle through.

    Q: The North-South Choke is best attempted when you have established dominant north-south control and the opponent is relatively flat on their back. To avoid giving up your back, maintain constant pressure with your chest on their chest and your hips low and slightly angled. Your head should be glued to their chest, preventing them from turning into you or creating the space needed to attack your back.

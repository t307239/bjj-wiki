North-South Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

This choke is applied from the North-South position, aiming to submit an opponent lying on their back. White belts often struggle due to insufficient pressure and incorrect weight distribution, leading to a weak choke. The key is creating a fulcrum with your hips and using your shoulder to constrict the carotid artery.

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish North-South: From a dominant position (e.g., side control), transition to the North-South position with your head facing your opponent's feet.
    2. Secure the Collar Grip: Reach your right arm across your opponent's neck, inserting your hand deep into their gi collar, palm facing you.
    3. Establish Shoulder Pressure: Simultaneously, your left arm should hook under your opponent's far armpit, creating a strong frame.
    4. Align Your Hips: Drive your hips down and forward, pinning your chest to your opponent's chest, creating a tight seal.
    5. Create the Fulcrum: Drive your left knee towards your opponent's head while keeping your right knee planted, creating a strong base and hip angle.
    6. Apply Downward Pressure: Use your right collar grip to pull your opponent's head towards your chest, while simultaneously driving your hips forward.
    7. Constrict the Artery: Your left shoulder should press directly into the side of your opponent's neck, compressing the carotid artery.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly rotating your torso: This can lead to hyperextension or tearing of the cervical spine ligaments, causing neck sprains or worse. Always maintain a stable torso and drive with your hips.
    • Overextending your arms to pull: This ineffective grip often results in shoulder impingement or rotator cuff tears. Focus on driving your hips and using your shoulder as the primary choking mechanism.
    • Allowing your opponent to bridge or escape by not establishing a solid base: This can lead to your knee or ankle being trapped and twisted, resulting in ligament damage. Ensure your knee-on-chest pressure is consistent and your hips are heavy.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo grip and hip placement practice: Perform the collar grip and hip drive 20 times per side.
    2. Partnered positional drilling (no resistance): From North-South, have a partner lie still. Practice the full sequence 10 times.
    3. Light resistance positional drilling: Partner offers minimal resistance (e.g., framing). Execute the choke 8 times per side.
    4. Controlled choke execution: Partner actively defends, but allows the choke to be applied slowly. Practice 6 times per side.
    5. Flow rolling with focus on North-South: Attempt the choke when the position is achieved, with 50% resistance. Perform for 5 minutes.
    6. Live rolling with intentional setup: Aim to achieve North-South and apply the choke against 75% resistance. Perform for 5 minutes.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent turtles up from side control and you can secure a head-and-arm position.
    • After successfully transitioning from side control to the North-South position.
    • When your opponent attempts to escape from bottom side control by turning into you, creating an opening.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Opponent turns into you: Immediately drive your hips forward and secure the collar grip before they can flatten out. Use your shoulder to maintain pressure.
    • Opponent attempts to shrimp away: If they create space, use your knee-on-chest pressure to prevent them from regaining guard. Drive your hips further forward.
    • Opponent defends by framing with their arms: Use your body weight to break their frames by pushing your chest into their chest and driving your hips down.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the North-South choke?

    Wrist pain typically stems from gripping too hard with your fingers or trying to pull the collar with your arm instead of driving your hips. Ensure your grip is firm but relaxed, and focus on using your shoulder and hip pressure to create the choke. The collar grip is for leverage, not for brute strength.

    Q: How do I apply the North-South choke effectively against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise body mechanics are paraMount. Focus on establishing a very tight chest-to-chest connection and driving your hips forward aggressively to create a strong fulcrum. Ensure your shoulder is deeply dug into their neck, and use your body weight to compress, rather than relying solely on arm strength.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to the North-South choke from side control?

    The optimal time is when your opponent attempts to turn into you to escape Side Control, or when they turtle up. These movements often leave their neck exposed and create the necessary space for you to establish the North-South position and secure the collar grip before they can fully recover their defense.

Knee Slice Pass: The Ultimate White Belt BJJ Guide

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Blue Belt Guide

This pass starts from a dominant side control position, aiming to break the opponent's Closed Guard or half guard by controlling their legs and hips.

White belts often struggle by trying to force the pass with raw strength, neglecting the crucial body mechanics and weight distribution needed for control.

The key insight is using your knee as a wedge to create space and your hip as a lever to break their base, not just pushing with your arms.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From side control, establish an underhook with your right arm, gripping the opponent's left hip bone with your hand. Your left arm establishes a cross-face grip on their right cheek, preventing them from turning into you.
    2. Post your left knee on the mat, about 6-8 inches away from their hips, creating a stable base. Your left foot remains tucked. Your right knee is bent, ready to drive forward.
    3. Lower your hips until they are level with or slightly below the opponent's hips. Your chest should be pressed firmly against their chest.
    4. Begin to drive your right knee forward, aiming to slide it across the opponent's body, specifically targeting the space between their thighs. This is the "slice" motion.
    5. As your right knee slices across, simultaneously drive your right hip down and forward, using it to apply pressure and control their left hip. Your weight should be distributed over their hips and upper thigh.
    6. Continue to drive your right knee forward and your right hip down, forcing their legs to open and creating a clear path for your right leg to step over their body.
    7. Once your right leg is positioned to step over their hips, lift your left leg and step it around their head to establish side control. Maintain constant pressure with your hips and chest.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Knee on Mat Impact: Driving your knee directly onto a hard mat without proper padding or support can cause patellar tendonitis or bursitis. Always ensure your knee is protected by a mat or use a knee pad.
    • Hip Collision Injury: Forcing your hip into the opponent's hip at an extreme angle can lead to hip impingement or labral tears. Maintain a slightly angled hip position and drive forward and down, not directly into them.
    • Ankle Twisting: When stepping your left leg around their head, if your foot is not properly controlled, you can invert your ankle, leading to a sprain or ligament tear. Keep your ankle neutral and your foot pointing slightly outward as you step.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Drive and Knee Slice Motion (0% resistance): Practice driving your knee forward and hip down while maintaining chest-to-chest pressure on a dummy or mat. 10 repetitions.
    2. Dummy Pass Entry (25% resistance): With a training partner providing minimal resistance (just lying there), practice establishing grips and driving your knee across. 10 repetitions per side.
    3. Controlled Pass with Light Resistance (50% resistance): Your partner offers light, passive resistance by not actively framing or gripping. Focus on the hip and knee movement. 5 repetitions per side.
    4. Half Guard Pass Entry (75% resistance): Your partner is in half guard and offers active but not aggressive defense (e.g., a loose cross-face). Focus on breaking their underhook and driving the knee. 5 repetitions per side.
    5. Full Guard Break and Transition (90% resistance): Your partner actively defends the closed guard or half guard, but you are not trying to submit or achieve perfect position. Focus on the mechanics of the pass. 3 repetitions per side.
    6. Live Rolling - Focus on Pass (100% resistance): During rolling, dedicate rounds to attempting the Knee Slice Pass whenever the opportunity arises, focusing on the mechanics rather than just the outcome. 3 rounds.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent has a strong closed guard and is trying to flatten you out.
    • When the opponent is in half guard, and you have secured a strong cross-face and underhook.
    • When the opponent attempts to shrimp away, creating an opening for your knee to slice across.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Retention (Hip Escape): If you feel the knee coming, immediately shrimp your hips away to create space and prevent the knee from slicing between your legs. This allows you to re-establish your guard.
    • Underhook Recovery (Re-guard): As the knee slices across, try to secure an underhook and use your free leg to hook their hip, shrimping your body back to your guard.
    • Counter-Pass (Leg Drag): If the opponent's weight is too far forward, you can attempt to grab their slicing leg, pull it across your body, and transition to a leg drag pass.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do the Knee Slice Pass?

    Your knee might hurt because you are driving it directly into the opponent's hip bone or thigh with excessive force, or your knee cap is making direct contact with the mat. Ensure your knee is positioned to slice *between* their legs and that your hip is driving the pressure, not solely your knee. Consider using a knee pad for mat contact.

    Q: How do I Knee Slice Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paraMount. Focus on controlling their hips with your underhook and cross-face to break their base. Drive your hip down and forward to create the slicing motion, using their size against them by pinning their hips. Do not try to overpower them; use precise angles and weight distribution.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from a failed Knee Slice Pass attempt?

    If your opponent successfully defends by re-guarding or creating a strong frame, forcing the pass will lead to a compromised position. Immediately look to recover your own guard, transition to a leg drag if their leg is exposed, or reset to a more dominant Side Control position. Don't get stuck in a bad position trying to force a failed pass.

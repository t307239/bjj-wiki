Knee Slice Pass: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

The Knee Slice Pass is a fundamental guard pass initiated from a standing or kneeling position against a Closed Guard or half guard. Its goal is to break the opponent's guard and establish side control.

White belts often struggle by muscling the pass, neglecting hip pressure, or allowing their knee to be trapped, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key mechanical insight is using your shin as a wedge and driving your hip forward to create a suffocating pressure that collapses the opponent's legs.

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip: Secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a left-arm sleeve grip (or wrist if sleeve is unavailable). Maintain a strong frame with your left forearm pressing against the opponent's right hip bone.
    2. Base: Kneel with your right knee slightly behind the opponent's hip and your left knee forward, perpendicular to their body, forming a wide, stable base.
    3. Weight Transfer: Drive your weight forward and slightly downward onto the opponent's hips, initiating a slight forward lean.
    4. Knee Insertion: Drive your right shin *across* the opponent's body, aiming to place it firmly on the mat on the opposite side of their hips.
    5. Hip Pressure: As your shin drives through, simultaneously drive your *right hip* forward and down, directly into the opponent's hip pocket.
    6. Leg Drive: Use your left leg to post on the mat and push yourself forward, creating forward momentum.
    7. Clearing the Guard: Continue driving your hip and chest forward, using your right shin to keep their legs separated, until your right knee is on the mat and your left leg is free.
    8. Transition to Side Control: Once the guard is broken, immediately shift your weight onto their hips and chest, driving your left knee towards their armpit to secure side control.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Knee on Mat Impact: Driving your knee directly onto a hard mat without proper padding or control can cause direct trauma to the patella and surrounding cartilage, leading to bursitis or chondromalacia.
    • Incorrect Hip Drive: Attempting to pass by lifting your hips too high and rotating them outward instead of driving them forward can strain the lumbar spine and sacroiliac joint.
    • Allowing Leg Re-capture: If your shin is not driven aggressively across and your hip pressure is insufficient, the opponent can easily re-capture their guard or secure a sweep by trapping your knee between their legs, risking anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) or medial collateral ligament (MCL) sprains.
    • Premature Leg Release: Releasing the pressure on the opponent's hips too early to 'reach' for a control can allow them to shrimp and recover guard, potentially trapping your arm or leg.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Movement Drill (0% Resistance): Practice the knee slice motion without an opponent. Focus on driving the hip forward and sweeping the shin across, aiming for a clean mat contact. (20 reps)
    2. Partnered Static Drill (25% Resistance): With a partner lying on their back, practice the grips and hip drive. The partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the correct body mechanics. (15 reps)
    3. Guard Break Drill (50% Resistance): Partner attempts to maintain closed guard. Focus on breaking the guard with the knee slice motion and establishing a chest-to-chest connection. (10 reps)
    4. Half Guard Pass Drill (75% Resistance): Partner starts in half guard. Practice the knee slice pass, focusing on clearing their leg and establishing side control. Partner resists subtly. (10 reps)
    5. Full Guard Pass Drill (90% Resistance): Partner starts in full guard and actively resists the pass. Focus on maintaining hip pressure and driving through. (5 reps)
    6. Live Rolling (100% Resistance): Integrate the knee slice pass into live rolling scenarios, attempting it when the opportunity arises. (3-5 minute rounds)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is in closed guard and their hips are slightly off the mat, creating a small opening.
    • When the opponent is in half guard and their knee shield is not perfectly tight against your chest.
    • After a failed sweep attempt where the opponent is momentarily off-balance and their guard is loose.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Elbow Trap & Shrimp: If the opponent drives their shin across and doesn't maintain hip pressure, trap their knee with your elbow and shrimp your hips away to create space and potentially recover guard or initiate a sweep.
    • Knee Re-capture (Sweetheart Sweep Setup): As the knee slice enters, if you can keep your shin tight to their hip, invert slightly and bring your opposite leg back to the inside, potentially setting up a sweetheart sweep or other reversal.
    • Stand Up & Disengage: If the pass is clearly failing and the opponent is gaining positional advantage, immediately stand up by driving your feet into the mat and creating distance to reset.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Knee Slice Pass?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are not driving your hip forward with enough force, causing your knee to bear excessive weight directly on the mat. Ensure your right hip is driving *into* the opponent's hip pocket as your shin slices across, transferring the primary pressure to your hip and core, not just your knee. Also, consider using a knee pad for added protection.

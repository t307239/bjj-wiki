Knee Slice Pass: A Biomechanically Accurate BJJ Guide for White Belts

This pass starts from a position where your opponent is on their back, attempting to control your legs with their guard. The goal is to bypass their legs and establish Side Control.

White belts often fail because they rely on brute strength or incorrect weight distribution, leading to ineffective passes and potential injury.

The core mechanical principle is driving your knee into the opponent's hip crease, creating a fulcrum to slice your leg through their guard.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Starting Position: Opponent's guard is closed or open, you are kneeling or standing in front of their hips.
    2. Grip: Secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand (if passing to your right) and a cross-sleeve grip with your left hand on their left arm. Alternatively, grip both of their pants at the knees.
    3. Posture & Base: Maintain an upright posture with your hips slightly lower than your shoulders. Your base should be wide, with knees slightly bent, providing stability.
    4. Initiate the Slice: Drive your right knee forward and inward, aiming to place it directly into the crease of their left hip joint.
    5. Weight Transfer: Simultaneously, lean your upper body weight forward, driving your chest towards their chest, and shift your weight onto your slicing knee.
    6. Leg Movement: As your knee drives in, begin to slide your left leg (the one not slicing) backward and around their hip, clearing their legs.
    7. Hip Pressure: Continue to drive your slicing knee into their hip, using it as a wedge to create space and prevent them from re-guarding.
    8. Establish Side Control: Once your left leg is past their hips, lower your left knee to the mat and bring your hips down, achieving a stable side control position with your chest on their chest.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Knee Drive: Driving your knee directly into the opponent's shin instead of their hip crease can hyperextend their knee joint, leading to ligament tears (ACL/MCL).
    • Swaying Back: Arching your lower back to create space instead of driving forward with your hips puts immense pressure on your lumbar spine, risking disc herniation.
    • Lifting the Hips Too High: When attempting to slice, if you lift your hips excessively off the mat, you create an opening for them to sweep or reverse you, and can strain your hip flexors trying to maintain an unnatural position.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Static Hip Drive: From kneeling, practice driving one knee into your own hip crease and holding for 10 seconds. Repeat 10 times per leg. (0% resistance)
    2. Solo Dynamic Hip Drive & Leg Swing: From kneeling, drive one knee into your hip crease and swing the other leg around, mimicking the pass motion. Repeat 10 times per side. (0% resistance)
    3. Partner Static Position Hold: Your partner lies on their back. You kneel, establish grips, and drive your knee into their hip without pressure. They hold their guard. Repeat 5 times per side. (25% resistance)
    4. Partner Controlled Slice: Your partner maintains a relaxed guard. You execute the Knee Slice Pass with light pressure. They offer minimal resistance, focusing on allowing the pass. Repeat 5 times per side. (50% resistance)
    5. Partner Active Guard: Your partner actively tries to maintain their guard. You work to execute the pass, and they can attempt to reguard or block with light pressure. Focus on maintaining your base. Repeat 5 times per side. (75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling - Controlled Sparring: Execute the Knee Slice Pass during sparring. Focus on proper mechanics and maintaining control. Your partner attempts to defend and recover guard. (90% resistance)
    7. Live Rolling - Full Sparring: Apply the Knee Slice Pass in full sparring. Focus on timing, pressure, and adapting to your partner's reactions. (100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is trying to establish a closed guard and you are postured up.
    • When your opponent's legs are extended, creating an opening at the hips.
    • When they attempt to pull guard or establish a half guard with an unstable base.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Reguarding (Hip Escape & Knee Shield): If the passer's knee is not deeply into your hip, immediately hip escape away and bring your opposite knee up to create a knee shield, preventing them from fully clearing your legs.
    • Stacking (Forward Pressure): If the passer is too upright and not driving their chest into you, you can attempt to stack them by walking your hips back and driving your chest forward over their shoulders, collapsing their posture and potentially reversing.
    • Leg Pummeling (Inside Control): As the passer attempts to slice, aggressively pummel your leg (the one being sliced) to the inside of their passing leg. This allows you to potentially gain an underhook on their leg or establish butterfly guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to Knee Slice Pass?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are driving it directly into your opponent's tibia (shin bone) or fibula (outer lower leg bone) instead of their hip crease. This hyperextends their knee or puts direct blunt force on your patella. Always aim to slice your knee *past* their hip and into the soft tissue of their hip joint, using your shin to brace against their thigh.

    Q: How do I Knee Slice Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are key, not brute force. Focus on securing a strong cross-collar grip to break their posture and control their head. Drive your slicing knee *deep* into their hip crease with significant upward pressure on your collar grip to help elevate their hips slightly. Ensure your slicing knee is the fulcrum, and your other leg is actively moving around their frame.

    Q: The ideal transition is when you are standing in front of your opponent, they attempt to close their guard or establish an open guard, and you can quickly break their posture. As they try to re-establish control, you can immediately drop your weight and drive your knee into their hip to initiate the slice before they fully solidify their leg structure.

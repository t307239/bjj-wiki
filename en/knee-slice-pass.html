Knee Slice Pass: A Biomechanically Precise BJJ Guide for White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Blue Belt Guide

This pass starts from a standing or kneeling position against an opponent's guard, aiming to break their grip and establish Side Control.

White belts often struggle by attacking the legs too directly or allowing their upper body to be controlled, leading to sweeps or submissions.

The core principle is using your shin as a wedge to displace their hip and create a narrow lane for your knee to slice through their legs.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand and a cross-sleeve grip (left wrist) with your left hand.
    2. Lower your hips to a kneeling position, ensuring your base is wide with knees shoulder-width apart.
    3. Drive your right shoulder into the opponent's left hip, angling your torso 45 degrees to their left.
    4. Begin to slide your right knee towards your own left elbow, aiming it outside their left thigh.
    5. As your right knee moves forward, your left knee simultaneously drives forward, creating a "sandwich" effect on their legs.
    6. Crucially, keep your hips low and drive your chest into their hips, preventing them from sitting up.
    7. Continue to drive your right knee across their body until it is past their centerline, allowing you to clear their legs and establish side control.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect: Lunging forward with your hips high and right knee pointed directly at their groin. Biomechanical failure: This puts excessive pressure on your own knee joint, risking ACL or meniscus tears, and allows the opponent to easily shrimp away. Correct movement: Drive your shoulder into their hip and slice your knee across their thigh, keeping hips low.
    • Incorrect: Grabbing their pants at the ankle and pulling them towards you while stepping over. Biomechanical failure: This strains your lower back and can lead to disc compression, while also giving them leverage to sweep. Correct movement: Secure grips on the collar and sleeve, and use your body weight to drive past their legs.
    • Incorrect: Trying to force your knee through their legs without first displacing their hip. Biomechanical failure: This can cause your knee to impact their femur directly, potentially damaging your patella or their tibia. Correct movement: Use your shoulder and chest to create space and drive their hip away before sliding your knee.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip displacement and knee slice motion without a partner, focusing on hip angle and knee path. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the full Knee Slice Pass sequence. Focus on grip fighting and hip pressure. (20 reps per side)
    3. Light resistance drill: Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the hip displacement and knee slice. (30 reps)
    4. Controlled sparring (25% resistance): Apply the Knee Slice Pass against a partner who is actively trying to defend but not explosively. (3 rounds)
    5. Controlled sparring (50% resistance): Increase resistance. Your partner will actively try to prevent the pass, requiring more hip pressure and timing. (4 rounds)
    6. Live rolling (75% resistance): Attempt the Knee Slice Pass during live rolling. Focus on applying the technique with commitment and adjusting to their defenses. (5 rounds)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is playing a closed guard and you have broken their grip on your pants.
    • When the opponent is in half guard and you have established strong cross-face pressure and hip control.
    • When the opponent attempts a sweep or submission from open guard and their base is compromised.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Shin-to-Shin: If the passer attempts to slice their knee across, immediately bring your opposite shin across their body to create a "shin-to-shin" barrier, preventing them from progressing. This frames your leg between their legs.
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): As the knee slices, perform a sharp hip escape away from the direction of the pass, creating space to re-establish guard or transition to a better position.
    • Re-Guard (Guard Recovery): If the passer gets their knee too deep, use your legs to create a frame and shrimp your hips back to attempt to re-establish your guard or transition to a butterfly guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to Knee Slice Pass?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are attempting to drive your knee directly through their legs without first displacing their hip. This creates a direct collision between your patella and their femur or tibia, risking injury. Ensure you are leading with your shoulder into their hip to create space and angling your knee across their thigh, not directly at their knee joint.

    Q: How do I Knee Slice Pass against a bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and hip pressure are paraMount. Focus on establishing superior grips to control their upper body and prevent them from using their weight effectively. Drive your shoulder into their hip with significant force to displace their weight, and keep your hips extremely low to minimize the distance they can bridge or stack you. Your goal is to make their size a disadvantage by controlling their base.

    Q: You should transition if the opponent successfully frames your knee with their shin (shin-to-shin), or if they manage to create significant space by shrimping away. Continuing to force a stalled Knee Slice Pass can lead to you being swept or giving up dominant positions. If they achieve shin-to-shin, consider transitioning to a leg drag or a pressure pass. If they create space, try to re-establish control and attempt a different passing angle or a stack pass.

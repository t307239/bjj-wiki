Knee Slice Pass: The Essential BJJ Technique for White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Blue Belt Guide

The Knee Slice Pass is a fundamental guard pass initiated from a standing or kneeling position against an opponent's guard, aiming to establish Side Control.

White belts often struggle because they lack the precise weight distribution and body positioning required, leading to predictable and easily countered movements.

The key mechanical insight is using your knee as a wedge to sever the opponent's connection, while your hips drive forward to create the necessary pressure.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Starting Position: Opponent in closed guard, you are kneeling with your hips close to their hips.
    2. Grip: Secure an over-under grip on the opponent's hips or a cross-collar grip with your opposite arm.
    3. Initial Movement: Drive your lead knee (the one on the same side as your under-hook or cross-collar grip) towards the opponent's hip crease.
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight forward, driving your chest towards their chest, creating a forward pressure.
    5. Knee Placement: Your lead knee should aim to bisect the opponent's legs, driving their hip away from your body.
    6. Hip Drive: Simultaneously, drive your hips forward and down, aiming to get your hip bone over their thigh.
    7. Clearing the Leg: As your knee slices through, use your shin to push their far leg away, preventing them from re-guarding.
    8. Securing Side Control: Once their legs are separated, bring your trailing leg forward to establish side control, maintaining chest-to-chest pressure.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Knee Placement: Driving your knee directly into the opponent's knee joint or shin can hyperextend their knee or cause ligament damage (ACL/MCL).
    • Correct Movement: Aim your knee to slice *across* the opponent's hip, not directly into their leg, and maintain a controlled forward drive.
    • Excessive Forward Lean: Leaning too far forward without driving your hips can cause your head to dip, exposing your neck to chokes and creating an unstable base.
    • Correct Movement: Keep your head up and actively drive your hips forward to maintain a strong base and prevent your head from dropping.
    • Jerking Motion: Attempting to rip the legs apart with arm strength alone instead of using hip pressure can strain your shoulders and elbows.
    • Correct Movement: Utilize your body weight and hip drive to 'slice' the knee through, using your arms primarily for control and balance.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Movement: Practice driving your hips forward and backward, simulating the hip drive of the pass, 30 reps.
    2. Knee Slice Motion: On your knees, practice driving one knee across your body as if slicing, maintaining a stable base, 20 reps per side.
    3. Partnered Isolation (0% Resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the grip and the initial knee slice motion, focusing on hip pressure, 5 reps per side.
    4. Partnered Control (25% Resistance): Partner offers light resistance, allowing you to focus on driving your knee and hips through the guard.
    5. Controlled Pass (50% Resistance): Partner actively tries to prevent the pass but not to submit, focus on maintaining pressure and clearing the legs.
    6. Live Rolling (75-90% Resistance): Apply the Knee Slice Pass against a resisting opponent, focusing on the mechanics under pressure.
    7. Live Rolling (100% Resistance): Execute the Knee Slice Pass in a full sparring match, aiming for successful transitions to side control.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent's knees are not fully locked, creating a slight gap in their closed guard.
    • When you have established a strong, controlling grip on their hips or collar, preventing them from creating frames.
    • When the opponent attempts to shrimp or create space, leaving an opening for your knee to penetrate.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Re-Guard: If your knee slice is too slow, the opponent can bring their far leg back over your hip to re-establish guard.
    • Mechanics: This happens when your hip drive is insufficient or your clearing leg is not actively pushing their leg away.
    • Hip Escape and Frame: The opponent can hip escape and use their arm to frame between your chest and their body, creating space.
    • Mechanics: This is effective if you are leaning too far forward and not driving your hips down and through.
    • Stacking: If you get too high and your base is unstable, the opponent can attempt to stack you by pushing your shoulders over your head.
    • Mechanics: This is a consequence of poor hip pressure and an unstable base, often caused by trying to force the pass with arm strength alone.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do the Knee Slice Pass against a white belt?

    This pain often occurs when you're driving your knee directly into the opponent's tibia or knee joint. The biomechanical failure is applying blunt force to a vulnerable joint. Instead, focus on slicing your knee *across* their hip bone, using your hip drive to create pressure and sever their guard connection, not to impact their leg directly.

    Q: How can I effectively Knee Slice Pass against a significantly larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise weight distribution are paraMount. Focus on getting your hips *underneath* their hips to create a fulcrum, rather than trying to overpower them. Use your chest to chest pressure to control their upper body, and drive your knee through the space created by their legs, ensuring your hips are lower than theirs to generate driving power.

    Q: Why can't I get my Knee Slice Pass to work, my opponent just keeps their guard closed?

    Your opponent is likely maintaining a strong Closed Guard because you are not effectively breaking their connection. The key is to use your grips to slightly elevate their hips, creating just enough space to drive your knee through. Once your knee is in position, your hip drive must be forward and down, actively pushing their leg away to prevent them from re-closing the guard. Don't just push, slice and drive.

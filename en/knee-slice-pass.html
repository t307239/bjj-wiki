Knee Slice Pass: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

This pass starts from a position of control over your opponent's legs, typically from half-guard or Closed Guard. Its goal is to break their guard and establish side control.

White belts often struggle by trying to force the pass with raw strength, neglecting the crucial leverage points and body positioning.

The key insight is using your knee as a wedge and your hips as the driving force, creating a stable base while displacing their legs.

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip: Secure a grip on the opponent's far hip with your near hand and their far pant leg (shin area) with your far hand. Maintain a strong connection.
    2. Knee Placement: Drive your near knee (the one closer to their hips) onto the mat, aiming it towards their hip crease. This knee acts as a fulcrum.
    3. Hip Angle: Angle your hips sharply, creating a 45-degree angle relative to your opponent's body. Your hips should be lower than their hips.
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight forward onto your lead knee and the ball of your far foot. Your body should form a stable triangle.
    5. Leg Drive: Use your far leg (the one gripping the pants) to drive your opponent's leg across your body, creating space.
    6. Torso Pressure: As you drive, lower your chest and head onto their chest, applying downward and forward pressure. Your shoulder should be pressing into their sternum.
    7. Hip Drive: Simultaneously, drive your hips forward and under their hips, using your core and glutes for power. This actively collapses their base.
    8. Transition: Once their legs are cleared, bring your back leg forward to establish side control, maintaining chest-to-chest pressure.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Knee Placement: Placing your knee too far up on the thigh can lead to a knee-on-groin pressure, potentially damaging the collateral ligaments of your knee joint. The correct placement is on the mat, aimed at the hip crease.
    • Over-Extension of Hip: Driving your hips too far back and upward can expose your lower back to hyperextension or strain, especially if the opponent counter-grapples. Maintain a forward-driving hip angle and keep your core engaged.
    • Lack of Head and Chest Pressure: Trying to pass solely by pushing with your legs and arms neglects the vital torso connection. This can result in your opponent easily re-guarding or escaping. Always maintain strong head and chest pressure into their sternum.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Walkthrough (0%): Practice the leg movements, hip angles, and grip transitions without a partner. Focus on the sequence and body mechanics. (20 reps)
    2. Static Partner (0%): Have a partner lie on their back. Practice securing grips and moving your hips and knees through the motions, focusing on establishing the correct angles and pressure. (15 reps)
    3. Light Resistance (25%): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to practice the hip drive and leg clearing. Focus on maintaining your base. (10 reps per side)
    4. Controlled Pass (50%): Your partner actively tries to defend but without aggressive counter-movements. Focus on the weight transfer and clearing their legs. (10 reps per side)
    5. Simulated Pass (75%): Your partner defends with more intention, attempting to block your knee or shrimp away. Focus on maintaining your base and adjusting your pressure. (5 reps per side)
    6. Live Rolling (90-100%): Integrate the Knee Slice Pass into live rolling scenarios. Focus on recognizing opportunities and executing the pass under pressure. (As opportunities arise in rolls)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is in a closed guard and you have established a strong grip on their pants and hip.
    • Opponent is in a half-guard with their knee shield not fully established or you have broken it.
    • Opponent attempts a common guard retention move like shrimping away, creating an opening.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Shrimping Away: If the passer drives their knee into your thigh and you feel your hips being compressed, shrimp your hips away from their knee. This creates space to re-establish guard or move to a different position.
    • Elbow Escape: If the passer's knee is too high and causing discomfort, you can try to create space by driving your elbow into the mat and pushing your hips back. This can disrupt their base and allow you to recover guard.
    • Re-Guard: As the passer attempts to drive forward, use your free leg to hook their hip or leg and pull it back towards you, re-establishing your guard. This requires quick hip movement and a strong connection.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Knee Slice Pass?

    Your knee pain is likely due to placing your knee directly on your opponent's thigh or shin, creating shearing forces. Instead, aim to place your knee on the mat, acting as a pivot point and driving your hips forward. This biomechanically shifts the pressure away from your knee joint and onto your hips and core.

    Q: How do I Knee Slice Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision become paraMount. Focus on securing deep grips on their pants and hip to control their base. Drive your hips lower than theirs and use your head and shoulder to create a strong connection, preventing them from using their size to simply push you off. Think of it as collapsing their structure rather than overpowering them.

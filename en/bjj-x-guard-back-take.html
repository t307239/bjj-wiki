    More Questions

    What are the common mistakes when trying to transition from X-guard to a back take?

    A common mistake is not securing the opponent's leg properly, allowing them to shrimp out. Another is to overcommit to the sweep before establishing control for the back take, leaving you exposed.

    How do I prevent my opponent from posturing up and escaping when I'm in X-guard and aiming for the back?

    Maintaining tight control of their hips and legs is crucial. Use your legs to deny them space to posture, and consider using your free arm to control their arm or shoulder to limit their movement.

    What are the key grips I should be looking for in X-guard to set up a back take?

    Focus on securing a strong grip on their ankle or shin to control their leg, and use your other arm to grip their hip or belt to prevent them from posturing up. Some variations also involve controlling their arm.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: When I try to transition to the back from X-Guard, my opponent keeps their hips too low, and I can't get my hooks in, what am I doing wrong?

    Ensure your inside leg is actively extending and pushing their hip away, creating space. Simultaneously, use your outside leg's knee to 'climb' up their torso towards their armpit, driving your hips forward to secure the back.

    Q: How can I prevent my opponent from posturing up and escaping when I'm trying to switch to the back from X-Guard?

    As you extend your inside leg to create separation, keep your shin across their hip bone to control their base. Simultaneously, use your other leg to hook behind their knee or thigh, preventing them from standing and driving your hips in to maintain control.

    Q: My X-Guard back take feels very slow and my opponent easily defends, what's a key detail to make it faster against a resisting opponent?

    Focus on a swift, explosive hip drive forward as you extend your inside leg. This immediate forward momentum, combined with a tight curl of your hooking leg around their thigh, will prevent them from reacting and allow you to secure the back before they can establish a strong defense.

Worm Guard: A Precise BJJ Technique Guide for White Belts

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Purple Belt Requirements

Worm Guard is an open guard position initiated from your back, aiming to control your opponent's legs and hips to set up sweeps and submissions.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body awareness and attempting to force movements rather than using leverage.

The core mechanic is creating a strong, flexible frame with your legs and hips to off-balance and manipulate your opponent's base.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Starting from your back, establish a standard open guard base with knees bent, feet wide.
    2. Secure a 'S-Grip' on your opponent's right ankle with your left hand, thumb pointing up, palm facing you.
    3. Simultaneously, secure a 'Collar Grip' on your opponent's left lapel with your right hand, fingers inside the lapel.
    4. Hook your right shin across the outside of your opponent's right thigh, creating a perpendicular frame.
    5. Place your left foot on your opponent's left hip, maintaining a constant pressure pushing away.
    6. Rotate your hips to your left, creating an angle of approximately 45 degrees relative to your opponent.
    7. Shift your weight onto your left hip, using your right shin as a lever to control their leg's inward movement.
    8. Extend your left leg slightly, driving your left foot into their hip to maintain distance and control their posture.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly extending your leg to push their hip: This can hyperextend your knee joint, leading to ACL or MCL tears. Always maintain a slight bend in your knee and push with your foot and calf, not the full extension of your leg.
    • Gripping the opponent's ankle too tightly with a pronated forearm: This puts excessive strain on your wrist and elbow, risking a sprain or tendonitis. Maintain a neutral wrist and grip with your fingers and thumb, allowing for subtle adjustments.
    • Allowing your opponent to stack you by not maintaining hip angle: This can lead to spinal compression injuries if they drive their weight directly down. Continuously adjust your hip angle and use your left foot on their hip to create space and prevent them from collapsing onto you.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg and arm movements of setting up the S-grip and collar grip, focusing on hip rotation and shin placement. (50 reps each side)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Your partner stands passively. Execute the full Worm Guard setup, focusing on grip accuracy and frame creation. (20 reps each side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to establish grips and frames. Focus on maintaining control. (25% resistance, 15 reps each side)
    4. Partner drill (moderate resistance): Your partner attempts to posture up or pass. React by adjusting your frames and grips to maintain your guard. (50% resistance, 10 reps each side)
    5. Partner drill (advanced resistance): Your partner actively tries to pass your guard. Focus on transitioning between sweeps and maintaining control. (75% resistance, 5 reps each side)
    6. Live rolling: Implement Worm Guard during rolling, focusing on setting it up and using it to initiate sweeps or submissions. (90% resistance, 5 minutes)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is posturing up slightly, creating an opening to attack their base.
    • When your opponent attempts a knee-slice or pressure pass from your open guard.
    • When you have established good grips on their ankles or shins and can transition to the Worm Guard setup.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass: Opponent drives forward, attempting to collapse your guard by driving their hips onto yours. Counter by maintaining your hip angle, pushing their hips away with your foot on their hip, and using your S-grip to pull their leg in.
    • Toreando Pass: Opponent circles their hips to the outside, trying to step around your legs. Counter by using your collar grip to pull them back in and your S-grip to control their leg, adjusting your hip angle to face them.
    • Knee Slice Pass: Opponent tries to drive their knee across your body. Counter by creating a strong frame with your shin and foot on their hip, and using your S-grip to prevent them from advancing their knee.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to grip their ankle in Worm Guard?

    This is usually due to gripping with a pronated (palm down) forearm and forcing the grip with your fingers. Instead, maintain a neutral wrist, grip with your fingers and thumb, and use your forearm to apply pressure. Think of it as a 'cupped' hand grip around their ankle, allowing for subtle adjustments and preventing hyperextension.

    Q: How do I use Worm Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes even more critical. Focus on creating a very strong frame with your shin on their thigh and your foot on their hip. Use your S-grip to control their leg's movement and your collar grip to break their posture. Your hip angle is key; constantly work to get your hips off to the side, making it harder for them to stack you.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from a standard open guard to Worm Guard?

    The ideal time is when your opponent is slightly off-balance or creating an opening by posturing up. If they are too tightly controlled in a closed or Half Guard, Worm Guard setup is difficult. Look for moments when they are shifting weight or attempting to move their hips, as this indicates a potential for you to exploit their base.

Precise Stack Pass Technique for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Stack Pass is a pressure-based guard pass typically initiated from a Closed Guard or open guard position. It aims to break the opponent's posture and control their legs to establish side control.

White belts often struggle because they rely on brute strength rather than positional mechanics. They might try to force their way through, leading to ineffective passes and potential injury.

The core mechanic is creating a heavy, forward-driving pressure that collapses the opponent's base and allows you to off-balance them to one side.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grips: Secure a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand and a strong sleeve grip (left sleeve) with your left hand. Ensure your knuckles are pointed towards the ceiling.
    2. Base: Establish a wide, stable base with your knees slightly bent and your hips low, almost in a squatting position.
    3. Posture: Maintain an upright posture, preventing your opponent from collapsing you forward.
    4. Initial Pressure: Begin to drive your hips forward and slightly down, creating a 'stacking' sensation against the opponent's hips.
    5. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your lead leg (left leg) as you begin to pull their right leg towards you with your right hand on the collar.
    6. Leg Control: With your left hand on their sleeve, begin to peel their right leg away and across your body, creating an angle.
    7. Hip Drive: Continue driving your hips forward and to your right, aiming to get your left knee to the mat on the outside of their hip.
    8. Head Position: Keep your head up and facing forward, driving your forehead towards their shoulder to maintain forward pressure.
    9. Final Pass: As their base collapses, step your right leg through and over their hips to secure side control.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Knee Injury Risk: A white belt might aggressively drive their knee directly into the opponent's hip socket without proper angling. This can cause a hyperextension or direct impact injury to the collateral ligaments of the knee.
    • Lower Back Strain: Attempting to 'muscle' the pass by hunching over and pulling with the back, rather than driving with the hips and legs. This can lead to acute lumbar strain due to excessive spinal flexion under load.
    • Wrist and Elbow Sprains: Gripping too tightly with the hands and wrists, or allowing the opponent to twist the arm during the sleeve pull, can result in ligamentous sprains of the wrist or elbow joints.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Grip and Posture Drill: Practice securing the collar and sleeve grips, then holding a stable base with hips low and chest up for 30 seconds. Repeat 10 times.
    2. Solo Hip Drive Drill: From your knees, practice driving your hips forward and slightly down, simulating the initial stacking pressure, for 30 seconds. Repeat 10 times.
    3. Partnered 'Simulated' Stack Pass (0% Resistance): With a partner lying on their back, practice your grips, posture, and the initial hip drive and weight transfer. Partner remains passive. 5 reps each side.
    4. Partnered 'Passive Guard' Stack Pass (25% Resistance): Partner maintains a very loose, unresisted guard. Focus on executing the full sequence with light pressure. 5 reps each side.
    5. Partnered 'Light Resistance' Stack Pass (50% Resistance): Partner actively tries to recompose guard or hip escape but offers no strong grips or frames. Focus on maintaining pressure and adjusting to their subtle movements. 5 reps each side.
    6. Partnered 'Moderate Resistance' Stack Pass (75% Resistance): Partner actively attempts to frame and resist the pass, but not with full intensity. Focus on breaking frames and maintaining forward pressure. 5 reps each side.
    7. Live Rolling Application (90-100% Resistance): Attempt the Stack Pass in rolling situations, focusing on successful execution against a resisting opponent. Aim for 3 successful passes per round.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is turtled or has their knees close to their chest, limiting their ability to hip escape.
    • When your opponent attempts to shrimp or create space, and you can quickly close the distance and drive forward.
    • When your opponent has a weak or broken posture, making them susceptible to forward pressure.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Recomposition: As the passer drives forward, the defender can use their hands to frame against the passer's hips and knees, creating space to bring their knees back between their legs.
    • Hip Escape to Reguard: If the passer gets too deep, the defender can use a strong hip escape to create an angle and re-establish guard from the side.
    • Turtle: If the passer is too heavy and the defender cannot create space, they can tuck their chin and turn into a turtle position, forcing the passer to deal with a new positional challenge.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Stack Pass?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are driving your knee directly into your opponent's hip socket without proper angling. This puts immense shear force on your knee joint. To fix this, ensure your knee is driving *across* their hip, aiming to land slightly outside their pelvis as you step your other leg through, creating a more stable, angled base.

    Q: How can I Stack Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a bigger opponent, brute force is your enemy. Focus on creating a tight, forward-driving pressure that compresses their base. Use the collar grip to pull their head down and their hips forward, while your other hand controls a sleeve to prevent them from framing effectively. Drive with your legs and hips, not just your arms.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Stack Pass, and what if they pull me into their guard?

    The Stack Pass is best attempted when your opponent is Turtled or has their knees tightly tucked, limiting their mobility. If they pull you into their guard, you have a few options: immediately transition to a knee-slice pass if you can get your knee across, or reset to a stable base and reassess. Do not try to force a stack pass if you are already deep in their guard.

X-Guard Sweep: Your First BJJ Sweep

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The X-Guard Sweep originates from a Closed Guard or open guard position, aiming to unbalance your opponent and take the top position. It leverages leg entanglements to create leverage for a sweep.

White belts often struggle with this sweep due to a lack of understanding of body mechanics and an over-reliance on brute force. They might grip incorrectly or fail to generate the necessary hip movement.

The core principle of the X-Guard Sweep is to use your legs to elevate and destabilize your opponent's base, creating a moment of imbalance that allows you to transition to a superior position.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip opponent's left pant leg with your right hand, securing a deep grip just above the ankle bone. Your left hand grips opponent's right pant leg at the knee.
    2. Place your right foot (inside leg) on the opponent's left hip crease, creating a frame. Your left foot (outside leg) hooks behind the opponent's right knee.
    3. Sit up by bringing your hips forward and slightly to your left, creating a strong base. Your spine should be relatively upright.
    4. Elevate your opponent's left leg by pulling down on their pant leg with your right hand. Simultaneously, push their right knee with your left foot.
    5. Drive your hips forward and up, using your right leg on their hip to push them away and your left leg to scoop their knee.
    6. As your opponent's weight shifts to their right leg, drive your right foot into their left hip, creating a powerful lever.
    7. Drive your chest into their left thigh and simultaneously extend your right leg to lift their left leg upwards, initiating the sweep.
    8. As they fall, transition your hips to the side and drive your left knee forward to establish side control. Maintain grips.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Gripping too low on the pant leg (below the ankle) reduces your leverage, potentially causing you to hyperextend your own ankle joint when lifting.
    • Failing to maintain a strong frame with your inside foot on the hip allows your opponent to collapse into you, risking knee valgus stress on your own knee.
    • Attempting to lift with your arms alone instead of driving your hips forward and up leads to shoulder strain and ineffective leverage, often causing you to fall back onto your own tailbone.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg entanglements and hip movement without a partner. Focus on the transitions between grips and foot placement. (10 reps)
    2. Partner drilling (static): With a partner kneeling, practice the grip acquisition and hip elevation without any resistance. (20 reps)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Partner offers minimal push. Focus on driving your hips and executing the sweep motion. (30 reps total, 15 each side)
    4. Partner drilling (simulated): Partner actively tries to posture up. You focus on maintaining your X-Guard position and executing the sweep when an opening appears. (50 reps total, 25 each side)
    5. Flow drilling: Execute the X-Guard Sweep as part of a sequence, e.g., from closed guard. Partner offers moderate resistance. (1 minute rounds, 5 rounds)
    6. Live rolling (controlled): Attempt the X-Guard Sweep during sparring with a higher-ranking belt who is aware and will offer appropriate resistance. (3 minute rounds, 3 rounds)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up and has a wide base from your closed guard.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your open guard and their weight is distributed unevenly.
    • When you have successfully established the X-Guard position and have control of their legs.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass Defense: If your opponent attempts to stack pass, widen your base, drive your hips back, and use your free leg to push their chest away. Maintain your X-guard grips.
    • Kneeling Base: If your opponent stabilizes by kneeling, maintain your X-guard and look to transition to a single leg X-guard by extending your inside leg and bringing your outside foot to their hip.
    • Standing Posture: If your opponent stands up completely, release your leg grips and immediately transition to a butterfly guard or single leg X-guard to prevent them from posturing over you.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the X-Guard Sweep from closed guard?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are allowing your opponent to collapse into you or you are not properly framing with your inside foot on their hip. This can cause your knee to buckle inwards (valgus stress). Ensure you maintain a strong frame with your right foot on their hip crease and drive your hips forward, creating space and preventing their weight from compromising your knee joint.

    Q: How do I X-Guard Sweep against a much bigger and heavier opponent?

    Against a heavier opponent, leverage becomes even more critical. Focus on precise grip placement on their pants, aiming for the ankle. Elevate their leg higher and drive your hips with more conviction. Think about using their weight against them by creating a sharp angle and a quick, explosive lift rather than trying to overpower them with pure strength. Timing is key; sweep when they are slightly off-balance.

    Q: When is the best time to transition into X-Guard for a sweep?

    The ideal time to transition into X-Guard for a sweep is when your opponent is actively trying to pass your guard and their weight is forward and distributed unevenly. This often happens when they commit to a knee-slide or toreando pass. As they move, quickly adjust your leg position to entangle their legs and secure the X-Guard grips before they can recover their base.

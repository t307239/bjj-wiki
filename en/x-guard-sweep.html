The X-Guard Sweep is initiated from an open guard position, aiming to invert your hips and off-balance your opponent to create a sweep opportunity.

Many white belts struggle by relying on brute strength or poor body positioning, leading to ineffective attempts.

The key is to use your legs to control and elevate their base, creating a lever to tilt them over.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip: Establish a collar grip with your non-sweeping arm and an ankle grip on the opponent's foot with your hand on the same side as the collar grip.
    2. Entry: Sit up slightly, bringing your opponent's leg between your hips. Your hips should be angled slightly to your side.
    3. Hook: Place your inside leg (same side as ankle grip) behind the opponent's knee pit, hooking their hamstring.
    4. Elevation: Drive your hooking leg’s hip upwards, creating a strong upward pressure against their knee.
    5. Base Shift: Simultaneously, pull down on the collar grip and pull your ankle grip towards you, creating a pulling force.
    6. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your hips, lifting your opponent's base off the mat with the combined pulling and lifting forces.
    7. Sweep Execution: As their weight shifts forward and they lose balance, extend your hooking leg and drive your hips forward to complete the sweep, aiming to land in their guard or side control.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Knee hyperextension: A white belt might try to 'kick' their hooking leg straight, overextending the knee joint and risking ligament damage (ACL/MCL).

    The correct movement is a controlled hip elevation, not a forceful leg extension.

    • Ankle/Foot Sprain: Grabbing the ankle too loosely or attempting to 'yank' the foot can lead to the opponent's foot slipping out and causing a twisting ankle injury.

    Maintain a firm, secure grip around the ankle and focus on pulling the foot towards your body.

    • Lower Back Strain: Trying to lift the opponent by arching the back instead of driving the hips will strain the lumbar spine.

    Focus on generating power through hip extension and core engagement.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Elevation Drill: From a seated position, practice lifting your hips off the ground using only your legs and core, simulating the X-guard hook action. (10 reps)
    2. Solo Grip and Pull Drill: Practice gripping your own ankle and a gi lapel, then practice the pulling and lifting motion without an opponent. (10 reps)
    3. Partnered Entry and Hook Drill (0% Resistance): Your partner stands. You sit up and establish the X-guard entry and hook. Partner stands still. (5 reps per side)
    4. Partnered Sweep Drill (25% Resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to your sweep attempt. Focus on the timing of the pull and lift. (5 reps per side)
    5. Partnered Sweep Drill (50% Resistance): Partner actively tries to post their base and resist the sweep. Focus on maintaining your structure. (5 reps per side)
    6. Live Rolling (75% Resistance): Attempt the sweep during live rolling, prioritizing correct mechanics over success. (5 attempts)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is posturing up and their weight is forward.
    • When you have successfully isolated one of their legs within your guard.
    • When they are attempting to pass your guard and you can invert to attack their base.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass: If you are too slow to elevate, the opponent can drive their weight forward and stack you, crushing your hips and potentially injuring your lower back.

    Maintain a strong hip frame and be ready to switch to a different guard if the sweep is not viable.

    • Knee Re-Pistol: The opponent can try to bring their free leg back to their hips and 'pistol' their knee, making it difficult to elevate and potentially creating an opportunity for them to pass.

    Keep your inside hook tight and be prepared to adjust your angle or transition to a sweep from a different guard.

    • Leg Un-Hook: The opponent can try to pull their hooked leg out or step over your body to escape the X-guard.

    Keep your hooking leg tight, use your hips to control their leg, and be ready to switch grips or transition to a different attack.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my hip hurt when I try the X-Guard Sweep?

    Hip pain during the X-Guard Sweep often stems from incorrect hip angulation and insufficient upward drive. Instead of driving your hip directly into your opponent's knee pit, you might be forcing a sideways rotation that strains your hip capsule. Ensure your hips are angled slightly towards your opponent and you are actively pushing your hooking leg's hip upwards, not just rotating.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paramount. Focus on precise timing and control rather than brute force. Use your collar grip to break their posture and pull them forward. Elevate their knee with your hooked leg, creating a pivot point. The key is to use their weight against them by generating a strong pulling and lifting action simultaneously, rather than trying to muscle them over.

    Q: You should consider switching if your opponent successfully stabilizes their base and resists your primary sweep attempt. If they manage to 'stack' you effectively or if their free leg becomes too difficult to control, your X-guard might be compromised. Be ready to transition to a different guard like de la riva or spider guard, or even attempt a sweep from a different angle if the X-guard sweep is no longer viable.

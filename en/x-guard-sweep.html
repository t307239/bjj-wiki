The X-Guard sweep is initiated from a foundational open guard position, aiming to elevate your opponent and transition to a dominant top position, typically mount or side control.

White belts often struggle with this sweep due to a lack of understanding of hip pressure and weight distribution, leading to ineffective attempts or lost balance.

The core mechanical principle is using your legs to create a fulcrum, lifting and unbalancing your opponent by controlling their base and driving your hips forward.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish an X-guard by placing one foot (e.g., right foot) on your opponent's hip and hooking your shin across the front of their thigh, with your knee pointing towards their opposite side.
    2. Secure a grip on your opponent's left pant leg, just above the knee, with your left hand. Your right arm should reach across to secure their right collar or shoulder.
    3. Drive your hips backward and slightly to your left, creating a strong base and pulling your opponent's leg towards you.
    4. Simultaneously, begin to elevate your opponent's right leg by pushing with your right foot on their hip, angling your body to the left.
    5. As your opponent's weight shifts onto their remaining leg, drive your hips forward and upward, using your legs to lift their elevated leg.
    6. Extend your right leg from the hip, pushing their right thigh upwards and away from your body, while simultaneously pulling their left pant leg towards you.
    7. Transition your hips to their side as they fall, maintaining control of their pant leg and collar to prevent them from recovering guard or posturing up.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Gripping too low on the pant leg: This allows your opponent to easily pull their leg out, leaving your knee vulnerable to hyperextension injuries if they step over it.
    • Incorrect hip angle and driving forward too early: This can result in you being stacked, leading to spinal compression or neck injuries.
    • Not establishing a strong base with your non-sweeping leg: This causes you to lose balance and fall over, potentially injuring your ankle or knee from an awkward fall.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice establishing the X-guard position and the leg elevation motion without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Your partner stands passively. Focus on executing the exact grip changes, hip angles, and leg movements. (50 reps)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the sweep mechanics. Focus on maintaining your base. (25 reps)
    4. Controlled X-guard sweeps: Your partner actively tries to defend but does not resist the sweep mechanics fully. Focus on timing your hip drive. (20 reps per side)
    5. Live rolling (limited X-guard): Only attempt X-guard sweeps for 1-minute intervals. Focus on applying the learned mechanics. (5 x 1-minute rounds)
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the X-guard sweep whenever the opportunity arises in a regular rolling session. (10 minutes)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up and applying pressure from your closed guard.
    • When your opponent is attempting to pass your open guard and their weight is forward.
    • When you have successfully isolated one of your opponent's legs and have control of their pant leg.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • To defend the X-Guard sweep, immediately drive your hips forward and downward, creating a strong base and preventing your opponent from elevating your leg.
    • If your opponent has initiated the sweep, attempt to bring your free leg back to a defensive position or sprawl to flatten them out, negating their hip drive.
    • If your opponent has successfully elevated your leg, immediately try to step your other leg over their head or body to establish a guard pass or escape the position.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the X-Guard Sweep?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are allowing your opponent to step over it or you are not maintaining proper knee alignment. Ensure your knee is always tucked behind their thigh and your shin is across the front. When they try to step over, immediately bring your free leg to their hip or Sprawl to flatten them.

    Q: How do I X-Guard Sweep against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a bigger opponent, focus on speed and leverage. Instead of relying on brute strength to lift, use your hips to create a strong fulcrum. Drive your hips forward and upward precisely when they commit their weight. Secure a deep grip on their pant leg and use your foot on their hip to create a strong pushing force.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate the X-Guard Sweep?

    The optimal time is when your opponent's weight is distributed forward, typically when they are posturing up or attempting to pass your guard. Look for moments of imbalance. If they are too static or have their weight heavily on their hips, the sweep will be much harder to execute effectively.

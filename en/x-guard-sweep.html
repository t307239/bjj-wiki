Precise X-Guard Sweep Technique for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The X-Guard Sweep is initiated from a position where one of your legs is hooked inside the opponent's thigh, and the other controls their hip, aiming to disrupt their base and elevate yourself.

White belts often struggle with this by trying to force the sweep with upper body strength, neglecting the crucial leg mechanics and hip movement.

The key insight is using your legs to create leverage and your hips to drive forward, effectively unbalancing your opponent and initiating the sweep.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip opponent's right shin with your left hand (overhook). Grip opponent's left ankle with your right hand (underhook).
    2. Place your left foot on the opponent's right hip crease, toes pointing upwards.
    3. Place your right foot on the opponent's left thigh, heel dug into the inner thigh crease.
    4. Shift your weight onto your left hip, creating a 45-degree angle between your torso and the opponent's legs.
    5. Drive your left foot into the opponent's hip crease, pushing their leg away slightly to create space.
    6. Simultaneously, extend your right leg, driving your heel towards the opponent's groin, while your right hand pulls their ankle towards you.
    7. As you extend your right leg, explosively drive your hips forward and upward, lifting the opponent's left leg and unbalancing them to their right.
    8. Once they are off balance, shrimp your hips to your left and bring your left leg through to establish a top position, ideally side control or mount.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying excessive upward force with your hands on the opponent's shin and ankle without hip drive can hyperextend your own elbows and wrists, risking ligament tears.
    • Instead, focus on driving your hips forward to create the sweep leverage, using your hands for control and slight pulling.
    • Incorrectly placing your hooking foot (left foot on hip) too high or too low can lead to your knee being vulnerable to strikes or hyperflexion injuries if the opponent falls heavily.
    • Ensure your left foot is firmly planted on the hip crease, providing a stable base to push from.
    • Sweeping with a straight back and bent knees, rather than driving with your hips, can strain your lower back and put you in a vulnerable position if the sweep fails.
    • Maintain a slight bend in your knees and drive the sweep motion from your pelvis and core.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg positioning, hip angle, and hip drive motion without a partner. Focus on smooth transitions between leg placements. (10 reps)
    2. Partner drill - static: Opponent lies on their back. You get into X-Guard position and practice the hip drive and leg extension motion. Opponent offers no resistance. (15 reps)
    3. Partner drill - controlled resistance: Opponent provides light weight and minimal defense. Focus on executing the sweep with correct hip drive. (20 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner drill - increasing resistance: Opponent starts to actively resist by posturing up or trying to step out. Focus on maintaining control and adapting your hip drive. (20 reps, 50% resistance)
    5. Live rolling - positional sparring: Start from X-Guard. Focus solely on executing the sweep and transitioning to a top position. Opponent defends actively. (3 rounds x 2 minutes, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling - full rolling: Integrate the X-Guard Sweep into your rolling. Look for opportunities to initiate the sweep from the guard. (5 minutes, 90% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured up and you have good control of their legs.
    • When the opponent attempts a guard pass and you can invert into X-Guard.
    • When the opponent is heavy on top and you want to create space to attack.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • When the opponent attempts the sweep, actively drive your weight forward and down, flattening your base to resist the hip elevation.
    • If the opponent commits to the sweep, quickly bring your free leg (the one not being hooked) back and to the side, creating a base to prevent your balance from being broken.
    • If the opponent's hip drive is weak, the opponent can attempt to step their free leg over your head and into a pass, or to sit back and regain guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the X-Guard Sweep?

    Your knee may hurt if your hooking leg (left leg) is not properly positioned on the opponent's hip crease. If it's too high, you risk hyperextending your knee. If it's too low, you lose leverage. Ensure your foot is flat on the hip bone, and your knee is bent, creating a stable, supportive base for your hip drive.

    Q: How do I X-Guard Sweep against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a bigger opponent, leverage is paraMount. Focus on a very tight X-Guard grip, ensuring your hooking leg is deep on their hip crease. Use your hips to drive *forward* and *up*, not just up. Think of scooping their weight with your hips. Your grip on their shin and ankle becomes critical for controlling their base.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from X-Guard Sweep to another sweep?

    If your opponent successfully blocks your hip drive by posting their foot or driving their weight down heavily, the X-Guard Sweep might be compromised. In this scenario, look to transition by either shrimping your hips out to reset or by using your freed leg to attack their other leg for a different sweep, such as a Tripod Sweep.

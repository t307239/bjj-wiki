The X-Guard Sweep is initiated from an open guard position, aiming to elevate and unbalance your opponent to pass their legs and achieve a dominant position.

Many white belts struggle because they focus on pulling rather than creating leverage and proper hip mechanics.

The core principle is using your legs as levers to invert your opponent's base, making them fall.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a deep collar grip with your right hand, pulling your opponent's gi towards your sternum. Your left hand secures their right knee-weld.
    2. Place your left foot on your opponent's right hip crease, creating a frame. Your right foot hooks behind their right ankle.
    3. Shift your weight onto your left hip, angling your torso approximately 45 degrees away from your opponent. Your hips are slightly elevated off the mat.
    4. Scoot your hips back slightly, extending your left leg to create space. Your right leg remains anchored to their ankle.
    5. Drive your left foot into their hip crease while simultaneously pulling their knee towards your chest with your right hand.
    6. As you drive and pull, rotate your hips to your left, lifting your opponent's right leg and unbalancing them to their right.
    7. Immediately extend your right leg, pushing their ankle away and up, while continuing to drive your left foot into their hip to complete the sweep.
    8. As they fall, transition your left foot to their hip for a stable base and your right foot to secure their leg to prevent them from reguarding.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Knee Hyperextension: Grabbing the opponent's ankle and extending your leg forcefully without proper hip rotation can cause your own knee to hyperextend, damaging the ACL. Maintain a slight bend in your knee and focus on the hip rotation to initiate the sweep.
    • Ankle/Foot Injury: Trying to 'kick' the opponent's leg away with your foot from the ankle hook without proper leverage can lead to your foot being twisted or your ankle being sprained. Use your entire leg as a lever, driving with your hip and pulling with your arms.
    • Lower Back Strain: Arching your lower back excessively to lift the opponent instead of using hip elevation and leg drive can cause lumbar strain. Keep your core engaged and utilize the mechanics of your hips and legs for the lift.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Mobility Drill: Practice hip elevation and scooping motions with your legs, focusing on the 45-degree hip angle. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    2. Static X-Guard Setup: From open guard, practice achieving the left foot on hip and right foot on ankle grip, focusing on hip angle. (15 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    3. Assisted Sweep (No Resistance): Partner lies on their back. You set up X-Guard and practice the full sweep motion with no resistance from your partner. (10 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Assisted Sweep (Light Resistance): Partner offers mild resistance by trying to posture up slightly. You execute the sweep, focusing on driving and pulling. (15 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Controlled Sweep (Partner Attempts to Recover Guard): Partner actively tries to recover their guard or resist the sweep. You focus on maintaining control and finishing the sweep. (20 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling Sweep Attempt: During rolling, actively look for opportunities to initiate the X-Guard Sweep, focusing on proper mechanics and reaction to partner's defense. (5 attempts per round, 90-100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up slightly, looking to pass your open guard.
    • When your opponent has established knee-on-belly or side control and you are looking to escape or reverse.
    • When your opponent is attempting a guard pass like a toreando or leg drag and their weight is forward.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass Defense: If your opponent attempts a stack pass, immediately drive your left foot into their hip and sit up, creating a strong frame to prevent them from crushing you. Keep your hips mobile.
    • Leg Defense (By Opponent): If the opponent starts to pull their leg out of your ankle hook, focus on tightening your grip on their knee-weld and driving your left foot deeper into their hip crease to maintain control and unbalance.
    • Guard Recovery (By Opponent): If the opponent successfully recovers their guard after the sweep, immediately switch to a leg drag or knee slice pass attempt as their base is compromised.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the X-Guard Sweep?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are hyperextending it when hooking the ankle. Instead of trying to 'kick' with your leg, focus on using your hip to drive and your arms to pull. Keep a slight bend in your knee and ensure your hip rotation is the primary driver of the sweep to protect your knee joint.

    Q: How do I X-Guard Sweep against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a bigger opponent, leverage is even more critical. Ensure your initial collar grip is tight and you are pulling their weight towards you. Focus on creating a significant hip angle (45 degrees) and driving your foot into their hip crease forcefully to create the unbalancing lever. Your weight distribution and the timing of your drive are key.

    Q: If your opponent successfully bases out or resists the initial drive, don't force it. Recognize the failure and immediately transition. If they are still in front of you, you can attempt a scissor sweep or tripod sweep. If they are trying to posture back up, consider transitioning to a triangle choke setup.

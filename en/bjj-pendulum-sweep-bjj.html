    More Questions

    How do I prevent my opponent from stacking me during a pendulum sweep?

    To prevent stacking, keep your hips low and push off the mat with your free leg. Actively use your shoulder and arm to create space and maintain a strong base, preventing them from driving their weight down on you.

    What's the most common mistake people make with the pendulum sweep?

    A common mistake is not committing to the hip escape and leg extension. Many people hesitate, allowing their opponent to react and defend, or they don't generate enough momentum to lift and sweep effectively.

    When is the pendulum sweep most effective?

    The pendulum sweep is most effective when your opponent is postured up or trying to pass your guard from a high position. It's also great when they are leaning heavily on one side, creating an imbalance you can exploit.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my opponent's arm get stuck and I can't complete the Pendulum Sweep even though I'm rotating my hips?

    This usually happens when your hips aren't rotating with enough perpendicular force into their torso; instead of just turning, actively drive your hips *across* their body, creating a wedge that forces their arm to extend and become vulnerable. Ensure your shoulder on the same side as the trapped arm is driving into their hip to prevent them from bridging out.

    Q: How can I generate enough hip pressure to execute a Pendulum Sweep effectively when my opponent is much heavier than me?

    Focus on using your body's leverage by anchoring your shin across their hip crease and using your opposite leg to drive your heel towards your glutes, creating a strong fulcrum. Simultaneously, drive your chest into their chest and your shoulder into their hip to control their weight distribution and prevent them from posturing up.

    Q: What's the proper way to control my opponent's legs during a Pendulum Sweep to ensure they can't easily reguard or escape?

    Secure your shin across their hip and use your other leg's foot to hook behind their knee or calf, preventing them from straightening their legs. As you initiate the sweep, keep your hooked foot actively pulling their leg towards you while your hips drive forward, maintaining a tight connection that limits their mobility.

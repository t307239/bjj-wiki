    More Questions

    How do I start analyzing my BJJ matches after a competition?

    Begin by watching your matches with a critical but objective eye. Focus on identifying recurring patterns in your successes and failures, rather than just dwelling on individual mistakes. It's helpful to have a notebook or digital document to jot down key observations as you go.

    What are the most important things to look for in a post-competition BJJ analysis?

    Prioritize understanding why you succeeded or failed in specific situations. Look for your preferred transitions, common submission attempts, and the positions where you felt most comfortable or uncomfortable. Analyzing your opponent's strategies against you is also crucial for identifying weaknesses.

    How can I use post-competition analysis to improve my BJJ game?

    Translate your observations into actionable training goals. If you consistently got swept from guard, dedicate more mat time to guard retention drills. If you missed a submission opportunity, practice that specific technique until it becomes second nature. Consistent analysis and targeted training are the keys to progress.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why do I feel like I'm always gassing out during my Post-Competition Analysis, even if I felt fine in training?

    During competition, your nervous system is highly activated, leading to increased muscle tension and a faster heart rate. This elevated state burns through your glycogen stores and oxygen much quicker than the controlled environment of training, causing premature fatigue.

    Q: How can I improve my Post-Competition Analysis when my opponent is much stronger and heavier than me?

    Focus on leverage and timing rather than brute strength. Utilize proper hip movement to create angles and off-balance your heavier opponent, and use their weight against them by controlling their base and structure with precise grips and body positioning.

    Q: I keep getting stuck in bad positions during Post-Competition Analysis and can't escape. What am I doing wrong biomechanically?

    You're likely not using your hips effectively to create space or frame. Instead of pushing with your arms, drive your hips into your opponent's center of gravity to create a gap, allowing you to shrimp out or establish a better defensive structure.

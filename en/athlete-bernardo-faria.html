Bernardo Faria

"The Half Guard King"
🌍 BR 🥋 Black Belt ⚖️ Super Heavyweight 🏫 Alliance
  • IBJJF World Champion 5×
  • ADCC Champion 2015
  • Pan American Champion 4×
Bernardo Faria, nicknamed 'The Half Guard King', is one of the most accomplished Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu athletes from Brazilian. Known for exceptional skill in half guard, deep half guard, double under pass, omoplata, Bernardo has earned a reputation as one of the sport's most technically refined competitors. Training out of Alliance, they have amassed an impressive collection of world titles and competition victories across gi and no-gi formats.

Biography

Bernardo Faria began training Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu and quickly demonstrated natural talent for the sport. Born in 1987, they progressed through the belt ranks at an accelerated pace, earning their black belt and immediately competing at the highest levels. Their affiliation with Alliance provided world-class training partners and coaching that accelerated their development.

On the competition circuit, Bernardo Faria accumulated title after title: IBJJF World Champion 5×; ADCC Champion 2015; Pan American Champion 4×. Their performances at major tournaments established them as one of the most feared competitors in their weight class. Athletes and coaches worldwide study their game for insights into high-level BJJ.

Beyond competition, Bernardo Faria has contributed to the BJJ community through teaching, instructionals, and demonstrating that technical mastery can prevail over physical advantages. Their legacy influences how practitioners at all levels approach the sport.

Today, Bernardo Faria continues to train, teach, and compete, passing on the lessons of their championship career to the next generation of BJJ athletes.

Fighting Style

Bernardo Faria's game is defined by excellence in half guard, Deep Half Guard, double under pass, omoplata. Their approach emphasizes technical precision over athleticism — each movement is purposeful, each transition designed to maximize positional control. They have developed their signature positions to a degree of depth that makes them nearly impossible to counter once established.

What makes Bernardo's style particularly effective is the seamless connection between offense and defense. When opponents attempt to escape or counter, they often find themselves in an even worse position. This quality — turning defense into offense — is the hallmark of elite BJJ.

Signature Technique

Bernardo Faria's most iconic technique is the Half Guard. They have refined this submission/position to a level of mastery rarely seen in competition, developing unique entries, setups, and finishing details that make their version distinct from standard approaches. Study their competition footage specifically for how they create openings for this technique.

In-Depth: Bernardo Faria

Biomechanics & Physics

Bernardo Faria's dominant half guard game is a masterclass in applied biomechanics. His fundamental understanding of leverage is evident in his pressure passing. When establishing his trademark over-under pass or double-under pass, he utilizes the iliotibial band and the lateral epicondyle of the humerus to create opposing forces. By driving his shoulder into the opponent's hip socket (acetabulum) and anchoring his arm around their waist or thigh, he establishes a fulcrum. The upward force generated by his hips and legs, combined with the downward pressure of his upper body, creates a significant shear force that disrupts the opponent's base and mobility. In scrambles and sweeps from deep half guard, Faria manipulates the opponent's center of gravity by isolating a leg and using his own pelvic girdle as a base. His ability to elevate his hips and drive forward, using his scapula against their femur, allows him to generate the torque necessary to initiate a sweep, often by leveraging their own weight against them.

Common Mistakes (Specific to This Technique)

  • **Insufficient Hip Pressure in Over-Under Pass:** Allowing the opponent to create space by bridging or shrimping because the passer fails to drive their shoulder into the hip and keep their hips heavy. This negates the leverage Faria expertly uses.
  • **Over-extending the Body in Deep Half Guard:** When attempting a sweep, fighters often try to 'pull' their opponent over. Faria's method involves driving *into* the opponent with his hips, using his own body mass as the primary engine for the sweep, rather than relying solely on pulling mechanics.
  • **Losing the Grip Fight in Half Guard:** Failing to maintain strong grips on the opponent's pants or hips allows them to establish better posture and escape the half guard before Faria can apply his signature pressure.

Variations & Counters

Faria's half guard is not a static position but a dynamic platform for transitions and attacks. From his deep half guard, if the opponent attempts to stack him, he is adept at transitioning to a technical stand-up or utilizing a leg drag sweep. His double-under pass, when met with resistance, can fluidly morph into an arm-in guillotine choke or transition to a side-control position like the K-mount. Conversely, opponents attempting to pass Faria's half guard are often met with his omoplata or a high-risk ankle lock when they overcommit their weight. The key is his seamless integration of passing, sweeping, and submission threats, forcing his opponent into a perpetual defensive reaction where he can dictate the engagement. His ability to attack submissions while maintaining control of the half guard leg is a hallmark of his game.

Drilling Recommendations

For a foundational understanding of Faria's half guard, drill the "knee shield to deep half guard" transition. Start in standard half guard, establish a strong knee shield with 10-15 reps, then transition to deep half guard, focusing on hooking the opponent's leg and securing the seatbelt grip. Next, practice the double-under pass setup. From guard or half guard, drill securing the double-under grip, focusing on driving hips forward and maintaining chest-to-chest contact for 20-30 reps, with a resisting partner making small adjustments. Finally, drill the omoplata entry from half guard. From a standard half guard, practice entering the omoplata by sweeping the opponent's leg over your shoulder, focusing on maintaining hip control and finishing the rotation for 15-20 reps per side. Progress to live rolling with a focus on implementing one of these concepts within the first 5 minutes of each round.

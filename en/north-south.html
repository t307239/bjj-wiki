The North-South position is achieved when your head is near your opponent's hips and their head is near your hips, creating a perpendicular alignment.

White belts often struggle with this position by giving up chest-to-chest pressure or allowing their opponent to turn into them.

The key is maintaining a tight "head-to-hip" connection while controlling the opponent's shoulders and hips with your own body.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From side control, transition your hips towards your opponent's head.
    2. Place your chest directly over their sternum, aligning your head towards their hip line.
    3. Hook your opponent's near armpit with your arm, establishing a "seatbelt" grip (your forearm across their chest, hand gripping your own bicep).
    4. Place your other hand on their far shoulder, controlling their upper body rotation.
    5. Drive your hips down and slightly forward, creating downward pressure.
    6. Ensure your knees are bent and your base is wide, with your shins pressing into the mat.
    7. Maintain a tight frame; do not let your chest fall away from their body.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly reaching for the opponent's head: This leaves your own head exposed and can lead to a guillotine choke or allows the opponent to create space and turn. The neck and cervical spine are vulnerable to hyperextension or flexion injuries.
    • Letting your hips sag: This reduces your control and allows the opponent to bridge or shrimp away. This can strain the lumbar spine and hip flexors.
    • Spreading your knees wide and flat: This makes you unstable and prone to sweeps. It can also put undue stress on the knee ligaments (MCL/LCL) if you are forced to hold an awkward position.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice assuming the North-South position from a mock side control, focusing on hip and chest placement (10 reps, 0% resistance).
    2. Partner drilling (static): Your partner lies flat. You assume North-South position, focusing on achieving the correct chest-to-sternum and head-to-hip alignment (10 reps, 0% resistance).
    3. Partner drilling (slow movement): Your partner attempts to resist slightly by bridging. You maintain North-South, focusing on hip pressure and shoulder control (10 reps, 25% resistance).
    4. Partner drilling (controlled transitions): Start from side control. You transition to North-South, and your partner attempts to escape. Focus on maintaining the position (10 reps, 50% resistance).
    5. Live rolling (limited goal): During rolling, consciously attempt to achieve North-South from side control. Your partner defends normally (5 attempts, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling (full integration): Actively seek to establish and maintain North-South position during regular sparring. Focus on control and preventing escapes (3 rounds, 100% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent turns into you during side control, creating space.
    • After successfully transitioning from a failed guard pass where you end up perpendicular to your opponent.
    • When you have established dominant side control and are looking to advance your position or set up submissions.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Sweep to Guard: If the opponent's hips are too far away, use your legs to create a frame and shrimp your hips, bringing your knees to your chest to re-establish guard.
    • Hip Escape to Side Control: If the opponent is pressuring heavily, use a hip escape to regain a standard side control position or transition to knee-on-belly.
    • Turtle: If the opponent is struggling to maintain control, they may attempt to "turtle" by dropping to their hands and knees. You can then look to secure a seatbelt grip and attack the back.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try to hold North-South position?

    Your neck is likely hurting because you are craning your head upwards to try and see your opponent's face, or you are allowing their head to lift your own. Instead, keep your head neutral and tucked slightly towards your shoulder, aiming to align your ear with their hip. Focus on driving your chest down onto their sternum and using your arms to control their shoulders and hips.

    Q: How can I maintain North-South position against a much bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage your weight distribution and hip pressure. Ensure your chest is glued to their sternum and your hips are driving forward and down, not just hovering. Use your arms to control their shoulders and hips tightly, preventing them from creating space to bridge or turn. Think of it as pinning them like a "crab," with your body weight distributed across their torso.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from side control to North-South?

    The optimal time to transition to North-South is when your opponent begins to turn into you from Side Control, or when you've achieved a dominant side control where their hips are slightly away from yours. This creates an opening where you can drive your hips forward and establish the perpendicular alignment, rather than forcing a transition when they are already settled.

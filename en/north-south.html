North-South is a dominant top position achieved when your head is near your opponent's head and your hips are near their hips, perpendicular to their body.

White belts often struggle by collapsing onto their opponent or losing control due to poor weight distribution and incorrect framing.

The key is maintaining a strong, active base with your hips low and your chest pressed into their sternum, creating a suffocating pressure.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From side control, transition your hips towards your opponent's head.
    2. Your chest should press firmly onto their sternum, head facing their head.
    3. Place your right hand (if opponent is facing left) on their left hip bone, fingers pointing towards their feet.
    4. Place your left hand on their left collarbone, creating a frame.
    5. Drive your hips downwards, aligning them perpendicular to your opponent's torso.
    6. Maintain a wide base with your knees slightly bent, distributing weight evenly over their hips and chest.
    7. Your head should be close to theirs, but not resting on their chin; aim for the side of their head near their ear.
    8. Actively drive your chest into their sternum, creating pressure and limiting their ability to bridge.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Collapsing: A white belt might sag their hips, bringing their chest down but losing hip pressure. This allows the opponent to create space and potentially escape. The fix is to keep hips higher than the chest, driving forward.
    • Head on Chin: Placing the head directly on the opponent's chin can lead to neck crank pain for the opponent and a weak control point for you. The fix is to aim for the side of the head, maintaining a strong upper body connection.
    • Narrow Base: A white belt might keep their knees too close together, making them unstable and easily swept. The fix is to widen the base, creating a stable platform by keeping knees slightly wider than shoulder-width.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Movement (0%): Practice transitioning from side control to North-South, focusing on hip and chest pressure. 10 reps each side.
    2. Partner Static Hold (25%): Your partner lies flat. You achieve North-South and hold for 15 seconds, focusing on perfect weight distribution. 5 reps each side.
    3. Controlled Transition (50%): You transition to North-South. Your partner gently resists by trying to bridge or shrimp. You maintain position. 5 reps each side.
    4. Light Pressure Drill (75%): You apply North-South pressure. Your partner tries to escape using basic bridging or shrimping. You focus on maintaining control. 3 minutes.
    5. Limited Escapes (90%): You hold North-South. Your partner attempts any escape from the position. You react to maintain. 3 minutes.
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Practice transitioning to and maintaining North-South during live rolling scenarios.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • After successfully passing the guard and ending up in a perpendicular alignment.
    • When your opponent is turtled and you can secure a strong cross-face.
    • When you successfully transition from a failed submission attempt on the ground.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Bridging: The opponent bridges their hips upwards and towards your head. You counter by driving your hips down and slightly turning into their bridge, maintaining chest-to-chest pressure.
    • Shrimping: The opponent shrimping away creates space. You counter by driving your hips forward, closing the gap, and maintaining a tight cross-face.
    • Head Escape: The opponent tries to turn their head away. You counter by maintaining your cross-face grip and driving your shoulder into their head, preventing them from creating space.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I hold North-South?

    This often happens when you're pressing too much weight onto your shoulder joint rather than distributing it through your chest and hips. Ensure your chest is firmly on their sternum and your hips are driving downwards, creating a stable base. Avoid letting your head bear the brunt of the pressure; use it for alignment, not weight.

    Q: How can I maintain North-South against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise angles are crucial. Focus on keeping your hips lower than theirs, creating a strong frame with your arms and chest. Drive your weight into their sternum and hips, making it difficult for them to generate upward or sideways movement. Use your head to control their head's direction.

    Q: When is the best time to attack a submission from North-South?

    The primary submission from North-South is the North-South Choke. This is best attempted when your opponent is flat on their back, their head is somewhat trapped, and you have established a strong chest-to-chest connection. Ensure you have a good cross-face grip and can secure their far-side collar before initiating the choke to avoid giving up control.

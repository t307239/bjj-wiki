North-South Position: Your Essential BJJ Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

North-South is a dominant positional control achieved when you are perpendicular to your opponent's head, facing their feet, with your chest on their chest.

It offers superior leverage for submissions and transitions, but white belts often struggle due to poor weight distribution.

Key insight: Think of creating a "crushing vise" with your hips and shoulders, pinning their upper body to the mat.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From side-control, transition by walking your hips towards your opponent's head.
    2. Place your chest directly over their chest, perpendicular to their body's long axis.
    3. Your lead shoulder should be near their far armpit, and your trail shoulder near their chin.
    4. Pin their head towards the mat with your chin or the side of your head, avoiding pressure on the throat.
    5. Hook your opponent's near arm with your far arm (e.g., left arm hooks their right arm).
    6. Squeeze your knees together and drive your hips down, creating a "U" shape with your body.
    7. Maintain head control by keeping your head tight to their neck, preventing them from turning in.
    8. Your base should be wide, with your feet planted firmly on the mat, supporting your weight.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly placing your head: If your head is too high, you allow your opponent to create space and bridge. This can strain your cervical spine (neck) and shoulders. The correct position is your chin or side of your head pressed into their neck/upper chest.
    • Allowing opponent to turn in: If you don't control their head and near arm, they can roll towards you. This can cause your knee to hit their face, potentially injuring your kneecap, or lead to a shoulder dislocation for you if they trap your arm. Always secure the head and near arm.
    • Insufficient hip pressure: If your hips are too high, you lose control and your opponent can escape. This can lead to your back being exposed, risking a takedown or submission attempt against you. Drive your hips down and create a tight frame.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo positional awareness drill: Practice moving from side-control to North-South without a partner, focusing on hip and shoulder placement. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    2. Static North-South hold: Assume North-South position on a partner lying flat, holding for 30 seconds, then switching. Focus on maintaining chest-to-chest pressure. (5 reps per side, 10% resistance)
    3. Controlled North-South escape drill: Partner attempts to escape from North-South while you maintain the position, focusing on hip and head control. (5 minutes, 25% resistance)
    4. North-South to armbar transition drill: From North-South, practice transitioning to an armbar attempt. Focus on maintaining control during the transition. (5 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. North-South to kimura transition drill: From North-South, practice transitioning to a kimura attempt. Focus on maintaining control during the transition. (5 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    6. Light sparring: Practice maintaining North-South position during a live roll for 1 minute, then switch. Focus on control and transitions. (3 rounds, 75% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have successfully passed your opponent's guard and are in a dominant side-control position.
    • After successfully defending a submission from side-control and ending up in a perpendicular alignment.
    • When your opponent attempts a guard pass and you are able to reverse the situation.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Elbow Escape: If your opponent is in North-South, drive your elbow closest to their head into the mat. Shrimp your hips away, creating space to bring your knee between you and them, then recover guard. This requires precise timing and core strength.
    • Bridging and Turning: From North-South, if the pressure is not too heavy, try a powerful bridge towards your opponent's head, then immediately turn into them, attempting to reclaim guard or achieve a better position. This is risky and requires good body mechanics.
    • Hip Escape and Re-Guard: If you feel the pressure is manageable, focus on a strong hip escape to create an inch of space, then immediately bring your knee up to their hip, and use your foot to push their chest away, allowing you to return to your guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I'm in North-South?

    Your neck likely hurts because you are incorrectly posturing your head too high, allowing your opponent to "stack" on you or apply pressure directly to your head. The correct technique involves tucking your chin slightly and pressing the side of your head or your chin firmly into the upper chest/neck area of your opponent, distributing your weight through your body, not just your neck.

    Q: How can I get to North-South against a much bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on leverage over strength. Instead of just driving forward, use your hips to "walk" around their head. Once perpendicular, drive your hips down HARD, creating a "U" shape with your body. Control their near arm with your far arm, and use your head to pin their chin to their chest to prevent them from turning.

    Q: The optimal time to transition from side-control to North-South is when you feel your opponent is "flat" on their back and not actively bridging or turning into you. Look for an opening where their hips are relatively stationary. A common trigger is when they are trying to shrimp away from your side-control but haven't created enough space.

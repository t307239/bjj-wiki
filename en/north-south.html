North-South Position: Your Essential BJJ Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The North-South position is a dominant positional control achieved when you are perpendicular to your opponent's head, facing their feet, with your chest on their upper chest.

White belts often struggle by not establishing proper weight distribution and allowing their opponent to bridge or shrimp effectively.

The key is maintaining a heavy, aligned base that prevents your opponent from creating space or escaping by using their hips.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From side control, transition your hips to be perpendicular to your opponent's head, facing their feet.
    2. Place your chest onto their upper chest, aiming to align your sternum with their sternum.
    3. Establish a cross-collar grip with your right hand on their left collar, and your left hand on their right collar.
    4. Drive your hips down and slightly forward, creating pressure into their chest.
    5. Keep your knees wide and tucked towards their armpits, preventing them from bridging.
    6. Ensure your base is wide and stable, with your weight distributed evenly across your hips and knees.
    7. Maintain head control by posting your forehead on their head or shoulder, preventing them from turning their head.
    8. Your arms should form a frame, preventing them from creating space with their arms.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly placing your chest too high on their neck: This can lead to hyperextension or strain of your cervical spine and their cervical spine, especially if they bridge forcefully. The correct placement is on their upper chest, aligning your sternum.
    • Allowing knees to drift wide and flat: This creates a large surface area for your opponent to bridge into and escape, potentially causing knee ligament strain (ACL/MCL) from twisting forces. Keep knees tucked and angled towards their armpits.
    • Overextending your arms to grip: This leaves your elbows vulnerable to attacks like armbars if your opponent creates space. Maintain a strong, controlled frame with your elbows slightly bent and under your shoulders.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo positioning drill: Practice transitioning from side control to North-South, focusing on hip and chest alignment. (10 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Static North-South control: Have a partner lie flat, and practice establishing the correct grips and weight distribution without movement. (10 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Light pressure drill: With a compliant partner, practice holding North-South and applying gentle chest pressure, focusing on maintaining base. (5 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled transition drill: Practice transitioning to North-South and holding for 5 seconds, then releasing and returning to a neutral position. (10 reps, 50% resistance)
    5. Positional sparring (North-South focus): Start from North-South and have the opponent attempt to escape, while you focus on maintaining control. (5 rounds x 1 minute, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling with positional emphasis: Try to achieve and maintain North-South position during rolling, focusing on correct mechanics. (2 rounds x 3 minutes, 90% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have successfully passed your opponent's guard and established side control, but their hips are turned away from you.
    • After a failed submission attempt from side control, where you end up perpendicular to their head.
    • When your opponent attempts to shrimp but you can maintain chest-to-chest pressure.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): If the opponent is too far away, creating space with your hips and bridging slightly to create an angle to shrimp out.
    • Framing and Bridging: If the opponent has too much chest pressure, use your forearms to frame against their chest and bridge powerfully, creating space to recover guard.
    • Re-guarding: If the opponent's weight is too far forward, use your legs to hook their hips and attempt to bring your guard back into play.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try to hold North-South?

    Your neck is likely hurting because you are using it to support your weight or to try and control your opponent's head too aggressively. Instead of placing pressure on your neck, ensure your weight is distributed through your hips and chest onto their upper chest. Your head should be used for light steering or balance, not for bearing significant force. Focus on aligning your sternum with theirs and driving your hips down.

    Q: How can I maintain North-South against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise weight distribution become critical. Focus on getting your chest *lower* on their chest, almost pinning their sternum. Keep your hips heavy and drive them forward. Crucially, ensure your knees are tucked tightly towards their armpits to prevent them from creating space with their hips. Use your collar grips to maintain control and prevent them from turning into you.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to North-South from side control?

    The optimal time to transition to North-South from Side Control is when your opponent's hips are turned away from you, creating a natural angle. If you are directly on their side, attempting a North-South transition can leave you exposed. Look for the moment after they've attempted to shrimp or turn, and you can quickly adjust your hips to be perpendicular to their head, driving your chest into their upper chest.

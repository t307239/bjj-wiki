North-South Position: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

North-South is a dominant grappling position where your body is perpendicular to your opponent's, head-to-head.

It offers superior control and creates opportunities for submissions and transitions.

White belts often struggle by not understanding weight distribution and creating space for the opponent.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From side-control, establish a cross-face with your lead arm, pressing your bicep into the opponent's jawline.
    2. Secure a collar grip with your trail hand, gripping the opponent's gi lapel near their sternum.
    3. Shift your hips forward, driving your chest into the opponent's upper chest, aligning your sternum with their chin.
    4. Place your lead knee on the mat beside their head, creating a base.
    5. Your trail leg extends back, with your foot planted firmly on the mat, creating a stable base.
    6. Your hips should be low, with your pelvis angled slightly forward, pressing your weight down.
    7. Maintain head pressure by tucking your chin and driving your forehead into their jaw area, creating a 'headlock' effect.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect hip angle: Placing hips too high or too far back. This allows the opponent to bridge and escape by creating space under your hips, leading to knee or hip ligament strain when you're unable to support their weight.
    • Lack of head pressure: Letting your head lift or not driving your forehead into their jaw. This allows the opponent to turn their head and create space to shrimp, potentially leading to shoulder dislocation if your arm gets caught.
    • No leg extension: Keeping your trail leg bent or too close. This creates an unstable base and makes you susceptible to sweeps, potentially causing ankle sprains or knee ligament tears if you fall off balance.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip shift and weight transfer from side-control to North-South without a partner. (10 reps each side)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Partner lies passively. Focus on achieving the exact grips and weight distribution. (10 reps each side)
    3. Light resistance drilling: Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the control. Focus on maintaining head pressure. (5 reps each side, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled drilling: Partner attempts to escape but is limited. Work on securing the position and preventing their immediate escapes. (5 reps each side, 50% resistance)
    5. Positional sparring: Focus solely on achieving and maintaining North-South position. Partner actively tries to escape. (3 rounds, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Integrate North-South into regular sparring, focusing on maintaining control and transitioning to submissions. (2 rounds, 90-100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have successfully passed the opponent's guard and are in a deep side-control.
    • When the opponent attempts a guard pass and you can reverse the situation.
    • When the opponent is turtled and you can establish head-to-head control.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Opponent bridges and turns: If the opponent bridges and turns their hips towards you, immediately drive your hips forward and continue your weight transfer, aiming to end up in side-control or mount.
    • Opponent creates space and shrimp: If the opponent creates space by shrimping, establish a strong cross-face and use your lead knee to block their hip, preventing further escape.
    • Opponent attacks your head: If the opponent attempts to grab your head, maintain your grip on their collar and use your shoulder to create frames, preventing them from getting a good grip on your neck.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to get to North-South?

    This often happens when you don't establish proper head pressure and cross-face. If your head is not driving into their jaw, they can turn their head and create space. When you then try to shift your weight, your shoulder can get caught and leverage your arm incorrectly, leading to strain or dislocation.

    Q: How can I maintain North-South against a bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on precise weight distribution and hip angle. Drive your sternum into their upper chest and keep your hips low, almost scraping the mat. Your collar grip should be tight to prevent them from creating space, and your head pressure needs to be constant to limit their head movement.

    Q: Is North-South a good position to start submissions from?

    Yes, North-South is excellent for submissions. The primary submission is the North-South choke (also known as the Kani Basami choke), but you can also transition to Armbars or kimuras if the opponent defends aggressively. The key is to maintain control and not rush the submission.

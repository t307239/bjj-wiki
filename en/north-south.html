North-South is a dominant control position where you face your opponent's head while they face your feet. It offers excellent leverage for submissions and transitions.

White belts often struggle by collapsing their base, allowing the opponent to bridge or escape easily. They also tend to frame poorly, leaving openings.

The key is maintaining a tight, compressed base with your chest on their chest, preventing them from creating space to move.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From side control, transition your hips towards your opponent's head, aligning your chest perpendicular to theirs.
    2. Place your chest firmly on their upper chest or sternum, driving your weight down.
    3. Extend your legs back, creating a wide, stable base, with your feet hip-width apart.
    4. Grip their far-side collar with your near-side hand and their near-side sleeve with your far-side hand, pulling them towards you.
    5. Alternatively, grip both of their sleeves with your hands, keeping elbows tight.
    6. Drive your hips forward, compressing their chest and restricting their breathing.
    7. Ensure your head is not directly over their head, but slightly to the side, preventing them from head-butting or framing effectively.
    8. Maintain constant pressure by shifting your weight from your hips to your chest.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Releasing your chest pressure and allowing your hips to sag, creating a gap between your torso and theirs. This can lead to the opponent bridging by pushing off your hips, potentially damaging your lumbar spine due to hyperextension.
    • Sprawling your legs too narrowly, creating a weak base. This makes you susceptible to the opponent rolling you over, which can cause knee or ankle ligament sprains if your legs get tangled.
    • Allowing your head to be in a vulnerable position, directly beside their head. This exposes your neck to a potential guillotine choke attempt, and if they drive forward, can cause cervical strain.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice entering North-South from a simulated side control. Focus on hip and chest placement. (10 reps per side)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Enter North-South and hold for 10 seconds. Focus on maintaining pressure. (5 reps per side)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Enter North-South and maintain the position for 30 seconds. Opponent attempts to create space. (5 reps per side)
    4. Transition drilling: Enter North-South, then transition to a submission attempt (e.g., North-South choke). (5 reps per side)
    5. Positional sparring (50% resistance): Start from neutral or side control, aim to achieve and maintain North-South for 1 minute. (5 rounds)
    6. Live rolling (75% resistance): Focus on achieving North-South position as a primary goal. (3 rounds)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have established a dominant side control and your opponent is trying to shrimp away.
    • When you have successfully transitioned from a guard pass and your opponent is flat on their back.
    • When your opponent attempts a weak guard recovery and you can secure a head-and-arm control.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Bridge and Roll: From North-South, the opponent bridges their hips towards your head and attempts to roll you over, creating space to recover guard. This requires a strong hip drive.
    • Shrimp and Recover Guard: The opponent shrimps their hips away from your chest pressure, creating enough space to bring their knee inside and re-establish guard.
    • Wall Walk: If the opponent has you in North-South and you are on your knees, you can attempt to walk your feet back towards your hands, creating a wall to push off and stand up.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my back hurt when I try to hold North-South position?

    Your back pain likely stems from hyperextending your lumbar spine by arching your back. Instead of arching, focus on driving your hips forward and compressing your opponent's chest with your sternum. This transfers the pressure to your core and legs, not your lower back.

    Q: How do I maintain North-South against a much bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, your primary goal is to prevent them from creating space. Focus on keeping your chest glued to their chest, and your hips driving forward. Use your legs to create a wide, stable base, and grip their sleeves to control their posture, rather than trying to physically overpower them.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from side control to North-South?

    The ideal time to transition is when your opponent is actively trying to shrimp away from your Side Control. As they create space, you can step your hips over their head, driving your chest down to maintain control. This timing prevents them from fully recovering their guard.

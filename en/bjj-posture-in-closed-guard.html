    More Questions

    Why is posture so important in BJJ?

    Good posture is fundamental because it allows you to control your opponent's base and prevent them from generating leverage for submissions. It's your first line of defense and offense, enabling you to maintain a strong position and attack effectively.

    How do I keep my posture up when someone is pulling my guard?

    To maintain posture against a guard pull, keep your hips low and your back straight, driving your weight forward. Keep your hands engaged on their hips or upper body to prevent them from closing the distance or creating angles.

    What are the key elements of good posture from the guard?

    From guard, good posture involves keeping your back straight, your knees tucked towards your chest, and your feet actively pushing against your opponent's hips. This creates a strong base and allows you to control their distance and balance.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck hurt so much when I try to maintain good posture to prevent submissions from the guard?

    Neck pain often arises from hyperextension or craning your neck upwards. To prevent this, focus on keeping your chin tucked slightly towards your sternum and engaging your upper back muscles to create a rigid spine, distributing the force across your shoulders and upper torso rather than isolating it in your cervical spine.

    Q: How can I improve my posture to prevent submissions like armbars when my opponent is much bigger than me?

    Against a larger opponent, prioritize creating a strong base by widening your stance and lowering your center of gravity. Engage your core by bracing your abdominal muscles and imagine 'stacking' your hips beneath you, making it difficult for them to generate leverage to extend your limbs.

    Q: What are the key body mechanics to use in my posture to stop my training partner from getting submissions when they are on top?

    To effectively prevent submissions from top position, maintain an upright torso with your chest proud and shoulders pulled back, creating a stable platform. Simultaneously, keep your hips close to your opponent's hips, preventing them from creating space to isolate limbs or attack your neck.

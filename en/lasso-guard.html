Lasso Guard: Your First Steps in BJJ

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Blue Belt Guide

Lasso Guard is an open guard position where one of your legs wraps around your opponent's arm, creating a strong control point.

White belts often struggle with Lasso Guard due to a lack of understanding of leverage and frame creation, leading to ineffective control.

The core concept is using your leg as a flexible vise, preventing your opponent from posturing up and creating opportunities for sweeps and attacks.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From closed guard, break your opponent's posture by pulling their collar with both hands, driving your hips forward slightly.
    2. As they attempt to stand or create space, insert your right leg behind their right arm, hooking their bicep with your shin.
    3. Simultaneously, grip their right sleeve with your left hand, specifically on the wrist or forearm area, creating a "lasso" effect.
    4. Your left leg remains on the outside of their body, acting as a frame to maintain distance and control their hip.
    5. Drive your hips into the mat and slightly to your left, creating a base and preventing them from easily passing.
    6. Use your left hand (on their sleeve) to pull their arm across your body, deepening the lasso and limiting their mobility.
    7. Your right hand can now grip their far collar or shoulder to further control their posture and set up sweeps.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to "pull hard" on the sleeve without proper hip engagement can strain your shoulder joint's rotator cuff ligaments, leading to impingement.
    • Flexing your knee joint excessively while your leg is wrapped around the opponent's arm, without maintaining shin-to-bicep contact, can lead to knee ligament (MCL/LCL) tears.
    • Allowing your opponent to stack your hips directly by not maintaining your left leg's frame creates a dangerous anterior pelvic tilt, risking lumbar spine compression.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo: Practice leg wrapping and sleeve gripping motion without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Solo: Practice hip movement and frame creation with the "lasso" leg in place. (50 reps)
    3. Partner Drill (0% resistance): Opponent stands passively. Execute the Lasso Guard setup and hold. (10 reps per side)
    4. Partner Drill (25% resistance): Opponent offers slight resistance to posture. Practice maintaining the Lasso and hip frame. (10 reps per side)
    5. Partner Drill (50% resistance): Opponent attempts to break the Lasso grip. Focus on grip strength and hip control. (10 reps per side)
    6. Live Rolling (75% resistance): Implement Lasso Guard in rolling, focusing on maintaining the position and transitioning. (5 minutes)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is posturing up from your closed guard, attempting to create space or pass.
    • When your opponent is trying to "grip fight" from the outside of your guard, giving you an opportunity to isolate an arm.
    • When you have established a solid closed guard and are looking to transition to a more dynamic open guard control.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Elbow-to-knee Stretch: From Lasso, extend your right leg and drive your right knee towards your opponent's chest while pulling their sleeve across your body. This breaks their posture and sets up sweeps.
    • Hip Bump Sweep: While maintaining the Lasso, shrimp your hips to the left and bridge your hips upwards, driving your left knee towards their hip to create an angle for a sweep.
    • Toreando Pass Defense: If they attempt a Toreando pass, use your Lasso leg to hook their hip and your free leg to push their opposite hip away, maintaining your guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to grip their sleeve for Lasso Guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs because you're gripping too tightly with the palm and fingers, putting excessive strain on the carpal bones and ligaments. Instead, focus on a "loose fist" grip with your thumb wrapped around the sleeve, and use your forearm and bicep to create the pulling tension. This distributes the force more evenly and protects your wrist joint.

    Q: How can I use Lasso Guard effectively against a much bigger and stronger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise hip movement are paraMount. Instead of relying on brute strength to maintain the lasso, focus on using your hips to create angles and drive them off-balance. Keep your lasso leg tight on their bicep and use your free leg to control their hip, preventing them from "crushing" you. Your goal is to disrupt their base, not to overpower them.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from Lasso Guard to an attack like a triangle choke?

    The ideal time to transition to a Triangle Choke from Lasso Guard is when your opponent is trying to "bail" out of the lasso by pulling their arm away, or when they are attempting to posture up aggressively. This movement often creates an opening where their arm is extended and their head is within striking distance, making the triangle choke setup more efficient and less prone to being defended.

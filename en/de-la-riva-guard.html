De La Riva Guard: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Blue Belt Guide

The De La Riva Guard is an open guard position initiated from the guard player's back on the mat. It aims to disrupt the opponent's base and create angles for sweeps and submissions.

White belts often struggle with a lack of understanding of weight distribution and frame creation, leading to easily passed guards.

The core principle is using the 'hook' leg to control distance and create off-balancing opportunities by manipulating the opponent's base.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip: Opponent's right leg. Grab their right shin/ankle with your left hand. Your left foot hooks behind their right knee, ankle pointing upwards.
    2. Grip: Opponent's left collar with your right hand. Your right foot is flat on the mat, hip slightly elevated.
    3. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your right hip, creating a slight angle away from your opponent.
    4. Frame: Use your left elbow to create a frame against their right thigh, preventing them from easily stepping forward.
    5. Hook Engagement: Apply a gentle outward pressure with your left foot behind their knee, while simultaneously pulling their collar with your right hand.
    6. Hip Movement: Drive your hips forward slightly, creating a lever action with your hooked leg.
    7. Angle Creation: As they try to stabilize, use your left foot to push their right knee away, creating a shooting angle for your hips.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Hook Placement: Placing your left foot too high on the thigh or too low on the calf can lead to knee hyperextension or ankle sprains for you, and allows the opponent to easily step out.
    • Over-Reliance on Pulling: Yanking the collar without hip engagement results in a loss of posture and allows the opponent to stack you, risking spinal compression.
    • Lack of Hip Elevation: Keeping your hips flat on the mat prevents you from creating the necessary leverage and angle, making the guard easy to pass.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Grip and Hook Practice: Practice establishing the collar grip and the De La Riva hook on a stationary object (e.g., a dummy or a rolled-up gi). (20 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    2. Partnered Grip and Hook Setup: With a compliant partner lying on their back, practice establishing the collar grip and the De La Riva hook. Partner remains still. (20 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    3. Basic De La Riva Frame Drill: Partner attempts to step forward. You practice maintaining the collar grip, De La Riva hook, and elbow frame. Focus on hip movement. (10 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. De La Riva Sweep Entry Drill: Practice entering the De La Riva guard and initiating a basic sweep (e.g., a pendulum sweep). Partner offers minimal resistance. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. De La Riva Guard Retention: Partner attempts to pass. You focus on maintaining the De La Riva hook and creating frames to prevent the pass. (10 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    6. Controlled Rolling: Engage in light rolling with a partner experienced in De La Riva, focusing on establishing and maintaining the guard. (5 minutes, 90% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up and trying to apply pressure from your guard.
    • When your opponent is standing and trying to establish a heavy base.
    • When you have successfully transitioned from another guard (e.g., butterfly) and need to create distance.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee Slice Pass Defense: If the opponent drives their knee across your body, immediately bring your right leg (the non-hooked leg) over their hip and establish a closed guard or half guard to prevent the pass.
    • Stack Pass Defense: If the opponent attempts to stack you, widen your base by pushing their hips away with your free leg and simultaneously pull on their collar to disrupt their posture.
    • Leg Drag Defense: If the opponent tries to drag your hooked leg across, use your free leg to push their hip away and re-establish your guard or transition to a different open guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to hook their leg for De La Riva Guard?

    This often happens when the hook is placed too high on the thigh, causing excessive pressure on your knee joint. Ensure your foot is placed behind their knee, with your ankle pointing upwards, creating a 'hook' rather than a 'push' against the knee itself. This biomechanical alignment protects your ligaments.

    Q: How do I use De La Riva Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on creating angles and using their weight against them. Your collar grip is crucial to break their posture and prevent them from simply powering through. Use your hooked leg to subtly shift their weight, making them unstable, and look for sweep opportunities that exploit their forward momentum.

    Q: When should I transition out of De La Riva Guard instead of trying to sweep?

    If your opponent successfully stabilizes their base and prevents you from creating angles or off-balancing them, it's time to transition. Look for opportunities to switch to a Closed Guard, half guard, or even a single leg takedown if they overcommit to a pass. Don't force a sweep that isn't there biomechanically.

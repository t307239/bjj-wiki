De La Riva Guard: A Biomechanical Guide for BJJ White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Blue Belt Guide

The De La Riva Guard is an open guard position used to control distance and off-balance an opponent, typically initiated from a seated or supine position.

White belts often struggle by treating it like a static grip fight, neglecting hip movement and leg pressure, leading to easy passes.

The core principle is using your outside leg hook to disrupt your opponent's base and create angles for sweeps and transitions.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a standard open guard stance, opponent facing you. Your hips are slightly elevated off the mat.
    2. With your right leg, hook the outside of your opponent's left thigh, just above the knee. Your foot should be flexed, toes pointing up.
    3. Simultaneously, establish a collar grip with your right hand on their gi collar, and a sleeve grip with your left hand on their left gi sleeve.
    4. Shift your hips to your left, creating a 45-degree angle relative to your opponent. Your base is wide, knees bent.
    5. Extend your left leg, placing your foot on your opponent's hip or thigh to create a frame and prevent them from closing the distance.
    6. Apply gentle pressure with your right leg hook, pulling their left leg slightly towards you while simultaneously pushing their upper body away with your left leg frame.
    7. Use your collar and sleeve grips to pull their upper body down and to your left, initiating a weight transfer that compromises their balance.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly hooking the opponent's leg too high (above the knee joint) can strain the knee ligament when they step or drive forward, leading to ACL/MCL tears.
    • Failing to maintain a frame with the non-hooking leg allows the opponent to drive forward and crush your guard, potentially causing lumbar spine compression.
    • Gripping too tightly on the opponent's leg without hip movement can lead to wrist and elbow hyperextension if they attempt to break your grip forcefully.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo: Practice establishing the De La Riva hook and frame with no opponent (10 reps per side).
    2. Solo: Practice the hip shift and angle creation without grips (10 reps per side).
    3. Partner Drill (0% resistance): Opponent stands, you establish the guard. Practice grip fighting and initial hook placement (5 reps per side).
    4. Partner Drill (25% resistance): Opponent attempts to step forward. You practice maintaining the hook and frame, resetting if lost (5 reps per side).
    5. Partner Drill (50% resistance): Opponent attempts a basic pass (e.g., torreando). You focus on maintaining distance and control with the hook and frame (5 reps per side).
    6. Live Rolling (75% resistance): Focus solely on establishing and maintaining the De La Riva guard, attempting one sweep if the opportunity arises (3 minutes).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is standing and has a knee-on-the-ground or is in a deep stance.
    • When you have successfully disengaged from a closed guard or half guard.
    • When your opponent is attempting to pressure pass and you can isolate one of their legs.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Standing Base: If the opponent tries to step forward, widen your base and use your non-hooking leg frame to push their weight back, maintaining your hip angle.
    • Guard Retention: If they try to pull their leg out, immediately switch your hooking leg or transition to a different open guard like Spider Guard by extending your hooking leg and placing your foot on their hip.
    • Pass Prevention: If they try to step around, use your collar and sleeve grips to pull their upper body towards you, forcing them to maintain their balance and preventing them from achieving a strong passing posture.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try to use the De La Riva hook on my opponent's leg?

    This often occurs when your foot is not properly flexed, causing your ankle to be in a vulnerable, extended position. Ensure your toes are pointed up towards the ceiling and your foot is actively 'hooking' the outside of their thigh. This engages your calf and shin muscles more effectively, creating a stronger, more stable hook and protecting your ankle joint from hyperextension.

    Q: How can I effectively use the De La Riva Guard against a significantly bigger and heavier opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus intensely on maintaining distance and disrupting their base. Use your non-hooking leg (the one on their hip) to create a strong frame and consistently push their weight away. Your De La Riva hook should be actively used to pull their leg towards you, forcing them to constantly adjust their balance and preventing them from using their weight effectively to crush you.

    Q: Is it okay to let my opponent's leg 'slide' up my shin in De La Riva Guard?

    No, allowing their leg to slide up your shin is a critical mistake that often leads to a Guard Pass. The ideal De La Riva hook is placed on the outside of their thigh, just above the knee. If their leg slides up, it compromises your control and allows them to more easily step around or pass your guard. Immediately reset your hook to the thigh if this happens.

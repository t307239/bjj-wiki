De La Riva Guard: The Ultimate BJJ Beginner's Guide

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Blue Belt Guide

The De La Riva Guard is an open guard position designed to control distance and create sweep or submission opportunities by hooking an opponent's leg.

White belts often struggle by collapsing their structure, allowing the opponent to easily pass or stack them.

The key is maintaining a strong frame with your arms and using your hooked leg to actively disrupt their base, not just passively hook.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a seated or kneeling position, establish a standard collar grip (one hand on the opponent's gi collar, opposite hand on the same side sleeve).
    2. Extend your right leg (if right-handed) and place your right foot on the opponent's left thigh, approximately 4-6 inches above the knee.
    3. Simultaneously, wrap your right arm around the opponent's left leg, securing a grip on their left ankle or the cuff of their gi pants.
    4. Drive your left knee towards the opponent's hip, creating a frame and preventing them from collapsing onto you.
    5. Keep your left foot planted firmly on the mat, providing a base and allowing for hip movement.
    6. Maintain tension in your hooked leg's ankle, keeping it flexed to prevent the opponent from easily pulling their leg out.
    7. Your hips should be angled slightly away from the opponent, creating space for your leg frames and disrupting their posture.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Hooking the opponent's leg with a limp ankle: This allows the opponent to easily step over and pass the guard, potentially leading to a knee hyperextension injury if they apply weight incorrectly.
    • Collapsing your frame by letting your elbow touch your knee: This removes your structural integrity, allowing the opponent to stack you, risking spinal compression or neck injury.
    • Extending your arms straight without a frame: This makes your arms vulnerable to being controlled and can lead to wrist or elbow hyperextension if the opponent applies pressure.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice transitioning into the De La Riva hook and maintaining the frame without a partner (5 minutes).
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Partner stands, you establish De La Riva. Focus on grip and frame (10 reps per side).
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner attempts to step through. You focus on maintaining the hook and frame (15 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Partner drill (controlled movement): Partner attempts to walk forward. You practice sweeping or transitioning (10 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Live rolling (positional sparring): Start from De La Riva guard. Focus on maintaining the guard and executing one sweep or submission (3 rounds, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling (full sparring): Integrate De La Riva guard into your rolling. Focus on situational awareness and adaptation (5 minutes, 90% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured up and attempting to pass your closed guard.
    • When the opponent is standing and has not yet established a strong base over your legs.
    • When you are able to secure a deep collar and sleeve grip on the opponent.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Drag Pass: Opponent steps their inside leg over your hooked leg, then drives their hips forward to control your leg and pass.
    • Toreando Pass: Opponent circles their hips around to your side, attempting to step past your hooked leg.
    • Stack Pass: Opponent drives their weight directly down onto your hooked leg and hips, trying to flatten you out.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I do De La Riva Guard?

    Your ankle might hurt if you are allowing your hooked foot to become limp or dorsiflexed (pulled up towards your shin) when the opponent applies pressure. To prevent this, actively flex your foot, keeping your toes pointed away from you, and maintain consistent tension in your calf muscle. This creates a more rigid structure that distributes pressure better.

    Q: How do I De La Riva Guard against a bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, your frame becomes even more critical. Ensure your non-hooked leg's knee is driving hard into their hip to maintain distance and prevent them from easily collapsing on you. Use your collar and sleeve grips to control their posture and prevent them from generating forward pressure, making it harder for them to use their weight advantage.

    Q: You should consider switching your De La Riva hook when the opponent demonstrates a clear intention to advance their pass on the side of your current hook. For example, if they are trying to step their outside leg around your hooked leg, you might switch your hook to their other leg to create a new barrier or transition to a different guard.

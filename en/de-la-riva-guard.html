De La Riva Guard is an open guard position initiated from the guard player's back, aiming to control distance and off-balance the opponent for sweeps or submissions.

White belts often struggle by overextending limbs or neglecting hip pressure, leading to easily defended positions or injury.

The core principle is using the DLR hook to create a lever, constantly adjusting hip angle and maintaining frame to disrupt the opponent's base.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Opponent stands or kneels facing you. You are on your back, knees bent.
    2. Secure a standard Collar Grip with your right hand on their left collar, palm facing up.
    3. With your left foot, hook behind their right shin, just above the ankle bone, with the sole of your foot perpendicular to their leg.
    4. Place your right foot on their left hip, knee bent at 90 degrees, creating a frame.
    5. Arch your lower back slightly, pushing your hips forward to create distance and leverage.
    6. Maintain a wide base with your left foot on the mat, preventing them from easily passing.
    7. Begin to actively pull their collar down and forward while pushing their hip away with your right foot.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Hooking the opponent's foot with your foot instead of their shin: This places excessive strain on your ankle ligaments (e.g., anterior talofibular ligament) by forcing unnatural dorsiflexion, potentially causing sprains. Maintain the hook on the shin, just above the ankle bone.
    • Extending your DLR leg straight: This removes your body's ability to generate power and leaves your knee vulnerable to hyperextension or impact, risking ACL/MCL tears. Keep a slight bend in your DLR knee.
    • Over-relying on pulling the collar: This neglects the crucial hip push and frame, allowing the opponent to close distance easily and potentially leading to a stack pass. Always use your right foot to push the hip and maintain frame.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice establishing the DLR hook and hip frame, focusing on hip movement and posture for 2 minutes (0% resistance).
    2. Partner drill: Opponent stands passively. Practice securing the DLR hook and collar grip, then executing the hip push and pull for 3 reps per side (0% resistance).
    3. Partner drill: Opponent offers light resistance to the hook. Practice maintaining the hook and frame while they lightly try to pull their leg free for 5 reps per side (25% resistance).
    4. Partner drill: Opponent attempts a simple guard pass (e.g., knee slice). Practice the DLR hook and hip frame to prevent them from establishing control for 5 reps per side (50% resistance).
    5. Partner drill: Opponent actively attempts to pass your guard. Focus on maintaining the DLR hook and frame to create space and prevent the pass for 3 minutes (75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Apply the DLR Guard against a resisting opponent, focusing on maintaining the hook and frame for 5 minutes (100% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent attempts to establish a knee-on-belly or side control position.
    • When the opponent is standing or in a low, stable base, allowing for a good DLR hook.
    • When you are on your back and the opponent is posturing up, creating space to insert the hook.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: Opponent drives forward, putting weight on your hips and shoulders. Counter by driving your hips up and away, re-establishing your frame or switching to a different guard.
    • Leg Cut Pass: Opponent steps their hips over your DLR leg to cut it off. Counter by bringing your free leg across to create a spider guard hook or by transitioning to a single leg X-guard.
    • Grip Break: Opponent breaks your collar grip. Counter by immediately establishing an underhook on their arm or by transitioning to a different open guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I do De La Riva Guard?

    Your ankle pain likely stems from overextending your DLR leg or hooking too high on the opponent's foot. When the leg is straight, your ankle ligaments are strained. The hook should be on the shin, just above the ankle bone, with a slight bend in the knee. This protects your ankle and allows for better leverage.

    Q: How do I use De La Riva Guard against a bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on using your hip and frame to create space and disrupt their balance rather than relying solely on pulling. Your right foot on their hip is crucial for pushing and maintaining distance. Continuously adjust your hip angle to prevent them from collapsing on you and use the DLR hook to off-balance them.

    Q: You should consider switching guards if the opponent consistently passes your DLR hook by stepping their hips over your leg, or if they are effectively stacking you without giving you any sweep opportunities. Transitioning to a single leg X-guard or a butterfly guard can be effective responses.

De La Riva Guard: A White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Blue Belt Guide

The De La Riva Guard is an open guard position where one leg hooks the opponent's leg, creating distance and control for sweeps and submissions.

White belts often struggle by over-relying on brute strength or creating weak frames, leading to immediate Guard Passes.

The core principle is using your hooked leg to control their base and your free leg to manage distance and create off-balancing angles.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a seated or kneeling position, establish a sleeve grip on the opponent's same-side arm (e.g., right sleeve grip for right De La Riva).
    2. Simultaneously, establish a pant grip on the opponent's same-side pant leg, just above the knee.
    3. Swing your hooking leg (e.g., right leg) to hook behind the opponent's right leg, just below the knee, with your shin perpendicular to their thigh.
    4. Place your free foot (e.g., left foot) on the opponent's hip, creating a frame and maintaining space.
    5. Shift your hips to your left, creating an angle. Your base should be wide enough for stability.
    6. Use your sleeve grip to pull their arm across your body and your hooking leg to elevate their heel slightly, disrupting their base.
    7. Maintain tension on your hooking leg and a solid frame with your free foot to prevent them from closing the distance.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Hooking Leg Placement: Placing the hook too high on the thigh or too low on the calf can lead to knee hyperextension (valgus stress) if the opponent drives forward, damaging the MCL.
    • Correction: Hook precisely below the knee, keeping your shin parallel to their thigh, and maintain a slight bend in your knee.
    • Over-Extension of the Free Leg: Pushing too hard with the free foot on the hip without a stable base can cause your hip to rotate outward, exposing your knee to a direct impact or twisting injury.
    • Correction: Use your free foot as a controlled frame, not a battering ram, and ensure your hips are angled away from their direct pressure.
    • Ignoring Sleeve Grip Control: Releasing the sleeve grip allows the opponent to post their arm and drive through, potentially causing your hooking leg to absorb the full force, risking ankle sprains or calf tears.
    • Correction: Maintain a strong, consistent sleeve grip and use it to pull their arm across your body, negating their posting ability.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Footwork and Hook Placement: Practice swinging your hooking leg and placing your free foot on an imaginary hip, focusing on hip angle and balance. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance).
    2. Partnered Static Hook: Partner stands, you establish De La Riva hook and frame. Hold for 10 seconds, focusing on grip and posture. (5 reps per side, 0% resistance).
    3. Controlled Distance Management: Partner attempts to step in. You use your free foot frame and hooking leg to maintain distance. Partner applies minimal forward pressure. (5 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Basic Sweep Attempt (Controlled): From static hook, attempt a simple sweep like a scissor sweep, with partner offering minimal resistance to the sweep motion. (5 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Introduction to Guard Passing Defense: Partner attempts a basic knee-slice pass. You focus on maintaining the De La Riva hook and using your free leg to block their knee. (5 reps per side, 75% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling - Guard Retention Focus: Roll with a willing partner, focusing solely on maintaining the De La Riva guard and preventing the pass. Submissions are secondary. (3 x 2-minute rounds, 90% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is posturing up and has their weight forward.
    • Opponent is attempting to pass your guard from a standing or kneeling position.
    • You are in open guard and want to initiate a sweep or submission sequence from a dynamic position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee-Slice Pass Defense: Opponent drives their knee across your body. Use your free foot to block their knee and your hooking leg to elevate their hip, creating space to shrimp out.
    • Torreando Pass Defense: Opponent attempts to circle their legs around yours. Maintain your sleeve grip and use your hooking leg to sweep their lead leg, forcing them to re-engage or lose balance.
    • Stack Pass Defense: Opponent drives their weight straight down. Use your free foot on their hip to create a powerful frame, and simultaneously pull their sleeve across your body to break their posture and create an angle to escape.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to use De La Riva Guard?

    Knee pain in De La Riva Guard often stems from excessive valgus stress, meaning your knee is bending inward. This happens when your hooking leg is placed too high on the thigh, or you're not maintaining a slight bend in your knee. Ensure your hook is just below the knee and keep your shin perpendicular to their thigh, actively preventing inward collapse.

    Q: How do I use De La Riva Guard against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise angles are paraMount. Focus on using your free foot on their hip to create a strong frame and control distance, preventing them from crushing you. Your hooking leg should be used to subtly elevate their heel and disrupt their base, rather than trying to 'lift' their entire leg, which is impossible against significant weight.

    Q: The optimal time to switch from De La Riva Guard is when your opponent successfully closes the distance and begins to apply significant forward pressure, making your sweeping angles ineffective. At this point, you can transition to a closed guard or a butterfly guard by bringing your hooking leg back and using your free foot to push their hip, creating space to regather your base.

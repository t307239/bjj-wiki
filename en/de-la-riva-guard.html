De La Riva Guard: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Blue Belt Guide

De La Riva Guard is an open guard position initiated from the guard player's back, aiming to control the opponent's lead leg and create off-balancing opportunities for sweeps and submissions.

White belts often struggle by overextending their limbs, creating weak frames, or not understanding the importance of hip connection and off-balancing.

The core principle is using your hooked leg to control their base, allowing your free leg and hips to generate the necessary leverage for movement and attack.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a seated guard position, extend your right leg forward.
    2. Grip the opponent's left ankle with your right hand, thumb facing inward, palm gripping the Achilles tendon area.
    3. Place your left foot on the opponent's left thigh, knee pointing slightly outward, creating a frame.
    4. Hook your left leg behind the opponent's right thigh, with your shin parallel to the mat and foot flexed.
    5. Maintain a slight forward lean with your torso, keeping your hips close to the opponent's hips.
    6. Use your right hand on their ankle to control their base and pull them slightly forward and off-center.
    7. Your left foot on their thigh acts as a pivot point, allowing you to shift your weight and angle your hips.
    8. Initiate sweeps by driving your left knee towards their hip while simultaneously pulling their ankle forward and to the side with your right hand.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Extending your right leg straight into the opponent's knee: This can lead to knee hyperextension injuries for both you and your opponent, especially if they are heavy or apply forward pressure. Always maintain a slight bend in your right knee.
    • Gripping too low on the opponent's leg (e.g., the foot): This offers less control over their base and can allow them to easily step out or pass. Grip higher on the shin/ankle area for better leverage.
    • Letting your hips detach from the opponent's hips: This creates space for them to pass your guard and reduces your ability to off-balance them. Keep your hips connected and use your frame to maintain this connection.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Drilling: Practice the leg placement, hook, and ankle grip with no opponent. Focus on the feeling of the hook and the ankle grip. (50 reps)
    2. Partnered Drilling (Static): Your partner stands still. You practice entering the De La Riva guard, securing grips, and holding the position. (20 reps)
    3. Partnered Drilling (Light Resistance - 25%): Partner gently resists as you enter the guard. Focus on maintaining grips and hook. (30 reps)
    4. Guard Retention Drill (50%): Partner attempts a basic pass (e.g., knee slice). You practice maintaining the De La Riva hook and frame to prevent the pass. (40 reps)
    5. Sweep Application Drill (75%): Partner cooperates lightly on a sweep attempt from De La Riva. Focus on the hip movement and leg drive. (20 reps per sweep)
    6. Live Rolling (90%): Implement De La Riva guard in rolling, focusing on establishing and maintaining the guard, and attempting sweeps. (5 minutes)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is attempting to pass your guard by stepping their lead leg forward.
    • When the opponent is posturing up with their weight primarily on their feet.
    • When you have established a strong base and can control their lead leg.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Passer Steps Forward: As they step, reach for their ankle with your opposite hand and establish the De La Riva hook with your leg. Your hips should be angled to prevent them from squaring up.
    • Opponent Stacks: If they try to stack you, extend your right leg further to create distance and use your left foot on their thigh to push them away while maintaining the hook.
    • Opponent Pulls Leg Out: If they pull their leg free, immediately transition to a different guard like Spider Guard or Butterfly Guard, or use your free leg to establish a strong frame on their hips.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try to hook my leg for De La Riva Guard?

    Your ankle may be hurting because you are trying to force the hook with your ankle or foot. The hook should be applied by flexing your foot and using the back of your calf and hamstring to wrap around their thigh. Ensure your shin is approximately parallel to the mat, not perpendicular, to avoid stressing your ankle joint.

    Q: How do I effectively use De La Riva Guard against a much larger and heavier opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on maintaining superior hip connection and off-balancing. Use your ankle grip to pull their weight forward and to the side, making them unstable. Your left foot on their thigh becomes crucial for creating leverage and pushing their weight away, preventing them from simply crushing you.

    Q: When should I transition from De La Riva Guard to another guard or attempt a sweep?

    You should look to transition or sweep when you feel the opponent's weight shift significantly forward or to one side due to your ankle pull. If they are too stable or are actively pushing into you without giving you an off-balancing opportunity, it's better to re-establish your frames and look for a different entry or switch to a more defensive guard.

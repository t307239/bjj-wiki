The De La Riva Guard is an open-guard position where one leg hooks an opponent's leg, creating distance and control. It allows for sweeps and submissions by disrupting the opponent's base and balance.

White belts often struggle with the De La Riva Guard due to a lack of understanding of hip mechanics and framing. They tend to rely on brute strength rather than leverage.

The core principle is maintaining your hip-to-hip connection while using your hooked leg as a lever to off-balance your opponent and control distance.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a seated open guard, place your left foot on your opponent's right hip, maintaining a knee-on-belly angle with your hips.
    2. Hook your right foot around the outside of your opponent's right shin, with your heel closer to their knee than their ankle.
    3. Grip your opponent's right pants cuff with your left hand, pulling it towards your left hip to create tension.
    4. Grip your opponent's left collar with your right hand, pulling it down and slightly to your right to break their posture.
    5. Keep your left leg extended and slightly bent, creating a frame between your hips and your opponent's right leg.
    6. Shift your weight onto your left hip, creating a base that is perpendicular to your opponent's center of gravity.
    7. Use your hooked right foot to gently pull their right leg forward and to the side, while simultaneously pushing their upper body away with your left leg and pulling their collar.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly extending your hooked leg: This can lead to your knee or ankle ligaments being hyperextended if your opponent steps or pivots, causing a sprain or tear. Always maintain a slight bend in your hooked knee.
    • Gripping too low on the pants: If you grip too low, you lose leverage and your opponent can easily step out. Grip at the cuff, pulling towards your hip.
    • Letting your hips collapse: If your hips fall back, you lose your base and your opponent can pass. Maintain your hip-to-hip connection and a strong frame with your unhooked leg.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice the leg hook and grip placement 20 times per side, focusing on hip angle and foot placement. (0% resistance)
    2. Partner drill: With a compliant partner, practice establishing the De La Riva hook and grips. Focus on maintaining your frames. 10 reps per side. (25% resistance)
    3. Posture control drill: Partner attempts to posture up, you practice pulling the collar and pants to break their posture. 10 reps per side. (50% resistance)
    4. Basic sweep drill: Practice a simple scissor sweep from De La Riva, with partner offering minimal resistance. 5 reps per side. (75% resistance)
    5. Controlled sweep attempt: Partner offers light resistance to your sweep attempt, allowing you to feel how to generate sweep power. 5 reps per side. (90% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Attempt to establish and utilize the De La Riva Guard in rolling. Focus on maintaining position and understanding when to transition. 3 rounds. (100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is attempting to pass your open guard by driving forward.
    • Opponent is postured up, creating an opportunity to attack their base.
    • You are being pressured in your guard and need to create space and control.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Torreando Pass: Opponent steps their hips to one side and walks around your guard. Defend by maintaining your frames and switching your guard.
    • Knee Slice Pass: Opponent drives their knee across your body to pass. Defend by creating a strong frame with your unhooked leg and shrimp away.
    • Stack Pass: Opponent drives their weight directly forward, attempting to crush your guard. Defend by creating space with your legs and using your grips to pull them forward.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do De La Riva Guard?

    Knee pain in De La Riva Guard often stems from hyperextension or torque. Ensure your hooked knee has a slight bend, never locking it out. Also, avoid letting your opponent step their weight directly onto your hooked shin; use your unhooked leg to frame and create space.

    Q: How do I use De La Riva Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on using your grips to control their posture and create angles. Your primary goal is to prevent them from establishing heavy pressure. Use your hooked leg to actively off-balance them rather than just holding. Maintaining a strong frame with your unhooked leg is crucial.

    Q: When should I switch from De La Riva Guard to another guard like Closed Guard or Half Guard?

    You should transition if your opponent successfully collapses your frames or passes your legs. If they are too heavy and you can't create space, consider switching to a more secure position like half guard. If they get too close and you can't maintain distance, a controlled transition to Closed Guard might be necessary.

Ffion Davies

"The Welsh Wizard"

Ffion Davies, nicknamed 'The Welsh Wizard', is one of the most accomplished Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu athletes from Welsh / British. Known for exceptional skill in heel hook, leg entanglement, 50 50 guard, triangle choke, Ffion has earned a reputation as one of the sport's most technically refined competitors. Training out of 10th Planet Jiu-Jitsu, they have amassed an impressive collection of world titles and competition victories across gi and no-gi formats.

Biography

Ffion Davies began training Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu and quickly demonstrated natural talent for the sport. On the competition circuit, Ffion Davies accumulated title after title: ADCC 60kg Champion 2022; EBI Champion; IBJJF European Champion. Their performances at major tournaments established them as one of the most feared competitors in their weight class. Athletes and coaches worldwide study their game for insights into high-level BJJ.



Beyond competition, Ffion Davies has contributed to the BJJ community through teaching, instructionals, and demonstrating that technical mastery can prevail over physical advantages. Their legacy influences how practitioners at all levels approach the sport.



Today, Ffion Davies continues to train, teach, and compete, passing on the lessons of their championship career to the next generation of BJJ athletes.

Fighting Style

Ffion Davies's game is defined by excellence in Heel Hook, leg entanglement, 50 50 guard, triangle choke. Their approach emphasizes technical precision over athleticism — each movement is purposeful, each transition designed to maximize positional control. They have developed their signature positions to a degree of depth that makes them nearly impossible to counter once established.

What makes Ffion's style particularly effective is the seamless connection between offense and defense. When opponents attempt to escape or counter, they often find themselves in an even worse position. This quality — turning defense into offense — is the hallmark of elite BJJ.

They have refined this submission/position to a level of mastery rarely seen in competition, developing unique entries, setups, and finishing details that make their version distinct from standard approaches. In-Depth: Ffion Davies

Biomechanics & Physics

Ffion Davies's mastery of leg entanglements, particularly the 50/50 guard and heel hook mechanics, is a testament to her understanding of biomechanical principles. When operating from the 50/50, she leverages the opposing torques created by hip rotation and knee flexion. By controlling the opponent's hip with her own femur and tibia, she stabilizes her base while applying torque through her own leg. The critical point of leverage for a heel hook is the knee joint. Davies precisely aligns her body to maximize the rotational force applied to the opponent's tibia and fibula, specifically targeting the deltoid ligament and posterior cruciate ligament. Her precise hip positioning ensures that the opposing force vector, generated by the opponent's attempt to escape, is absorbed efficiently, preventing them from creating the necessary counter-leverage to break the hold. The physics involved in her submissions often center on creating a fulcrum at the opponent's knee and applying pressure radially, exploiting the inherent structural weaknesses of the human leg when isolated and twisted.

Common Mistakes (Specific to This Technique)

  • **Prematurely extending the attacking leg:** Opponents often straighten their leg to try and "muscle" out of the 50/50, which disconnects their hip from your control and allows them to create space for an escape or to even invert.
  • **Insufficient hip connection:** Failing to maintain a tight connection between your hips and the opponent's hips in the 50/50 leaves a gap, allowing them to shrimp out or establish a stronger defensive base.
  • **Ignoring the opponent's free leg:** Focusing solely on the leg being attacked and neglecting to control or even attack the opponent's free leg can lead to sweeps or transitions from their side.

Variations & Counters

Davies's game isn't solely predicated on static positions. Her ability to transition seamlessly from various leg entanglements into submission threats is a key differentiator. For instance, a common sequence involves using the 50/50 guard to bait an opponent into attempting a stand-up or a hip escape. As they react, she can pivot her hips to transition into a straight ankle lock or even a toehold by re-angling her body and adjusting her grips. Conversely, when facing a strong leg lock defense, particularly someone attempting to "stack" or pass guard aggressively, Davies can utilize a knee-tap sweep to immediately threaten the back or transition to a single-leg X-guard. Her triangle choke, often set up from guard or even half guard, can also become a transition point. If the opponent defends the triangle by posturing up or attempting to pass, she can use that moment of instability to secure a leg entanglement or switch to an armbar if the limb is extended. This dynamic approach makes her attacks difficult to predict and defend against.

Drilling Recommendations

To emulate Davies's precision in leg entanglements, focus on progressive resistance drills. Begin with static positional drilling of the 50/50 guard, ensuring perfect hip control and knee-on-hip alignment for 5-minute rounds with zero resistance. Progress to drills where your training partner actively tries to escape by shrimping and hip escaping, but without creating significant pressure, for 5-minute rounds. Introduce the specific heel hook finish, focusing on maintaining tightness and proper rotational mechanics against a partner who minimally resists by trying to turn their foot out. For triangle chokes, drill the setup from closed guard and open guard against a partner who attempts to posture up and pass, focusing on maintaining hip control and creating the necessary angle for the submission. Aim for 10-15 repetitions of each specific transition or submission setup per limb. Always prioritize maintaining body control and correct limb placement over speed.

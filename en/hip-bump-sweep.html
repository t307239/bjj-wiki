The Hip Bump Sweep is initiated from Closed Guard, aiming to reverse position and end up on top, usually in side control or mount.

Many white belts fail because they rely on brute strength or jerky, uncoordinated movements, neglecting the subtle weight shifts required.

The core principle is using your hip to create a lever, displacing your opponent's weight and opening the path for your own ascent.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish closed guard with both legs around the opponent's torso, feet interlocked behind their back.
    2. Secure a strong grip: Left hand on opponent's right collar, right hand on opponent's right sleeve, pulling them slightly towards your left side.
    3. Create a frame: Place your left forearm against the opponent's right hip bone, creating space.
    4. Initiate the hip bump: Arch your lower back and drive your right hip upwards and slightly towards your left, aiming to lift their right hip off the mat.
    5. Simultaneously, shift your weight onto your right elbow and forearm, keeping your hips elevated.
    6. As their right hip lifts, bring your right knee across their body, aiming to place it on the mat near their armpit.
    7. Extend your left leg, pushing off their hip to clear your hips and transition to a top position, ideally right-side-up.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Trying to lift the opponent straight up with your arms: This strains the shoulder joint's rotator cuff and can lead to tears or impingement; instead, focus on driving your hip upwards.
    • Extending your legs too forcefully without a proper hip drive: This can hyperextend the knees, risking ACL or meniscus tears; always use the hip bump as the primary initiator.
    • Releasing grips prematurely before clearing hips: This leaves you vulnerable to being controlled or swept back; maintain collar and sleeve control until your hips are clear and you are establishing top control.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip arch, hip drive, and knee-across movement without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the entire sequence from closed guard, focusing on grip detail and hip movement. (10 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers light resistance to the hip bump, focusing on maintaining frames and hip angle. (10 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner attempts to posture up slightly, requiring more deliberate hip and weight transfer. (10 reps per side)
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner actively tries to defend by posturing or shrimping, forcing you to adjust your timing and hip drive. (10 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Apply the Hip Bump Sweep during rolling, focusing on execution under pressure. (5 attempts)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured up slightly, with their weight forward and their hips close to yours.
    • When the opponent attempts a guard pass, exposing their hips and weight distribution.
    • When you have established strong collar and sleeve grips, allowing you to control their posture.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • When the opponent attempts the hip bump, the defender can immediately shrimp their hips away and re-establish a closed guard or transition to a different guard.
    • If the defender feels their hip lifting, they can drop their weight back down and use their arms to create a strong frame against the attacker's hips and chest.
    • If the defender is unable to prevent the sweep, they should focus on landing on their side or back in a way that allows for a quick recovery to their base or a transition to a more dominant position.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my hip hurt when I try to Hip Bump Sweep?

    If your hip hurts, you are likely not rotating your pelvis correctly. Instead of just lifting, you need to drive your hip *up and across* your opponent's body, using your core muscles to generate the power. Ensure your supporting elbow is firmly planted to create a stable base, preventing your own hip from twisting unnaturally.

    Q: How do I Hip Bump Sweep against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paraMount. Focus on their weight distribution; if they are leaning forward, it's your opportunity. Instead of trying to lift their entire weight, aim to disrupt their base by focusing the hip bump on the *edge* of their hip closest to you, creating a tipping point. Use your collar grip to pull their upper body towards you as you bump.

    Q: I keep getting stuck after the Hip Bump Sweep, what am I doing wrong?

    The common mistake is not following through with the transition. After bumping their hip and bringing your knee across, you must immediately drive your own hips forward and up, using your supporting arm as a pivot. Think of it as climbing over their body; your right knee should plant near their armpit, and your left leg extends to help you stand up or settle into Side Control.

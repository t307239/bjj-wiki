The Hip Bump Sweep originates from your Closed Guard and aims to reverse the positional advantage, placing you on top.

White belts often fail by muscling the sweep or losing their frames, leading to stagnation or injury.

The key is using your hip's leverage to unbalance your opponent and create space, not brute force.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grips: Establish a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (left arm) with your left hand.
    2. Frame: Place your forearms against your opponent's biceps, creating a tight frame to prevent them from posturing up.
    3. Hip Angle: Begin to shift your hips to your right, angling your pelvis approximately 45 degrees away from your opponent.
    4. Weight Transfer: Simultaneously, use your right leg to hook behind your opponent's left knee, while your left leg remains inside.
    5. The Bump: Drive your right hip forcefully upwards and towards your opponent's left side, creating a sharp unbalancing motion.
    6. Head and Shoulder Drive: As you bump, drive your head and left shoulder into your opponent's chest, further aiding the unbalance.
    7. Leg Extension and Sweep: Extend your right leg while pulling with your grips to complete the sweep, transitioning to top position.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Knee Placement: Placing your hooking knee too far forward or outside the opponent's knee exposes your own knee joint. This can lead to a hyperextension injury or ligament tear (e.g., ACL or MCL) if the opponent drops their weight suddenly.
    • Lifting with the Back: Trying to lift the opponent with your back muscles instead of hip drive. This strains the lumbar spine, risking disc compression or muscle pulls.
    • Over-extending the Arm During Sweep: Reaching too far to pull the opponent over without maintaining a strong hip bump. This can strain the shoulder joint or elbow ligaments.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Movement: Practice hip shrimping and hip bumping motions in isolation (20 reps each).
    2. Closed Guard Frame and Hip: From closed guard, practice establishing frames and hip angles without grips (10 reps).
    3. Grip and Frame Drill: With a partner providing passive resistance, practice establishing grips and frames (10 reps each side).
    4. Controlled Hip Bump Sweep (0% Resistance): Execute the sweep with a partner who offers no resistance, focusing on perfect mechanics (5 reps each side).
    5. Light Resistance Hip Bump Sweep (25% Resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the timing and leverage (10 reps each side).
    6. Live Rolling (50-75% Resistance): Attempt the sweep during live rolling with controlled intensity, focusing on application and reaction (5 attempts each round).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is postured up slightly, creating an opening for a hip escape.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard by stacking or pressuring heavily.
    • When your opponent is kneeling or sitting close, making it difficult for them to base effectively.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: If the opponent attempts to stack you, maintain your frames and shrimp your hips out to the side to avoid being flattened, then re-establish guard.
    • Leg Slice/Pass: If the opponent tries to slice their leg through, maintain tight hips and use your frames to prevent them from isolating your leg, then use a hip escape to regain guard.
    • Knee Tap Counter: If the opponent tries to knee tap, keep your hips tight and your knees close to your chest, using your frames to push their head away and create space to shrimp out.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my hip bump sweep feel weak and not unbalance my opponent?

    Your hip bump sweep likely feels weak because you are not generating enough upward and forward force with your hip. Ensure your hip is angling 45 degrees away from your opponent and you are driving your hip bone upwards, not just pushing with your leg. The power comes from the hip pivot, not just leg extension.

    Q: How can I perform the Hip Bump Sweep effectively against a much larger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paraMount. Focus on getting your hip *under* their center of gravity. Instead of just bumping, try to use your collar grip to pull their head down and slightly to the side as you bump. This breaks their base more effectively. Ensure your frames are solid to prevent them from crushing you.

    Q: When should I stop trying the Hip Bump Sweep and switch to another technique?

    You should stop attempting the Hip Bump Sweep if your opponent consistently prevents your hip from making contact or if they are too heavy/stable to unbalance. If they successfully defend by posturing up or framing against your hip, it's time to switch. Look for opportunities to transition to a triangle or Armbar if they overcommit to defending the sweep.

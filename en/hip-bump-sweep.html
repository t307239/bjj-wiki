The Hip Bump Sweep is a fundamental sweep initiated from the Closed Guard, aiming to reverse your opponent's position and end up on top.

White belts often struggle because they rely on brute strength rather than leverage, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The core mechanical principle is creating a strong base with your legs and using your hips to generate explosive upward and sideways momentum.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish strong closed guard grips: one hand on opponent's left collar, the other on their left sleeve. Ensure your knees are tight around their hips.
    2. Shift your weight slightly to your right, bringing your right hip off the mat by about 10-15 degrees.
    3. With your left hand on the collar, pull the opponent's upper body diagonally towards your left side, creating a slight off-balance.
    4. Simultaneously, drive your right knee forward and upward, towards their right armpit. Your right hip should lift off the mat.
    5. Execute a powerful hip bump: drive your hips upwards and to your right, simultaneously extending your left leg to create a fulcrum.
    6. As your hips elevate, your opponent's weight distribution will shift forward and to their left, compromising their base.
    7. Immediately transition your base to your left hip and right knee on the mat, driving your right leg over their body to secure top position.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to 'push' the opponent away with straight arms: This can hyperextend your elbow joint, leading to ligamentous damage (e.g., UCL sprain) due to excessive valgus stress.
    • Arching your back excessively to lift hips: This puts immense strain on the lumbar spine's intervertebral discs, risking disc herniation or facet joint injury.
    • Letting your knees open wide during the hip bump: This exposes your hips and pelvis to potential impacts and can strain the adductor muscles (groin) if not controlled.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip elevation and bump motion without a partner, focusing on hip mobility and core engagement. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the entire Hip Bump Sweep sequence, focusing on grip fighting and hip movement. (10 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to grips and hip movement. Focus on timing the hip bump. (10 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner actively defends grips but allows the sweep attempt. Focus on maintaining control and hip drive. (10 reps per side)
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner defends grips and actively tries to prevent the sweep. Focus on adapting your hip drive and frame. (5 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling (90% intensity): Apply the Hip Bump Sweep in a controlled rolling scenario. Focus on recovery if the sweep is not completed. (5 attempts)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When opponent is posturing down heavily into your closed guard.
    • When opponent attempts to stack pass directly into your closed guard.
    • When opponent's weight is distributed forward, making them susceptible to a forward sweep.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass Defense: If opponent attempts to stack, maintain tight closed guard and drive your hips forward and up to meet their pressure, preventing them from collapsing you.
    • Knee Slice Prevention: If opponent begins to transition to a knee slice pass, immediately widen your base and shrimp your hips away to regain distance and prevent the pass.
    • Guard Retention: If the sweep is unsuccessful and you end up in a scramble, focus on re-establishing your guard by shrimping out and re-engaging your legs.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my hip hurt when I try the Hip Bump Sweep in BJJ?

    Hip pain during the Hip Bump Sweep often stems from improper hip elevation. Instead of driving your hips directly upwards and sideways with your gluteal and core muscles, you might be twisting your pelvis incorrectly or putting excessive torque on the hip joint. Focus on a strong upward drive from your hips, keeping your core engaged and your knees tight to your opponent's body.

    Q: How can I use the Hip Bump Sweep against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paraMount. Focus on creating a strong frame with your arms to control their posture and prevent them from posturing up. Use your legs to create a tight closed guard, then employ the hip bump with maximum explosive power, aiming to elevate your hips just enough to shift their weight significantly. Don't try to lift their entire body; aim to unbalance them.

    Q: I keep getting my Hip Bump Sweep countered, what am I doing wrong?

    Common counters occur when your hip bump is telegraphed or lacks the explosive drive. Ensure you are creating a clear off-balance with your grips and upper body pull *before* initiating the hip bump. Also, ensure your hip elevation is sharp and immediate, not a slow, grinding movement. If they start to counter, be ready to transition to a Guard Pass attempt or re-establish your guard.

BJJ Hip Bump Sweep: The Ultimate White Belt Technique Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Hip Bump Sweep originates from Closed Guard and aims to reverse the position, placing you on top. It disrupts the opponent's base and creates an angle for you to come up.

White belts often fail by pushing instead of hinging, creating a weak lever and telegraphing their intentions. This leads to predictable and easily defended attempts.

The core mechanic is the explosive hip extension and rotation, using your opponent's weight against them to create a dynamic angle for sweep. It’s a powerful, yet subtle, positional reversal.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish closed guard. Secure two collar grips with your thumbs inside the lapels, approximately at the opponent's sternum level. Keep your elbows tight to your body.
    2. With your right hand grip, pull the opponent's right arm across your body towards your left hip. Simultaneously, with your left hand grip, pull their left arm across your body towards your right hip. This creates a cross-face pressure.
    3. Extend your hips slightly, creating a small space between your hips and the opponent's hips. This is the setup for the hip bump.
    4. Drive your *right* hip forward and to the *left*, while simultaneously rotating your torso to the *left*. Imagine you are trying to bump your right hip into their left hip.
    5. As you drive your hip, simultaneously extend your *left* leg upwards, using it as a lever. Your left knee should point towards the ceiling.
    6. This combined hip drive and leg extension will create an angle, causing the opponent to fall to their left side.
    7. As they fall, immediately bring your *right* knee to the mat and drive your hips forward to establish side control, maintaining your collar grips to prevent them from reguarding.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Pushing straight forward with your hands instead of driving your hips: This strains the shoulder joint (glenohumeral joint) and can lead to rotator cuff tears or impingement. The correct action is a hip hinge and rotation.
    • Extending your legs straight out to push the opponent away: This can hyperextend the knees, risking ACL or meniscus tears. Your legs should create a frame and then one leg acts as a lever during the hip drive.
    • Sweeping to the side without bringing your hips forward: This often results in losing your base and the opponent recovering guard or passing. Always transition to side control immediately by bringing your right knee to the mat and driving your hips forward.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip hinge and rotation motion without a partner. Focus on the feeling of driving your hip and extending your leg. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): From closed guard, have your partner hold a light grip. Practice securing collar grips and executing the hip bump motion, focusing on the exact hip movement and leg extension. (20 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to execute the sweep. Focus on the timing of hip drive and leg extension. (15 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to prevent the sweep, but allows you to feel the mechanics. Focus on breaking their base with the hip bump. (15 reps per side)
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner actively defends. Focus on completing the sweep and transitioning to side control. (10 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Attempt the Hip Bump Sweep in a controlled sparring scenario. Focus on applying the technique under pressure and transitioning smoothly. (5 attempts per round)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is posturing up heavily in your closed guard, creating a vertical base.
    • Opponent is attempting to stack you, their weight is forward and they are off-balance.
    • Opponent is leaning heavily to one side, creating an obvious angle for you to exploit.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stacking defense: If the opponent attempts the hip bump, immediately drive your hips back towards them and secure a strong cross-face with your forearm to prevent them from creating the angle. Maintain a tight guard.
    • Knee shield: As they attempt to bump, bring your opposite knee (the one on the side they are trying to sweep you towards) up to create a barrier and prevent them from fully rotating you. This disrupts the lever.
    • Posture control: If you feel the hip bump attempt, immediately break their posture by pulling them forward and down, collapsing their base and making the sweep difficult. Secure a strong grip on their collar or shoulder.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to Hip Bump Sweep?

    Wrist pain often stems from gripping too hard with your fingers and not engaging your forearm and bicep. When you pull with the collar grips, ensure your thumbs are inside the lapels and your forearms are flexed, driving the opponent's arms across your body. Avoid just using finger strength, which overloads the wrist joint. Focus on a full-body pull initiated by your back and hips.

    Q: How can I Hip Bump Sweep against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, the principle of leverage becomes even more critical. Instead of trying to muscle them, focus on creating a significant angle. Ensure you're pulling their arms across your body tightly to break their base. The key is the explosive hip drive and rotation; think about generating maximum torque with your hips and core, rather than brute force. Drive your hip into their hip with speed and precision.

    Q: I keep getting stuck after the Hip Bump Sweep, what am I doing wrong?

    This is a common issue; you're likely not transitioning quickly enough to Side Control. Immediately after you feel the opponent begin to fall, bring your right knee to the mat and drive your hips *forward*. Do not pause to admire your sweep. Your goal is to land in a dominant position, so focus on securing the side control by connecting your chest to their chest and controlling their hips.

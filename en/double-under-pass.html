Double Under Pass: The Ultimate BJJ Technique Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Double Under Pass is initiated from the guard, aiming to secure a dominant side-control or Mount position. It bypasses the opponent's legs by passing your arms under both of theirs. Many white belts struggle due to a lack of understanding of hip pressure and weight distribution. The key mechanical insight is to drive your hips forward and down, using your chest to pin their hips, not just your arms to hold their legs.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Start with opponent in closed guard. Establish a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (e.g., left sleeve) with your left hand. Your base should be wide, knees bent, hips low.
    2. Drive your hips forward aggressively, aiming to get your pelvis in contact with the opponent's hips.
    3. Simultaneously, begin to slide your right arm (collar grip arm) under the opponent's right leg, aiming to get it past their hip bone.
    4. As your right arm passes their hip, transition your left hand (sleeve grip) to secure a grip on the opponent's left ankle or pant cuff, as close to the foot as possible.
    5. Drive your right shoulder into the opponent's right hip and use your right arm to help pull their right leg across your body, clearing it.
    6. With your left hand controlling their ankle/pant, begin to transfer your weight forward. Your chest should be pressed against their hips, and your hips should be driving forward and slightly to their left.
    7. As you clear their right leg, step your right foot out to the side (your right) and begin to bring your left foot forward, establishing a strong base on the mat.
    8. Continue driving your hips forward and down, pinning their hips with your chest. Your goal is to have your hips lower than theirs.
    9. Secure your side-control position by establishing a cross-face grip with your right arm and controlling their far hip with your left arm, maintaining forward pressure.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly trying to "pull" the opponent's legs over with only arm strength: This can lead to biceps tears or elbow hyperextension if the opponent resists strongly.
    • Letting your hips fall back instead of driving forward: This creates space for the opponent to sweep or recover guard, and puts your lower back at risk of strain from an unsupported arch.
    • Failing to secure a strong grip on the ankle/pant cuff: This allows the opponent to easily pull their leg back, negating the pass and potentially leading to a leg entanglement or sweep attempt against you.
    • Standing too upright: This leaves your base unstable and vulnerable to sweeps, while also making it difficult to generate the necessary hip pressure for the pass.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip-driving and arm-sliding motion without a partner. Focus on the weight transfer and hip angle. (10 reps)
    2. Static partner drill (0% resistance): Partner lies on their back, allowing you to practice the grips and hip drive. Focus on getting your arms under and driving forward. (10 reps)
    3. Light pressure drill (25% resistance): Partner in closed guard, offering minimal resistance. Practice driving your hips and clearing the legs. (10 reps)
    4. Controlled guard pass (50% resistance): Partner attempts to maintain guard but doesn't actively resist the pass. Focus on maintaining forward pressure and hip contact. (10 reps)
    5. Guard recovery drill (75% resistance): Partner actively tries to recover guard after you begin the pass. Focus on maintaining your position and finishing the pass. (10 reps)
    6. Live rolling (90-100% resistance): Apply the Double Under Pass in sparring. Focus on executing the steps accurately under pressure. (3-minute rounds)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent has a tight closed guard and you have established grips on their pants or collar.
    • When the opponent attempts to open their guard or transitions to an open guard position.
    • When the opponent is posturing up, creating a small window to drive through.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Guard Recovery (Hip Escape and Leg Post): If the passer's hips fall back, the defender can hip escape towards the open side and post their leg to create space and re-establish guard.
    • Sweep (Scissor Sweep or Hip Bump Sweep): If the passer overcommits and their weight is too far forward, the defender can use their free leg to hook the passer's leg and execute a sweep.
    • Submission (Triangle Choke or Armbar): If the passer's head comes too low or they leave an arm exposed during the pass, the defender can attempt a submission from their guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my back hurt when I try the Double Under Pass?

    Your back is likely hurting because you are arching your lower back to compensate for a lack of forward hip pressure. Instead of hinging at your hips and driving your pelvis into the opponent's, you're leaning back. To fix this, focus on bending your knees deeply and driving your hips forward and down, keeping your spine neutral and your chest pressed against their hips.

    Q: How do I Double Under Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise weight distribution are even more critical. Focus on getting your hips significantly lower than theirs before initiating the pass. Use your head to drive into their hip and your chest to pin their hips to the mat. Grip fighting is also paramount; secure strong grips on their pants or ankles to prevent them from using their size to shrimp away.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Double Under Pass, and what if they keep their guard closed?

    The Double Under Pass is best attempted when the opponent begins to open their guard or posture up, creating a slight gap. If they maintain a tight Closed Guard, the pass becomes much harder. In this scenario, focus on breaking their guard first with pressure, hip bumps, or by attacking their arms to create an opening before attempting the Double Under Pass.

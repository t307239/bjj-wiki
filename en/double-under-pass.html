Mastering the Double Under Pass in BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

This pass starts from a standing or kneeling position facing an opponent's Closed Guard. Its goal is to bypass their legs and establish side control.

White belts often fail because they try to force the pass with raw strength instead of using leverage and body mechanics.

The key insight is to use your hips to drive through their legs, creating space and trapping them.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Secure a grip on both of the opponent's ankles with a "palm-to-shin" grip, thumbs pointing upwards.
    2. Lower your hips by bending your knees, creating a base wider than your shoulders.
    3. Drive your chest forward and down, aiming your sternum towards the opponent's sternum.
    4. Simultaneously, drive your hips forward and slightly to one side, forcing your knees past their hips.
    5. As your knees clear their hips, "walk" your feet forward, keeping your base wide.
    6. Grip their ankles securely, ensuring your forearms are tight against their shins to prevent them from regripping.
    7. Once your knees are past their hips and your base is secure, shift your weight onto your feet and drive forward to establish side control.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to "pull" the opponent's legs apart with straight arms: This can lead to elbow hyperextension, damaging the ulnar collateral ligament. Instead, use your hips to drive forward.
    • Allowing the opponent's knees to stay between your legs: This can result in a knee injury if they bridge or shrimp aggressively. Maintain a wide base and keep your knees driving past their hips.
    • Standing up too quickly after securing ankle grips: This allows the opponent to easily re-guard or attack your legs. Keep your hips low and drive forward to clear their guard first.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo: Practice the hip-driving and knee-clearing motion without a partner, focusing on hip angle and base width (50 reps).
    2. Solo: Practice the ankle grip and hip drive, imagining an opponent's legs (50 reps).
    3. Partner Drill (0% Resistance): Opponent holds closed guard. You practice securing ankle grips and driving hips through, with no attempt to pass (20 reps per side).
    4. Partner Drill (25% Resistance): Opponent offers minimal resistance to grips and hip drive. Focus on clearing their legs (20 reps per side).
    5. Partner Drill (50% Resistance): Opponent actively tries to maintain guard. You focus on maintaining grip and driving through their hips (10 reps per side).
    6. Live Rolling (75% Resistance): Apply the pass in sparring, focusing on the mechanics and not just success. Accept when the pass fails (5 attempts).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent's legs are slightly open or untangled.
    • When the opponent attempts a sweep and their hips become exposed.
    • When transitioning from a failed submission attempt where their guard is briefly open.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Re-guarding: If the passer's knees get stuck, the defender can shrimp their hips and bring their legs back to closed guard.
    • Hip Escape and Knee Shield: If the passer drives past the hips but remains in the same plane, the defender can hip escape and create a knee shield to prevent side control.
    • Sweeping from the pass: If the passer's base is compromised during the pass, the defender can use a tripod sweep or scissor sweep to reverse position.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my back hurt when I try the Double Under Pass?

    Your back is likely hurting because you are rounding your spine and trying to lift your opponent's legs with your back muscles. Instead, keep your chest up and drive through your hips and legs. Bend your knees to lower your center of gravity, and use the powerful muscles of your glutes and quads to propel your hips forward.

    Q: How do I perform the Double Under Pass against a much bigger opponent who has tight closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is even more critical. Focus on getting your hips lower than theirs before initiating the pass. Secure a strong "palm-to-shin" grip on their ankles, and drive your chest into their sternum to break their posture. Then, use a powerful hip drive to force your knees past their hips, creating space to advance.

    Q: When should I stop trying the Double Under Pass and switch to another guard pass?

    You should switch if you consistently cannot get your knees past the opponent's hips or if they are effectively "stacking" you. If your grips are constantly being broken, or if you find yourself stuck in their guard with no progress, it's time to disengage and try a different approach like a knee slice or Torreando Pass.

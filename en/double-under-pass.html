Mastering the Double Under Pass in BJJ: A White Belt's Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

This pass starts from a standing or kneeling position facing a guard player, aiming to secure Side Control. It's a fundamental pass that bypasses the opponent's legs. White belts often struggle because they focus on speed over control, leading to predictable movements and loss of balance. The key insight is using your head and shoulder pressure to drive through their legs, creating a strong, unshakeable base.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a kneeling or staggered stance, establish an underhook grip with your right arm around the opponent's left hip/waist, palm facing your own face. Your left hand grips their right pant leg around the knee.
    2. Lower your hips by bending your knees and pushing your weight forward, bringing your chest towards their abdomen. Your base should be shoulder-width apart.
    3. Drive your right shoulder into their left hip crease, angling your torso slightly to the right. Your head should be up and looking forward.
    4. Simultaneously, begin to slide your left hand down their right pant leg, aiming to control their ankle or foot.
    5. As you drive forward, your right knee should track forward in line with your right foot, creating a stable tripod base.
    6. Your opponent's legs will naturally try to re-guard; use your underhook to prevent them from bringing their hips back.
    7. Complete the pass by continuing to drive your head and shoulder through, bringing your right knee to the mat and securing side control with your hips low and chest tight.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly driving forward with hips too high: This strains the lumbar spine as the lower back hyperextends. The correct movement is to lower your hips by bending your knees, keeping your back relatively flat.
    • Gripping the pants too low (e.g., ankle only) and not establishing an underhook: This allows the opponent to easily shrimp away and re-guard, often resulting in the passer's knee getting trapped and potentially injured by a twist. Always secure the underhook first and use the pant grip for control.
    • Rushing the pass without establishing a solid base: This can lead to the opponent sweeping you. Your weight distribution is key; maintain a wide, stable base with knees bent and hips low.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip angles, arm placement, and head/shoulder drive motion without a partner. Focus on smooth transitions and maintaining a low center of gravity. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Your partner lies on their back in guard. Practice the entry, underhook, pant grip, and driving motion, focusing on precise body mechanics. (20 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal defense, allowing you to feel the pressure and movement. Focus on driving through their legs. (5 rounds, 1 minute each, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner drill (moderate resistance): Partner actively tries to keep their guard closed or frame against your pass. Focus on breaking their frames and driving. (5 rounds, 1 minute each, 50% resistance)
    5. Live rolling (controlled): Attempt the pass during sparring, but with clear communication to stop if something feels wrong. Focus on applying the learned mechanics. (3 rounds, 2 minutes each, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling (full intensity): Integrate the Double Under Pass into your rolling, aiming to execute it against a resisting opponent. (5 rounds, 3 minutes each, 90-100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent has a closed guard and you are postured up.
    • When the opponent attempts to switch from open guard to a more closed position.
    • When the opponent's hip mobility is limited, making it harder for them to re-guard effectively.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape and Frame: As the passer drives in, the guard player can hip escape away and create a frame with their forearm between the passer's chest and their own hips, preventing the drive.
    • Leg Pummeling: The guard player can actively pummel their legs (e.g., bring their knee inside the passer's armpit) to create space and prevent the double under grip from being secured.
    • Sweep Attempt: If the passer's base is compromised during the pass, the guard player can look to initiate a sweep by using their feet on the passer's hips or shoulders to create leverage.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Double Under Pass?

    Knee pain often occurs because you're driving your knee directly into the mat without proper support, or your opponent is trapping your leg. Ensure your driving knee comes down in line with your ankle and foot, creating a stable tripod. Always maintain your Underhook to prevent your opponent from posturing up and trapping your leg in a compromising position.

    Q: How do I perform the Double Under Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paraMount. Focus on getting low and driving your shoulder into their hip crease rather than trying to muscle through. Use their weight against them by angling your body and driving their momentum past you. Secure a deep underhook and don't let go, using it to control their posture and prevent them from re-guarding.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from a Torreando Pass to a Double Under Pass?

    The Torreando Pass is effective when you can circle around the opponent's legs. If they start to turn their hips in to face you or bring their knees towards their chest to re-establish guard, it's an opportune moment to switch to the Double Under. This happens when their legs are no longer extended and you can get your head and shoulders underneath their hips to drive through.

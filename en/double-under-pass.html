The Double Under Pass: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

The Double Under Pass is a fundamental Guard Pass initiated from a standing or kneeling position, aiming to control both of the opponent's legs simultaneously to advance past their guard.

White belts often struggle with this pass due to a lack of hip pressure, insufficient leg control, and a tendency to rely on arm strength over body weight transfer.

The core principle is to use your hips to drive forward, forcing your opponent's legs out of the way while maintaining tight control on their ankles or shins.

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip: Secure two underhooks on the opponent's thighs, just above the knees. Your forearms should be tight against their hamstrings, palms facing upwards.
    2. Posture: Establish strong upper body posture, keeping your back straight and head up, looking forward.
    3. Weight Distribution: Lower your hips slightly, bringing them closer to the mat than your opponent's hips. Your base should be shoulder-width apart.
    4. Drive Forward: Initiate a powerful forward drive, pushing your hips through your opponent's legs. Imagine driving your sternum towards their groin.
    5. Leg Control: Maintain constant tension on your grips. As you drive, use your forearms to push their thighs outwards, clearing space.
    6. Head Position: Keep your head up and slightly to one side, preventing them from establishing head control or framing.
    7. Advance Base: Step your lead leg forward, then bring your trail leg up to establish a knee-on-belly or side control position, maintaining pressure.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Error: Grabbing the opponent's pants too high and not driving hips forward. **Damage:** Lumbar spine hyperextension, leading to muscle strain or disc injury. **Correction:** Grip the thighs/hamstrings and actively drive your hips forward and down.
    • Error: Letting your head drop and trying to pull the legs apart with your arms. **Damage:** Neck hyperextension or flexion, risking cervical strain or nerve impingement. **Correction:** Maintain upright posture and use hip drive to displace their legs.
    • Error: Widening your base too much or not keeping knees bent. **Damage:** Knee valgus collapse (knees caving inward), leading to ACL or MCL sprains. **Correction:** Keep knees tracking over toes with a stable, shoulder-width base.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Drive Drill: Practice driving your hips forward while maintaining a strong posture, focusing on the hip hinge and forward momentum. (50 reps)
    2. Static Double Under Grip & Drive: Partner lies on their back. You secure double under grips and practice the forward hip drive without moving your feet. (20 reps)
    3. Moving Double Under Drive: Partner lies on their back. You secure grips and drive forward, taking small steps to advance. Partner offers minimal resistance. (20 reps)
    4. Light Resistance Double Under: Partner in guard. You secure grips and drive, with partner attempting to maintain guard. Focus on hip pressure. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Controlled Double Under Pass: Live rolling scenario with a focus on executing the Double Under Pass. Opponent actively defends. (5 minutes, 75% resistance)
    6. Full Speed Double Under Pass: Integrate the Double Under Pass into live rolling with maximum effort from both participants. (5 minutes, 100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is in your closed guard and attempts to open it or create space.
    • Opponent is in an open guard (e.g., spider guard, de la riva guard) and you have an opportunity to secure both leg grips.
    • Opponent is trying to shrimp away or create distance from your pressure.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Retention: If the passer secures the double under, the defender can try to maintain hip connection, keeping their knees bent and hips mobile to prevent the forward drive. This involves actively pushing into the passer's hips.
    • Reguarding: The defender can attempt to circle their hips and bring a knee back in between their legs to re-establish guard, often by pushing off the passer's hip or shoulder.
    • Leg Pummeling: The defender can try to pummel their legs, specifically shins and knees, to create space and break the passer's control, often by flexing their feet and rotating their ankles outwards.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try the Double Under Pass?

    Your lower back pain is likely due to hyperextension. Instead of driving your hips forward and down, you might be arching your back. Focus on hinging at your hips and using your glutes and hamstrings to generate power, keeping your core engaged and your spine neutral.

    Q: How can I perform the Double Under Pass effectively against a much bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, precision and leverage are key. Focus on getting your grips *deep* on their hamstrings, just above the knee. Drive your hips *under* their center of gravity, not just forward. Use your head and shoulder to apply downward pressure, and maintain a tight posture to prevent them from using their size to push you away.

    Q: When is the best moment to initiate the Double Under Pass from the standing position?

    The ideal moment from standing is when your opponent has just attempted to create space or is off-balance. Look for a brief window where their hips are not fully connected to yours, or when they've committed to a movement that momentarily opens their legs. Secure your grips immediately and drive forward with your hips before they can re-establish a strong base.

This choke is initiated from the Closed Guard, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain by compressing the carotid arteries.

Many white belts struggle by relying on raw strength instead of leverage and proper body mechanics.

Success hinges on controlling the opponent's posture and creating a tight 'frame' with your arms and hips.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a closed guard by securing your feet across the opponent's back, ankles crossed.
    2. Reach your right hand inside the opponent's gi collar, gripping just below their sternum, thumb inside the lapel.
    3. Reach your left hand inside the opponent's left gi collar, gripping at the same depth as the right hand, thumb inside the lapel.
    4. Arch your lower back, bringing your hips off the mat slightly, creating a small gap.
    5. Pull your opponent's head down towards your chest by driving your elbows downwards.
    6. Simultaneously, drive your hips upwards into their torso, creating pressure and limiting their ability to posture up.
    7. Tighten the choke by squeezing your elbows towards your body and lifting your hips, compressing the carotid arteries.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Gripping too high on the collar: This can strain your shoulder joint's rotator cuff due to excessive external rotation and abduction.

    The correct grip is lower, near the sternum, allowing for a more controlled internal rotation and adduction.

    • Tensing the neck muscles excessively: This can lead to neck strain or whiplash if the opponent resists incorrectly.

    Focus on relaxing your neck and allowing the lapels to do the work, using your hips for power.

    • Overextending the arms when pulling: This can hyperextend your elbows and wrists, risking ligament damage.

    Keep a slight bend in your elbows and use your lats and hips to drive the opponent down.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the grip-taking and hip-up motion without a partner (20 reps).
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Focus on the exact grip placement and hip movement with a compliant partner (20 reps).
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance - 25%): Partner attempts to posture up slightly; you focus on controlling their posture and executing the choke mechanics.
    4. Partner drilling (50%): Partner actively tries to break your guard and posture up; focus on maintaining control and applying the choke.
    5. Partner drilling (75%): Partner defends more actively, attempting to create space and escape; practice maintaining grips and adjusting your hips.
    6. Live rolling (90%): Apply the choke in a sparring situation, focusing on transitioning to it when the opportunity arises.

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent's posture is broken and their head is within your reach.
    • When you have a strong, secure closed guard and can prevent them from posturing up.
    • As a follow-up to a failed sweep or submission attempt where you maintain closed guard.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Posture Up: The opponent straightens their back and drives their hips into you, creating space and relieving pressure.
    • Grip Break: The opponent uses their hands to peel your lapel grips off their collar.
    • Guard Pass Attempt: The opponent attempts to create space by pushing your knees apart and passing to side control.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Cross Collar Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often stems from gripping the lapel with your palm facing upwards or trying to 'pull' the opponent's head down with your wrists alone. This puts excessive dorsiflexion on your wrist. To fix this, ensure your thumbs are tucked inside the lapel for a more neutral grip and use your forearms and upper back to drive the opponent's head down, keeping your wrists relatively straight.

    Q: How can I Cross Collar Choke a bigger opponent when I'm smaller in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paraMount. Focus on controlling their posture by keeping your hips active and driving them into your torso. Instead of pulling directly, use your body weight and hip elevation to create the choking pressure. Ensure your grips are deep and secure, preventing them from creating space to posture up.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate the Cross Collar Choke from closed guard?

    The ideal time is when the opponent's posture is broken and their head is low, making it difficult for them to defend. This often happens after they attempt to pass your guard or when they are trying to establish a dominant top position. Look for moments of stillness or when they are leaning forward, allowing you to secure your grips and initiate the hip-drive.

The Biomechanically Precise Cross Collar Choke for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

This choke originates from the Closed Guard position, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain via the carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle by pulling too hard or not understanding the subtle mechanics, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The core principle is using your hips and leverage to create a fulcrum, rather than brute strength, to compress the arteries.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Secure your left hand with a standard "gi collar" grip on your opponent's right lapel, thumb inside, fingers outside, approximately two finger-widths from the center of the collarbone.
    2. Secure your right hand with a standard "gi collar" grip on your opponent's left lapel, thumb inside, fingers outside, at the same depth as the first grip.
    3. Bring your knees together tightly, creating a "closed guard" posture, ensuring your hips are elevated slightly off the mat.
    4. Arch your lower back, pushing your hips upwards towards your opponent's hips, creating a slight forward lean.
    5. Simultaneously, pull your opponent's head down towards your chest by pulling with both arms, creating a slight "craning" motion.
    6. Shift your weight back slightly by bending your knees, while maintaining upward hip pressure and downward head pressure.
    7. Apply pressure by squeezing your knees together and driving your hips forward, while continuing the inward pull with your arms, compressing the lapels against the carotid arteries.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying downward force with your arms alone without hip elevation: this strains the rotator cuff and shoulder capsule due to excessive torque on the humerus.
    • Gripping too low on the collar or too wide: this reduces the effective leverage and can lead to the opponent easily posturing up, potentially hyperextending your neck if you pull too hard.
    • Trying to "muscle" the choke by arching your back excessively without hip drive: this can strain the lumbar spine and offers less effective arterial compression.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the grip acquisition and hip movement with no resistance. (50 reps)
    2. Static partner drill: With a compliant partner, practice the grip and hip movement, holding the final position for 5 seconds. (25 reps)
    3. Controlled resistance drill: Partner offers minimal resistance as you execute the choke, focusing on hip and arm coordination. (25 reps)
    4. Light positional sparring: Practice from closed guard against a partner resisting lightly, aiming to establish the choke position. (5 minutes, 25% resistance)
    5. Moderate positional sparring: Focus on executing the choke during closed guard exchanges, allowing for moderate resistance. (5 minutes, 50% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the Cross Collar Choke in live sparring, prioritizing correct mechanics over immediate success. (10 minutes, 75% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is postured up in your closed guard, creating a frame with their hands on your thighs.
    • When your opponent is attempting to pass your guard and their head is within reach of your lapel grips.
    • When your opponent is turtled or on their hands and knees, and you can secure one or both lapels.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Posture Up: The opponent straightens their back and lifts their hips away, breaking the choke's structure.
    • Grip Break: The opponent uses their hands to peel off your collar grips, often by placing their thumbs on the inside of your knuckles.
    • Hip Escape to Open Guard: The opponent uses a hip escape to create space and transition to an open guard, negating the closed guard control.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do a Cross Collar Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you're trying to generate choking pressure solely by flexing your wrists inwards. This puts excessive strain on the carpal bones and ligaments. Instead, focus on using your entire forearm and the power of your hips to drive the lapels into the neck. Keep your wrists neutral or slightly extended, allowing your forearms and core to do the work.

    Q: How can I effectively apply the Cross Collar Choke against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, brute strength is your enemy. Focus on leveraging your hips to create a powerful upward thrust, driving their weight into your chest and then using that momentum to pull them down. Secure deep, precise grips on their lapels, as close to the center as possible, to maximize the effectiveness of your smaller frame. Ensure your knees are tightly locked to prevent them from posturing up.

    Q: The optimal time to transition to a Cross Collar Choke is when your opponent attempts to posture up or create space. As they try to straighten their back and lift their hips, this is your cue to immediately lock your feet, arch your hips, and secure your grips. This timing exploits their movement and makes it harder for them to defend, as they are already committing to an upward motion.

Cross Collar Choke: The Essential BJJ Technique Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

This choke is initiated from the Closed Guard, aiming to restrict blood flow to the opponent's brain. White belts often struggle due to poor posture control and uncontrolled limb movements. The key is generating leverage through hip elevation and precise collar grips, not brute strength.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From closed guard, establish a "high-and-wide" posture control by placing both feet on the opponent's hips, knees bent at 90 degrees. 2. Reach your right hand under the opponent's left lapel, gripping deep near the sternum. 3. Reach your left hand over the opponent's right shoulder, gripping their right lapel, thumb inside, near the collarbone. 4. Contract your biceps to pull the lapels taut, creating tension across the throat. 5. Simultaneously, elevate your hips slightly off the mat by engaging your glutes and lower back. 6. Drive your elbows down and slightly outward, creating a "V" shape with your arms and torso. 7. Maintain your feet on the hips and keep your knees squeezed to prevent the opponent from creating space or posturing up. 8. Apply continuous, controlled pressure by squeezing your biceps and driving your elbows downward.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Grabbing the opponent's uniform too loosely or too high on the neck: This fails to compress the carotid arteries effectively and can lead to the opponent easily escaping the grip. The correct grip is deep in the collar, near the sternum and collarbone, applying pressure to the sides of the neck.
    • Yanking with the arms without hip elevation: This strains the biceps and shoulders, leading to fatigue and a weak choke. The power comes from driving the elbows down and elevating the hips, not just pulling.
    • Allowing the opponent to posture up: If the opponent straightens their back and lifts their head, the choke becomes ineffective and opens opportunities for them to pass your guard. Maintain consistent pressure on the hips with your feet and keep your knees tight.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the grip setup and hip elevation motion 20 times without a partner (0% resistance). 2. Partner drilling (static): With a compliant partner in your closed guard, practice establishing the grips and hip elevation 15 times per side (25% resistance). 3. Partner drilling (posture control): With a partner, focus on maintaining hip control while they attempt to posture up, practicing the choke setup 10 times (50% resistance). 4. Flow drilling: Execute the choke setup and completion from closed guard, allowing your partner to defend and reset 10 times (75% resistance). 5. Controlled rolling: Attempt the Cross Collar Choke during rolling, focusing on proper setup and execution without forcing the submission 5 times (90% resistance). 6. Live rolling: Incorporate the Cross Collar Choke into your rolling sessions, aiming for submission when the opportunity arises (100% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is in your closed guard and attempts to posture up.
    • When your opponent is pressing their chest into yours, creating a tight guard.
    • When your opponent is posturing down to pass your guard, offering their neck.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Head and Arm Control (e.g., Kimura from guard): If the opponent attempts to grip your arms to break the choke, secure one of their arms and transition to a Kimura grip, using your hips to create leverage for a shoulder lock.
    • Guard Pass Attempt (e.g., Toreando Pass): If the opponent creates space by pushing your knees apart, use your feet to push their hips away and transition to a sweep or attempt a guard pass yourself.
    • Standing Up to Break Grip: If the opponent has a strong grip and you cannot break it, stand up while maintaining your guard, using your legs to create distance and then untangle your arms.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Cross Collar Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you're gripping the lapel with your palm facing upwards or your wrist bent at an extreme angle. Ensure your thumbs are inside the collar and your wrists remain relatively straight and neutral, gripping the fabric with your fingers. The pressure should be generated by your biceps and a slight downward drive of your elbows, not by bending your wrists.

    Q: How do I apply the Cross Collar Choke effectively against a much larger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paraMount. Focus intensely on controlling their posture by keeping your feet high on their hips and your knees squeezed tight. Elevate your hips significantly off the mat, creating a strong base, and drive your elbows down forcefully to compress the carotid arteries. Do not rely on pulling power; use your entire body's weight and leverage.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to the Cross Collar Choke if my opponent is posturing up?

    The ideal moment is as your opponent begins to straighten their back and lift their head. As they create that vertical space, quickly establish your high-and-wide grip on the lapels. Simultaneously, initiate your hip elevation and elbow drive. This timing capitalizes on their momentary loss of control and creates the necessary pressure before they can fully recover their posture.

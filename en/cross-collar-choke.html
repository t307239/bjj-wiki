Cross Collar Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

This choke is typically initiated from the Closed Guard position, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain via the carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength rather than precise leverage and weight distribution.

The key insight is using your hips and shoulder pressure to create a vice-like grip with the lapels, not just pulling with your arms.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From closed guard, establish two deep cross-collar grips: right hand grips opponent's left lapel near the sternum, left hand grips opponent's right lapel near the shoulder.
    2. Keep your hips tight and slightly elevated, creating a strong base against your opponent's torso.
    3. Begin to 'climb' your grips, sliding your hands further into the lapels, ensuring your knuckles are pointing downwards.
    4. Bring your opponent's head towards your chest by tucking your chin and slightly arching your upper back.
    5. Simultaneously, drive your hips forward and slightly to your left (if right-handed), creating a rotational torque.
    6. Use your forearms to apply inward pressure on the lapels, compressing the carotid arteries against your opponent's neck.
    7. Maintain a stable closed guard, preventing your opponent from posturing up or creating space.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Gripping too shallowly on the lapels: This allows your opponent to easily defend by posturing up, potentially leading to a strained neck or shoulder from ineffective pulling.
    • Pushing with your feet on the hips instead of driving hips forward: This creates a weak base and prevents the necessary pelvic rotation, risking a hip flexor strain.
    • Trying to 'muscle' the choke without proper hip and shoulder pressure: This can lead to a strained back or shoulder joint as you rely on isolated arm strength rather than coordinated body mechanics.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the grip setup and hip drive motion without a partner. (10 reps per side)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Execute the full technique with a willing partner who remains passive. (10 reps per side)
    3. Light resistance drill: Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the correct pressure. (5 reps per side)
    4. Controlled resistance drill: Partner actively tries to defend slightly, simulating a real situation. (5 reps per side)
    5. Flow rolling: Incorporate the choke into a rolling scenario with a focus on technique, not submission. (2 minutes)
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the choke during a regular roll with full resistance. (5 minutes)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is postured up slightly in your closed guard.
    • When you have secured deep cross-collar grips and have control of their posture.
    • When your opponent attempts to create space by pushing on your hips.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Defense 1 (Opponent's response): If the choke is being applied, the defender should immediately try to break one or both collar grips by cupping the attacker's wrists with their own hands and pulling outwards.
    • Defense 2 (Opponent's response): The defender can attempt to 'hide' their neck by tucking their chin tightly to their chest and turning their head away from the choking arm.
    • Defense 3 (Opponent's response): If the attacker's hips are too far forward, the defender can try to create space by shoving the attacker's hips away with their hands or by straightening their legs.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Cross Collar Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs from gripping the lapels too tightly with your fingers extended and then trying to pull down with pure wrist flexion. Instead, grip with your knuckles pointing down and use your entire forearm and shoulder to drive the pressure into their neck, keeping your wrists in a more neutral, strong position.

    Q: How can I do the Cross Collar Choke if my opponent is much bigger than me?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on using your hips and shoulder pressure to create the choke, not raw arm strength. Ensure your closed guard is tight, preventing them from posturing. Drive your hips forward aggressively and use your body weight to apply the rotational pressure, making the lapel grip do the work.

    Q: I can't seem to finish the Cross Collar Choke, my opponent just taps their leg. What am I doing wrong?

    Tapping the leg usually means they are defending the Hip Escape or trying to create space. You're likely not generating enough rotational pressure with your hips and torso. Ensure you are driving your hips forward and slightly to the side, creating a twisting motion that compresses the arteries, rather than just pulling with your arms.

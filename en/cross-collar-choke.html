The Cross Collar Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

This choke is primarily initiated from the Closed Guard position, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain via the carotid arteries.

Many white belts grasp incorrectly, applying pressure with their forearms instead of using the lapels for leverage.

The key is hip extension and precise collar placement, creating a controlled, crushing pressure that is difficult to escape.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a closed guard: hips connected to opponent's hips, knees locked together.
    2. With your right hand, secure the opponent's left lapel at the collarbone, thumb inside the lapel.
    3. With your left hand, secure the opponent's right lapel, also at the collarbone, thumb inside.
    4. Slightly shift your hips to your left, creating an angle and bringing your right shoulder closer to their left shoulder.
    5. Squeeze your knees together and drive your hips upward, lifting your pelvis off the mat.
    6. Arch your back and pull your elbows down and slightly back, driving the lapels into the sides of their neck.
    7. Maintain constant hip pressure and a tight knee connection to prevent them from posturing up.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Gripping too low on the lapel: This can lead to ineffective pressure and strain on your own wrists and elbows, as the lapel will not reach the carotid arteries. The correct grip is high on the collarbone.
    • Pulling with arms only: This engages smaller muscle groups and leads to fatigue, while also risking elbow hyperextension if the opponent resists strongly. Instead, use hip extension and thoracic arching to generate power.
    • Not creating an angle: Remaining square with the opponent allows them to maintain a strong base and makes the choke ineffective, while also increasing the risk of a shoulder or neck injury if they bridge forcefully.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice securing the lapel grips and closing your guard without a partner. (20 reps)
    2. Solo drill: Practice the hip lift and back arch motion with correct grips. (20 reps)
    3. Partner drill (0% resistance): Partner lies on their back, you establish closed guard and practice the choke mechanics slowly. (10 reps each side)
    4. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner actively tries to prevent grips but doesn't resist the choke itself. (10 reps each side)
    5. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner attempts to break grips and create space, but not full escape. (10 reps each side)
    6. Live rolling (75% resistance): Attempt the choke during rolling, focusing on correct mechanics. (5 minutes)
    7. Live rolling (90-100% resistance): Integrate the choke into your regular rolling sessions. (10 minutes)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent has a loose posture in your closed guard.
    • When they attempt to posture up, creating an opening for your grips.
    • When they are trying to stack you, allowing you to catch the lapels.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Elbow escape: If the choke is being applied, the defender can attempt to bring their choking-arm elbow to their own hip, creating space to relieve pressure.
    • Hip escape: The defender can try to hip escape away from the choking arm, creating distance and breaking the choke's structure.
    • Grip break: The defender can reach across their body and attempt to peel the choking grips off their lapels.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Cross Collar Choke from closed guard?

    Your wrist likely hurts because you are gripping the lapel with your fingers extended and trying to pull directly with your forearm muscles. To fix this, ensure your thumbs are inside the lapel, creating a stronger fist grip. Focus on driving your elbows down and back, using your lats and back muscles, and arching your spine to generate pressure, not just your wrists.

    Q: How do I Cross Collar Choke a much bigger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, grip strength and proper leverage are paraMount. Focus on getting deep, thumb-in grips on their highest lapel points. Instead of trying to muscle it, use your hip extension to lift your pelvis off the mat and create an angle. Your weight transfer should be towards your left side, driving your right shoulder into their left, which helps to break their posture and secure the choke.

    Q: When is the best time to set up the Cross Collar Choke in BJJ?

    The ideal time to set up the Cross Collar Choke is when your opponent is trying to posture up from your closed guard. As they extend their spine to create space, their neck becomes exposed. This is your opportunity to quickly secure the deep lapel grips. Also, if they attempt a stack pass, you can use that upward pressure to help you catch the lapels and initiate the choke.

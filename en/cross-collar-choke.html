Cross Collar Choke: A Biomechanically Precise BJJ Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

This choke is typically applied from the Closed Guard position, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain via the carotid arteries.

Many white belts struggle because they focus on brute force rather than precise leverage and connection.

The key mechanical insight is using your hips to create space and your arms to pinch the opponent's neck, generating a powerful, controlled choke.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From closed guard, secure a "Standard Lapel Grip" with your right hand on the opponent's left lapel, thumb inside, fingers outside, approximately at the sternal notch level.
    2. With your left hand, secure a "Crossed Lapel Grip" on the opponent's right lapel, thumb inside, fingers outside, also at the sternal notch level.
    3. Bring your hips forward slightly, creating a small gap between your pelvis and the opponent's hips, while maintaining a tight leg lock.
    4. Arch your lower back and simultaneously pull both lapels towards the center of your chest, pinching your elbows inwards.
    5. Turn your wrists inwards, away from the opponent's neck, as if trying to unscrew jars, applying pressure directly to the carotid arteries.
    6. Keep your head pressed firmly against the opponent's shoulder, preventing them from posturing up or creating space.
    7. Maintain a stable base by keeping your feet locked, ensuring your opponent cannot easily break your guard or escape the pressure.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying excessive upward force on the lapels with straight arms: This can strain your shoulder joint's rotator cuff by hyperextending the humerus, leading to tears. Instead, focus on pinching your elbows inwards and drawing the lapels towards your chest.
    • Tensing your neck and shoulders unnecessarily: This creates an opening for the opponent to create space and can lead to neck strain or whiplash if they resist with head movement. Relax your upper body and focus the pressure through your arms and hips.
    • Allowing the opponent to create significant space between your hips and theirs: This compromises your ability to generate leverage and can lead to your guard being passed or the choke being ineffective. Maintain a tight, connected closed guard with your legs.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Drilling (0% resistance): Practice securing the grips and performing the hip movement and arm pinch motion without an opponent. (20 reps)
    2. Partnered Drilling - Grip Focus (25% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice securing the grips and making the initial hip movement. Partner offers minimal resistance to grip breaks. (15 reps)
    3. Partnered Drilling - Arm Pinch and Hip Drive (50% resistance): Focus on the arm pinch and hip drive sequence. Partner allows the movement but resists the choke slightly. (15 reps)
    4. Partnered Drilling - Full Choke Execution (75% resistance): Execute the full Cross Collar Choke from closed guard. Partner provides moderate resistance, allowing you to feel the choke develop. (10 reps)
    5. Flow Rolling - Guard Retention with Choke Attempts (90% resistance): Integrate the Cross Collar Choke into flow rolling. Attempt the choke when opportunities arise, but prioritize guard retention. (5 minutes)
    6. Live Rolling (100% resistance): Attempt the Cross Collar Choke during live rolling. Focus on correct mechanics over immediate submission. (3 rounds)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent's posture is broken and they are within your closed guard.
    • When the opponent attempts to posture up, creating an opening for your grips.
    • When you have secured a strong grip on both lapels and feel you can control their posture.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • "Elbow Escape to Break Grip": When the opponent attempts the choke, immediately drive your elbow into their hip, creating space to break their grip on your lapel. Then, focus on clearing their legs.
    • "Head and Arm Control to Posture Up": If the opponent has a weak grip, use your arms to frame against their shoulders and neck, then drive your hips down and posture up to break their guard.
    • "Spinning Behind": If the opponent's grips are not fully committed, you can attempt to spin your hips to the side and escape the guard by driving your weight forward.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Cross Collar Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you are trying to generate choking pressure by bending your wrists outwards, away from your body. This strains the wrist extensors. Instead, keep your wrists straight and perpendicular to your forearms, and focus on pinching your elbows inwards and driving your hips forward to create the choking pressure. This utilizes the stronger muscles of your shoulders and back.

    Q: How can I effectively apply the Cross Collar Choke against a much larger and stronger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, brute force is your enemy. Focus on precision. Ensure your leg lock is tight to control their posture, preventing them from leaning forward. Use your hips to create a small pocket of space, then drive your elbows inwards and slightly upwards. The key is to maintain a tight connection and use your body weight to augment the pinching action of your arms, rather than trying to overpower them.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to a Cross Collar Choke from my closed guard, and what if my opponent defends by tucking their chin?

    The ideal time is when the opponent's posture is broken, and they are leaning into you, or when they attempt to posture up, creating an opening. If they tuck their chin, it makes the direct carotid choke harder. In this scenario, you can transition to an "Armbar from Guard" by isolating an arm, or use the lapel grips to "Sweep" them by using the lapels to control their balance and drive your hips.

Cross Collar Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

This choke is initiated from Closed Guard, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain via the carotid arteries. White belts often struggle due to a lack of control and improper weight distribution, leading to ineffective attempts. The key mechanical insight is using your hips to create leverage and your arms to drive the choking hands into the neck, not just pulling.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish **Closed Guard**: Sit up, bring knees to chest, feet locked behind opponent's thighs. Maintain a strong base by keeping your hips low.
    2. Secure **Right Collar Grip**: Reach across your body with your left hand and grip the fabric of the opponent's right lapel, thumb inside the collar.
    3. Secure **Left Collar Grip**: Reach across your body with your right hand and grip the fabric of the opponent's left lapel, thumb inside the collar. Ensure equal depth and pressure on both lapels.
    4. Adjust **Hip Angle**: Tilt your hips slightly to your left, creating a subtle angle. This allows your right arm to drive deeper into the opponent's neck.
    5. **Drive Opponent's Head Down**: Using your left arm's grip, pull the opponent's head down towards your chest, compressing the space.
    6. **Execute the Squeeze**: With your right hand's grip, drive your elbow down and slightly back. Simultaneously, pull your left elbow towards your own left hip, creating a scissor-like action.
    7. **Maintain Pressure**: Keep your knees tight and your hips engaged to prevent the opponent from posturing up or escaping. Your chest should be pressed against their chest.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • **Incorrect Grip Depth**: Gripping too shallowly on the lapels allows the opponent to easily pull their head out. This can lead to shoulder strain from overreaching.
    • **Correct Action**: Ensure your thumb is inside the collar for a deep, secure grip, and the fabric is cinched.
    • **Jerking Motions**: Yanking at the lapels with straight arms without hip engagement. This is ineffective and can cause bicep or elbow tendonitis.
    • **Correct Action**: Use your hips to drive the choke, integrating your body's power.
    • **Allowing Opponent to Posture**: Not keeping your knees tight and hips engaged, allowing the opponent to straighten their back. This dissipates the choke's pressure and can lead to neck strain for both parties.
    • **Correct Action**: Maintain a tight closed guard with active hip pressure.

    Drill Progressions

    1. **Solo Grip Practice**: From a seated position, practice reaching for and securing deep collar grips on your own gi. (20 reps)
    2. **Guard Closure Drill**: Practice transitioning from a slightly open guard to a tight closed guard, focusing on locking your feet. (20 reps)
    3. **Static Grip & Squeeze Drill**: With a partner (0% resistance), practice establishing the grips and performing the squeezing motion without any movement. (10 reps)
    4. **Controlled Choke Drill**: With a partner (25% resistance), practice the full choke sequence, allowing them to gently resist your initial setup. Focus on hip movement. (10 reps)
    5. **Timed Choke Drill**: With a partner (50% resistance), attempt the choke for 10 seconds. If unsuccessful, reset. Focus on maintaining guard integrity. (5 reps)
    6. **Live Rolling Application**: Attempt the choke in live rolling (75-90% resistance), prioritizing correct mechanics over immediate success. (5 minutes)

    When to Use & Counters

    • **WHEN TO ATTEMPT**:
    • When the opponent tries to posture up from your closed guard, creating space you can exploit.
    • When the opponent is turtled or trying to escape your closed guard, presenting their neck.
    • When you have established a strong closed guard and can control their posture and prevent them from creating frames.
    • **PRIMARY COUNTERS**:
    • **Defending the Grip**: If the opponent's grips are shallow, they are not effective. Try to peel their hands off the collar, or use your hands to push their choking arm away.
    • **Posturing Up**: If the opponent attempts the choke, immediately drive your hips down and try to posture up, breaking their guard and relieving pressure on your neck.
    • **Head Escape**: As the choke is being applied, turn your head slightly away from the choking arm and try to create space with your chin.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Cross Collar Choke?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you rely solely on your hands to generate force, leading to hyperextension or twisting. The correct biomechanics involve using your hips to drive the choke, with your arms acting as levers. Focus on squeezing with your forearms and driving your elbows, rather than just pulling with your wrists.

    Q: How can I effectively Cross Collar Choke a much larger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paraMount. Focus on creating a strong angle with your hips and using your entire body weight. Ensure your collar grips are deep and secure. Drive their head down aggressively with one arm while simultaneously using the other arm to drive their shoulder down and create a crushing pressure.

    Q: Is the Cross Collar Choke a fast submission, and should I panic if it doesn't work immediately?

    The Cross Collar Choke can be fast if applied correctly with proper leverage and pressure. However, it's not always immediate, especially against experienced opponents. Panicking leads to poor mechanics and wasted energy. If the choke is not working, focus on maintaining your guard, resetting your grips, and adjusting your body position for a better angle.

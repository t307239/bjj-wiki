This fundamental choke is primarily applied from the Closed Guard position. It aims to restrict blood flow to the brain via the carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle by relying on raw strength rather than leverage and precise body positioning. This leads to ineffective chokes and potential injury.

The key insight is using your hips and shoulder to create a fulcrum, compressing the arteries against the opponent's neck. This is not a pull; it's a controlled squeeze.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From closed guard, establish two high collar grips. Your right hand grips the opponent's left lapel, and your left hand grips their right lapel. Fingers should be inside the collar.
    2. Ensure your knuckles are facing upwards, and your thumbs are outside the collar material for a secure grip. Your elbows should be tucked in close to your body.
    3. Lift your hips slightly off the mat, bringing your knees towards your chest to maintain a tight guard. This creates a stable base and disconnects their hips from yours.
    4. Rotate your shoulders inward, creating tension on the lapels. Your right shoulder moves towards their left side, and your left shoulder moves towards their right side.
    5. Simultaneously, drive your hips forward and slightly upward. This motion acts as the primary force generator, compressing the lapels.
    6. Walk your feet up your opponent's back or sides if needed to deepen the pressure and prevent them from posturing up. Maintain a strong hip connection.
    7. As you drive your hips forward, slightly arch your back and tuck your chin. This amplifies the pressure by elongating their neck and tightening the choke.
    8. The choke is applied when you feel them tap or when their breathing becomes significantly restricted. Do not hold past the tap.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying excessive upward pulling force with your arms: This can strain your biceps and shoulders. The primary force comes from hip drive, not arm strength. Correct by focusing on driving your hips forward and using your arms only to maintain grip and angle.
    • Gripping too low on the collar: This reduces the leverage and effectiveness of the choke, often leading to a weak application. The correct grip is high on the collar, near the sternum. Correct by ensuring your knuckles are near their clavicle.
    • Allowing the opponent to posture up by not maintaining a tight guard: This compromises your base and the effectiveness of the choke, putting strain on your hips and lower back. Maintain a strong hip connection and keep your knees tight to their chest. Correct by actively squeezing your knees and lifting your hips.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the grip establishment and hip drive motion without a partner. Focus on the feeling of creating pressure. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): With a compliant partner in your guard, practice establishing grips and performing the hip drive motion, stopping before full pressure. (25 reps)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Your partner offers minimal resistance to your hip drive, allowing you to feel the mechanics of the choke. (25 reps)
    4. Closed guard control drill: Focus on maintaining tight closed guard and establishing grips. Partner tries to break guard. You practice resetting grips and hip drive. (5 minutes)
    5. Submission drilling: Execute the choke with light resistance from your partner, focusing on the timing of the hip drive and shoulder rotation. Partner taps early. (10 reps)
    6. Live rolling (controlled): Attempt the choke during controlled rolling sessions, prioritizing correct mechanics over submission. If the opportunity arises, go for it. (5 rounds, 50% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up slightly in your closed guard, creating space for your arms to enter the collar.
    • When your opponent is trying to stack you, but you maintain a tight guard and can control their posture.
    • After successfully sweeping or transitioning to guard and your opponent is still trying to regain their base.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Posture Up and Grip Break: The opponent straightens their back, creating space, and uses their hands to pry your collar grips open. Focus on maintaining head control and preventing posture.
    • Elbow Escape: The opponent tucks their elbows tightly to their body, making it difficult to establish deep collar grips. Work to create space with your hips and bait them into extending their arms.
    • Shoulder Pressure and Hip Escape: The opponent drives their shoulder into your chest, attempting to break your guard and relieve pressure on the neck. Use your legs to create frames and your hips to shrimp away.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Cross Collar Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often stems from gripping too tightly with your fingers, overextending your wrist, or trying to pull the lapels with your arms alone. Ensure your grips are firm but not tense, and your wrists are in a neutral position. The primary force should come from driving your hips forward, not yanking with your arms.

    Q: How can I effectively apply the Cross Collar Choke against a bigger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paraMount. Focus on a high, tight guard to minimize their posture and hip mobility. Drive your hips forward with maximum force, using your entire body's weight transfer. Elevate your hips to create a fulcrum, and ensure your collar grips are deep and secure to maximize compression.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate the Cross Collar Choke, and how do I avoid getting my guard passed while setting it up?

    Initiate the Cross Collar Choke when your opponent attempts to posture up or stack you, creating an opening. To avoid guard passes, maintain constant pressure with your knees and hips. If they attempt to break your grips, immediately transition to a different attack or control position, such as an Armbar or hip bump sweep.

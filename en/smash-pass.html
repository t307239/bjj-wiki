The Smash Pass is a guard passing technique initiated from your knees or standing, aiming to break your opponent's Closed Guard and establish side control.

White belts often struggle by over-relying on brute strength and failing to understand pressure application and hip mechanics.

The core insight is using your shoulder and hip to drive *down* and *across*, collapsing the guard rather than trying to force it open.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip opponent's pants at the ankle (Shin-to-Ankle grip) with your hands, thumbs inside.
    2. Place your knee directly behind your opponent's hip on the same side as your ankle grip, creating a strong base.
    3. Drive your chest towards their hip, keeping your back straight and head up, initiating a forward lean.
    4. Simultaneously, begin to 'smash' your shoulder into their hip, perpendicular to their body's long axis.
    5. Transfer your weight forward, driving your shoulder and chest into their hip and thigh, forcing them to flatten.
    6. As their guard breaks, step your opposite leg (the one not by their hip) forward, clearing their leg.
    7. Secure your opponent's far hip with your free hand (Hip grip) and drive your chest into their torso to establish side control.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Knee Hyperextension: Extending your knee directly under your opponent's weight when they bridge can cause anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) tears. Maintain a slight bend in your knee and ensure it tracks over your toes.
    • Lumbar Spine Compression: Rounding your back and driving forward with a flexed spine can lead to disc herniation. Keep your back straight, engage your core, and drive with your hips.
    • Shoulder Impingement: Forcing your shoulder directly into an opponent's hip without proper alignment can cause rotator cuff impingement. Ensure your shoulder is driving *down* and *across* their hip, not directly into it.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Mobility Drill: Practice driving your shoulder into a padded object (like a heavy bag) while keeping your back straight and hips angled. 20 reps per side.
    2. Passive Guard Break Drill: Your partner lies on their back. You execute the Smash Pass grips and pressure without resistance, focusing on the hip drive and weight transfer. 5 reps per side.
    3. Light Resistance Guard Break: Partner offers minimal resistance (e.g., just holding your arms). Focus on clearing their leg and establishing side control. 5 reps per side.
    4. Controlled Pass with Hip Grip: Partner offers moderate resistance. Focus on securing the far hip grip and driving to side control. 5 reps per side.
    5. Scenario Drilling (e.g., from Closed Guard): Partner attempts to keep guard closed. You work to break it and pass, with partner offering 50% resistance to retention. 5 reps per side.
    6. Live Rolling (Controlled): Implement the Smash Pass during rolling, focusing on correct mechanics, not just success. 2-minute rounds, 100% effort on mechanics, 75% on outcome.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent has their knees tightly locked in closed guard.
    • When your opponent attempts a weak or broken open guard.
    • When your opponent tries to 'sit up' and create space from guard.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Retention: Maintain a strong knee shield and hip escape to prevent the initial shoulder pressure from penetrating.
    • Leg Pummeling: Actively pummel your legs to re-establish guard or create space, preventing the leg clear.
    • Hip Escape and Re-Guard: If the guard is broken, immediately hip escape and attempt to re-establish guard (e.g., butterfly guard or half guard).

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Smash Pass?

    Wrist pain often stems from gripping too tightly with an extended wrist or not using your forearm and bicep to absorb pressure. Ensure your grip is firm but relaxed, and your wrist remains neutral. Drive with your shoulder and chest, not by pulling with your arms alone.

    Q: How do I Smash Pass against a much bigger opponent with a strong closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on precise angles and leverage. Instead of a direct forward drive, slightly angle your body to attack their hip from the side. Use your shoulder to drive *down* into their hip and thigh, collapsing their base before attempting to clear the leg.

    Q: I keep getting triangle choked when I try to Smash Pass. What am I doing wrong?

    Triangle Chokes often occur when your head gets too close to their centerline and you don't maintain head control. Keep your head up and slightly turned away from the direction you're passing. Drive your shoulder into their hip to keep them flat, preventing them from creating the necessary angle for the triangle.

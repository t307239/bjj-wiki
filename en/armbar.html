Armbar (Juji-Gatame)

Also called: arm bar, juji-gatame, cross armlock, chave de braço

An armbar straightens the opponent's elbow against your hips while your legs pin their shoulder and head. This guide covers the armbar from closed guard.

Position: closed guardBelt: white and upGi and No-GiRisk: high (elbow)

    When to use it

    Use it from closed guard when the opponent leaves one hand on your chest or belly and their posture is broken forward.

    Steps (from closed guard)

    1. Isolate one arm. Hold their wrist (or sleeve in the Gi) on your chest and cup the back of the same elbow with your other hand. Pull them forward with your legs so they cannot sit back.
    2. Cut the angle. Put the foot on the other side on their hip and push to turn your body sideways to them. Your head moves away from the trapped arm and your hips come under their shoulder.
    3. Climb high. Keep the knee on the trapped-arm side tight in their armpit, then swing that same leg over their face. Squeeze your knees together so there is no gap around the arm.
    4. Finish slowly. Keep their thumb pointing up and their wrist on your chest. Lift your hips upward a little at a time; the elbow bends the wrong way over your hips, so stop the moment they tap.

    Common mistakes

    • Space between your hips and their shoulder. They pull the elbow out through the gap. Slide your hips in until your crotch touches their armpit.
    • Knees apart or leg not over the face. They lift their head, posture up and stack you. Pinch your knees and press the calf down on their face.

    Their counters and your next move

    • They stack you (drive their weight onto your shoulders): turn further sideways, or switch to a triangle by bringing the leg over their face down behind their neck.
    • They rotate the thumb down (hitchhiker escape): follow the turn and roll to the omoplata on the same arm.
    • They pull the arm free: keep the leg on their back, catch the other arm, and attack the triangle.
    • They lock their hands together: keep the position tight and break the grip one hand at a time; do not jerk the arm.

    Rules

    Legal at every belt in IBJJF adult divisions, Gi and No-Gi. A white belt may not start it by jumping to guard on a standing opponent. See the IBJJF rules for kids and juvenile divisions.

    ⚠️ Safety

    In training, lift your hips slowly and let go at the first tap or when your partner says stop. If you are stacked and cannot move, do not force the finish. As the defender, tap before your elbow is fully straight; do not try to spin out of a finished armbar.

    FAQ

    Q: What is the difference between an armbar in Gi and No-Gi?

    In the Gi you can hold the sleeve, so the arm is easier to keep on your chest. In No-Gi you hold the wrist with both hands and squeeze your knees harder, because sweat lets the arm slide out.

    Q: Is the armbar safe for white belts to practice?

    Yes, if both partners follow two rules: the attacker raises the hips slowly and lets go at the first tap, and the defender taps before the elbow is fully straight.

    Q: How does an armbar differ from a kimura?

    The armbar straightens the elbow against your hips. The kimura keeps the arm bent and rotates the shoulder with a figure-four grip. See the full comparison in Armbar vs Kimura.

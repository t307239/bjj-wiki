    Common Mistakes in Advanced Game Plan

    Rushing the Setup

    Attempting to finish before proper mechanics are in place results in failed attempts and positional loss. Prioritize position before submission.

    Using Strength Over Technique

    Muscling through setups creates bad habits and fails against stronger or more skilled opponents. Focus on leverage and angles.

    Skipping Drilling

    Techniques only become available in live rolling after extensive drilling. Regular repetition builds the muscle memory needed for execution under pressure.

    Ignoring Defensive Reactions

    Every technique has common counters. Learn the most frequent defensive reactions and have follow-up attacks ready.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How long does it take to learn Advanced Game Plan?

    Most practitioners develop functional competency with Advanced Game Plan within 3–6 months of consistent drilling. Mastery — the ability to execute reliably in live rolling against resisting opponents — typically takes 1–2 years.

    Is Advanced Game Plan effective for beginners?

    Yes. Advanced Game Plan is part of the core BJJ curriculum and taught at all belt levels. Beginners should focus on the fundamental mechanics and concepts before refining advanced entries.

    How often should I drill Advanced Game Plan?

    3–5 times per week is ideal for rapid skill acquisition. Even 10 focused repetitions per session compounds over time — consistency matters more than volume.

    What positions connect to Advanced Game Plan?

    BJJ is a linked system. Advanced Game Plan flows naturally to and from related positions. Study transitions in both directions to build a complete positional game.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: When I try to set up the Game Plan for Advanced from guard, why does my opponent always seem to be able to post their arm and prevent me from getting the angle?

    This often happens when you're not effectively using your hips to create space. Instead of just pulling your opponent's arm, focus on hip escaping to the side, creating a slight gap, and then driving your shoulder into their bicep to break their posture and secure the angle.

    Q: I feel like I'm not generating enough power to finish the Game Plan for Advanced. What are the key leverage points I should be focusing on with my body?

    The power in the Game Plan for Advanced comes from driving your hips forward and upward, not just pulling with your arms. Focus on using your core to generate the hip thrust, while simultaneously using your shoulder to create a fulcrum against their neck and your opposite arm to control their posture by pulling their head towards your chest.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How do I adapt my game plan when my opponent isn't following my expected strategies?

    The core of an advanced game plan is adaptability. If your opponent deviates, immediately assess their new position and identify their immediate threats and vulnerabilities. Then, quickly pivot to a counter-strategy that exploits their current situation, rather than trying to force your original plan.

    What are the key mental components of an advanced BJJ game plan?

    Beyond technical execution, advanced game planning involves intense mental focus, pattern recognition, and strategic foresight. This includes anticipating your opponent's next moves, understanding your own strengths and weaknesses under pressure, and maintaining composure to make optimal decisions even when fatigued or in a difficult position.

    How can I effectively scout and analyze my opponent's BJJ game before a match?

    Scouting involves observing their preferred positions, common submission attacks, defensive habits, and transition tendencies. Look for patterns in their guard retention, passing styles, and how they react to pressure. This information allows you to build a proactive game plan tailored to exploit their weaknesses and neutralize their strengths.

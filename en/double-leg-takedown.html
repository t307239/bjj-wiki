Mastering the Double Leg Takedown in BJJ

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Blue Belt Guide

This technique starts from a standing position, typically initiated when your opponent is within grappling range. Its goal is to take your opponent to the mat and establish a dominant top position.

White belts often fail due to a lack of commitment, improper weight distribution, and fear of falling. They might lunge with their head too high or fail to secure a solid base.

The key mechanical insight is driving through your opponent with your hips low and forward, using your legs to elevate and your head as a wedge to control their balance.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Stance: Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent. Maintain a balanced base with your weight centered.
    2. Initiation: From a distance, take one step forward with your lead leg (e.g., left leg) and simultaneously shoot your hips back and down, lowering your level. Your lead knee should be slightly in front of your lead foot, with your shin vertical.
    3. Grip: As your lead leg shoots, secure a "double leg grip" by reaching around both of your opponent's thighs with your arms. Your palms should be on the back of their thighs, just above the knee crease.
    4. Head Position: Drive your head into your opponent's torso, typically on their hip or sternum, on the same side as your lead leg. Your head should be driving forward, not down.
    5. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight from your back leg onto your lead leg, creating forward momentum. Your hips should be driving forward and slightly under your opponent's center of gravity.
    6. Leg Drive: Explode upward and forward using your rear leg (e.g., right leg). Extend your hips and drive your opponent forward and off balance.
    7. Completion: As you drive, bring your rear leg forward to step through and secure your position on top. Your goal is to land in a dominant position like side control or mount, with your opponent on their back.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Head Position: Lunging with your head up and forward towards your opponent's face or chest can lead to neck strain or whiplash. The correct movement is driving your head into their torso, acting as a wedge to control their balance.
    • Knee Hyperextension: Shooting too low and driving with excessive force without proper hip control can cause your lead knee to buckle or hyperextend, potentially damaging the ACL or meniscus. The correct movement involves maintaining a slight bend in the lead knee and driving with your hips.
    • Lack of Hip Drive: Relying solely on arm strength to lift your opponent or failing to drive your hips forward leaves you vulnerable to being swept or taken down yourself. This can lead to lower back strain. The correct movement emphasizes a powerful hip drive to unbalance your opponent.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Shadow Drilling (0%): Practice the footwork and hip movement without a partner. Focus on the low level and hip drive. (20 reps)
    2. Partner Placement (0%): Have a partner stand passively. Practice shooting your legs and securing the double leg grip around their thighs. Focus on head position and grip. (20 reps)
    3. Controlled Entry (25%): With a passive partner, practice the full motion of shooting, gripping, and driving. The partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the mechanics. (15 reps)
    4. Light Resistance (50%): Your partner offers light resistance by leaning back slightly. Focus on driving through their resistance and maintaining your base. (15 reps)
    5. Active Defense (75%): Your partner actively tries to sprawl or defend. Focus on adjusting your level and grip to secure the takedown. (10 reps)
    6. Live Rolling (90-100%): Integrate the double leg takedown into live rolling scenarios. Focus on timing and commitment, accepting that some attempts will fail.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent over-commits to a forward movement, creating an opening.
    • When your opponent is posturing up from your guard, giving you a chance to shoot.
    • During transitions, when your opponent is momentarily off-balance or not fully squared up.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Sprawl: When you feel the opponent shoot, explosively drop your hips to the mat, extending your legs back. This prevents them from securing the leg grip and drives their head away.
    • Whizzer (Overhook): As the opponent shoots, secure an overhook (whizzer) on their lead arm. This allows you to control their upper body and prevent them from driving forward, often enabling you to circle out or off-balance them.
    • Circle Out: If the opponent secures the grip but fails to drive effectively, use your hips to circle away from their driving force, breaking their connection and creating space to re-establish your base.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do a Double Leg Takedown in BJJ?

    Your knee pain likely stems from hyperextension or twisting during the shot. When you shoot, your lead knee should maintain a slight bend, and your hips must drive forward and under the opponent, not just down. Ensure your shin stays relatively vertical and avoid letting your knee go past your toes or twist inwards.

    Q: How can I do a Double Leg Takedown against a much bigger BJJ opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are crucial. Focus on getting your hips lower than theirs before you shoot, making their size less of an advantage. Secure a deep grip around their thighs and drive forward with your hips, using their own forward momentum against them. A strong head drive into their torso is essential to control their balance.

    Q: I keep getting my Double Leg Takedown stuffed, what am I doing wrong?

    Your double leg takedown is likely being stuffed because you're telegraphing your move, shooting too high, or not driving with your hips. Ensure your shot is explosive and low. Focus on driving your hips forward and under your opponent's center of gravity, while keeping your head engaged into their torso. This combination creates an unbalancing force they cannot easily resist.

This sweep initiates from Closed Guard, aiming to invert your opponent and establish a superior position like mount or side control.

White belts often falter by relying on brute strength or poor hip positioning, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key insight is using your opponent's forward momentum against them by creating a strong lever with your legs and hips.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip: Secure a deep collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip on their left arm with your left hand.
    2. Leg Placement: Place your left foot on their right hip crease, knee pointing towards the ceiling.
    3. Hip Angle: Simultaneously, hook your right foot behind their left knee joint, angling your hips to 45 degrees away from them.
    4. Weight Transfer: Begin to elevate your hips off the mat, driving your left foot into their hip and pulling their left arm across your body.
    5. Leverage Creation: As your hips rise, your right leg acts as a fulcrum, using the hook behind their knee to create a strong lever.
    6. Rotation: Simultaneously, pull their collar grip down and towards your left shoulder, initiating a rotational force.
    7. Completion: Drive your hips upwards and to the right, swinging your right leg over their head as they fall forward, landing in mount or side control.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Knee Extension: Extending your knee forcefully outwards while attempting to hook behind their knee can hyperextend the knee joint, potentially damaging the ACL or PCL.
    • Correct Movement: Maintain a slightly bent knee when hooking, and focus on the hip rotation rather than just leg extension.
    • Forward Lumbar Flexion: Rounding your lower back and collapsing your chest towards your knees while trying to lift your hips can strain the lumbar spine and intervertebral discs.
    • Correct Movement: Keep your chest up and focus on driving your hips vertically using your glutes and core, maintaining a neutral spine.
    • Over-Reliance on Arm Pulling: Pulling solely with your arms without hip elevation and leg drive leads to poor leverage and can strain your shoulder or elbow joints.
    • Correct Movement: Integrate a powerful hip lift and leg push/pull with a controlled arm pull to generate sweeping momentum.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Elevation & Leg Hook Drill: Practice lifting your hips and hooking your leg behind an imaginary knee. 10 reps per side.
    2. Partnered Hip Elevation & Leg Hook (No Resistance): With a partner lying on their back, practice the hip elevation and leg hook motion. 10 reps per side.
    3. Partnered Grip & Hip Elevation (Light Resistance): Partner provides light collar and sleeve grips. You practice hip elevation and initial leg drive. 5 reps per side.
    4. Full Sweep Motion (Light Resistance): Partner cooperates by slightly leaning in. Execute the full sweep motion with controlled speed. 5 reps per side.
    5. Overhead Sweep Against Static Opponent: Partner stands still, you execute the sweep. 5 reps per side.
    6. Overhead Sweep Against Cooperative Partner: Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the timing and weight transfer. 5 reps per side.
    7. Live Rolling (Controlled): Attempt the Overhead Sweep in rolling situations with a focus on technique, not outcome. 2-minute rounds, 50% resistance.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up high in your closed guard, creating space.
    • When your opponent is leaning heavily forward, attempting to pass your guard.
    • When you have established a strong collar and sleeve grip, preventing easy posture breakdown.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Pass Attempt: If your opponent attempts a knee-slice or toreando pass while you're setting up, immediately transition to a leg drag or maintain tight guard to prevent the pass.
    • Stacking Defense: If your opponent tries to stack you, widen your base, extend your legs, and push their hips away to break the stack before continuing the sweep.
    • Re-Grip Adjustment: If your opponent defends by posturing up and breaking grips, quickly secure new collar and sleeve grips or transition to an open guard sweep.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try the Overhead Sweep from closed guard?

    This often happens when you round your lower back (lumbar flexion) to try and lift your hips. Instead, keep your chest up and focus on driving your hips vertically using your glutes and core muscles. Think of it as pushing your hips towards the ceiling, not curling your torso.

    Q: How can I use the Overhead Sweep against a much larger and heavier opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paraMount. Focus on creating a very tight hook behind their knee and a strong pull on the collar. Ensure your hips are elevated significantly before initiating the rotation; a small hip lift won't generate enough force. Drive their weight forward into the sweep.

    Q: When is the best moment to attempt the Overhead Sweep, and what if my opponent is too low?

    The Overhead Sweep is most effective when your opponent is posturing up or leaning forward, creating an angle you can exploit. If they are too low, trying to force the sweep can lead to them passing your guard. In this scenario, adjust your grips, attempt to create space by pushing their hips away, and transition to a sweep that works better for a lower opponent, like a Scissor Sweep.

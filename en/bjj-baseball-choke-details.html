    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck feel strained and not quite get the full pressure when I try to apply the Baseball Choke from side control?

    This often happens when you're not properly isolating the opponent's head and shoulder. Ensure your bicep is deep on their neck, creating a fulcrum, and your forearm is pressing into their carotid artery, while your other hand pulls their shoulder towards your chest to prevent them from creating space.

    Q: How can I ensure a tight Baseball Choke against a much larger opponent who seems to have more strength to resist?

    Focus on leverage and body positioning over brute force. Drive your shoulder into their shoulder blade to control their posture, and use your hips to shrimp your base closer to their head, allowing you to sink the choke deeper and utilize your body weight for pressure.

    Q: What's the key adjustment to make the Baseball Choke finish faster and more effectively, instead of it feeling like a slow squeeze?

    The speed comes from a sharp, downward pull with your choking arm and simultaneously driving your chest into their head. This creates a shearing effect that cuts off blood flow rapidly, rather than just a steady, passive squeeze.

    More Questions

    How do I prevent my opponent from defending the baseball choke?

    Focus on maintaining tight control of your opponent's posture and head. Use your free arm to keep their head tucked, and your legs to control their hips to limit their ability to shrimp or turn away. A strong grip on their collar is also crucial for preventing escape.

    What are the common mistakes when applying the baseball choke?

    A common mistake is not getting a deep enough grip on the collar, which reduces the choking pressure. Another error is not using your body weight effectively to drive down and forward, making the choke reliant solely on arm strength. Ensure your hips are engaged and you're driving your shoulder into their neck.

    When is the baseball choke most effective?

    The baseball choke is most effective when your opponent is turtled or has their head down, making it difficult for them to defend. It can also be a good option from side control or mount when you can isolate one side of their neck and establish a dominant position. It's particularly useful when they are trying to escape by turning into you.

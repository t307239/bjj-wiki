Deep Half Guard: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide to BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

Deep Half Guard is a powerful position from which to attack or sweep, typically initiated when your opponent has passed your initial guard. It aims to create leverage and control their base from underneath.

White belts often struggle by presenting a flat surface or by pushing their hips away, negating the leverage needed for sweeps.

The key insight is to use your bottom leg to control their lead leg's ankle while your top leg creates a wedge under their hip, allowing you to lift and off-balance.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Opponent establishes a knee-on-belly or pressure pass, their lead leg is forward.
    2. You secure a "hook grip" on their lead ankle/shin with your bottom leg, foot facing their heel.
    3. Your top leg "wipes" across their hip, placing your hamstring just inside their hip crease, knee pointing slightly upwards.
    4. Your bottom arm secures a "collar grip" (lapel or neck) or "underhook" on their torso.
    5. Your top arm secures a "pant grip" on their lead leg, just above the knee.
    6. You drive your hips *towards* your opponent, tucking your chin and creating a strong posterior tilt of your pelvis.
    7. Initiate the sweep by extending your bottom leg, lifting their ankle, and simultaneously driving your hips upwards, rotating your body to face them.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly extending your bottom leg in a straight line while your opponent has heavy pressure: This can hyperextend your knee, damaging the ACL or PCL.
    • Correct movement: Keep a slight bend in your bottom knee and drive your hips forward first.
    • Trying to "power up" by arching your back: This strains the lumbar spine and offers no leverage.
    • Correct movement: Tilt your pelvis posteriorly and drive your hips upwards, not your chest.
    • Letting your top leg fall to the mat or stay too far away from the hip: This removes the crucial hip wedge and allows your opponent to pass easily.
    • Correct movement: Maintain constant contact and pressure with your top leg's hamstring against their hip.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg placement and hip drive motion without a partner. Focus on the feeling of the wedge and hip tilt. (10 reps per side)
    2. Partner drilling (0% resistance): Partner stands, you practice entering Deep Half Guard and performing the hip drive. Partner offers no resistance to the sweep. (5 reps per side)
    3. Partner drilling (25% resistance): Partner stands, you enter Deep Half Guard. Partner lightly resists the sweep by trying to maintain balance. (5 reps per side)
    4. Partner drilling (50% resistance): Partner is on their knees, you enter Deep Half Guard. Partner resists the sweep by trying to post their free leg or hip escape. (5 reps per side)
    5. Live rolling (75% resistance): Start from a neutral position where Deep Half Guard is a natural transition. Focus on establishing the position and attempting the sweep. (3 rounds)
    6. Live rolling (90-100% resistance): Integrate Deep Half Guard entry and sweeps into regular rolling. Aim to recognize opportunities and execute the technique under pressure. (5 rounds)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When an opponent aggressively passes your guard and their lead leg is within your reach.
    • When you are defending a knee-on-belly or side control and manage to get your bottom leg underneath their lead leg.
    • When transitioning from a failed sweep or submission attempt, and your opponent is in a forward-leaning posture.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • "Stack Pass" Defense: If your opponent attempts to stack you, immediately drive your hips *forward* and create a strong frame with your forearm on their chest, preventing them from collapsing your base.
    • "Leg Extension" Sweep: If your opponent tries to pull their trapped leg straight out, use your bottom leg to drive their ankle further away, creating space to shrimp your hips out and re-establish guard or transition to a sweep.
    • "Hip Escape and Re-Guard": If the sweep is not viable, use your top leg to push their hip away slightly, shrimp your hips out to create space, and bring your other leg back into a closed or open guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get into Deep Half Guard?

    Knee pain in Deep Half Guard typically stems from improper leg placement and excessive extension under pressure. If your bottom leg is too straight and your opponent is driving forward, your ACL is at risk of hyperextension. Ensure your bottom knee maintains a slight bend and prioritize driving your hips forward to create the wedge before initiating the sweep.

    Q: How do I use Deep Half Guard against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paraMount. Focus on securing a deep collar grip or underhook to control their posture. Ensure your top leg's hamstring is firmly wedged against their hip to prevent them from driving through. Instead of trying to lift them directly, use your hip drive and ankle control to off-balance them and create angles for sweeps, rather than relying on raw strength.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from Deep Half Guard to another position?

    The ideal time to transition from Deep Half Guard is when you feel your opponent's weight distribution shift and their base becomes unstable. If you've successfully established the hip wedge and ankle control, and they are leaning forward, this is your cue to initiate the sweep. If they successfully defend your sweep attempt and regain a stable base, it's often safer to disengage by shrimping out and re-establishing your guard.

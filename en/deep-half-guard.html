Deep Half Guard: The White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

Deep Half Guard is a position where your opponent is inside your legs, typically after you've failed a guard pass or are being pressured. It's a transitional guard designed to prevent immediate passes and create opportunities for sweeps or submissions.

White belts often struggle because they lack the foundational body awareness to maintain the correct angles and frames. They may rely on brute strength or simply get flattened out, leading to frustration and potential injury.

The key mechanical insight is creating a strong Underhook and using your shin as a wedge to prevent your opponent from flattening you, while simultaneously working to elevate their weight and off-balance them.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Opponent's weight is forward, you are on your back with one leg trapped between their legs. Secure a deep underhook with your arm closest to their hip, hooking your hand onto the back of their thigh or hip crease.
    2. Your free leg (the one not trapping their leg) should wrap around their closest leg, placing your foot on their hip or behind their knee to create a frame and prevent them from stepping out.
    3. Arch your back and drive your hips into their thigh, creating a wedge with your trapped leg's shin. This prevents them from driving their weight straight down.
    4. Your torso should be angled away from your opponent's hips, creating a 'C' shape with your body. This makes it harder for them to apply forward pressure.
    5. Use your underhooking arm to pull their hip towards you and push their knee away with your free leg's foot on their hip. This starts to create an off-balancing angle.
    6. Begin to elevate your hips by driving your feet into the mat and pushing your trapped leg's shin into their thigh. Simultaneously, use your underhook to pull their hip. This creates a lever.
    7. As their weight shifts, drive your hips up and forward, aiming to bring your trapped leg around to a more dominant position (e.g., transitioning to a sweep or a different guard).

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Knee Hyperextension: Trying to 'kick out' your trapped leg with excessive force when your opponent is postured up and driving forward. This can cause a hyperextension injury to the knee joint's posterior cruciate ligament (PCL).
    • Correct: Maintain the shin wedge and use hip elevation and underhook pull to create off-balancing angles, rather than outright kicking.
    • Ankle Sprains/Twists: Gripping too tightly with your foot on the opponent's hip when they are actively stepping their leg over yours, causing your ankle to twist inwards or outwards.
    • Correct: Use your foot on the hip as a frame and a point of pressure, not a vice-like grip. Focus on pushing and maintaining the angle.
    • Lower Back Strain: Arching your back excessively and trying to 'lift' the opponent with your arms alone, without proper hip drive and weight transfer.
    • Correct: Initiate the movement with a strong hip drive into their thigh, then use your arms to augment the off-balancing motion.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Elevation & Underhook Drill: Practice arching your back, driving hips up, and reaching for the underhook without a partner. (10 reps each side, 0% resistance).
    2. Static Frame & Wedge Drill: Have a partner stand still and gently apply forward pressure. Practice creating your shin wedge and free leg frame. Focus on maintaining the angle. (5 reps, 25% resistance).
    3. Underhook & Hip Pull Drill: With a partner gently trying to advance, focus solely on securing the deep underhook and pulling their hip in while maintaining your frames. (5 reps, 50% resistance).
    4. Controlled Hip Elevation & Sweep Attempt: With a partner providing light pressure, practice executing the hip elevation and off-balancing motion. Partner gently resists being swept. (5 reps, 75% resistance).
    5. Transition to Deep Half Guard & Sweep: Start from a position where you can enter Deep Half Guard. Partner attempts a pass, you enter Deep Half, and attempt a sweep. Partner offers minimal resistance to the sweep. (5 reps, 90% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling - Deep Half Guard Focus: Implement Deep Half Guard during live rolling. Focus on entering the position, maintaining your frames, and attempting sweeps or transitions when the opportunity arises. (3 x 5-minute rounds, 100% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent has achieved a strong cross-face and is driving their weight forward, flattening your guard.
    • When you have lost your grip on your opponent's legs and they are stepping their leg over your hip in a pass attempt.
    • When you are in a scrambled situation and find yourself on your back with one leg trapped between your opponent's legs.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The Knee-Slice Pass Defense: If your opponent attempts a knee-slice pass while you are in Deep Half Guard, focus on driving your hips into their knee and using your underhook to turn into them and attempt to regain guard or establish a better position.
    • The Stack Pass Defense: If your opponent attempts to 'stack' you by driving their weight straight down, use your shin wedge and hip drive to create space. Push into their knee with your trapped shin and use your underhook to create an angle to escape.
    • The Leg Drag Defense: If your opponent tries to leg drag your trapped leg, immediately drive your hips into their chest and use your free leg to push their hips away, creating space to either recover guard or transition to a different position.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get out of deep half guard?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are trying to 'kick out' or forcefully straighten your trapped leg against your opponent's forward pressure. This can strain ligaments like the PCL. Instead, focus on maintaining the shin wedge against their thigh and using hip elevation and your underhook to create off-balancing angles. This reduces direct stress on the knee joint.

    Q: How do I use Deep Half Guard against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a bigger opponent, your frames and angles become even more critical. Focus on creating a tight shin wedge with your trapped leg and use your free leg's foot on their hip to create a strong frame, preventing them from flattening you. Prioritize the deep underhook to control their hip and use your hip drive to off-balance their weight. Don't try to lift them; instead, use leverage to make them feel heavy and off-balance.

    Q: When is the best time to try and sweep from Deep Half Guard?

    The best time to attempt a sweep from Deep Half Guard is when you have successfully created an off-balancing angle. This typically occurs when you've established a strong underhook, driven your hips into their thigh creating a wedge, and can feel their weight shift forward or to the side. At this moment, a strong hip drive and pull with your underhook can lead to a sweep.

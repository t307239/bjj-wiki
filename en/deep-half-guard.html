Deep Half Guard: The White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

Deep Half Guard is a position where one leg is trapped between the opponent's legs, and your body is angled towards their hip, aiming to disrupt their base for sweeps or transitions.

White belts often struggle by staying too flat, allowing the opponent to easily stack them, or by reaching for grips without establishing a stable base.

The core mechanic is creating a strong, upward-angled frame with your body and using your trapped leg as a lever against the opponent's shin to off-balance them.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a compromised half guard, secure an underhook on the opponent's far leg, gripping their thigh with your forearm and hand. Your head should be on the outside of their hip line.
    2. Trap their closest leg by placing your shin across the front of their shin, with your knee pointed towards the ceiling. Your foot should be flexed.
    3. Drive your hips forward and upward, creating an acute angle between your torso and the mat, aiming your sternum towards their knee.
    4. Establish a strong frame with your non-trapped arm across their hip, preventing them from posturing up or stepping their leg over.
    5. Utilize your trapped leg to push against their shin. This creates a fulcrum, allowing you to begin elevating their leg and shifting their weight.
    6. Simultaneously, drive your hips into their trapped leg, using your underhook to pull their hip towards you and your frame to push their upper body away.
    7. As their weight shifts, begin to turn your hips and drive forward, aiming to bring your trapped leg free and transition to a better position or sweep.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly extending your knee joint when trapping their leg: This can lead to hyperextension of the knee and ACL/MCL tears if the opponent applies pressure or bridges incorrectly.
    • Arching your lower back excessively to create space: This puts immense strain on the lumbar spine, potentially causing disc compression or facet joint injury.
    • Gripping the opponent's pants too high on the thigh without a solid base: This often leads to a loss of your own base and a forward fall, risking head and neck impacts.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip angle and leg trap motion without a partner. Focus on the body's alignment and the feel of the leg trap. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner drill (static): Have a partner stand passively. Practice entering Deep Half Guard and establishing the initial grips and frames. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    3. Partner drill (slow push): Partner gently pushes forward. Practice maintaining your frames and hip angle, resisting the forward pressure. (10 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner drill (weight shift): Partner applies light weight and attempts to stack. Focus on your leg drive and hip lift to prevent being flattened. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Sweep attempts (controlled): Partner allows you to attempt a sweep from Deep Half Guard. Focus on the transition and follow-through. (5 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Integrate Deep Half Guard into your rolling. Focus on achieving the position and attempting one technique. (3 rounds, 90-100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent passes your guard and lands in a position where your legs are tangled, and they are on top of your thigh.
    • When you are being stacked in half guard and need to create space and disrupt their base.
    • When you have secured an underhook on their leg and are actively trying to invert or create an off-balancing opportunity.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: Opponent drives their weight directly down onto your hips and upper back, aiming to crush your frame and pin you.
    • Knee Slice Pass: Opponent brings their knee across your body, intending to pass your legs by stepping their knee to the mat on the opposite side of your trapped leg.
    • Hip Escape to Guard Recovery: You can often escape Deep Half Guard by performing a strong hip escape, creating space to bring your legs back together and re-establish a closed guard or open guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get into Deep Half Guard?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are either hyperextending it when trapping the opponent's leg, or you are allowing their shin to press directly against your kneecap. Ensure your knee is angled upwards and your foot is flexed, creating a stable frame. Avoid letting their shin dig into your joint.

    Q: How do I use Deep Half Guard against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paraMount. Focus on creating a strong upward-angled frame with your hips and using your trapped leg as a lever against their shin. Don't try to muscle them; instead, use their weight against them by driving your hips into their trapped leg and initiating a subtle, off-balancing rotation.

    Q: When should I transition from Deep Half Guard to something else?

    You should transition if your opponent successfully stacks you, negating your frames. Also, if you have secured the Deep Half Guard but cannot advance or sweep after a few seconds, it's often better to escape and reset to avoid being controlled or passed. Look for opportunities to invert or recover guard if the sweep is not immediate.

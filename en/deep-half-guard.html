Deep Half Guard is a position where you are on your back, controlling one of your opponent's legs with your legs, while your hips are significantly underneath theirs.

White belts often struggle with Deep Half Guard because they lack precise body control and rely on brute strength, leading to ineffective grips and poor posture.

The ONE key mechanical insight is creating a strong, leveraged "hook" with your bottom leg and using your top leg to "trap" your opponent's hip for control and subsequent attacks.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Start from a position where your opponent is postured up and inside your closed guard or has passed your legs to one side.
    2. Secure a "head and arm" control by reaching your arm across your opponent's body and gripping their far collar or shoulder.
    3. Simultaneously, bring your bottom leg (the one on the same side as the head and arm control) up and hook it deeply behind your opponent's knee.
    4. Position your hips so they are perpendicular to your opponent's body, aiming to get your pelvis underneath their center of gravity.
    5. Bring your top leg (the one on the opposite side of the head and arm control) to trap your opponent's hip by placing your shin across their hip bone.
    6. Create a "frame" with your top arm, pushing against your opponent's hip or thigh to maintain distance and prevent them from posturing up.
    7. Actively "pull" your opponent's head and shoulder towards you with your head and arm control while "pushing" their hip away with your trapped leg to create off-balancing leverage.
    8. Drive your hips upward, using your trapped leg as a fulcrum, to elevate your opponent and initiate sweeps or transitions.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Grabbing your opponent's foot/ankle and "pulling" them towards you: This can lead to a knee-bar or ankle lock by hyperextending your own knee or ankle joint.
    • Allowing your opponent to "stack" you by not creating a strong frame with your top arm: This can result in spinal compression injuries or neck strain from the pressure.
    • Trying to "sit up" forcefully from Deep Half Guard without proper hip connection: This can torque your lower back and cause strain or injury to the lumbar spine.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg hook and hip trap mechanics without a partner (10 reps each side).
    2. Partner drilling (0% resistance): Your partner stands passively while you practice entering Deep Half Guard and establishing grips (5 reps each side).
    3. Partner drilling (25% resistance): Your partner offers light resistance to your entry and hip movement (10 reps each side).
    4. Partner drilling (50% resistance): Your partner attempts to defend your entry and hip elevation, and you focus on maintaining the trap and hook (15 reps each side).
    5. Partner drilling (75% resistance): Your partner actively tries to pass your legs and escape the position, while you focus on sweeps and transitions (20 reps each side).
    6. Live rolling (90-100% resistance): Apply Deep Half Guard concepts in sparring, focusing on maintaining control and executing techniques, not just winning (5-minute rounds).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard to one side and their weight is distributed forward.
    • When your opponent is postured up and you have an opportunity to "underhook" their leg.
    • When you are in a scramble and find yourself on your back with one of your opponent's legs trapped.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • "Stack Pass" Defense: If your opponent stacks you, immediately drive your hips forward and use your trapped leg to "kick" their leg out, creating space to recover guard.
    • "Knee Slice" Defense: If your opponent attempts a knee slice pass, focus on keeping your hips heavy and using your top leg to "frame" their hip, preventing them from cutting through.
    • "Guard Recovery" from Deep Half: If your opponent escapes your hip trap, immediately focus on bringing your trapped leg back across your body to re-establish a closed or open guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to hook my opponent's leg in Deep Half Guard?

    Your knee might hurt if you are "pulling" your opponent's foot towards you instead of "hooking" behind their knee. The correct biomechanic is to place your shin across the back of their knee and use your hamstring to create the "hook." Avoid rotating your knee joint outward; keep your knee aligned with your hip.

    Q: How can I use Deep Half Guard against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paraMount. Focus on getting your hips underneath their center of gravity by driving your pelvis up. Use your top leg to "trap" their hip and prevent them from posturing. Your goal is to "unbalance" them by creating a fulcrum with your hooked leg and driving them forward, rather than trying to lift them directly.

    Q: Yes, Deep Half Guard is an excellent position for white belts to learn because it teaches fundamental concepts of body control, leverage, and hip movement. It's a position where you are less reliant on flexibility and more on understanding how to use your body's mechanics to create advantageous angles and off-balance your opponent.

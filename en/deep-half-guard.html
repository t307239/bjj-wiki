Deep Half Guard is a position where you are on your back, controlling one of your opponent's legs with your own leg, aiming to advance your position or attack.

Many white belts get stuck here because they try to force movements without understanding the underlying leverage and weight distribution.

The key is to create a strong, stable base and use your hips to generate power, not just your arms.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Starting from a bottom half-guard position, secure your opponent's lead leg by placing your left shin across their right shin and your right shin under their right thigh.
    2. Grip your opponent's right pant leg with your right hand, just above the knee, creating a strong anchor.
    3. Your left arm should frame against your opponent's right hip, preventing them from posturing up or driving forward.
    4. Drive your hips towards your opponent's right hip, creating a 45-degree angle between your torso and theirs.
    5. Maintain contact with your left knee on the mat, acting as a pivot point.
    6. As you begin to drive, subtly shift your weight onto your left shoulder, initiating a 'hip-up' motion.
    7. Use the pant grip to pull their leg closer to your body while simultaneously driving your hips forward and up, scooping their leg with your shin.
    8. This combined action aims to break their base and create an opportunity to sweep or transition.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly shrimping out: Trying to shrimp by pushing your hips straight back without maintaining leg control can lead to your opponent stepping over your leg, potentially tearing your ACL or MCL.
    • Overextending the arm: Grabbing the pants too low and pulling with your arm instead of your hips can strain your shoulder or elbow ligaments.
    • Allowing opponent to stack: Not maintaining the frame with your arm against the hip allows your opponent to 'stack' their weight onto you, compressing your spine and potentially causing disc issues.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip-driving and leg-scooping motion without a partner, focusing on the 45-degree hip angle and the frame. (10 reps each side)
    2. Static partner drill: Your partner lies flat on their back. You enter Deep Half Guard and practice the hip drive and leg scoop to achieve the correct angle. (5 reps each side)
    3. Passive partner drill (0% resistance): Your partner allows you to enter Deep Half Guard and offers minimal resistance as you practice the sweep mechanics. (10 reps each side)
    4. Light resistance drill (25% resistance): Your partner offers slight pressure, trying to prevent your hip drive, but does not actively defend. (10 reps each side)
    5. Controlled resistance drill (50% resistance): Your partner actively tries to maintain their base and prevent the sweep, allowing you to work on your timing and leverage. (15 reps each side)
    6. Live rolling (75-100% resistance): Integrate Deep Half Guard into sparring, focusing on recognizing opportunities and executing the technique with full intent. (5 minutes)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is posturing over you in your guard and has their weight distributed forward.
    • When you have successfully isolated one of your opponent's legs in a half-guard scenario.
    • When your opponent is trying to pass your guard from a knee-on-belly or side control position and you can catch their leg.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Leg over the head: If your opponent tries to stack you, quickly bring your free leg up and over their head to create space and potentially a triangle choke setup.
    • Hip escape and re-guard: If they manage to stabilize their base, hip escape to create space and re-establish a closed or open guard.
    • Knee shield: If they are trying to crush you, push their knee away with your own knee to create distance and prevent them from collapsing your posture.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to get into Deep Half Guard?

    This often happens when you're using your arm to try and pull your opponent's leg into position. Instead, focus on gripping their pant leg just above the knee and using your hips to drive forward and scoop their leg. Your arm should be framing their hip, not pulling their leg.

    Q: How do I use Deep Half Guard against a much bigger and heavier opponent?

    Against a heavier opponent, leverage becomes even more critical. Focus on getting your hips underneath them by driving them to a 45-degree angle. Use their weight against them by timing your hip explosion as they try to drive forward or posture up. A strong pant grip is essential to control their base.

    Q: When should I try to sweep from Deep Half Guard vs. just holding the position?

    You should attempt a sweep when you feel your opponent's base is unstable and you have achieved the 45-degree hip angle. If they are firmly planted and their weight is distributed evenly, it's better to maintain the frame, secure your grips, and look for an opportunity to transition or improve your position rather than forcing a sweep that might fail.

Deep Half Guard: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide to BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

Deep Half Guard is initiated from a bottom position when your opponent has established a knee-on-belly or close pressure pass.

White belts often struggle by creating a static frame or by attempting to push their opponent away too forcefully, leading to lost position.

The fundamental principle is to use your leg as a lever and your body to off-balance your opponent towards your guard.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Opponent's weight is forward, their knee is near your hip bone. Your left leg hooks behind their right knee, with your left foot on their inner thigh, creating a "hook".
    2. Your right leg slides between their legs, with your right foot on their hip bone, creating a "frame" and preventing them from stacking.
    3. Your left arm secures a "collar grip" on their gi, pulling their upper body towards you.
    4. Your right arm secures an "arm-pant grip" on their right pant leg, near the knee.
    5. Tilt your pelvis towards the ceiling, creating space and angling your body away from your opponent's center line.
    6. Drive your left shoulder into their hip, using your collar grip to pull their upper body forward and down.
    7. Simultaneously, "kick" your right leg out from under their hip, driving your right foot into their hip bone to propel them forward and off balance.
    8. As they fall, drive your hips up and forward, bringing your left leg to your chest to secure a "closed guard" or transition to a "half guard" sweep.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly extending your right leg and pushing directly into your opponent's knee: This can cause hyperextension of the opponent's knee ligament (MCL/LCL) or your own knee if they push back, resulting in ligament tears.
    • Trying to "sit up" by arching your back: This puts immense strain on your lumbar spine and can lead to disc compression injuries, especially if your opponent is heavy.
    • Gripping too low on the pant leg and not securing the collar grip: This allows your opponent to easily pass your leg and will not provide sufficient leverage to off-balance them, leading to potential shoulder or elbow hyperextension if you twist incorrectly.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo movement: Practice the leg entry and hip tilt motion without a partner, focusing on the feel of creating space (10 reps).
    2. Static partner drill: With a compliant partner on their knees, practice entering Deep Half Guard and establishing grips, focusing on body positioning (20 reps).
    3. Controlled entry and sweep attempt: Partner provides minimal resistance (0% resistance). You enter, establish grips, and execute the "kick out" motion, partner allows themselves to be swept (30 reps).
    4. Light resistance drill: Partner actively tries to prevent the entry and sweep (25% resistance). Focus on maintaining grips and hip angle (40 reps).
    5. Moderate resistance drill: Partner applies pressure and attempts to pass (50% resistance). Focus on creating space and driving forward with the sweep (50 reps).
    6. Live rolling: Integrate Deep Half Guard into positional sparring (75-90% resistance). Focus on timing and reaction to opponent's movement.

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent establishes a strong knee-on-belly pass attempt and their knee is close to your hip.
    • When your opponent is postured up and leaning heavily forward, creating an opportunity for an off-balance.
    • When you are stuck in a "half guard" and your opponent is pressuring down, allowing you to "dig" your leg deeper.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • "Stack Pass": If the opponent realizes you are entering Deep Half Guard, they will attempt to stack their weight directly onto your chest. Maintain your "frame" with your right leg on their hip and drive your shoulder into their hip to prevent them from collapsing you.
    • "Leg Swing": If your opponent tries to "swing" their leg over your head to pass, maintain your collar grip and continue to drive your shoulder into their hip. Use your right leg to "block" their swinging leg at the knee.
    • "Escape to Turtle": If the opponent maintains a very tight pressure and you cannot create space, prioritize protecting your neck and spine by returning to a "turtle" position, then working to establish "guard" again.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get into Deep Half Guard?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are trying to "muscle" your leg into position or are not properly creating space with your hips and frame. When entering, ensure your foot is on their hip and your other leg hooks behind their knee *before* you drive your hips up. Avoid forcing your knee directly into their leg.

    Q: How can I use Deep Half Guard against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paraMount. Focus on the "hip angle" and "off-balance" mechanics. Use your collar grip to pull their weight forward and your "frame" leg on their hip to create the necessary leverage. Don't try to lift them; instead, drive your body into them as you execute the leg sweep motion to make them fall.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from Deep Half Guard to another position?

    The ideal time to switch is *after* you have successfully off-balanced your opponent and they are falling. As they lose balance, you can use this moment to bring your left leg to your chest to secure "Closed Guard" or to "climb" your legs for a sweep like the "tripod sweep". Do not try to switch positions while they are still stable.

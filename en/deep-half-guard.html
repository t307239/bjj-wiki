Deep Half Guard: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

Deep Half Guard is a position initiated from the bottom of a half guard where your opponent has significant control over one of your legs.

White belts often struggle by overextending or using raw strength, leading to control loss or injury.

The key is to use your body as a lever and maintain constant, controlled pressure to disrupt their base and create sweep opportunities.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Entry: From bottom half guard, secure your opponent's lead leg by placing your shin across the outside of their thigh, just above the knee.
    2. Grip: Simultaneously, secure a deep collar grip (lapel) with your non-trapped arm, reaching across their chest.
    3. Hip Elevation: Drive your hips upwards and to the side, creating a wedge with your trapped leg's knee between their legs.
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your supporting hip, angling your torso towards your opponent's side, making them off-balance.
    5. Head Position: Tuck your head to their chest, creating a strong frame and preventing them from posturing up.
    6. Leg Control: Maintain a tight lock on their lead leg with your shin and knee, preventing them from easily passing.
    7. Sweep Initiation: Begin to shrimp your hips out slightly while maintaining the collar grip, pulling them forward and to the side to initiate a sweep.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Knee Extension: Extending your trapped leg straight out to create space can hyperextend your knee joint, potentially tearing the ACL or MCL.
    • Solution: Always keep a slight bend in your trapped knee and use it to actively wedge between your opponent's legs.
    • Over-reliance on Arm Strength: Pulling with your collar grip excessively without hip engagement causes shoulder strain and allows your opponent to stabilize.
    • Solution: Focus on driving your hips and using your body weight to create leverage, with the collar grip as an addition, not the primary force.
    • Head Up & Exposed: Keeping your head up exposes your neck to chokes and allows your opponent to easily stack you, leading to spinal compression.
    • Solution: Tuck your chin to your chest and drive your head into your opponent's torso to create a solid frame.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Elevation & Framing: Practice entering Deep Half Guard against an imaginary opponent, focusing on hip elevation and head/collar grip. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Partnered Entry & Grip: With a compliant partner, practice entering Deep Half Guard and securing the collar grip. (30 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    3. Controlled Hip Drive & Wedge: Work on driving your hips up and wedging your knee into the opponent's legs while maintaining the collar grip. (40 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    4. Sweep Initiation Practice: From the established Deep Half Guard position, practice the hip shrimping and pulling motion to initiate a sweep. (30 reps per side, 60% resistance)
    5. Defending Against Stacking: Have a partner gently attempt to stack you from Deep Half Guard, and practice maintaining your frame and hip position. (20 reps per side, 70% resistance)
    6. Light Live Rolling: Engage in light sparring, focusing on achieving and maintaining Deep Half Guard and attempting sweeps. (5-minute rounds, 75% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent has established a strong half guard and is controlling your lead leg.
    • When you feel your opponent is posturing up significantly, creating an opportunity to drop your hips.
    • When you are actively trying to transition from a defensive half guard to a more offensive position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass Defense: If your opponent attempts to stack you, immediately tuck your chin, drive your head into their chest, and use your free leg to push their hips away.
    • Knee Slice Pass Prevention: If they attempt a knee slice, drive your hips to the side and use your collar grip to pull their upper body towards you, disrupting their base.
    • Guard Retention: If they try to disengage or pass by stepping over your trapped leg, maintain your hip connection and use your collar grip to pull them back into the half guard position.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get into Deep Half Guard?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are extending your trapped leg forcefully. This can cause hyperextension or put undue stress on the ligaments. Always maintain a slight bend in your trapped knee and use it actively to wedge between your opponent's legs, creating a stable frame rather than a point of strain.

    Q: How can I use Deep Half Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is key. Focus on driving your hips up and using your body weight to create an angle, rather than trying to overpower them. Your collar grip should be used to pull their upper body forward, disrupting their balance and making their weight advantage less impactful. Keep your head tucked firmly to maintain control.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a sweep from Deep Half Guard?

    The optimal time to sweep is when you feel your opponent's weight shift forward as they attempt to pass or advance their position. As they lean in, drive your hips up and to the side, then use your collar grip to pull their upper body towards you while simultaneously shrimp-ing your hips out. This combination of upward pressure and pulling creates an imbalance that leads to a sweep.

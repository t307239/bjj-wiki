    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try to bridge to escape side control in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu?

    Neck pain during a bridge often stems from hyperextending your cervical spine, meaning your head is pushed too far back. Instead, focus on driving your hips up by engaging your glutes and contracting your core, while keeping your chin tucked to your chest to protect your neck and create a stable base.

    Q: How can I generate more power when bridging to escape a submission hold, like an armbar, as a beginner?

    To maximize bridging power, initiate the movement by simultaneously driving your hips upward and arching your lower back, creating a fulcrum. Simultaneously, push off the mat with your feet and brace your core to transfer that energy efficiently through your hips and into the opponent.

    Q: What are the key foot and hip positions to use for a powerful bridge against a much larger opponent in BJJ?

    Plant your feet firmly on the mat with knees bent, creating a stable base, and drive your hips up by squeezing your glutes and engaging your core. This hip extension, combined with the leverage generated by your feet pushing into the mat, allows you to lift and create space even against a heavier opponent.

    More Questions

    How do I bridge effectively in BJJ?

    To bridge effectively, plant your feet firmly on the mat, shoulder-width apart. Drive through your heels and hips, creating a powerful upward and backward motion. Keep your core engaged and your head pressed into the mat for stability.

    What is the purpose of bridging in BJJ?

    Bridging is a fundamental escape and positioning technique in BJJ. It's primarily used to create space, escape submissions like the armbar or triangle choke, and to shrimp out from underneath a dominant opponent.

    How can I generate more power when bridging?

    To generate more power, focus on a coordinated explosive movement from your hips and legs. Think about pushing the mat away with your feet and simultaneously lifting your hips as high as possible. A strong core engagement is crucial for transferring this power effectively.

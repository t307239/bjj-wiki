Sickle Sweep: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Sickle Sweep is initiated from Closed Guard, aiming to disrupt your opponent's base and off-balance them for a sweep. It's a dynamic sweep requiring precise timing and body control.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of leverage and hip movement, leading to ineffective attempts or injury. They tend to muscle the sweep rather than use technique.

The core mechanical insight is using your shin as a lever against the opponent's hip while simultaneously creating an angle with your hips to generate rotational force.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From closed guard, establish a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (left sleeve) with your left hand.
    2. Shift your hips slightly to your left, creating a 45-degree angle between your torso and your opponent's torso.
    3. Bring your right knee across your body, aiming to place your right shin directly across the front of your opponent's left hip bone.
    4. At the same time, drive your left hip forward and slightly upward, creating a fulcrum point.
    5. Pull your opponent forward and slightly to their left with your grips, while simultaneously extending your right leg to push their hip away.
    6. As their weight shifts forward and to their left, pivot on your left hip and drive your right shin upward and across their body.
    7. Continue the rotational motion with your hips and a strong pull on their collar and sleeve to complete the sweep, ending in a dominant position.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to lift the opponent with your arms alone while keeping your hips flat on the mat: This can strain your shoulders and lower back, as the power is not originating from the hips. The correct movement involves driving your hips forward and rotating.
    • Extending your right leg too forcefully without hip rotation, causing your opponent to stumble sideways but not fall: This can lead to knee hyperextension or ligament tears in your knee if they resist unexpectedly. Focus on the combined action of hip drive and shin leverage.
    • Grabbing the opponent's pants with a death grip and pulling: This limits your ability to adjust your angle and can lead to wrist or elbow injuries if they apply counter-pressure. Maintain control of the collar and sleeve for better leverage and body control.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo execution of hip angles and shin placement without a partner (5 reps each side).
    2. Partner drills: Opponent remains passive, you execute the sweep from closed guard (10 reps each side, 25% resistance).
    3. Partner drills: Opponent offers light resistance to your hip and collar grips (10 reps each side, 50% resistance).
    4. Partner drills: Opponent attempts to defend by posturing up or pushing your knee away (10 reps each side, 75% resistance).
    5. Flow rolling: Incorporate the Sickle Sweep into positional sparring from closed guard (5 minutes, 90% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Actively look for opportunities to execute the Sickle Sweep in sparring (10 minutes, 100% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is postured up slightly, creating space between your hips and theirs.
    • Opponent is leaning forward, giving you a clear target for your shin on their hip.
    • Opponent is trying to stack you in closed guard, which often leaves their hips vulnerable.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my hip hurt when I try to do the Sickle Sweep from closed guard?

    Hip pain during the Sickle Sweep often stems from attempting to force the sweep with your legs and back, rather than using your core and hip rotation. Ensure you are creating a 45-degree angle with your hips and driving your hips forward and up as a fulcrum, rather than just pushing with your leg. This biomechanical shift will engage your glutes and core, protecting your hip.

    Q: How can I effectively Sickle Sweep a much larger and stronger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paraMount. Focus on precise timing and a strong off-balancing pull with your grips before initiating the shin lever. Ensure your right shin is firmly planted on their hip bone and drive your left hip forward with maximum force to create the rotational torque. Do not try to lift them; use their weight against them by pulling them into your lever.

    Q: I keep getting my guard passed when I attempt the Sickle Sweep; what am I doing wrong?

    Guard Passing during the Sickle Sweep often occurs when your hip angle is not sharp enough, or you delay the sweep initiation. If your opponent senses the sweep attempt, they will often drive forward to pass. Make sure your hips are at a distinct 45-degree angle and initiate the pull and shin placement simultaneously. If they start to drive, be prepared to disengage and re-guard by shrimping your hips away.

Sickle Sweep: A Biomechanically Precise Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Sickle Sweep is initiated from Closed Guard, aiming to off-balance your opponent and transition to a superior position. It leverages hip mobility and precise leg action.

White belts often struggle by initiating with upper body strength or an incorrect leg angle, leading to lost grips and no sweep. Focus on the hip rotation and leg extension.

The core principle is creating a lever with your leg, using your hip as the fulcrum to unbalance your opponent's base and initiate the sweep.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Grip opponent's right sleeve with your left hand (cross-collar grip is also viable, but sleeve offers more control for beginners). Grip their right pant leg at the ankle with your right hand.
    2. Shimmy your hips to your left, creating a slight angle. Your hips should be perpendicular to your opponent's center line.
    3. Bring your right knee towards your chest, tucking it close to your opponent's right hip. This creates a fulcrum.
    4. Extend your left leg straight, driving your left foot into your opponent's left hip crease. This is your pushing/framing leg.
    5. Simultaneously, rotate your hips to the right, keeping your left leg extended. Your right leg, anchored at the ankle, will pivot.
    6. Drive your left foot into their hip, pushing them away and creating space while your right leg acts as a sickle, hooking their ankle and pulling it across their body.
    7. As their weight shifts, drive your hips up and to your left, using your right foot to continue the ankle hook and your left foot to push their hip.
    8. Transition immediately to a mount or side control as they fall, maintaining control of the sleeve and pant leg.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly extending your right leg straight out while attempting to hook the ankle: This can cause hyperextension or sprains in your knee and ankle ligaments, as the force is misdirected. The correct movement involves a scooping motion originating from the hip, with the knee slightly bent initially.
    • Grabbing the opponent's ankle with a loose grip and attempting to pull: This often results in losing the grip and can lead to your hand being injured by a retaliatory grip break or an armbar. Maintain a firm grip on the pant leg at the ankle and use your hip rotation for power.
    • Not shimming hips to create an angle: Trying to sweep with hips square to the opponent leads to a direct push, not a sweep, and can strain your lower back. Shimmying to the side creates the necessary leverage and hip angle for the sickle motion.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip shimmy, leg positioning, and sickle motion without a partner. Focus on the feel of the hip rotation and leg extension. (10 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a partner lying down, practice the exact grips, hip movement, and leg action. Partner remains passive. (20 reps)
    3. Light resistance drill: Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the contact points and adjust your leverage. Focus on maintaining grips and hip angle. (30 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled resistance drill: Partner actively tries to resist but does not actively defend. You focus on executing the sweep with proper mechanics, feeling the shift in their weight. (30 reps, 50% resistance)
    5. Dynamic resistance drill: Partner actively tries to prevent the sweep using body weight and framing but without advanced defense. Focus on adapting to their resistance. (30 reps, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the Sickle Sweep in rolling scenarios, focusing on timing, grip retention, and follow-up. Prioritize executing the mechanics correctly over landing the sweep every time. (5 minutes, 90-100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • Opponent is postured up slightly in your closed guard, creating a slight space between their hips and yours.
    • Opponent is leaning forward heavily, making their base unstable.
    • You have secure sleeve and pant grips, allowing you to control their limb.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape and Re-Guard: As the sickle leg begins its motion, perform a strong hip escape to your opposite side, breaking the angle and preventing the hook from completing. Immediately attempt to re-establish a strong closed guard or transition to open guard.
    • Posture Up and Grip Break: If the opponent's shin is too far away or they are too low, posture up slightly and use your free hand to break their sleeve grip forcefully. This removes your primary control point for the sweep.
    • Knee Shield and Push: If the sickle leg is caught or the sweep is failing, use your free leg (the one pushing the hip) to create a knee shield, pushing their hip away. This creates space to disengage and reset your guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Sickle Sweep?

    Your knee pain likely stems from over-extension or a direct lateral force applied to the joint. When performing the sickle motion, ensure your right knee remains slightly bent as you hook the ankle, and the primary driving force comes from your hip rotation, not a straight leg push. Think of scooping the ankle rather than kicking it.

    Q: How can I Sickle Sweep a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on using their weight against them. Shimmy your hips aggressively to create a significant angle, making them off-balance. Your pushing leg (left leg) must drive hard into their hip, creating space. Then, the sickle leg's hook needs to be tight around their ankle and pull it across their body with sharp hip rotation.

    Q: When should I use the Sickle Sweep instead of a Scissor Sweep?

    The Sickle Sweep is more effective when your opponent is leaning forward or postured up, making their base unstable and their weight forward. The Scissor Sweep is generally better when the opponent is more upright and their weight is distributed more evenly or towards their heels, as it directly targets their base with your legs in a scissor-like motion.

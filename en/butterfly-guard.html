Butterfly Guard: The White Belt's Biomechanically Accurate Guide

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Blue Belt Guide

Butterfly Guard is a dynamic BJJ guard initiated from a seated position, aiming to control opponent's legs and create off-balancing opportunities.

Many white belts struggle by relying on upper body strength or incorrect hip positioning, leading to ineffective control and submission vulnerability.

The core principle is using your hooked legs to elevate and off-balance, creating openings for sweeps and transitions by manipulating your opponent's base.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a seated position, place your feet flat on the mat, knees bent at approximately 90 degrees, hips slightly elevated off the mat.
    2. Hook your shins *inside* your opponent's thighs, with your knees pointing slightly outwards, creating a 'butterfly wing' shape.
    3. Secure grips on your opponent's pants, specifically the inseam just above the knee, using both hands for equal pressure and control.
    4. Drive your hips forward and slightly up, creating a wedge between your opponent's legs and their torso.
    5. Maintain a strong base by keeping your feet relatively close together, preventing your opponent from easily passing to one side.
    6. Use your hooked feet to slightly pull your opponent's knees towards your chest, disrupting their posture and balance.
    7. Initiate a sweep by extending one leg while simultaneously shrimping your hips to that side, using the extended leg to elevate and the shrimping motion to drive forward and sideways.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly placing your feet outside the opponent's thighs instead of hooking inside: This can lead to the opponent easily passing your legs, and puts excessive strain on your knee ligaments (MCL/LCL) as you try to resist a forward pressure.
    • Letting your hips sag to the mat: This removes your primary source of elevation and off-balancing, allowing the opponent to establish heavy pressure passing and potentially hyperextending your knee if they stack you.
    • Gripping too high on the opponent's pants (e.g., thighs or hips): This provides less leverage for controlling their knee angle and initiating sweeps, making it easier for them to maintain their base and pass; grip the inseam near the knee for optimal control.
    • Trying to 'pull' the opponent down with your arms without hip elevation: This strains your shoulders and biceps unnecessarily, and allows the opponent to maintain their weight distribution, making sweeps impossible.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hook placement, hip elevation, and grip acquisition for 5 minutes, focusing on the feeling of wedging your hips. (0% resistance)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Have a partner stand in front of you. Practice entering Butterfly Guard, securing grips, and elevating your hips for 10 reps per side. (0% resistance)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Your partner gently tries to push your hips down. Practice maintaining hip elevation and the 'butterfly' hook for 10 reps per side. (25% resistance)
    4. Sweep entry drill: With a partner offering minimal resistance, practice initiating one sweep (e.g., scissor sweep entry) from Butterfly Guard, focusing on the hip extension and leg drive. 5 reps per side. (50% resistance)
    5. Controlled positional sparring: Start in Butterfly Guard against a partner who is actively trying to pass but not using full force. Focus on maintaining your guard and attempting sweeps. 3 rounds of 2 minutes. (75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Implement Butterfly Guard during rolling, focusing on maintaining the guard and looking for sweep opportunities. 1 round of 5 minutes. (100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is standing or kneeling in front of you, attempting to pass your open guard.
    • When you have successfully transitioned to your back from another position and need to establish a defensive or offensive guard.
    • When you have a strong grip on your opponent's pants and can prevent them from establishing a solid base.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass: If the opponent tries to 'stack' you by driving their weight directly forward, maintain your hip elevation and use your hooks to drive your hips away and to the side, creating space to recover your guard or transition.
    • Leg Drag: If the opponent attempts to pull one of your legs out to the side, use your other hook to maintain pressure on their hip and simultaneously secure a grip on the leg they are trying to drag, preventing them from isolating it.
    • Knee Slice Pass: When the opponent tries to drive their knee across your body, use your hooks to keep their hips away and your frames (forearms) to create space, then shimp your hips to the side to avoid the pressure and re-establish your guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to do Butterfly Guard?

    Lower back pain in Butterfly Guard often stems from excessive lumbar extension and insufficient hip elevation. Instead of arching your back to lift your hips, focus on engaging your glutes and core to 'wedge' your pelvis forward and upward. Ensure your feet are hooked *inside* the opponent's thighs and maintain a slight tension, allowing your legs to do the work of elevation, not just your spine.

    Q: How do I use Butterfly Guard effectively against a much bigger and heavier opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paraMount. Focus on using your hooks to actively 'pull' their knees towards your chest, disrupting their base and creating an angle. Your hip elevation becomes crucial; think of it as a 'wedge' to prevent them from crushing you. Prioritize sweeps that off-balance them by driving them forward or sideways, rather than trying to lift their entire weight.

    Q: When should I transition from Closed Guard to Butterfly Guard?

    Transitioning from Closed Guard to Butterfly Guard is often a proactive measure. If your opponent is posturing up heavily and breaking your Closed Guard, or if you feel them preparing a powerful pass, you can open your guard and immediately hook their legs to establish Butterfly. This transition is best initiated when you have strong grips on their pants and can quickly secure your hooks before they can establish a dominant passing position.

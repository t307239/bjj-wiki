Butterfly Guard: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide to BJJ

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Blue Belt Guide

The Butterfly Guard is an offensive guard position established from your back, aiming to elevate your opponent and sweep them. White belts often struggle due to a lack of fundamental body control and a reactive mindset.

The core mechanical insight is creating a stable, elevated base with your hips and using your legs as levers to off-balance and propel your opponent.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Assume a seated position on your back. Bend your knees to 90 degrees, feet flat on the mat.
    2. Place your feet inside your opponent's legs, roughly hip-width apart.
    3. Hook your feet under your opponent's shins, trapping their legs with your ankles crossed behind their calves.
    4. Grip your opponent's collar with both hands, or one collar and one sleeve, maintaining a tight connection.
    5. Arch your back slightly, bringing your hips off the mat to create an elevated base.
    6. Drive your knees forward and slightly upward, maintaining a wide base with your feet.
    7. Use your grips to pull your opponent forward and down, while simultaneously shrimping your hips back to create space and leverage.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly crossing ankles too tightly behind opponent's calves can hyperextend the knee joint, risking ACL or MCL tears. Always maintain a slight bend in your knee and ensure your feet are hooked, not locked.
    • Attempting to swing your opponent with straight legs and a flat back stresses the lumbar spine, leading to potential disc injury. Keep your back slightly arched and your legs bent, using your hips for power.
    • Gripping too low on the opponent's pants without a collar grip can lead to the opponent posturing up easily and collapsing your guard. Focus on upper body grips for control and leverage.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip elevation and knee drive motion without a partner. Focus on creating the 'butterfly' shape with your legs. 100 reps.
    2. Partner drilling (static): Opponent lies on their back, you establish Butterfly Guard. Practice gripping and shrimping hips. 50 reps per side.
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Opponent attempts to posture up. You practice maintaining your base and shrimping. 50 reps per side, 25% resistance.
    4. Sweep attempts (light resistance): Opponent provides minimal resistance. Practice executing a basic sweep (e.g., scissor sweep from butterfly). 50 reps per side, 50% resistance.
    5. Sweep attempts (moderate resistance): Opponent actively defends and tries to pass. Focus on timing and hip movement for sweeps. 50 reps per side, 75% resistance.
    6. Live rolling: Implement Butterfly Guard against a resisting opponent. Focus on establishing and maintaining the guard, then attempting sweeps. 5 rounds, 90-100% resistance.

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is close, in your guard, and posturing slightly upwards.
    • When you've successfully defended a guard pass and are on your back.
    • When you want to create space and initiate an offensive sweep from the bottom.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass: Opponent drives forward, placing their chest on your chest and pushing your hips down. Counter by shrimping your hips out to the side and attempting to recover guard or transition to a sweep.
    • Leg Drag Pass: Opponent isolates one of your legs and pulls it across their body. Counter by keeping your other leg hooked and using your free foot to push their hip away, creating space to re-establish your guard.
    • Knee Slice Pass: Opponent drives their knee across your body to pass. Counter by elevating your hips and using your butterfly hooks to create space and prevent their knee from reaching the mat.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to do Butterfly Guard?

    Your lower back pain likely stems from arching your back too much without adequate hip elevation. Instead of just arching, focus on actively driving your knees forward and shrimping your hips back. This creates a stable base and transfers power through your hips and legs, rather than straining your lumbar spine.

    Q: How can I use Butterfly Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is key. Focus on keeping your hips elevated and your hooks tight. Use your grips to control their posture and pull them into you, making their size less of an advantage. Prioritize sweeping by using their weight against them, rather than trying to muscle them.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from Butterfly Guard to another guard like Closed Guard?

    You should consider switching if the opponent is successfully posturing up and creating too much distance, making your butterfly hooks ineffective. If they are pressuring heavily and you can't create space, transitioning to Closed Guard by bringing your feet to their hips can offer a more secure control position.

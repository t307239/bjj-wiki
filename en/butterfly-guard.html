Precise Butterfly Guard Technique for BJJ White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Blue Belt Guide

Butterfly Guard is a dynamic guard position initiated from your back, aiming to create off-balancing opportunities. It allows you to control your opponent's base and set up sweeps or transitions.

White belts often struggle by keeping their hips too flat or their hooks too loose, negating the leverage and allowing passes.

The core of Butterfly Guard lies in elevating your hips and using your shins as hooks to disrupt your opponent's base and propel them forward.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Lie on your back with knees bent, feet flat on the mat. This is your starting point.
    2. Bring your feet in towards your glutes, creating a 90-degree bend at the knees and hips.
    3. Insert your shins *under* your opponent's thighs, creating a 'butterfly' hook with each leg.
    4. Your knees should be pointed towards the ceiling or slightly inward, not splayed outwards.
    5. Grip your opponent's far side collar with your near side hand, and their sleeve (elbow region) with your far side hand.
    6. Elevate your hips off the mat by contracting your glutes and core, pushing your pelvis slightly upwards.
    7. Maintain a light pressure with your shins against their thighs, creating tension and preventing them from posturing up easily.
    8. Your base should be narrow, with your feet relatively close together, to allow for hip movement.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly framing with your arms: Extending arms straight to push your opponent away can hyperextend your elbows or shoulders if they drive forward, leading to ligamentous tears.
    • Correct: Use your forearms to frame against their hips or biceps, maintaining a slight bend in your elbows.
    • Letting your hips drop flat to the mat: This removes your primary leverage, allowing your opponent to establish heavy pressure and potentially pass your guard.
    • Correct: Actively contract your glutes and core to keep your hips elevated, creating space.
    • Crossing your ankles when hooking: This makes your legs rigid and vulnerable to leg locks, and restricts your ability to shrimp or create angles.
    • Correct: Keep your shins parallel and your feet flexed, ready to adjust your hooks.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Elevation and Hook Placement: Practice lifting your hips and inserting your shins under imaginary thighs. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner Assisted Hook Placement: With a compliant partner standing, practice inserting your hooks under their thighs and elevating your hips. (20 reps per side, 10% resistance)
    3. Gripping and Frame Drills: Practice establishing collar and sleeve grips, then elevating hips. Focus on maintaining the hooks. (30 reps, 20% resistance)
    4. Basic Sweep Attempts (No Resistance): From the Butterfly Guard position, practice the mechanics of a basic scissor sweep, focusing on the hip lift and leg drive. (20 reps per side, 30% resistance)
    5. Light Flow Rolling: Attempt Butterfly Guard sweeps against a partner who is actively trying to prevent them but not pass aggressively. Focus on timing and hip movement. (5 minutes, 50% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling: Implement Butterfly Guard in live sparring, focusing on maintaining the position and attempting sweeps. Don't worry about success, focus on the mechanics. (3 rounds, 75% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is attempting to pass your guard from a standing or kneeling position.
    • When you are on your back and looking to create an offensive guard from a neutral or disadvantageous position.
    • When your opponent settles into a heavy, forward-leaning pressure, making it difficult to maintain other guards.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Knee Slice Pass: The opponent drives their knee across your body, attempting to flatten your hips and bypass your hooks. To counter, maintain active hooks, elevate your hips, and shrimp your hips away from the knee, creating space to re-guard or transition.
    • Toreando Pass: The opponent grabs your legs and attempts to spin around your hips. To counter, maintain strong collar and sleeve grips, drive your hips into their chest, and use your hooks to push their hips away as they try to circle.
    • Stack Pass: The opponent leans over you, driving their weight directly down onto your hips and shoulders. To counter, maintain elevated hips and frame with your forearms against their chest, then use your hooks to push their hips away and create an angle for a sweep.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my back hurt when I try to do Butterfly Guard?

    Your back is likely hurting because you are trying to generate power solely from your lumbar spine, rather than your hips and core. Ensure your hips are actively elevated off the mat by contracting your glutes and abdominal muscles. This transfers the load to your stronger posterior chain and core, taking pressure off your lower back.

    Q: How do I Butterfly Guard against a much bigger opponent who is just crushing me?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on maintaining active, tight hooks. Instead of trying to lift them entirely, use your hooks to create small, constant adjustments and off-balancing motions. Elevate your hips to prevent them from flattening you, and use your frames to create just enough space to shrimp and re-angle your body. Think 'disrupt' rather than 'lift'.

    Q: When should I switch from Closed Guard to Butterfly Guard?

    You should consider transitioning to Butterfly Guard when your opponent is effectively controlling your legs in Closed Guard and preventing you from creating sweeps or submissions, or when they are posturing up and making it difficult to maintain the closed position. The transition involves releasing your leg lock, shrimping your hips out slightly, and inserting your hooks under their thighs.

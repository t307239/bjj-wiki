Butterfly Guard is a dynamic BJJ guard initiated from a seated or supine position, aiming to disrupt the opponent's base and create sweep opportunities.

White belts often struggle by keeping their hips too flat or their knees too far apart, negating the leverage needed for effective control.

The core principle is using your shins and feet as hooks to elevate and off-balance your opponent, creating angles for sweeps.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Sit with your back on the mat, knees bent, feet flat. Your opponent is in front of you, preferably in your guard.
    2. Place your feet inside your opponent's legs, approximately hip-width apart, heels pointing towards your glutes.
    3. Hook your shins around the *outside* of your opponent's thighs, creating a "butterfly" shape with your legs.
    4. Grip your opponent's pants, just below the knee, with both hands. This is the "pant grip".
    5. Alternatively, grip one collar with one hand and the opposite pant leg with the other hand for more control.
    6. Tuck your chin to your chest and round your upper back slightly, creating a concave posture.
    7. Drive your knees inward towards each other, tightening the hooks around your opponent's legs and lifting them slightly off the mat.
    8. Shift your hips to one side, creating an angle, and begin to drive your opponent in that direction.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Arching the lower back excessively: This can lead to lumbar strain or disc injury. The correct movement is to round the upper back, not hyperextend the lower back.
    • Allowing opponent's knees to penetrate your guard: This compromises your hooks and can lead to a guard pass. Keep your knees driving inward and your shins actively hooked.
    • Lifting with straight legs: This is inefficient and can strain the hamstrings. Instead, use your hooked shins to elevate and drive your opponent by engaging your glutes and core.
    • Leaving feet exposed to leg attacks: Your feet are vulnerable. Always maintain the hooked position with your shins, preventing your opponent from isolating a foot for a heel hook.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Elevation Drill: From bottom position, practice lifting your hips off the mat while maintaining the butterfly hooks. 10 repetitions, 0% resistance.
    2. Solo Sweep Motion Drill: From bottom position, practice driving one knee in and shifting your hips to simulate a sweep attempt without an opponent. 10 repetitions per side, 0% resistance.
    3. Partner Drill - Gripping and Hooking: Your partner kneels in front of you. Practice establishing pant grips and hooking your shins. Partner remains static. 10 repetitions, 10% resistance.
    4. Partner Drill - Hip Shift and Elevation: With pant grips and hooks, practice driving your knees in and shifting your hips to one side. Partner offers minimal resistance to hip movement. 10 repetitions per side, 25% resistance.
    5. Partner Drill - Controlled Sweep Attempts: Your partner maintains a light base. Practice your butterfly sweep mechanics, aiming for a controlled lift and hip shift. Partner provides light pressure to resist. 5 repetitions per side, 50% resistance.
    6. Live Rolling - Limited Butterfly Guard: Execute butterfly guard techniques with a willing partner in a controlled rolling scenario. Focus on establishing the guard and attempting sweeps. 3 minutes, 75% resistance.

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up and has established a dominant grip on your gi.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard from a kneeling or standing position.
    • When you are in your guard and want to create dynamic sweep opportunities by disrupting their base.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Guard Pass (e.g., Knee Slice Pass): If the opponent drives their knee into your hip and attempts to pass to your side, you must immediately re-hook their leg with your opposite shin and drive your hips back to regain guard.
    • Stack Pass: If the opponent drives forward and attempts to stack you, create space by pushing their hips away with your hands and using your butterfly hooks to elevate one leg, allowing you to shrimp out.
    • Standing Up: If the opponent attempts to stand up from your butterfly guard, maintain your hooks and control their pants. Use your hips to drive them back down or transition to a single leg takedown if they overcommit.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to do Butterfly Guard?

    Lower back pain in Butterfly Guard typically stems from hyperextending your lumbar spine instead of rounding your upper back. To fix this, consciously tuck your chin to your chest and imagine creating a "C" shape with your upper back. This engages your core and protects your spine while maintaining the necessary leverage.

    Q: How do I effectively use Butterfly Guard against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paraMount. Focus on using your hooked shins to elevate their weight onto their toes, making them unstable. Drive your knees inward aggressively and shift your hips to one side to create the angle for your sweep. Don't try to muscle them; instead, use their weight against them.

    Q: When should I transition out of Butterfly Guard if my sweep attempt fails?

    If your sweep attempt is defended and your opponent stabilizes their base, do not persist with a failing motion. Immediately look to transition. If they are close, shrimp your hips out and re-establish Closed Guard. If they are driving forward to pass, use your hooks to create space and attempt a technical stand-up or a different sweep.

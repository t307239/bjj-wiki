Butterfly Guard: A White Belt's Guide to BJJ

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Blue Belt Guide

Butterfly guard is an offensive guard position where you sit on your opponent's hips, using your shins to hook their legs and create frames.

White belts often struggle by collapsing their structure, allowing the opponent to pass easily or leading to poor posture.

The core mechanic is using your hooked legs to elevate and off-balance, creating openings for sweeps and submissions.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a seated position, place your feet flat on the mat, knees bent at approximately 90 degrees. Your opponent is in front of you, likely in your guard.
    2. Hook your right shin across the front of your opponent's left thigh, just above the knee. Your foot should be flexed, toes pointing upwards.
    3. Hook your left shin across the front of your opponent's right thigh, also just above the knee. Your foot should be flexed, toes pointing upwards.
    4. Ensure your knees are angled slightly outwards, creating a stable platform. Your hips should be angled slightly towards one side, typically the side of your dominant hook.
    5. Establish a two-on-one grip on your opponent's sleeves, pulling them towards you and breaking their posture down. Alternatively, grip their collar and sleeve.
    6. Shift your weight onto your hips, creating a slight forward lean. This allows your hooked legs to generate upward pressure.
    7. Use your hooked legs to 'butterfly' your opponent's legs, lifting one leg slightly higher than the other to initiate a sweep or create space for a submission.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly hooking the opponent's legs too low (at the ankle) exposes your knees to hyperextension if they stack; your knees should be protected by their thighs.
    • Failing to maintain hip elevation and instead allowing your back to round allows the opponent to drive forward, potentially causing lumbar spine compression.
    • Gripping too tightly on the opponent's pants without actively using your hooks to create frames can lead to loss of control and easy passes.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice setting up the hooks and shifting weight without an opponent. (50 reps each side)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Partner stands, you set up butterfly guard and practice hip elevation. (25 reps each side, 25% resistance)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Partner attempts to stand or pressure. You focus on maintaining hooks and hip angle. (50 reps each side, 50% resistance)
    4. Sweep attempts (light resistance): Practice one specific sweep from butterfly guard, with partner offering minimal defense. (50 reps each side, 75% resistance)
    5. Guard retention drills: Partner attempts guard passes from butterfly guard. Focus on maintaining hooks and re-guarding. (10 x 1 minute rounds, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Implement butterfly guard during rolling, prioritizing control and sweep attempts. (3 x 5 minute rounds, 90% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is postured up and within your reach, allowing you to establish hooks.
    • When your opponent is trying to pass your guard and is within range to establish butterfly hooks.
    • When you are on your back and looking to transition to a more offensive guard or sweep.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass Defense: If opponent stacks, immediately drive your hips back and widen your stance, using your forearms to push their shoulders away. Maintain flexed feet on their thighs.
    • Leg Drag Defense: If opponent attempts a leg drag, keep your hips mobile. Use your free leg to hook their passing leg and shrimp away to re-establish guard. Your hooks are key here.
    • Knee Slice Pass Defense: If opponent attempts a knee slice, flatten your hips and use your shin frames to keep their knee away. Grip their hip and pull them into you to prevent the pass.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to set up Butterfly Guard?

    Your lower back pain is likely due to rounding your spine instead of maintaining a neutral, slightly arched posture. When you try to hook your opponent's legs, ensure your hips are elevated off the mat, creating a stable base. Think of it as sitting tall on your sit bones, with your hooked legs providing the upward leverage, not your back muscles straining.

    Q: How can I use Butterfly Guard effectively against a much heavier opponent in BJJ?

    Against a heavier opponent, focus on leverage and timing over brute strength. Use your butterfly hooks to constantly elevate and off-balance their weight. Instead of trying to lift them directly, aim to disrupt their base. Think of 'tipping' them over by creating a strong frame with your shins on their thighs and using your grips to pull their upper body towards you as you shift your hips.

    Q: When should I transition from Closed Guard to Butterfly Guard in BJJ?

    You should consider transitioning from Closed Guard to Butterfly Guard when your opponent is postured up and maintaining a strong base, making it difficult to break them down for submissions or sweeps from closed guard. By opening your guard and establishing butterfly hooks, you gain the ability to elevate and off-balance them, creating new attacking opportunities.

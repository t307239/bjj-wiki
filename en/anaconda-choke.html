Anaconda Choke: A Biomechanically Accurate BJJ Guide for White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Blue Belt Guide

The Anaconda Choke is a powerful submission typically initiated from a dominant Side Control or turtle position, aiming to constrict the opponent's carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body awareness and an overreliance on brute strength, leading to poor mechanics and potential injury.

The key to a successful Anaconda Choke lies in creating a tight, two-on-one grip on the opponent's neck and arm, using your shoulder to drive into their carotid artery.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From side control, secure a "cupped hand" grip on the opponent's far-side collar with your near-side hand, thumb inside.
    2. Simultaneously, reach your far-side arm underneath their head and grip your own bicep (your far-side bicep) with your near-side hand.
    3. Drive your chest down and forward, tucking your chin to your chest to create a "headlock" position.
    4. Shift your weight forward, placing your chest directly over their shoulder blade, angling your hips slightly towards their head.
    5. Squeeze your grip on your bicep, pulling their shoulder and neck into your bicep and shoulder.
    6. Drive your shoulder into their carotid artery, applying downward pressure with your chest and hips.
    7. Maintain a tight frame with your arms, preventing them from posturing up or creating space.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly "crunching" with your head: This can hyperextend the cervical spine, leading to neck sprains or disc issues. The neck should be neutral and tucked, not forced into flexion.
    • Over-extending your back: Trying to "muscle" the choke by arching your back excessively can strain lumbar ligaments. Maintain a stable, braced core and use hip pressure.
    • Gripping too loosely on your bicep: A weak bicep grip allows their arm to slip, reducing choke effectiveness and potentially leading to shoulder impingement if you pull incorrectly. Ensure a firm, locked grip on your own bicep.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice the grip sequence and body positioning (steps 1-4) without a partner. (20 reps)
    2. Solo drill: Focus on the shoulder drive and weight transfer (steps 5-7) with a dummy or rolled-up gi. (20 reps)
    3. Partner drill (0% resistance): From side control, have your partner remain completely passive while you execute the choke. (10 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the mechanics. (10 reps per side)
    5. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner actively defends slightly, forcing you to adjust your pressure and grips. (10 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling (75-90% resistance): Attempt the Anaconda Choke in sparring, focusing on clean execution and timing. (5-10 attempts)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have established dominant side control and the opponent is trying to shrimp away or bridge.
    • When the opponent turtles and you can secure a deep headlock position from the side.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard and you can transition to a front headlock or side control situation.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • "Stacking" defense: The opponent actively pushes their hips into you, creating space and relieving pressure on the carotid. To counter, maintain your chest-to-chest connection and drive your hips forward.
    • "Arm-out" defense: The opponent pulls their trapped arm free. To counter, ensure your grip on your bicep is secure and your shoulder is driving into their neck, not just their arm.
    • "Head-up" defense: The opponent postures up, creating space. To counter, maintain a low base and drive your hips forward, preventing them from gaining vertical posture.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my bicep grip feel weak when I try the Anaconda Choke?

    A weak bicep grip often stems from not fully locking your hand around your own bicep. Ensure your thumb is wrapped around your forearm and your fingers are gripping your bicep firmly. This creates a solid fulcrum. Also, ensure your shoulder is driving into their neck, not just their arm, which helps maintain the grip's integrity.

    Q: How can I apply the Anaconda Choke effectively against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on isolating their neck and arm with precision, not brute force. Ensure your shoulder is deep under their chin and your chest is firmly against their upper back. Use your hips to drive forward and down, leveraging their weight against them. A tight two-on-one grip on your own bicep is crucial for control.

    Q: Should I pull my opponent towards me or push them away when applying the Anaconda Choke?

    You should primarily drive your shoulder and chest into their neck and upper back, effectively "compressing" them. While there is a slight pulling action with your bicep grip, the main force comes from driving your body weight forward and downward. Think of it as a "crushing" motion rather than a "pulling" motion.

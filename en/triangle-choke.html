Triangle Choke: A Biomechanically Precise BJJ Guide for White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Blue Belt Guide

The Triangle Choke is a submission from the guard, primarily Closed Guard, that uses your legs to constrict blood flow to the opponent's brain.

White belts often struggle because they rely on brute strength or don't understand the leverage points, leading to inefficiency and missed submissions.

The key mechanical insight is creating a stable base and using your hips to drive your shin across the carotid artery, effectively locking the choke.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From closed guard, secure a standard collar grip with your right hand on the opponent's left collar, and your left hand on their right collar.
    2. Break the opponent's posture by pulling them forward and to your left, creating space to bring your right leg up.
    3. Swing your right leg over the opponent's left shoulder, aiming to place your right shin across the back of their neck.
    4. Immediately secure your right foot with your left hand, creating a 'figure-four' lock around the opponent's head and arm.
    5. Drive your hips forward and slightly to your right, creating an angle to cinch the choke.
    6. Bring your left leg up to meet your right shin, locking your left foot behind your right knee, ensuring your shin is tight across the carotid artery.
    7. Squeeze your knees together and drive your hips forward to complete the choke, ensuring your opponent's trapped arm is inside the triangle.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to 'crank' the choke by pulling with your arms while the legs are not properly positioned: This can strain the rotator cuff and shoulder capsule in the opponent, and lead to neck strain for the attacker.
    • Not driving hips forward and instead trying to squeeze legs tighter: This places excessive pressure on the knee joint of the choking leg, risking meniscus tears or ligament damage.
    • Allowing the opponent to stack your legs by not creating a strong hip angle: This can lead to hyperextension of the knee and ankle on the leg that is supposed to be locked, causing sprains or ligament tears.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice the leg movement from closed guard to the figure-four lock and hip drive without an opponent. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (static): With a compliant partner on their back, practice securing the grips and positioning your legs. Focus on hip angle and shin placement. (25 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to posture break. Focus on executing the leg swing and figure-four lock. (50% resistance, 10 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (medium resistance): Partner attempts to posture up slightly. Focus on maintaining the figure-four and driving hips. (75% resistance, 5 reps per side)
    5. Live rolling (controlled): Attempt the Triangle Choke from closed guard against a resisting but controlled opponent. Focus on completing the setup. (90% resistance, 3 attempts per round)
    6. Live rolling (full): Apply the Triangle Choke during regular sparring, focusing on recognizing opportunities and executing the technique. (100% resistance, 1 submission attempt per round)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent is postured down in your closed guard.
    • When an opponent tries to pass your guard and gets their arm trapped across your body.
    • When an opponent attempts a standing pass and you can secure a leg and arm.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stacking Pass: If the attacker's hips aren't driven forward, the defender can stack their weight by stepping over the attacker's head and driving down, relieving pressure.
    • Slicing the Leg: The defender can attempt to 'slice' their arm through the triangle, between the attacker's shin and their own neck, to relieve pressure.
    • Arm Escape: The defender can try to pull their trapped arm out of the triangle by stepping their feet away and creating space, then bringing their arm across their body.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Triangle Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs if you are gripping the opponent's collar too high or too low, or if you're not creating enough angle with your hips. Ensure your collar grip is at the sternoclavicular joint area and that you are driving your hips forward and to the side to create a perpendicular angle to the opponent's neck, rather than a straight squeeze.

    Q: How can I Triangle Choke a much bigger opponent when they keep stacking me?

    Against a larger opponent, the key is to prevent the stack by creating a strong hip angle and securing your figure-four lock early. As you swing your leg over, immediately grab your foot and drive your hips forward and to the side. If they start to stack, use your free leg to push off their hip or shoulder to maintain your angle and prevent them from collapsing your structure.

    Q: I can't seem to finish the Triangle Choke, my opponent just escapes. What am I doing wrong?

    Finishing the Triangle Choke requires a combination of proper setup and execution. Ensure your shin is across the carotid artery, not the trachea, and that your hips are driven forward. Crucially, squeeze your knees together and use your hips to push into the choke, rather than just pulling with your arms. Make sure the opponent's trapped arm is positioned inside the triangle, not outside.

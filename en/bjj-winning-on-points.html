    Common Mistakes in Winning On Points

    Rushing the Setup

    Attempting to finish before proper mechanics are in place results in failed attempts and positional loss. Prioritize position before submission.

    Using Strength Over Technique

    Muscling through setups creates bad habits and fails against stronger or more skilled opponents. Focus on leverage and angles.

    Skipping Drilling

    Techniques only become available in live rolling after extensive drilling. Regular repetition builds the muscle memory needed for execution under pressure.

    Ignoring Defensive Reactions

    Every technique has common counters. Learn the most frequent defensive reactions and have follow-up attacks ready.

    Training Tips for Winning On Points

    Shadow Drill at Full Speed

    Perform the technique slowly, then progressively increase to competition speed while maintaining crisp mechanics. Video yourself to catch form breakdowns.

    Use a Skilled Partner

    Training with a partner who can give realistic resistance and honest feedback accelerates technical development more than repetitions with a passive uke.

    Isolate Weak Phases

    Break the technique into phases and identify which phase breaks down under pressure. Spend disproportionate drilling time on that specific phase.

    Compete in Tournaments

    Competition reveals real weaknesses that controlled training obscures. Even white belts benefit from early competitive experience.

    More Questions

    How do I adjust my strategy when I'm ahead on points but the clock is running out?

    When ahead with little time left, prioritize control and defense. Avoid risky submissions that could lead to a reversal or sweep. Focus on maintaining dominant positions and minimizing your opponent's opportunities to score.

    What are the most common points I should be aiming for early in a match?

    Early in a match, focus on establishing dominant positions that grant points, such as takedowns for 2 points, or progressing to side control or mount for 3 and 4 points respectively. These positions also offer better control and opportunities for submission.

    How can I effectively use guard passing to score points without getting swept?

    When passing guard, aim for a clean and controlled pass to a dominant position like side control or mount. Maintain a strong base and weight distribution throughout the pass to prevent your opponent from using your momentum to sweep you.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: As a complete beginner in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, how can I effectively use the 'Winning on Points Strategy' to score points when I'm feeling overwhelmed and my opponent is much larger than me?

    To score points against a larger opponent using a points strategy, focus on controlling their base and leverage. When attempting a sweep, drive your hips low and forward, using your legs to elevate their center of gravity while simultaneously pushing their weight away with your arms and shoulders to create instability.

    Q: I'm struggling to maintain control and score points in my BJJ matches. What specific body positioning should I focus on to secure a dominant position and earn points with the 'Winning on Points Strategy' when my opponent is actively resisting?

    To secure a dominant position for points, maintain a strong base by keeping your hips heavy and your weight distributed over your opponent's center of mass. When in mount, drive your chest down and forward, keeping your knees tight to their body and your elbows tucked to prevent them from creating space to escape.

    Q: During a BJJ match, I find myself constantly defending and not scoring. How can I proactively implement the 'Winning on Points Strategy' to initiate takedowns and secure points from the feet before the grappling truly begins?

    To initiate takedowns and score points, use your lead leg to disrupt your opponent's balance by performing a low single-leg or double-leg attack. Drive through with your hips and chest, keeping your head up and your shoulder driving into their hip to maintain forward momentum and secure the takedown.

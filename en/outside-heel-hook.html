Outside Heel Hook: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

BROWN★★★★★Expert
Brown Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Brown Belt Guide

The Outside Heel Hook is a powerful leg lock that attacks the opponent's ankle and knee by hyperextending the knee joint. It's typically initiated when the opponent's leg is exposed, often from positions like the 50-50 guard or when transitioning between guards.

White belts often struggle with the Outside Heel Hook due to a lack of body awareness and an overreliance on brute strength, leading to missed submissions and potential injury. They may not understand the precise angles required to isolate the heel.

The key mechanical insight is to create a fulcrum with your hips and use your legs to drive the opponent's heel away from their body, thus creating torque on the knee and ankle.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Starting Position: Assume a "honeyhole" or "outside sankaku" position, with your opponent's leg between your legs. Your hips are angled towards their hips.
    2. Grip 1 (Inside Leg): Secure a "figure-four" grip on the opponent's heel. Your right arm (if attacking their left leg) wraps around the outside of their ankle, and your left arm goes under their shin, gripping your own right bicep. Your left knee should be tight against their hamstring.
    3. Grip 2 (Outside Leg): Your right leg hooks behind their knee, with your foot pointing towards their butt. Your left leg presses against their thigh, creating a frame.
    4. Hip Drive: Drive your hips forward and slightly upwards, creating pressure into their thigh with your left leg. This isolates their leg and prevents them from turning out.
    5. Heel Extraction: With your figure-four grip, pull the opponent's heel towards your chest, while simultaneously pushing their shin away with your left leg.
    6. Knee Extension: As you pull the heel and push the shin, your right leg (hooking behind their knee) extends, forcing their knee into hyperextension.
    7. Finishing Pressure: Maintain a tight figure-four grip and continue the hip drive. The submission occurs when their knee reaches its end range of motion.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Hip Angle: Trying to finish by "pulling hard" on the heel without driving your hips forward. This can cause a "pop" in the ACL or MCL due to uncontrolled shear force on the knee.
    • Over-rotation: Allowing the opponent to turn their hips too much while you are applying pressure. This can lead to a knee-on-belly injury where the knee is twisted beyond its natural range of motion.
    • Premature Release: Releasing the figure-four grip before the opponent taps or the submission is fully applied. This allows them to escape and can lead to uncontrolled leg entanglement, potentially injuring your own knee.
    • Gripping the Foot: Gripping only the foot instead of the heel and shin area. This provides less leverage and can result in the opponent easily escaping the grip, leading to a loss of position and potential injury.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Isolation Drill: Practice the figure-four grip on your own leg, focusing on the hip drive and heel extraction motion without any resistance. (50 reps per side)
    2. Partner Resistance Drill (No Submission): Your partner lies on their back. You secure the Outside Heel Hook position. Your partner offers minimal resistance to you driving your hips and extracting the heel, but does not allow the submission to be completed. (10 sets of 30 seconds)
    3. Controlled Entry Drill: From a stable 50-50 guard, practice entering the Outside Heel Hook position. Your partner offers light resistance to your entry. (20 reps per side)
    4. Tap Drill (Low Resistance): In the Outside Heel Hook position, apply the submission with approximately 25% of your maximum force. Your partner taps immediately. (15 reps per side)
    5. Controlled Rolling (Submission Focused): Roll with a partner who understands leg locks. You actively hunt for the Outside Heel Hook, with your partner defending and allowing the submission to be applied with 50% force before tapping. (5 minutes, 3 rounds)
    6. Live Rolling (Submission Aware): Roll with a partner, actively attempting the Outside Heel Hook when the opportunity arises. Your partner defends and applies counters as they normally would. (10 minutes, 5 rounds)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have achieved a stable 50-50 guard and can control the opponent's leg.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard and their leg is exposed to your side.
    • When you have successfully swept or reversed your opponent and their leg is vulnerable.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Outside Heel Hook even if my partner doesn't tap?

    This is likely due to improper hip engagement and torque application. If you are not driving your hips forward to create the fulcrum, you are likely using rotational force on the knee itself, which is not the target of the Outside Heel Hook. Ensure your hips are driving forward and your figure-four grip is tight to isolate the heel's rotation.

    Q: How can I finish the Outside Heel Hook against a much bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paraMount. Focus on securing a very tight figure-four grip on the heel and trapping their shin with your knee. Your hip drive is crucial to create the necessary angle and isolate their leg, preventing them from using their size to power out. Do not rely on brute strength; use the mechanics to your advantage.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to an Outside Heel Hook from Open Guard?

    The optimal time is when your opponent is actively trying to pass your guard and has committed their weight forward, exposing their leg. Specifically, if they are trying a knee-slice pass and their leg is on your hip, or if they are attempting a torando pass and their leg is extended. The key is to intercept their leg before they can fully stabilize their pass.

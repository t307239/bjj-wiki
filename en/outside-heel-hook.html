Outside Heel Hook: A White Belt's Precise BJJ Guide

BROWN★★★★★Expert
Brown Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → Brown Belt Requirements

The Outside Heel Hook is a powerful leg lock applied from various positions, aiming to hyperextend the opponent's knee. It targets the fibular collateral ligament (LCL) and the posterior cruciate ligament (PCL).

White belts often rush this technique, neglecting fundamental control and creating openings for counter-attacks or injury. Focus on structure, not just brute force.

The key insight is isolating the opponent's heel and using your own body as a lever to create torque on their knee joint, specifically rotating their foot inward.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a 50-50 guard or similar leg entanglement, establish a "figure-four" grip around the opponent's ankle. Your right arm secures the ankle bone (malleolus), and your left arm passes over your right forearm, gripping your own right bicep. Your left hand should be positioned just above the opponent's heel.
    2. Position your hips so your left hip is pressed firmly into the opponent's hamstring, preventing them from rotating their leg away. Your right leg should be extended, creating a stable base.
    3. Your chest presses into the opponent's thigh or hip to maintain positional control and prevent them from bridging or posturing up.
    4. Drive your hips forward and slightly upward, initiating a "hip extension" movement. This action elevates the opponent's leg and begins to apply pressure.
    5. Simultaneously, rotate your torso counter-clockwise (if applying to their right leg) and pull your gripping arm (your left arm) towards your chest. This creates the rotational torque.
    6. Keep your left leg bent at the knee and close to your own body, acting as a fulcrum. Your right leg remains extended, providing a stable base and preventing your opponent from escaping.
    7. The primary force is applied by pulling your left elbow towards your chest while simultaneously extending your hips forward. This creates a combined rotational and hyperextension force on the knee joint.
    8. Maintain constant pressure on the opponent's hamstring with your left hip throughout the entire submission. This is crucial for preventing escape.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Hip Drive: A white belt might try to "pull" with their arms alone, failing to use their hips. This leads to insufficient torque and allows the opponent to escape or counter. The damage risk is to the opponent's knee ligaments (ACL/PCL) due to a lack of controlled force.
    • Over-Rotation: Attempting to "crank" the heel with excessive uncontrolled rotation. This can severely damage the opponent's ankle ligaments (syndesmosis) or even fracture the fibula.
    • Lack of Hip Connection: Not maintaining consistent hip pressure on the opponent's hamstring. This allows the opponent to "spin out" and escape, potentially leaving your own leg in a vulnerable position.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Isolation Drill (0% Resistance): Practice the "figure-four" grip and hip placement on your own leg or a dummy. Focus on achieving the correct grip and hip pressure. 50 reps.
    2. Partnered Grip and Hip Control (0% Resistance): With a partner, lie on your back and have them place their leg in your guard. Practice securing the figure-four grip and establishing hip connection without any pressure. 50 reps.
    3. Controlled Hip Extension Drill (25% Resistance): Partner lies on their back. You secure the figure-four grip and hip connection. Partner gently resists by trying to pull their leg away. You focus on controlled hip extension. 25 reps each side.
    4. Slow, Controlled Submission Drill (50% Resistance): Partner lies on their back. You secure the grip and hip connection. Apply the submission very slowly and with minimal pressure, communicating constantly. Partner taps early. 25 reps each side.
    5. Positional Sparring (Heel Hook Focus) (75% Resistance): From a position where the Outside Heel Hook is available (e.g., 50-50 guard), spar for 3 minutes with the explicit goal of attacking or defending the heel hook. No submissions other than heel hooks are allowed. 3 rounds.
    6. Live Rolling (90% Resistance): Incorporate the Outside Heel Hook into regular sparring. Understand when to attack and when to defend, respecting the submission and tapping early. 5 minutes per round, 5 rounds.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have successfully entered the 50-50 guard and established control of the opponent's heel.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard and their leg is extended and accessible, creating an opportunity for a sweep and immediate heel hook.
    • During scrambles where you can isolate one of the opponent's legs and trap it before they can establish a solid base.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Spinning Escape: If the opponent attempts the Outside Heel Hook, the primary defense is to "spin" your body away from the direction of the torque. This involves rotating your hips and shoulders in the opposite direction of the heel hook, effectively releasing the pressure. You must maintain a strong base with your free leg.
    • Knee Shield/Frame: If the opponent is attempting to isolate your heel, use your free leg as a "knee shield" to create space and prevent them from fully securing the figure-four grip. Actively push their hips away with your knee and ankle to create separation.
    • Leg Release/Hand Fight: If the opponent has a grip on your heel, your immediate priority is to "hand fight" and break their grip. Dig your fingers into their grip, pry their hands open, or use your free hand to push their arms away. Simultaneously, try to pull your heel out by straightening your leg and rotating your foot.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my opponent's knee feel like it will explode when I try the Outside Heel Hook?

    This sensation often arises from improper hip mechanics. If you're relying solely on your arms to pull, you're creating a sharp, shearing force rather than a controlled rotational one. Ensure your hips are extending forward and pressing into their hamstring; this creates a stable base and allows your body's weight and leverage to apply the pressure, making the submission smoother and more effective.

    Q: How can I apply the Outside Heel Hook against a much larger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paraMount. Focus on getting a clean "figure-four" grip and anchoring your hips firmly into their hamstring to prevent them from using their mass to escape. Drive your hips forward strongly and use your torso rotation to apply the torque. Don't try to overpower them; out-leverage them by controlling their heel and using your body as a lever.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt an Outside Heel Hook in BJJ sparring?

    The most opportune moments are typically when the opponent is off-balance, attempting to pass your guard, or during scrambles. Specifically, if you can achieve a stable 50-50 guard or isolate one of their legs during a transition, you create a clear pathway. Avoid attempting it when they have a strong base and are actively defending your legs, as this increases your risk of injury.

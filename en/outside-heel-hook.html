The Outside Heel Hook is a submission targeting the knee and ankle, typically initiated from a 50-50 guard or similar leg entanglement. It aims to hyperextend the opponent's knee and twist their ankle for a tap.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of control, leading to uncontrolled movements and potential injury. They may grip incorrectly or fail to secure proper body positioning.

The key insight is creating a strong, stable "figure four" with your legs and arms, using your hips to generate torque and isolate the opponent's heel.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a 50-50 guard, secure your opponent's leg: your right leg hooks behind their left thigh, and your left leg goes over their left shin. Your left foot is positioned on their hip or thigh to maintain distance.
    2. Grip their heel with your right hand, thumb up, fingers curled around the heel bone. Your left hand grips your own right bicep, creating a "figure four" around their ankle.
    3. Position your hips perpendicular to your opponent’s leg, ensuring your left hip is closer to their knee than your right hip.
    4. Drive your left knee towards the mat, creating pressure and preventing them from straightening their leg.
    5. Scoot your hips back slightly, creating tension on the heel hook.
    6. Engage your core and slightly lift your hips, driving your chest towards their knee.
    7. Simultaneously, pull your right heel towards your glutes while pushing your opponent's knee away with your left leg, creating a rotational force on the ankle and knee.
    8. Maintain a strong "figure four" with your arms to prevent them from escaping the grip.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to "pull hard" on the opponent's leg without proper hip alignment: This can cause uncontrolled torque on the opponent's knee and ankle, leading to ACL or MCL tears.
    • Gripping the opponent's foot by the toes or instep instead of the heel bone: This provides poor leverage and increases the risk of slipping. The correct grip is on the heel bone, thumb up.
    • Allowing the opponent's knee to straighten completely: This removes the necessary flexion for the submission and can lead to wrist or elbow injuries for the attacker when they try to force it.
    • Not maintaining hip perpendicularity: If your hips are parallel, you lose the leverage to generate torque, potentially leading to a compromised position for you and an increased risk of knee injury for them.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling of the "figure four" grip and hip positioning: 10 repetitions each side, 0% resistance.
    2. Partner drilling the grip and hip alignment with no leg movement: 10 repetitions each side, 10% resistance.
    3. Partner drilling the leg entanglement and grip setup, with the "victim" offering minimal resistance to the initial setup: 10 repetitions each side, 25% resistance.
    4. Partner drilling the full Outside Heel Hook mechanics without finishing, focusing on maintaining control and body position: 10 repetitions each side, 50% resistance.
    5. Partner drilling with controlled submission attempts, stopping immediately on verbal tap or feeling significant tension: 5 repetitions each side, 75% resistance.
    6. Live rolling with specific focus on attacking the Outside Heel Hook when the opportunity arises, emphasizing safety and control: 3 rounds, 90% resistance.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have achieved a stable 50-50 guard and have isolated one of your opponent's legs.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard and their weight is distributed in a way that exposes their heel.
    • During scrambles where you can quickly secure a leg entanglement and prevent them from establishing dominant control.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • "Heel Hook Defense (Defensive Turn)": When the Outside Heel Hook is applied, the defender should immediately turn their hips away from the attacker, creating slack in the hook. They then attempt to bring their free leg over the attacker's arm to break the "figure four" grip. This requires a quick hip escape and a bridging motion.
    • "Leg Release (Stacking Counter)": The defender can attempt to stack their weight onto the attacker, driving their hips forward and their shoulders down. This can break the attacker's control and relieve pressure on the heel. This is often combined with an attempt to free the trapped leg by pushing the attacker's hip away.
    • "Escape to Standing (If Possible)": In certain scenarios, if the attacker's control is not fully established, the defender might attempt to roll or drive forward to force a standing position. This requires explosive movement and a strong base to disengage.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try to do an Outside Heel Hook as a white belt?

    Your ankle likely hurts because you are not properly isolating your opponent's heel. The "figure four" grip with your arms and the precise hip positioning are crucial. Without them, you're likely applying twisting force to the wrong part of the foot or ankle, leading to a sprain instead of a submission. Ensure your grip is firmly on the heel bone and your hips are perpendicular to their leg.

    Q: How can I apply the Outside Heel Hook against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paraMount. Focus on creating a very tight "figure four" grip and ensuring your hips are as close to their knee as possible. Use your entire body weight, driving your chest towards their knee and scooting your hips back. The key is to generate torque through your hips and core, not just arm strength, which a larger opponent can easily resist.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt an Outside Heel Hook, and how do I avoid giving up my back?

    The Outside Heel Hook is best attempted when you have achieved a stable 50-50 guard or a similar leg entanglement where you have control of one of your opponent's legs. To avoid giving up your back, maintain active hips and always keep your body perpendicular to their leg. If they try to spin out, use your free leg to control their hip and prevent them from turning away completely.

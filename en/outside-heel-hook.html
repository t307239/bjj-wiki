Outside Heel Hook: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

The Outside Heel Hook is a submission targeting the opponent's heel, initiated when you can isolate and control one of their legs.

White belts often struggle due to lack of control and fear, leading to improper application and injury risk.

The key is creating a strong "figure four" grip on the leg and using hip pressure to hyperextend the knee and ankle simultaneously.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Setup: From a 50-50 guard or similar leg entanglement, ensure your opponent's heel is accessible.
    2. Grip 1 (Primary): Place your forearm across the opponent's shin, just above the ankle bone. Your hand cups the heel.
    3. Grip 2 (Secondary): With your other arm, reach *under* the opponent's thigh and grip your own bicep, creating a "figure four" around their calf.
    4. Hip Angle: Turn your hips away from the opponent's trapped leg, approximately 90 degrees, to create leverage.
    5. Weight Distribution: Drive your hips forward and slightly downward, pressing your chest into their thigh.
    6. Knee Drive: Extend your leg that is trapping their calf *away* from their body, creating separation and tension.
    7. Finishing Pressure: Simultaneously, pull your forearm (cupping the heel) *towards* your chest while maintaining hip pressure. This isolates and hyperextends the heel.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Grip Application: Gripping too high on the calf or ankle, allowing the opponent's heel to escape, leading to a loss of control and potential escape.
    • Knee Hyperextension Injury: Trying to "pull hard" without proper hip engagement can cause excessive torque on the knee joint, leading to ACL/MCL tears.
    • Ankle Sprain/Tear: Applying rotational force without isolating the heel correctly can lead to severe ankle ligament damage.
    • Hip Pressure Misalignment: Not turning hips sufficiently or driving them into the opponent's thigh fails to create the necessary leverage, making the submission ineffective and risking your own position.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Movement (0%): Practice the "figure four" grip and hip turn with no resistance, focusing on body positioning. (20 reps)
    2. Partnered Static Hold (25%): Your partner lies on their back, you isolate a leg and achieve the "figure four" grip. Hold for 10 seconds. (10 reps)
    3. Controlled Leg Isolation (50%): Partner offers slight resistance as you isolate the leg and establish the grip. Focus on smooth transitions. (15 reps)
    4. Tap-to-Tap Drill (75%): You attempt the Outside Heel Hook, partner taps immediately upon feeling pressure. Focus on technique, not speed. (20 reps)
    5. Controlled Submission (90%): Attempt the Outside Heel Hook with a partner who provides controlled resistance and taps early. Focus on the finishing mechanics. (15 reps)
    6. Live Rolling Application (100%): In rolling, look for opportunities to initiate the Outside Heel Hook, prioritizing safety and tapping if caught. (5 minutes)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent attempts a leg lock on you and you can reverse into a 50-50 or similar position.
    • When your opponent is on their back and you can effectively isolate one of their legs from a standing or kneeling position.
    • When you have successfully swept an opponent and they are turtling, presenting their legs.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Escape by Rotating Hips: If your opponent attempts the Outside Heel Hook, immediately rotate your hips *towards* their trapped leg, relieving pressure on your knee and ankle.
    • Stomp and Pull: Quickly stomp your free foot down and pull your trapped leg out of the "figure four" grip, creating space to escape.
    • Guard Retention/Reguard: If the submission is not fully locked, focus on bringing your knees to your chest and establishing a guard to prevent further leg isolation.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Outside Heel Hook?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are not properly engaging your hips to create leverage. Instead of a strong hip drive and rotation, you might be relying on brute pulling strength. Ensure your hips are turned 90 degrees away from the opponent's leg and you are driving your hips *forward* and *down*, not just pulling with your arms. This distributes the force correctly.

    Q: How can I apply an Outside Heel Hook against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, the key is precise body mechanics and leverage, not strength. Focus on securing a tight "figure four" grip and driving your hips forward with significant force. Use your body weight to create the necessary hyperextension. Do not rely on your arm strength to finish; your hips and core engagement are paraMount for generating the torque.

    Q: When is the best time to go for an Outside Heel Hook in a match?

    The Outside Heel Hook is best attempted when you have achieved a dominant leg entanglement, such as the 50-50 guard, or when you can isolate a Single Leg effectively. It's also a strong option when your opponent is trying to pass your guard and you can trap their leg during the process. Avoid attempting it when you are out of position or your own legs are vulnerable.

Your First Steps in Half Guard BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

Half Guard is a defensive and offensive position where one opponent has one leg trapped between their legs, while the other leg is free. It's a crucial stepping stone from Closed Guard to more open guard systems.

White belts often struggle here by over-relying on brute strength and neglecting fundamental body mechanics, leading to easy passes or submission attempts.

The core principle is using your trapped leg to create a strong frame and off-balance your opponent, enabling sweeps or transitions.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Opponent is in your guard, knee-on-belly or passing.
    2. Sit up, bring your right knee across their left hip. Your right shin is parallel to the mat.
    3. Trap their left leg between your right shin and thigh. Your left leg is free, positioned behind their right knee.
    4. Grip their left hip with your left hand, palm facing inward, fingers pointing up.
    5. Grip their left bicep or collar with your right hand, depending on their posture.
    6. Drive your hips towards the mat, creating a strong base and forward pressure.
    7. Use your trapped leg's knee to push into their hip, creating a wedge and preventing them from easily stacking you.
    8. Your free left leg acts as a lever to control distance and initiate sweeps.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Trying to pull opponent's head down with your free hand while their weight is on you: This can strain your neck ligaments and cause cervical hyperextension. Instead, focus on controlling their hip and bicep to maintain posture.
    • Extending your trapped leg straight out: This exposes your knee joint to hyperextension or knee-bar attacks. Keep your knee bent and your shin actively pushing into their hip.
    • Allowing your free leg to be hooked or controlled: This removes your leverage and makes you vulnerable to passes. Keep your free leg actively behind their knee or ready to shrimp out.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg trap and hip control motion with no resistance (10 reps per side).
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Opponent lies on their back. You practice entering Half Guard and establishing the hip and bicep grips (5 reps per side).
    3. Partner drill (light resistance - 25%): Opponent tries to lightly flatten you. You focus on maintaining your frame and hip connection.
    4. Partner drill (50%): Opponent attempts basic passes (e.g., knee slice). You practice shrimping and maintaining the leg trap.
    5. Partner drill (75%): Opponent attempts passes with moderate pressure. You focus on creating space and attempting a basic sweep (e.g., hip bump).
    6. Live rolling (90-100%): Integrate Half Guard into rolling, focusing on maintaining the position and attempting sweeps or transitions.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent attempts a knee-slice pass and their knee is near your hip.
    • When an opponent tries to pass your guard from a standing or kneeling position and gets too close.
    • When you are caught in a transition and need to stabilize your position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Control: Maintain a strong grip on the opponent's hip with your free hand. This prevents them from posturing up and driving forward. Your hip angle should be tight.
    • Leg Frame: Actively use your trapped leg to push into the opponent's hip or thigh. This creates space and prevents them from collapsing your guard. Keep your knee pointed towards their hip.
    • Shrimp Movement: If the opponent tries to flatten you, use your hips to shrimp away, creating space and re-angling your body to maintain the leg trap.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to hold Half Guard?

    Your knee might hurt if you are extending your trapped leg too much, putting undue stress on the ligaments. Ensure your knee remains bent, with your shin actively pressing into the opponent's hip, creating a stable frame. This biomechanical engagement protects the joint and utilizes your leg as a lever.

    Q: How can I use Half Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, prioritize establishing a strong frame with your trapped leg and controlling their hip. Use your free hand to grip their bicep or collar, preventing them from posturing up. Focus on creating small angles and using your body weight to off-balance them for sweeps, rather than trying to overpower them.

    Q: When should I try to escape Half Guard versus attacking from it?

    You should aim to attack from Half Guard when you have established a solid leg trap and hip connection, and the opponent's weight distribution is compromised. If you feel yourself being flattened, stacked, or their pass is progressing too quickly, your priority shifts to escaping by shrimping out and regaining guard.

Your First Steps in Half Guard: A White Belt BJJ Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

Half Guard is a defensive position where one of your legs is trapped between your opponent's legs. It's a crucial stepping stone from being completely flattened out.

Many white belts struggle because they try to muscle their way out or simply lie flat, inviting pressure and passes.

The core principle is creating a strong frame with your trapped leg and using your hips to generate movement and control.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From a supine position with your opponent in your guard, identify one leg trapped between their knees. Your other leg remains free.
    2. Secure a "collar grip" on your opponent's gi top or a "wrist grip" on their arm if no-gi.
    3. Place your free foot on your opponent's hip, creating a "frame" with your shin and knee perpendicular to their torso.
    4. Drive your trapped leg's knee across your opponent's body, aiming to get your knee inside their hip line.
    5. Simultaneously, rotate your hips to your side, creating a "shrimp" or "hip escape" motion.
    6. Use your free foot's pressure on their hip to create space and angle your body away from their chest.
    7. Maintain a "bulletproof" structure by keeping your hips tight and your frames active, preventing them from collapsing your guard.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly extending your trapped leg: This overstretches the knee joint, risking ACL/MCL tears as the opponent drives forward.
    • Lying completely flat on your back: This removes your base and allows the opponent to easily apply crushing pressure, risking rib or spinal injury.
    • Grabbing the opponent's ankle with your free hand: This opens your arm to kimura or armbar attacks and removes your hip frame, risking shoulder dislocation.
    • Over-gripping with your free hand on their hip: This can lead to wrist sprains or make it difficult to disengage and re-frame effectively.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Hip Escape Drill: From a supine position, practice shrimping (hip escape) 10 times, focusing on hip rotation and bringing your trapped knee across. 0% resistance.
    2. Frame and Shrimp Drill: Place a pillow or training dummy between your legs. Practice shrimping and then placing your free foot on the pillow's hip to create a frame. 10 reps each side. 0% resistance.
    3. Partnered Frame and Shrimp: With a passive partner, practice shrimping and framing their hip. Partner remains stationary. 10 reps each side. 25% resistance.
    4. Basic Half Guard Retention: Partner attempts to pass your guard. Focus on keeping your knee inside their hip and shrimping to maintain distance. 10 reps each side. 50% resistance.
    5. Half Guard Sweep Attempt (Passive): Partner allows you to establish a basic half guard. Practice your chosen sweep (e.g., scissor sweep) with their passive resistance. 5 reps. 75% resistance.
    6. Live Rolling: Implement Half Guard defensively and offensively in a controlled roll. Focus on maintaining frames and seeking opportunities to sweep or transition. 1 minute rounds.

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent has successfully passed one side of your closed guard and your leg is trapped.
    • When you are on your back and your opponent is posturing over you, attempting to flatten your guard.
    • When you have just been swept and are trying to recover guard or prevent a full pass.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee Shield Creation: Use your free leg's knee to push into your opponent's hip and chest, creating a strong frame that prevents them from collapsing your guard or advancing their pass.
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): Actively drive your hips away from your opponent, creating space and angling your body. This makes it difficult for them to maintain their pressure and allows you to potentially re-establish a stronger guard.
    • Underhook Acquisition: Reach under your opponent's armpit with your free arm and secure an "underhook" grip. This helps control their posture and provides a leverage point for sweeps and transitions.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to hold Half Guard?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are letting your opponent's weight drive directly into your knee joint. Instead of letting your leg be straight, bend your trapped knee and drive it across your opponent's body, aiming to get your knee inside their hip line. Use your free foot to create a frame on their hip to push them away and maintain space.

    Q: How can I use Half Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on creating frames and using your hips to move. Never let them flatten you out completely. Use your free leg to establish a "knee shield" on their hip, pushing them away. Actively "shrimp" your hips to create space and angle your body, making it harder for them to utilize their weight advantage.

    Q: Should I try to keep my trapped leg straight or bent in Half Guard?

    You should almost always keep your trapped leg bent at the knee. A bent knee allows you to create a "knee shield" and drive it across your opponent's body, preventing them from passing. A straight, extended leg is vulnerable to pressure and injury, and offers no offensive or defensive leverage.

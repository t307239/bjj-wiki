Half Guard is a defensive BJJ position where one of your legs is between your opponent’s legs, while your other leg is outside. It aims to prevent your opponent from achieving dominant positions like Side Control or mount.

White belts often struggle by framing incorrectly or trying to 'muscle' their way out, leading to pressure and potential submissions.

The key is to create a strong 'frame' with your legs and hips, using leverage to control distance and create opportunities for sweeps or transitions.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. From bottom position, facing your opponent, control their near-side hip with your far-side hand (e.g., right hand on opponent's right hip).
    2. Trap their near-side leg (e.g., opponent's right leg) between your legs, with your right shin across their thigh and your left foot on the mat or hip.
    3. Drive your hips back and to your left, creating a 45-degree angle between your torso and your opponent's torso.
    4. Secure your far-side arm (e.g., left arm) around their waist or underhook their far-side armpit, ensuring your elbow is tight to their body.
    5. Use your trapped leg (right leg) to create a 'wall' by flexing your knee and pushing your shin into their inner thigh, preventing them from passing.
    6. Your free leg (left leg) should be positioned to either hook their hip for leverage or to push their knee away, depending on their weight distribution.
    7. Maintain a constant 'pressure' by driving your hips back and slightly upwards, creating a wedge effect with your trapped leg.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Attempting to 'muscle' your way out by extending your legs straight: This can hyperextend your knees, damaging the ACL and MCL ligaments.
    • Allowing your opponent to flatten you out by not creating hip angle: This compromises your base and opens you up to shoulder pressure, risking a shoulder impingement.
    • Gripping your own shin or ankle for control: This creates a weak grip and can lead to wrist hyperextension, damaging the carpal ligaments when your opponent applies pressure.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice entering and maintaining Half Guard from bottom position against no resistance. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Opponent lies on their back, you practice establishing and holding Half Guard. (5 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Opponent attempts to pass your guard from Half Guard. You focus on maintaining the position and creating frames. (5 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner drill (medium resistance): Opponent attempts to pass or achieve side control. You practice sweeps from Half Guard. (5 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Live rolling (positional sparring): Start in Half Guard bottom and focus on either sweeping or transitioning to a better position. (3 rounds x 2 minutes, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Full rolling, focusing on using Half Guard to survive passes and create offensive opportunities. (3 rounds x 3 minutes, 90% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent has successfully passed your initial guard and is attempting to establish side control.
    • When you are on your back and your opponent is postured up or attempting a knee-slice pass.
    • When you have lost a dominant guard position and need to establish a safe intermediate position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Sweep: Execute a 'Hip Bump Sweep' by driving your hips up and to the side, creating an angle for you to shrimp and come up on top.
    • Transition to 'Deep Half Guard': If your opponent is pressuring heavily, slide your trapped leg deeper and hook their leg from the inside, creating a more dominant control.
    • Transition to 'Butterfly Guard': If your opponent is postured up, try to get both hooks in from Half Guard and transition to Butterfly Guard for sweep opportunities.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do Half Guard in BJJ?

    Knee pain in Half Guard often stems from hyperextension or twisting. When your opponent applies pressure, avoid letting your trapped leg straighten completely. Instead, keep a slight bend in your knee and use your shin to actively push against their thigh, creating a stable frame that protects your knee joint. Also, ensure your hip angle is correct to avoid twisting forces.

    Q: How do I set up a Half Guard sweep against a bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is key. Focus on creating a strong 'frame' with your trapped leg by actively pushing their knee away or posting your foot on their hip. Use your far-side arm to secure an Underhook or control their waist. Then, drive your hips back and to the side to create an angle, making it easier to shrimp and sweep them. Don't try to lift them; focus on off-balancing.

    Q: When should I transition from Half Guard to another position in BJJ?

    You should transition from Half Guard when your opponent is consistently defending your sweeps or when you see an opening. If they are pressuring heavily into you, consider transitioning to Deep Half Guard or Butterfly Guard. If they are posturing up and giving you space, look for opportunities to transition to a more offensive guard like X-Guard or even attempt a sweep to come up to your feet. Always be aware of their weight distribution.

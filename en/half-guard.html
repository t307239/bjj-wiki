Half Guard is a defensive BJJ position where one of your legs is trapped between your opponent's legs, while the other leg is free. It's primarily used to prevent immediate Guard Passes and create opportunities for sweeps or submissions.

White belts often struggle because they treat it as a static position, allowing their opponent to establish heavy pressure and control. They forget the active, dynamic nature of the guard.

The core principle of Half Guard is creating a strong frame with your free leg and hips to control distance and off-balance your opponent, enabling you to reclaim guard or attack.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Opponent is in your guard, their knee is inside your hip line, trapping your right leg between their thighs. Your left leg is free.
    2. Establish an underhook with your left arm, gripping their left hip bone or belt line. Your right arm frames against their right thigh, preventing them from advancing their knee further.
    3. Dig your left foot into the mat, angling your hips approximately 45 degrees away from your opponent's chest. This creates space and leverage.
    4. Use your free left leg to hook their right leg's ankle or shin, preventing them from easily stepping over your trapped leg.
    5. Maintain a strong base with your left foot on the mat, keeping your hips elevated slightly off the ground.
    6. Your right forearm should maintain pressure against their right thigh, acting as a wedge to prevent them from passing.
    7. Drive your hips forward slightly as you pull their hip towards you with your underhook, creating tension and off-balancing them.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrectly extending your trapped leg: This exposes your knee and ankle ligaments (ACL, MCL, PCL) to hyperextension and twisting injuries, especially during leg entanglement situations.
    • Allowing opponent to flatten you: When your hips are flush with the mat, you lose all leverage and create a direct line for passes. Keep your hips slightly elevated and angled.
    • Over-gripping with your free leg's foot: Gripping too tightly around the shin or ankle can lead to wrist and finger joint strain for you, and allows the opponent to easily break your grip.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip angle, frame placement, and underhook grip without a partner. (5 minutes, 0% resistance).
    2. Partner drill - Passive guard: Partner lies on their back, you practice establishing Half Guard and maintaining the frame and underhook. (5 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    3. Partner drill - Basic sweep setup: Partner lies on their back, you establish Half Guard, create the hip angle, and practice the initial hip drive for a sweep. (5 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    4. Partner drill - Guard retention: Partner attempts to pass from Half Guard (e.g., knee slice). You focus on using your frame and free leg to prevent the pass. (5 minutes, 75% resistance).
    5. Live rolling - Controlled sparring: Implement Half Guard defensively and offensively in a rolling scenario with limited submission attempts. (3 minutes, 90% resistance).
    6. Live rolling - Full sparring: Apply Half Guard techniques in a full rolling situation, focusing on sweep and submission opportunities. (5 minutes, 100% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent successfully passes your initial guard and traps one of your legs.
    • When you attempt a sweep from another guard and end up in a compromised position.
    • When your opponent is posturing up aggressively after you've lost your full guard control.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee Shield: Use your free leg to create a strong barrier against the opponent's chest, pushing them away and maintaining distance. Your foot should be flat against their hip or torso.
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): Actively push off the mat with your free foot and drive your hips away from the opponent. This creates space to bring your trapped leg free and potentially re-establish full guard.
    • Underhook and Hip Control: Secure a deep underhook with your arm and use your gripping hand to control their hip. This prevents them from posturing up and allows you to begin turning into them to reclaim guard.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get into Half Guard?

    Knee pain in Half Guard often stems from your trapped leg being forced into an unnatural angle. Ensure your free leg is actively creating a frame against the opponent's hip, and that your trapped leg is not twisted. Keep your hips angled away from your opponent, not square, to protect your knee joint.

    Q: How can I use Half Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on controlling their base and using your frames to create space. Your Underhook is crucial for preventing them from crushing you. Use your free leg to hook their ankle and prevent them from driving forward. Think about off-balancing them with hip movement rather than trying to overpower them.

    Q: When should I try to sweep from Half Guard vs. trying to get back to full guard?

    If your opponent has a strong underhook and is controlling your hips, prioritizing getting back to full guard is often safer. If you can establish a strong frame with your free leg and an underhook on their hip, and they are slightly off-balanced, a sweep attempt is a good option. Assess their posture and base before committing to a sweep.

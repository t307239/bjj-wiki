The heel hook is a submission targeting the knee joint by applying rotational pressure to the ankle and foot.

It is typically initiated from leg entanglement positions like the 50-50 guard or inside sankaku.

White belts often fail by applying uncontrolled torque, leading to injury.

The core principle is to isolate the opponent's heel and use your body to generate a fulcrum, creating torque on the knee.

This involves precise hip engagement and body positioning, not brute force.

Understanding the lever action is key to controlling the submission safely and effectively.

Success hinges on controlling the opponent's hip and ankle while creating a stable base.

Your hips must drive forward to create the necessary angle of attack on the knee.

This technique requires refinement and control, not aggression, to be effective and safe.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Secure an Inside Sankaku (triangle) around the opponent's leg with your legs, ensuring your shin is across their hamstring.
    2. Grip the opponent's heel with your hand, thumb pointing upwards, creating a "palm-to-heel" connection.
    3. Position your hips to be perpendicular to the opponent's femur, creating a strong base.
    4. Drive your hips forward and slightly upward, creating a fulcrum point at your own hip.
    5. Use your free leg to push against the opponent's hip or thigh to prevent them from escaping.
    6. Apply pressure by flexing your foot and pulling your gripped heel towards your chest, simultaneously extending your hips.
    7. Maintain a stable base by keeping your weight distributed evenly over your hips and lower back.
    8. Control the opponent's hip with your opposite arm to prevent them from turning out of the submission.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying pressure before the heel is fully isolated: This can cause hyperextension or lateral stress on the opponent's knee ligament (ACL/MCL).
    • Correct: Ensure the heel is securely gripped and the leg is properly trapped before applying torque.
    • "Pulling" the heel with straight arms: This puts excessive strain on your own shoulder and elbow, while inefficiently transferring force to the opponent's knee.
    • Correct: Use your hips and core to drive the submission, keeping your arms bent and engaged.
    • Allowing the opponent to turn their hips away: This reduces the torque on the knee and can lead to the opponent escaping or counter-attacking.
    • Correct: Use your free leg to control their hip and maintain a perpendicular angle.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg trapping and heel grip mechanics without a partner. Focus on hip movement and body positioning (10 reps per side).
    2. Partner drill (static): With a compliant partner, practice the grip and hip drive motion without any resistance (10 reps per side).
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance; focus on securing the position and driving hips. (5 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Partner drill (moderate resistance): Partner attempts to defend slightly. Focus on maintaining control and applying controlled pressure. (5 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Live rolling (controlled): Attempt the heel hook in live rolling with a clear focus on position and safety. Tap early and often. (3 attempts per side, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling (full): Integrate the heel hook attempt into normal rolling, understanding when to attack and when to disengage. (As opportunities arise, 90% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • From the 50-50 guard when you have achieved a strong leg entanglement.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard and their leg becomes accessible.
    • During scrambles where you can secure a dominant leg position.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • "Stacking" defense: The opponent drives their weight down onto your hips, relieving pressure on the knee.
    • Mechanics: The defender must create a strong base and drive their hips down forcefully, aiming to place their chest on your hips.
    • "Escape by turning" defense: The opponent attempts to turn their body away, breaking the rotational pressure.
    • Mechanics: The defender needs to rotate their hips and upper body away from the attacker's hip, creating space to disengage.
    • "Straightening the leg" defense: The opponent actively tries to straighten their leg to relieve the torque.
    • Mechanics: The attacker must maintain control of the heel and continue to apply pressure, preventing the leg from straightening.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do a Heel Hook?

    Your knee is likely hurting because you are not isolating the heel correctly or you are applying torque before securing the submission. The heel hook targets the rotational stability of the knee joint. If you are not trapping the opponent's leg properly, or if you are applying pressure with straight legs and no hip drive, you are essentially twisting the knee in an unnatural way, which can injure both you and your training partner. Ensure your shin is across the hamstring and your hips are driving forward to create the correct lever action.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paramount. Do not rely on strength. Focus on securing a tight inside sankaku and a strong heel grip. Use your hips to create the fulcrum and drive forward, keeping your base wide. Control their hip with your free leg to prevent them from bridging or turning out. The key is to make their limb the lever and your body the fulcrum, allowing you to apply torque effectively without needing superior strength.

    Q: The optimal time to attack with a heel hook is when the opponent's leg is exposed and you can establish a secure entanglement, such as the 50-50 guard or when they are trying to pass your guard. Avoid attempting it when you are in a compromised position or when the opponent has a strong base. The submission is most effective when the opponent is focused on other aspects of the fight, making their defensive reactions slower. Prioritize securing the position before committing to the submission.

🐢 Turtle Position Guide

🥋 White ★★★☆☆ Beginner

The turtle position is a defensive posture in BJJ that, when used correctly, becomes an offensive tool for back takes and guard recovery rather than a passive submission target.

Contents

    What Is the Turtle Position?

    The turtle position is when you are on all fours (knees and hands on the mat) with your back facing upward. It is often used when defending a guard pass, after a takedown, or when recovering from a bad position. While seemingly defensive, it is a launchpad for multiple attacks.

    Defensive Turtle Survival

    Defensive turtle fundamentals: keep your elbows tight to your sides to prevent hook insertion, keep your head low to prevent choke attacks, use constant motion (never be static), post on a foot or two to create better angles, and always keep your hips mobile.

    Attacking from Turtle

    The turtle position enables you to attack: stand-up and attack your opponent's legs, roll to guard by tucking and rolling to the side, take their back by rolling forward under them, or attack with a front headlock/arm drag when they reach over you.

    Defending Turtle Attacks

    Common turtle attacks to defend: clock choke (grip one arm, walk around and apply collar choke), bow-and-arrow from turtle (reach over your shoulder), back take (they insert hooks). Against clock choke: immediately circle toward them; against back take: strip hooks and stand up.

    Turtle as a System

    Elite wrestlers and judokas use turtle as a base to attack rather than just survive. Gordon Ryan has used turtle to drag opponents to their back. Judokas use turtle to set up lateral drop suplexes. Approach turtle as position zero in a larger system rather than a last resort.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Is turtle dangerous in BJJ?

    Turtle can be dangerous if you are passive — being static in turtle makes you vulnerable to clock chokes, back takes, and guillotines. Active turtle with constant motion and attacks is much safer and actually offensive.

    How do I take the back from turtle?

    Two main entries: the "seat belt from turtle" (reach one arm over the shoulder, one arm under the armpit, insert hooks from behind), or the "roll under" (when they turtle, dive under them and roll forward, ending with their back).

    What is the difference between turtle and a sprawl?

    A sprawl is a wrestling defense against a takedown — you kick your hips back to prevent being taken down. Turtle is a ground position when you are on hands and knees. Transitioning from a sprawl into a tight turtle can be a useful sequence.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is the main goal of the turtle position in BJJ?

    The primary goal of the turtle position is to protect your back and prevent your opponent from establishing dominant control. It's a defensive posture that allows you to regroup, escape bad positions, or set up your own offensive sequences.

    How do I defend my neck when in turtle position?

    To defend your neck, tuck your chin tightly to your chest and keep your head up. Wrap your arms around your knees or forearms, creating a strong, compact shell that makes it difficult for your opponent to attack your neck or arms.

    What are common transitions from the turtle position?

    From turtle, you can transition to various offensive or defensive movements. Common options include bridging to escape, turning to your knees to stand up, or attacking your opponent's legs for a sweep or submission once they commit to their attack.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I'm trying to defend in the 🐢 Turtle Position Guide?

    Neck pain in the turtle often stems from tucking your chin too tightly to your chest, creating excessive cervical flexion and pressure on the vertebrae. Instead, aim for a neutral spine and tuck your chin slightly, allowing your forehead and shoulders to absorb impact and create a strong defensive shell.

    Q: How can I effectively prevent my opponent from passing my guard when I'm in the 🐢 Turtle Position Guide?

    To prevent guard passes from turtle, focus on maintaining a tight base by keeping your hips low and connected to the mat. Use your forearms to create frames against your opponent's hips and shoulders, preventing them from establishing dominant pressure and allowing you to shrimp your hips away.

    Q: What's the best way to escape the 🐢 Turtle Position Guide when a bigger opponent is on top of me?

    To escape a larger opponent from turtle, prioritize creating space by bridging your hips up and away from their weight. Simultaneously, drive your shoulder into their hip or knee, using your body weight to off-balance them and create an opening to roll to your knees or back.

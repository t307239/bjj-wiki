Your First Rear Naked Choke: A BJJ Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Rear Naked Choke is applied from the back control position, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain. It's a fundamental submission that can end a match quickly.

Many white belts struggle because they rely on brute force instead of correct mechanics, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury. Focus on the setup and precise grip placement.

The key to the Rear Naked Choke is creating a stable platform with your body and using your biceps to compress the carotid arteries, not just squeezing with your arms.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Secure Back Mount: Ensure both hooks are in (feet on the inside of opponent's thighs) and your chest is firmly connected to their upper back. Your hips should be low and heavy, preventing them from turning in.
    2. Establish the Collar Grip: Reach your non-choking arm under their neck, palm facing upwards. Grab the fabric of their gi at the base of their neck, ensuring your hand is deep.
    3. Secure your bicep: Place the bicep of your choking arm against the side of their neck, just below the jawline. Your forearm should run across the front of their neck.
    4. Lock your grip: Bring your free hand to the bicep of your choking arm. Your fingers should interlock or your palm should cup the bicep, creating a secure "figure four" grip.
    5. Squeeze and Compress: Keeping your head tight to their head (preventing them from turning), begin to gently drive your locked arms forward. This compresses the carotid arteries on both sides of their neck.
    6. Apply Hip Pressure: Lower your hips further into their back. This creates a strong base and prevents them from escaping by bridging or rolling.
    7. Maintain Head Control: Your head should be in contact with their head or shoulder. This prevents them from creating space to turn their head or escape the choke.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Choking Arm Placement: Applying the choke with your forearm across the front of your own bicep instead of the opponent's neck. This can strain your shoulder ligaments (rotator cuff) due to improper leverage and hyperextension.
    • Over-squeezing with Forearms: Using only forearm pressure to squeeze the neck, rather than the biceps and the opponent's own shoulder. This can hyperextend the elbow joint or cause wrist sprains due to excessive, uncontrolled force.
    • Jerking Head Downwards: Pulling the opponent's head down violently with your choking arm without proper body connection. This can lead to neck hyperextension or facet joint injury in the cervical spine.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Grip Practice: From a seated position, practice the "figure four" grip and bicep connection. 10 reps per side.
    2. Partnered Grip Isolation: With a compliant partner on their hands and knees, practice establishing the collar grip and figure-four lock without any pressure. 10 reps.
    3. Back Mount Entry and Grip: Practice entering back mount and establishing the choke grips from a neutral position. Focus on control. 5 reps per side.
    4. Controlled Choke Application (0% Resistance): From back mount, apply the choke with extremely light pressure, focusing on the feeling of compression. Partner taps immediately. 5 reps per side.
    5. Controlled Choke Application (25% Resistance): Apply the choke with minimal resistance from the partner, focusing on maintaining your structure. Partner taps. 5 reps per side.
    6. Live Rolling (50% Resistance): Attempt the Rear Naked Choke in live rolling, allowing the partner to defend naturally but without full resistance. Focus on the setup and execution. 3 rounds.

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When you have successfully secured Back Mount with both hooks in.
    • When the opponent is turtled and you can secure a collar grip from behind.
    • During scrambles where you can establish dominant back control.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Defending the Collar Grip: When the opponent attempts the collar grip, immediately pull your collar away and cover your neck with your chin and shoulder. This prevents the grip from being secured.
    • Escaping the Figure Four: If the choke is being set up, try to peel the opponent's choking arm off your neck by pushing their forearm away with your hands and turning your head towards their choking arm.
    • Turning into the Choke: As the choke is being applied, attempt to turn your head and body *into* the choking arm. This can relieve pressure and create an opportunity to escape or counter.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Rear Naked Choke?

    Wrist pain often occurs if you're gripping too tightly with your fingers or trying to squeeze with your wrists instead of your biceps. Ensure your grip is a "figure four" with your forearm across their carotid and your other hand cupping your bicep. Focus the compression from your biceps and the opponent's shoulder.

    Q: How can I get the Rear Naked Choke against a bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are even more critical. Focus on establishing a deep collar grip and a tight figure-four. Use your hips to drive forward and your chest to maintain constant pressure on their upper back, preventing them from using their size to create space or power out.

    Q: Do I need to pull their head down to finish the Rear Naked Choke?

    No, pulling their head down is not the primary mechanism. The choke works by compressing the carotid arteries with your biceps and forearm against their neck and their own shoulder. Your head position should be tight to their head, preventing them from turning, not actively pulling them down.

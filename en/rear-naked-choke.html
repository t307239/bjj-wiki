Rear Naked Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

The Rear Naked Choke is typically applied from the back-Mount position, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain. White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength instead of precise leverage. The key mechanical insight is creating a "figure-four" lock with your arms to compress the carotid arteries against the opponent's neck.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Secure Back-Mount: Establish control with both hooks (feet inside opponent's thighs) and maintain chest-to-back connection. Your hips should be lower than the opponent's hips for stability.
    2. Right Arm Over Neck: Place your right arm across the front of the opponent's neck, aiming the bicep towards their trachea. Your right shoulder should press into their jawline.
    3. Grip the Bicep: With your left hand, grip the bicep of your right arm. Ensure a firm, non-slipping grasp on your own tricep muscle.
    4. Left Arm Under Chin: Bring your left arm underneath the opponent's chin, palm facing up or towards you.
    5. Grip the Left Bicep: With your right hand, grip the bicep of your left arm. This completes the "figure-four" arm configuration.
    6. Engage the Compression: Drive your right shoulder forward and slightly down, while simultaneously squeezing your right bicep (the one across the neck) towards your chest. Your left hand acts as a "locking pin" and stabilizer.
    7. Hip Drive and Posture: Slightly drive your hips forward and arch your back to create a tightening effect. Avoid pulling your opponent's head down; instead, create pressure with your arms and body.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Incorrect Arm Placement: Applying the choke with the forearm across the windpipe instead of the bicep. This can damage the trachea and is ineffective. Correct by ensuring your bicep presses against the carotid artery, not the windpipe.
    • Jerking Motion: Using a sharp, pulling motion with the arms to finish the choke. This can strain the rotator cuff and elbow ligaments in your own arms and is inefficient. Correct by using a steady, controlled squeezing pressure.
    • Over-Arching the Back: Excessive arching of the lower back to create pressure. This can lead to lumbar strain. Correct by focusing the pressure generation through the tightening of the arm "figure-four" and a slight hip drive.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo Grip and Arm Position Drill: Practice the "figure-four" arm setup and bicep grips without a partner. Focus on the feeling of the lock. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    2. Partnered Grip and Position Drill: With a compliant partner in back-mount, practice achieving the correct arm position and grips. Focus on the feel of the bicep lock. (5 minutes, 10% resistance)
    3. Controlled Choke Application Drill: With a compliant partner, practice executing the entire choke sequence from back-mount, focusing on the biomechanics of the squeeze. Partner taps early. (5 minutes, 25% resistance)
    4. Resisted Choke Application Drill: Work with a partner who offers light resistance to your setup and choke attempt. Focus on maintaining position and executing the squeeze. (5 minutes, 50% resistance)
    5. Live Rolling - Focus on Back Control: During rolling, prioritize securing and maintaining back-mount. Practice the setup and initial squeeze without necessarily needing to finish the choke. (3 rounds, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling - Full Execution: Attempt the Rear Naked Choke during live rolling, focusing on applying the correct biomechanics for a submission. (5 rounds, 90% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have successfully secured the back-mount position with both hooks in.
    • When the opponent attempts to peel your hooks off or turn into you from the back.
    • When the opponent's posture is broken, making it difficult for them to defend.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Defending the Grip: If the choking arm is not yet secured, the defender can try to grip-fight the attacker's arm and prevent the bicep-to-bicep grip. This involves actively pushing or pulling the attacker's forearm away.
    • Turning the Head: The defender can attempt to turn their head into the attacking arm. This creates space and relieves pressure on the carotid arteries, making the choke much weaker.
    • "The Wall" Defense: The defender can try to use their own arm or shoulder to create a "wall" against their neck, preventing the attacker's choking arm from fully compressing the arteries. This requires actively pressing their own forearm into their neck.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do a Rear Naked Choke?

    Wrist pain often occurs if you are gripping your own bicep too tightly or with an awkward angle, especially if you're using a "palm-to-palm" grip on your own arm. This puts excessive strain on the wrist joint. Instead, focus on a firm grip on your opponent's bicep with your choking hand, and then use your other hand to grip the bicep of your choking arm, creating a stable "figure-four" lock without over-bending your wrist.

    Q: How can I do a Rear Naked Choke against a bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, relying on strength is futile. Focus on superior body positioning and leverage. Ensure your chest is glued to their back, preventing them from creating space. When applying the choke, drive your hips forward and use your shoulder to create the pressure, rather than trying to pull their head down. A tight "figure-four" lock with precise arm placement is crucial for choking effectiveness regardless of size.

    Q: I can't seem to finish the Rear Naked Choke, even when I feel like I have a good grip. What am I doing wrong?

    You might be focusing too much on pulling your opponent's head down with your arms. The Rear Naked Choke works by compressing the carotid arteries against the neck. The primary pressure comes from driving your choking arm's bicep into their neck while squeezing your own bicep with your other hand, and a slight forward hip drive. Think of it as a squeezing motion, not a pulling motion.

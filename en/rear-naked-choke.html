Your First Rear Naked Choke: A White Belt's Guide to BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

The Rear Naked Choke is a submission from the back control position, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain. White belts often struggle by attempting to force it rather than controlling the mechanics. The key is applying sustained pressure with your forearm against the carotid artery and your bicep against the opposite side of the neck.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish back control with both hooks inside the opponent's thighs, maintaining a chest-to-back connection. Your hips should be slightly lower than their hips.
    2. Secure a collar grip with your dominant arm's hand, sliding your fingers deep into the opponent's gi collar. Your elbow should be pointing downwards.
    3. Bring your non-dominant arm across the opponent's chest, aiming to secure your dominant bicep with your non-dominant hand. Your chin should be tucked to your chest, not over their shoulder.
    4. Rotate your non-dominant shoulder forward, driving it into the opponent's shoulder joint. This creates a "seatbelt" control.
    5. Squeeze your knees together, driving your hips forward into the opponent's pelvis. This limits their ability to posture up or escape.
    6. Apply a "choking" pressure by driving your dominant forearm into the side of the opponent's neck, specifically on the carotid artery. Simultaneously, pull your shoulders back and down.
    7. Ensure your head is to the side of their head, not directly behind it, to prevent them from turning into you. Your body should be aligned with their spine.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Grasping the opponent's head instead of the collar: This can lead to a "face crank" injury, damaging the temporomandibular joint (jaw) or causing neck strain. The correct grip is on the gi collar, deep and firm.
    • Arching your back excessively to pull the opponent down: This puts immense pressure on your own lumbar spine, risking a herniated disc. Maintain a strong core and drive your hips forward instead.
    • Applying pressure with the elbow directly on the trachea: This is an ineffective choke that can cause severe pain and damage to the windpipe. Focus all pressure on the carotid artery with your forearm.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice the grip sequence and body positioning without a partner. Focus on smooth transitions and weight distribution. (10 reps per side)
    2. Static partner drill: With a compliant partner lying on their stomach, practice establishing back control and applying the choke mechanics without pressure. (10 reps per side)
    3. Light resistance drill: Partner offers minimal resistance to your grip and body positioning. Focus on maintaining control and applying the choke. (5 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled choke drill: Partner taps immediately when they feel pressure. Focus on the exact mechanics of the choke and achieving a clean tap. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. "Tap to pressure" drill: Partner resists slightly more, but taps as soon as significant choke pressure is felt. This helps you feel the correct application. (5 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the Rear Naked Choke when the opportunity arises in rolling. Focus on applying the technique correctly, not just getting the submission. (10 minutes rolling, 100% resistance)

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When you have successfully secured back control with both hooks in.
    • When the opponent attempts to turn into you from the back, creating an opening.
    • When the opponent exposes their neck while defending a takedown or sweep.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Defense 1 (Head and Arm Protection): The opponent tucks their chin tightly to their chest and turns their head away from the choking arm. This prevents the forearm from reaching the carotid artery. The attacker must adjust their angle or switch grips.
    • Defense 2 (Elbow Escape): The opponent uses their free arm to push against the attacker's choking arm or bicep, creating space to turn their head and relieve pressure. The attacker must maintain a tight grip and drive their shoulder into the opponent's face.
    • Defense 3 (Turning In): The opponent attempts to turn their hips towards the attacker's choking arm, creating an angle to face the attacker and break the choke. The attacker must maintain their hooks and hip pressure to prevent this turn.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Rear Naked Choke?

    Your wrist is likely hurting because you are gripping the collar too loosely or using your wrist to create the choking pressure. The pressure should come from your forearm and bicep squeezing the neck. Ensure your fingers are deep in the collar and your forearm is pressing directly onto the carotid artery, not just on the side of the neck.

    Q: How can I do a Rear Naked Choke against a bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, your primary goal is to use your body position and leverage, not brute strength. Maintain a tight chest-to-back connection to prevent them from creating space. Drive your hips forward into their pelvis to limit their mobility. Focus on a deep collar grip and a powerful squeeze with your forearm and bicep, ensuring your head is positioned to cut off their escape route.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Rear Naked Choke from the back control?

    The ideal time to attempt the Rear Naked Choke is when you have secured stable back control with both hooks in, and the opponent is struggling to escape or posture up. Look for moments when they expose their neck while trying to turn, or when you can flatten them out and create a strong base. Avoid attempting it when you are scrambling or have lost control of your hooks, as this makes you vulnerable to counters.

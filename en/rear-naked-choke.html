The Rear Naked Choke is a submission applied from the back control position, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain.

White belts often fail by muscling the choke, neglecting proper body alignment and grip security.

The key is to create a constricting loop with your arm and forearm against the carotid artery, supported by your bicep and the opponent's shoulder.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Secure the opponent's back with both hooks (feet inside opponent's thighs, ankles crossed).
    2. Place your right arm across the opponent's neck, bicep against their trachea.
    3. Grip your own left bicep with your right hand, forming a "figure-four" grip.
    4. Your left hand slides under the opponent's chin, palm facing upwards, to grip your right bicep.
    5. Rotate your right forearm inwards, driving your bicep into the opponent's carotid artery on the right side.
    6. Simultaneously, use your left hand to pull the opponent's head towards your chest, creating a strong opposing force.
    7. Maintain pressure by tucking your chin and driving your hips forward, ensuring your chest is tight against the opponent's back.
    8. Keep your opponent's head slightly tilted away from your choking arm to maximize pressure.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying the choke by squeezing with your hands: This can damage the metacarpophalangeal joints of your hands and lead to wrist sprains due to excessive hyperextension.

    The correct method is to use your forearm and bicep to compress the carotid artery, with your hands providing support and leverage.

    • Tucking your chin outwards instead of inwards: This allows the opponent to escape the choke more easily and can strain your neck muscles and cervical spine.

    Always tuck your chin towards your chest to maintain a stable structure and a tighter choke.

    • Overextending your elbow on the choking arm: This can lead to a hyperextended elbow joint, potentially causing ligamentous tears and a sprain.

    Focus on driving your bicep into the neck and using your shoulder and chest for power, not just raw arm extension.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the arm positioning and grip sequence on a dummy or pillow (10 reps).
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Practice securing the back and applying the choke with a compliant partner (20 reps per side).
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to the choke application (30 reps total, 15 per side).
    4. Controlled drilling: Partner attempts to defend lightly, focusing on maintaining position and grip (50 reps total, 25 per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Flow rolling: Practice the entry and submission from back control against a partner who actively defends but does not resist with full force (5 rounds, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the Rear Naked Choke during sparring, focusing on correct mechanics and patience (5 rounds, 90-100% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • From established back control position with hooks in.
    • When the opponent attempts to peel your arm off their neck.
    • After a successful sweep or takedown that grants you back position.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Chin Tuck Defense: The opponent tucks their chin tightly to their chest, preventing the choking arm from reaching the carotid artery.
    • Arm Escape: The opponent uses their free arm to push against your choking arm's elbow, creating space to relieve pressure.
    • Turning into the Choke: The opponent attempts to turn their body towards your choking arm, breaking the angle and relieving pressure on the neck.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Rear Naked Choke?

    Your wrist is likely hurting because you are using your hands to squeeze the choke, creating excessive flexion or extension at the wrist joint. The Rear Naked Choke is biomechanically achieved by compressing the carotid arteries with your forearm and bicep, not by squeezing with your hands. Ensure your hand is cupping your bicep, creating a stable frame, and focus on driving your forearm into the side of the opponent's neck.

    Q: How can I apply the Rear Naked Choke against a bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are crucial. Ensure you have a tight back control with deep hooks, preventing them from creating space. When applying the choke, focus on a high elbow position for your choking arm and ensure your bicep is firmly against their carotid artery. Drive your hips forward and use your chest to maintain constant pressure, rather than relying solely on arm strength. A strong "figure-four" grip with your own bicep is vital for leverage.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Rear Naked Choke from side control?

    The Rear Naked Choke is primarily a submission from back control, not side control. Attempting it from side control would require a transition to the back, such as a "sailor" or "grapevine" sweep that grants you dominant back position. If you are in side control and looking for a choke, consider options like the cross-collar choke or arm-Triangle Choke, which are designed for that position.

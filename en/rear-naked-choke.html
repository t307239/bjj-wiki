Rear Naked Choke: The Ultimate White Belt Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

This technique begins from the opponent's back and aims to subdue them by restricting blood flow to the brain. It is a fundamental submission in BJJ.

Many white belts struggle because they rely on brute strength rather than precise leverage and anatomical understanding. This leads to wasted energy and ineffective attempts.

The key mechanical insight is understanding how to create a vise with your arms, using your opponent's own shoulder and head as anchors to compress the carotid arteries.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Establish a secure seatbelt grip: your right arm goes over their left shoulder, left arm under their right shoulder. Your right hand grips your left bicep.
    2. Ensure your hips are connected to their hips, preventing them from turning into you. Maintain a narrow base for stability.
    3. Drive your chest into their upper back, creating forward pressure. This helps break their posture and makes them less mobile.
    4. Slide your choking arm (initially the one over their shoulder) deeper, aiming to get the crook of your elbow past their chin.
    5. Bring your non-choking hand to grip your choking arm's bicep. Your forearm should be across their carotid artery.
    6. Tuck your chin to your chest, creating a secure frame. This prevents them from posturing up or creating space.
    7. Squeeze your biceps together, simultaneously driving your hips forward and slightly upward. This compresses the carotid arteries.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying the choke with your bicep against the front of their neck instead of the side: this can hyperextend the cervical spine, risking a neck injury.
    • Gripping your own bicep with your non-choking hand too loosely: this allows their head to escape, rendering the choke ineffective and potentially leading to a strained shoulder.
    • Trying to pull their head down with your choking arm: this misuses your arm and can strain your shoulder joint; instead, use your body to create the pressure.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the arm positioning and grip transitions without a partner. (10 reps each side, 0% resistance).
    2. Partner drilling (passive): With a compliant partner, practice establishing the seatbelt grip and transitioning to the choke. (10 reps each side, 25% resistance).
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Practice the full sequence with a partner offering minimal resistance to the setup. (10 reps each side, 50% resistance).
    4. Controlled choke application: Practice applying the choke with a partner who taps immediately when they feel pressure. Focus on clean mechanics. (10 reps each side, 75% resistance).
    5. Back control to choke attempts: Start from the back control position and attempt the Rear Naked Choke against a partner defending. (5 reps each side, 90% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the Rear Naked Choke into live sparring, prioritizing proper setup and execution over immediate success. (5 minutes, 100% resistance).

    When to Use & Counters

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have secured the opponent's back with a strong seatbelt grip.
    • When the opponent attempts to turn into you from the back and you can transition to a front-facing choke setup.
    • When the opponent is turtled and you can secure hooks and attack the neck from behind.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Chin tuck and frame: The opponent tucks their chin to their chest and uses their hands to create space between your choking arm and their neck.
    • Shoulder escape: The opponent drives their shoulder into your choking arm, attempting to break the grip and create space.
    • Turning into the choke: The opponent turns their head and body towards your choking arm, trying to relieve pressure on the carotid arteries.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Rear Naked Choke?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you are gripping your own bicep too tightly or with an incorrect angle. Ensure your non-choking hand is gripping the bicep of your choking arm firmly but without excessive tension. The pressure should be applied through the forearm across the carotid artery, not by squeezing your own wrist.

    Q: How can a white belt use the Rear Naked Choke against a bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paraMount. Focus on establishing a tight seatbelt grip and sinking your choking arm as deep as possible, past their chin. Use your hips to drive forward and upward, creating a strong vise. Do not rely on strength; use their size against them by controlling their posture and movement.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from back control to the Rear Naked Choke?

    The optimal time is when you have secured both hooks and the opponent's posture is broken. If they attempt to turn into you, this is often an opportunity to secure the choke. Look for moments of slight imbalance or when they are trying to create space; these are prime opportunities to sink the choke.

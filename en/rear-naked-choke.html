Your First Rear Naked Choke: A White Belt's Guide to BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White Belt Technique
📖 See Full Guide → White Belt Guide

This choke is applied from the back control position, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain. It's a fundamental submission for BJJ practitioners.

Many white belts struggle by relying on brute strength or poor body positioning. This leads to inefficiency and potential injury.

The key is sustained pressure and precise anatomical leverage, not just squeezing.

Contents

    Grips & Mechanics

    1. Secure rear mount with both hooks inside your opponent's thighs, pelvis connected to their lower back.
    2. Reach one arm across your opponent's neck, placing your bicep against their carotid artery.
    3. Your other hand secures your own bicep (the 'seatbelt' grip), creating a strong frame.
    4. Rotate your hips slightly towards the side of the choking arm, aligning your shoulder with their head.
    5. Drive your shoulder into their neck, compressing the carotid artery unilaterally.
    6. Maintain a strong chest-to-back connection, preventing them from turning in.
    7. Tighten the choke by squeezing your biceps together and arching your back slightly, pushing your chest forward.

    ⚠️ White Belt Warnings

    • Applying the choke by squeezing with your hands alone: This can strain your wrists and elbows, damaging tendons and ligaments. Instead, use your entire upper body and back muscles to drive the choke.
    • Over-extending your neck while trying to finish: This puts immense pressure on your cervical spine and can lead to neck injury. Keep your head aligned with your spine and use your body to create the compression.
    • Allowing your opponent to gain underhooks or space on the side of the choke: This compromises the choke's effectiveness and can lead to escapes or counter-submissions like a guillotine. Maintain a tight connection and prevent them from creating space.

    Drill Progressions

    1. Solo drill: Practice securing rear mount and establishing the seatbelt grip and choking arm position without a partner (50 reps).
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Practice the entire sequence of establishing rear mount, applying the choke grips, and driving the choke with a compliant partner (20 reps per side).
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner resists slightly by shrugging or trying to create space. Focus on maintaining grips and body position (15 reps per side).
    4. Partner drill (moderate resistance): Partner actively tries to defend by turning in or creating space. Focus on maintaining control and finishing the choke when the opportunity arises (10 reps per side).
    5. Live rolling (controlled): Attempt the Rear Naked Choke from back control during controlled sparring, focusing on good technique over immediate submission (5 rounds).
    6. Live rolling (full): Execute the Rear Naked Choke during regular sparring, applying it whenever the back is secured (10 rounds).

    When to Use & Counters

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When you have successfully secured back control with hooks in.
    • When your opponent is turtled and you can establish hooks from behind.
    • When you transition from another submission attempt and land in a dominant back position.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Elbow-to-Bicep Defense: If the choking arm is not fully locked, the defender can try to bring their elbow to the inside of the attacker's choking bicep, creating space and breaking the grip.
    • Turning In: The defender can attempt to turn their hips and shoulders into the choking arm, relieving pressure from the carotid artery and potentially escaping the position.
    • Bridging and Shrimping (when hooks are lost): If the attacker loses control of the hooks, the defender can bridge and shrimp to create space and escape the back.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Rear Naked Choke?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you're gripping with your hands alone, trying to squeeze the choke. The correct biomechanics involve using your entire upper body: drive your choking shoulder into their neck and use your other hand to grip your own bicep, creating a frame. This transfers the pressure to your back and chest, not just your wrists.

    Q: How can I do a Rear Naked Choke against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on maintaining a tight connection and utilizing your hips. Ensure your hooks are deep and your pelvis is glued to their lower back. The key is to apply pressure from your shoulder into their neck and use your back muscles to drive the choke, rather than relying on arm strength which they will easily overpower.

    Q: When is the best time to go for the Rear Naked Choke from side control?

    The Rear Naked Choke is primarily a back-control submission. It is not typically attempted directly from Side Control. You must first secure the back position by attacking the opponent's base and transitioning your hooks in. Trying to force it from side control will likely lead to losing position.

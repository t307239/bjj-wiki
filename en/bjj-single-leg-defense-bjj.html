    Understanding Single Leg Defense

    The single leg takedown is one of the most fundamental attacks in wrestling and BJJ. Learning proper defense against it is critical for all levels. A strong single leg defense combines technical positioning, timing, and proper weight distribution.

    The Sprawl: Foundation of Defense

    1

    Recognize the Threat

    Identify when your opponent shoots for a single leg. Watch for their head drop and forward movement.

    2

    Sprawl Explosively

    Push your hips back and extend your legs behind you. This removes your legs from their grasp and creates distance.

    3

    Control the Head

    Post your hands on their back or shoulder. Control their head to prevent them from advancing.

    4

    Defend Sideway

    Turn perpendicular to their body. This prevents them from getting chest-to-chest.

    The Whizzer Counter

    Once you've sprawled, the whizzer (overhook) is your best tool. Hook their arm and rotate your hips into them to prevent the takedown.

    Weight Distribution Keys

    • Stay tall: Never collapse onto their shot
    • Post hands early: Catch them as they shoot, not after
    • Move your feet: Step back and to the side to create angles
    • Keep your base: Maintain a wide, stable stance
    Pro Tip: Practice your sprawl timing constantly. The best single leg defense happens before they fully commit to the shot. Drill the movement until it's automatic.

    Advanced Variations

    Once you master the basic sprawl, explore advanced defenses like the front headlock counter, underhook repositioning, and the knee slice escape if they do complete the takedown.

    Training Recommendations

    • Drill sprawls at 50% intensity with a partner
    • Practice against different shot angles
    • Combine with upper body defense (underhook/overhook)
    • Roll with wrestlers to refine your timing
    • Build leg strength to prevent being buckled

    Single leg defense is one of the most critical survival skills in BJJ. Master it early and you'll avoid countless takedowns throughout your grappling career.

    More Questions

    What's the most important thing to do when someone shoots for a single leg?

    Your primary goal is to establish a strong base and prevent them from getting a good grip on your leg. Immediately widen your stance and try to 'stuff' their head down to break their posture.

    How do I stop a single leg takedown if they've already grabbed my leg?

    Once they have your leg, focus on controlling their head and posture. Use your free leg to create distance or even hook their leg, and try to circle away from their grip to break their balance.

    What are some common mistakes beginners make when defending a single leg?

    A common mistake is panicking and trying to pull their leg straight up, which often makes it easier for the attacker to drive forward. Another error is not establishing a solid base, leading to being easily swept or taken down.

    Common BJJ Problems & FAQ

    Q: I keep getting taken down when I try to sprawl for a single leg defense in BJJ, what am I doing wrong with my hips?

    Your hips are likely not dropping low enough and your weight isn't being driven into your opponent's shoulders; instead of just pushing back, actively drive your hips down and forward to create a solid base and pressure, making it difficult for them to lift your leg.

    Q: When defending a single leg takedown in BJJ, how can I prevent my opponent from getting their head to the inside and finishing the takedown?

    To prevent the inside head position, focus on actively framing with your forearm against their head and neck, while simultaneously driving your shoulder into their bicep to create space and prevent them from circling their head to the inside.

    Q: How do I effectively transition from a failed single leg defense in BJJ to a better position instead of just getting swept?

    If your sprawl is failing, immediately transition to a 'guillotine' or 'front headlock' grip by reaching across their neck and trapping their head while simultaneously stepping your leg back to a more stable base. This allows you to control their posture and potentially transition to a better defensive or offensive position.

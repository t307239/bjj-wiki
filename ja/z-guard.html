Z-Guard BJJ: The White Belt Biomechanical Blueprint

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Z-Guard originates from an open guard position, aiming to create off-balancing and attack opportunities by controlling one of the opponent's legs.

White belts often struggle by overextending or creating insufficient frames, leading to poor control and potential injury.

The key is creating a strong, angled base with your hips and using your shin as a wedge to prevent forward pressure.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting from a seated open guard, extend your left leg straight. Your right leg will be the active Z-leg.
    2. Place the sole of your right foot on the opponent's left hip bone, angling your foot slightly inward.
    3. Hook your right knee towards your own chest, creating a strong inward tension that anchors your leg.
    4. Grip the opponent's left pants (at the knee or thigh area) with your right hand.
    5. Place your left hand on the opponent's right knee or shin to prevent them from easily stepping over.
    6. Shift your hips to your left, creating a 45-degree angle relative to the opponent's forward line.
    7. Use your left leg's shin to create a 'frame' against the opponent's right thigh, preventing them from closing the distance.
    8. Maintain a slight upward pressure with your right foot on their hip, constantly pushing their weight away.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly placing your right foot on the opponent's knee: This risks hyperextending the opponent's knee joint, leading to potential ligament tears (ACL/MCL).

    The correct placement is on the hip bone, using the shin as the primary frame, not the foot on their knee.

    • Extending your left leg too far forward: This compromises your base and creates an opening for them to pass, potentially straining your hip flexors.

    Keep your left leg slightly bent and actively 'framing' their thigh.

    • Rounding your back and losing hip connection: This makes you vulnerable to sweeps and allows them to drive forward, risking lower back compression.

    Maintain a proud chest and keep your hips actively engaged with the mat.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip angle, leg hook, and frame placement without a partner. 10 repetitions.
    2. Passive partner drilling: Partner stands still, you execute the Z-Guard setup and hold. 5 repetitions per side.
    3. Light pressure drilling: Partner applies minimal forward pressure, you maintain the Z-Guard. 5 repetitions per side.
    4. Guard retention drilling: Partner attempts a simple pass (e.g., knee-slice), you focus on maintaining Z-Guard frames. 5 repetitions per side.
    5. Submission drilling: From Z-Guard, attempt a sweep or submission with light resistance. 5 repetitions per side.
    6. Live rolling: Implement Z-Guard for 30-second intervals within a 3-minute round, focusing on setup and control. 3 rounds.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When opponent attempts to pass your closed guard by stepping forward.
    • When opponent is postured up slightly, creating space to insert your leg.
    • When you are on your back and can create a strong hip angle away from their pressure.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Drag: If opponent steps their right leg inside your Z-leg, grip their right ankle with your left hand and pull it across your body, transitioning to a leg drag.
    • Sweep (e.g., Hip Bump Sweep): Once Z-Guard is established and they're pressuring forward, shrimp your hips out and bridge upwards, using your Z-leg to push their hip away.
    • Transition to Butterfly Guard: If they try to collapse your Z-leg, you can often swing your right leg under their other leg and transition to a butterfly guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my right knee hurt when I try to set up Z-Guard?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are placing your foot too high on your opponent's leg, or you are trying to 'push' with your knee instead of 'hooking' with your foot and ankle. Ensure the sole of your foot is on their hip bone and your knee is bent, creating a strong inward hook. This positions your shin as the primary frame, not your knee joint.

    Q: How can I use Z-Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus heavily on hip angle and framing. Your goal is to prevent them from using their weight advantage to drive forward. Use your Z-leg's shin to create a strong, active frame against their thigh, and actively push their hip away with your foot. Maintain a wide base with your hips to distribute their pressure.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from closed guard to Z-Guard?

    The ideal time to switch is when your opponent is postured up slightly and attempting to break your closed guard by stepping their legs forward. As they create space and move their feet, you can open your guard and quickly insert your Z-leg onto their hip, simultaneously gripping their pants to establish control before they can re-establish their base.

Lapel Choke: A Precise BJJ Technique Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This choke is initiated from the closed guard, aiming to restrict blood flow to the opponent's brain via their carotid arteries. White belts often struggle due to a lack of body control and understanding of leverage.

The key is not brute force, but precise application of pressure through hip elevation and precise collar grip placement, creating a constricting force.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure a deep cross-collar grip with your right hand, thumb inside the lapel, knuckles pointing towards your opponent's sternum.
    2. With your left hand, secure a second cross-collar grip on the opposite lapel, again with thumb inside and knuckles aligned with the sternum.
    3. Arch your lower back and elevate your hips slightly, pressing your knees firmly into your opponent's hips.
    4. Rotate your right shoulder forward, pulling the right lapel towards your opponent's left carotid artery.
    5. Simultaneously, rotate your left shoulder forward, pulling the left lapel towards your opponent's right carotid artery.
    6. Drive your elbows down and slightly outwards, creating a vise-like effect on the neck.
    7. Maintain hip pressure and continue the shoulder rotation until submission is achieved.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Gripping too shallowly on the lapel: This fails to create sufficient pressure on the carotid arteries, leading to a weak choke and potential skin abrasion on the neck.
    • Correct: Deep grip with thumb inside, knuckles aligned with sternum for maximum artery compression.
    • Over-extending your arms and losing hip pressure: This allows your opponent to create space and relieve pressure, potentially injuring your own shoulder or elbow joints.
    • Correct: Maintain active hip pressure and use shoulder rotation, not just arm extension, to apply the choke.
    • Applying pressure with the edge of the lapel against the windpipe: This can cause discomfort and is not the primary mechanism of the choke, potentially leading to trachea injury.
    • Correct: Focus on compressing the carotid arteries on the sides of the neck with the bulk of the lapel.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice grip acquisition and hip elevation/rotation without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Partnered drilling (no resistance): Practice grip acquisition and the full choke mechanics with a compliant partner. (25 reps)
    3. Partnered drilling (light resistance): Partner lightly resists grips and hip pressure. Focus on maintaining control and executing the choke. (5 rounds x 30 seconds)
    4. Partnered drilling (moderate resistance): Partner actively tries to defend grips and escape. Focus on maintaining the choke setup. (5 rounds x 45 seconds)
    5. Scenario drilling: Start from closed guard and attempt the choke against a resisting opponent. (5 rounds x 1 minute)
    6. Live rolling (limited attempts): In rolling, you may attempt the Lapel Choke up to 3 times per round. Focus on correct execution and timing. (5 rounds x 5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When opponent is posturing up in your closed guard, creating space to attack the neck.
    • When opponent attempts to pass your guard and their posture is slightly compromised.
    • When opponent is defending your other submissions, opening up the opportunity for a choke.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Posture Up and Grip Break: Opponent extends their posture, creating space and breaking the collar grips by shucking your hands off the lapels.
    • Hip Escape and Frame: Opponent hips away, creating space, and uses their forearms to frame against your biceps, preventing the choke from tightening.
    • Head and Arm Control: Opponent tucks their chin and establishes a strong head and arm connection, making it difficult to isolate the neck for the choke.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Lapel Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain typically occurs from gripping the lapel too tightly with the fingers extended, putting strain on the wrist joint. Ensure your thumb is inside the collar for a stronger, more stable grip, and focus on using your forearm and shoulder rotation to apply pressure, not just finger strength. Maintain a neutral wrist position.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, you must prioritize hip elevation and precise grip placement. Instead of trying to pull them down, use your hips to create a slight angle, allowing you to dig your grips deeper. Focus on the rotational force from your shoulders and back, rather than pure arm strength, to constrict the carotid arteries.

    Q: When is the best time to go for a Lapel Choke in BJJ, and how do I avoid giving up my guard?

    The ideal time is when your opponent postures up, making their neck accessible. To avoid giving up your guard, maintain active knee pressure and a tight connection to their hips. As you secure your grips for the choke, ensure your hips are elevated and your legs remain locked, preventing them from easily creating space to pass.

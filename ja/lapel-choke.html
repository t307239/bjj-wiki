Lapel Choke Technique Guide for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This choke is initiated from the closed guard position, aiming to restrict blood flow to the opponent's brain. White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of leverage and pressure application.

The key insight is using your hips and the opponent's gi to create a vise-like grip, rather than relying solely on arm strength.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure a deep cross-collar grip with your right hand on the opponent's left lapel, thumb inside the collar. Your left hand grips the opponent's right lapel, thumb inside.
    2. Ensure your forearms are pressed firmly against the sides of the opponent's neck, creating a seal.
    3. Sit up slightly, driving your hips forward and upward, angling your pelvis towards your left.
    4. With your left hand, pull the opponent's right lapel across their throat, tucking it under their chin.
    5. Simultaneously, use your right forearm and hand to cinch the opponent's left lapel, creating pressure across the carotid artery.
    6. Arch your back and drive your hips further forward, tightening the choke by bringing your elbows closer together.
    7. Maintain a strong base by keeping your feet locked around the opponent's hips, preventing them from posturing up.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Gripping too high on the lapel: This can lead to excessive pressure on the windpipe (trachea), causing discomfort and potential injury. The correct grip is deep, at the base of the collarbone.
    • Using only arm strength to pull: This strains the shoulders and elbows, risking rotator cuff tears or bicep tendinitis. Instead, drive your hips forward and use your entire body's weight.
    • Not sealing the neck with forearms: This allows the opponent to create space and escape the choke. Ensure your forearms are pressed firmly against the sides of their neck.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill (0% resistance): Practice securing the grips and making the hip and torso movements without an opponent. 10 reps.
    2. Partner drill with no resistance (0% resistance): Practice the full sequence with a compliant partner, focusing on grip integrity and hip drive. 5 reps.
    3. Light resistance drill (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to posturing. Focus on maintaining your guard and executing the choke. 5 reps.
    4. Moderate resistance drill (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to create space and posture up. Work on maintaining control and finishing the choke. 5 reps.
    5. High resistance drill (75% resistance): Partner actively defends and attempts to escape. Focus on finishing the choke under pressure. 5 reps.
    6. Live rolling (90-100% resistance): Attempt the Lapel Choke during rolling. Focus on timing and application, accept that not every attempt will be successful. 3-5 attempts per roll.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured up slightly in your closed guard, creating an opening.
    • When the opponent is trying to stack you, and you can use their forward momentum against them.
    • When you have established a strong, tight closed guard and can control their posture.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape and Frame: If the choke is being applied, the defender can hip escape to create space and use their free arm to frame against the attacker's shoulder, breaking the seal.
    • Grip Release and Posture Up: The defender can attempt to grab the attacker's wrists and pull them away from their neck while simultaneously trying to explode upwards and break the guard.
    • Head and Arm Spin: If the attacker's head is too far to one side, the defender can try to spin their head out and establish a dominant position like side control or mount.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try the Lapel Choke from closed guard?

    Neck pain often occurs when the choke is applied incorrectly, focusing pressure on the windpipe (trachea) instead of the carotid arteries. Ensure your grips are deep on the lapels, with your forearms pressing against the sides of the neck, creating a seal. The choking pressure should come from driving your hips forward and arching your back, cinching the lapels across the arteries.

    Q: How can I use the Lapel Choke against a much bigger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise hip movement are crucial. Focus on getting deep grips and controlling their posture. Instead of pulling with your arms, use your hips to drive forward and upward, creating an angle. Once you initiate the choke, use your legs to keep them trapped and your body to apply the pressure, rather than trying to overpower them with brute strength.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to a Lapel Choke from closed guard?

    The optimal time to initiate a Lapel Choke is when the opponent is postured up even slightly and you have control of their upper body. Look for moments when they try to create space or drive forward, as this often presents an opportunity to secure your grips. Avoid attempting it when they are completely flat or have broken your guard's seal.

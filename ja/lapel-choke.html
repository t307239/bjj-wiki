Lapel Choke: The Essential BJJ Technique for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This technique is initiated from the closed guard, aiming to submit your opponent by constricting blood flow to the brain.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body awareness and relying on brute strength instead of leverage.

The key insight is using your hips to create a tight angle and your opponent's own lapel against their neck.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish closed guard: knees hugged to chest, feet locked behind opponent's back.
    2. Grip opponent's left lapel with your right hand, thumb inside the collar, reaching deep.
    3. Grip opponent's right lapel with your left hand, thumb inside the collar, also deep.
    4. Angle your hips 45 degrees to your left, lifting your hips slightly off the mat.
    5. Bring your right knee across your opponent's chest, creating downward pressure.
    6. Pull both lapels downwards and slightly towards your body, creating a figure-four grip on their neck.
    7. Squeeze your knees together and arch your back slightly, tightening the choke.
    8. Maintain hip pressure and keep your posture controlled to prevent escapes.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Grabbing the gi fabric too loosely: This leads to slipping grips and no pressure, potentially causing friction burns on your fingers.

    The correct grip involves digging your thumb inside the collar for maximum purchase.

    • Arching the back violently without hip control: This can hyperextend the lumbar spine, risking disc injury.

    Instead, focus on controlled hip elevation and a slight spinal extension.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice grip acquisition and hip angling on an empty mat (5 minutes, 0% resistance).
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Practice the full sequence of grips, hip movement, and choke application (10 reps, 0% resistance).
    3. Static drilling: Partner holds a neutral posture, you apply the choke without them resisting (5 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Dynamic drilling: Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the pressure and adjust (5 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Controlled sparring: Focus specifically on achieving the closed guard and attempting the lapel choke (5 minutes, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the lapel choke as opportunities arise during regular sparring (3 rounds, 90% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When opponent is postured high in your closed guard, creating space to work.
    • When opponent attempts to break your guard by pushing on your hips, creating an opening for grip.
    • When opponent is flat on their back, making it easier to establish the choke angle.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Guard break by posturing up: Opponent pushes their hips away and straightens their back, creating space and relieving pressure.
    • Grip escape by framing: Opponent uses their forearms to pry your hands off their collar, creating space to escape.
    • Underhook escape: Opponent secures an underhook and uses it to create space and posture up, breaking the choke.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Lapel Choke from closed guard in BJJ?

    Wrist pain often occurs from gripping too tightly with your fingers extended or from your opponent resisting by pulling their head away forcefully. To fix this, try to keep your wrist in a neutral position and use your thumb to dig into the collar for a more secure grip. Focus on using your body's leverage rather than just finger strength.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on getting your hips underneath them as you angle, and use your legs to create a tight, inescapable guard. Instead of pulling them down, use their weight against them by driving your knees into their chest and pulling the lapels down to their sternum, creating a constricting pressure.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate a Lapel Choke from closed guard in BJJ, and how do I know if it's working?

    The ideal time is when your opponent postures up, creating a gap between your hips and their chest. You'll know it's working when they start to visibly tense up, their breathing becomes shallow, and they may begin to tap. If they can still speak clearly and move freely, the choke is not tight enough, and you need to adjust your hip angle and grip.

Lapel Choke Technique for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This choke is initiated from closed guard, aiming to submit your opponent by compressing their carotid arteries.

Many white belts struggle because they lack precise control and instead rely on brute force.

The key is leveraging your hips and forearms to create a consistent, crushing pressure.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure your opponent's lapels with both hands, thumb-in grips, fingers deep inside the collar.
    2. Sit up slightly, bringing your hips closer to your opponent's, creating a tight connection.
    3. Place your forearms under their biceps, creating a frame that prevents them from posturing up.
    4. Drive your elbows down and slightly inward, creating pressure that pulls their shoulders forward.
    5. Simultaneously, drive your hips upward, lifting your opponent's base and forcing their chin down.
    6. Rotate your wrists inward, flattening your forearms against their neck and upper chest.
    7. Maintain constant pressure from your hips and forearms, ensuring the lapels tighten around the neck.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Gripping too shallowly in the collar: This can lead to the grips slipping and a loss of control, potentially straining the wrist joint (radiocarpal joint) due to sudden hyperextension when the opponent resists.
    • Arching your back excessively to create pressure: This places immense stress on the lumbar spine (vertebrae L1-L5) and can cause disc herniation or muscle strains; maintain a neutral spine and use hip drive instead.
    • Allowing your opponent to create space by posturing up: This negates the choke and can lead to your arms being trapped, risking shoulder impingement (subacromial impingement) if you try to pull them back forcefully against their frame.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the grip acquisition and body positioning without a partner. (10 reps)
    2. Static partner drill (0% resistance): Partner remains passive, allowing you to practice the full motion. (10 reps)
    3. Light resistance drill (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to your hip movement and posture control. (10 reps)
    4. Controlled resistance drill (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to break your guard and posture up, but without aggressive defense. (10 reps)
    5. Full resistance drill (75% resistance): Partner defends actively, attempting to escape and counter. (5 reps)
    6. Live rolling (90-100% resistance): Attempt the choke during sparring with full intensity. (2 rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up and you have a strong closed guard connection.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard and you can secure lapel grips.
    • When your opponent is turtled and you can secure lapel grips from behind.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Guard Pass (e.g., Torreando Pass): If the choke attempt is slow, the opponent can often step their leg over and pass the guard before the choke is fully applied.
    • Posture Up: If the white belt fails to establish a strong hip connection and forearm frame, the opponent can simply straighten their back and break the choke.
    • Grip Break: Opponents can specifically target and break your lapel grips by prying your fingers open or sliding their collar out.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do a Lapel Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often stems from gripping the lapel too shallowly or with an unnatural angle. Instead of a deep, thumb-in grip, you might be gripping the edge of the fabric. When you attempt to rotate your wrists to tighten the choke, this forces hyperextension on the radiocarpal joint. Ensure your fingers are deep within the collar and your wrists remain relatively neutral, allowing the pressure to come from your forearms and hips, not just wrist flexion.

    Q: How can I apply a Lapel Choke effectively against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on minimizing the space they can create. Your hips must be glued to theirs, and your forearms must establish a tight frame under their biceps to prevent them from posturing. Instead of pulling them down, use your hips to lift them slightly and drive their chin to their chest. The choke comes from the compression of the carotid arteries via the lapels, amplified by your hip elevation and forearm pressure, not from trying to overpower their size.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate a Lapel Choke from closed guard?

    The optimal time to initiate a Lapel Choke from closed guard is when your opponent is in a slightly compromised posture, either leaning forward or attempting to create space to pass. If they are perfectly upright and stable, your attempt will likely be unsuccessful and could lead to them breaking your guard. Look for moments when they are slightly off-balance or have their weight distributed forward, making it easier to secure the grips and drive your hips to finish the choke.

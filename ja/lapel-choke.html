Lapel Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanically Precise Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This technique, the Lapel Choke, is initiated from the closed guard position and aims to submit your opponent by restricting blood flow to the brain.

White belts often struggle with this due to a lack of understanding of leverage and bone-on-bone pressure, leading to ineffective attempts.

The key mechanical insight is using your opponent's own collar to create a constricting force against their carotid arteries, amplified by hip extension.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish a secure grip with your left hand on the opponent's right lapel, thumb inside, fingers gripping the fabric deeply near the collarbone.
    2. Simultaneously, establish a similar grip with your right hand on the opponent's left lapel, thumb inside, fingers gripping deeply.
    3. Ensure your knees are tight around their hips, creating a stable base with your feet locked behind their back.
    4. Arch your lower back, lifting your hips off the mat slightly, creating space to drive your opponent's shoulders down.
    5. With your grips secure, pull your elbows down and slightly back, drawing your opponent's upper body towards your chest.
    6. Simultaneously, extend your hips upwards, driving your pelvis into their torso.
    7. The pressure from your hands pulling down and your hips driving up compresses the lapels against their neck, eliciting the submission.
    8. Maintain tight knee control throughout to prevent them from posturing up or creating space.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Neck Crank Risk: Applying the choke by violently twisting your opponent's head instead of using hip and arm pressure can damage the cervical spine's ligaments and vertebrae.
    • Correct Movement: Focus on driving your hips up and pulling your elbows down, creating a crushing pressure with the lapels against the sides of the neck.
    • Shoulder Impingement Risk: Grabbing the lapels too high or too wide, and then attempting to pull with straight arms, can strain the rotator cuff muscles and shoulder capsule.
    • Correct Movement: Grip the lapels deeply near the collarbone and pull with bent elbows, keeping your shoulders down and back.
    • Wrist Sprain Risk: Gripping the lapels too tightly with a bent wrist or attempting to generate power solely through wrist flexion can lead to carpal ligament damage.
    • Correct Movement: Maintain a neutral wrist position, gripping the lapel with your fingers and palm, and generate power from your core and hips.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Grip & Hip Movement Drill: Practice establishing lapel grips and performing the hip extension and chest compression motion without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Partnered Grip & Hip Drive Drill (No Pressure): With a compliant partner in your closed guard, practice establishing grips and driving your hips up while they remain passive. (25 reps)
    3. Light Resistance Hip Drive Drill: Partner resists slightly by posturing up minimally; focus on using your hip drive to break their posture and apply the choke mechanics. (50 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled Choke Application Drill: Apply the choke with controlled pressure, stopping immediately when your partner taps or signals discomfort. Focus on correct mechanics, not speed. (50 reps, 50% resistance)
    5. Flow Rolling with Lapel Choke Focus: During flow rolling, actively look for opportunities to set up and attempt the lapel choke, focusing on smooth transitions and correct application. (5 minutes, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling with Lapel Choke Emphasis: During live rolling, actively pursue the lapel choke, focusing on maintaining positional control and executing the mechanics under full resistance. (5 minutes, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent's posture is broken and they are within your closed guard.
    • When your opponent attempts to posture up, creating an opportunity to drive your hips into them.
    • When you have secured deep lapel grips and have tight control of their upper body.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Posture Recovery: If your opponent is posturing up, immediately widen your knees to create space and prevent them from driving down.
    • Grip Break: If your opponent's grips are weak, they can attempt to peel your thumbs out or rip your hands off the lapels.
    • Guard Pass Attempt: A skilled opponent may try to pass your guard by creating space and moving around your legs, often by breaking your closed guard first.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Lapel Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs from gripping the lapel with an excessively bent or flexed wrist, or by trying to generate all the choking force with your wrists alone. To fix this, ensure your wrists are neutral and straight. Grip the lapel with your fingers and palm, and focus on driving your hips up and pulling your elbows down. This utilizes your larger muscle groups and core, reducing strain on your wrists.

    Q: How can I effectively use the Lapel Choke against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paramount. Focus on securing very deep grips on their lapels, as close to their collarbone as possible, to maximize the choking surface. Once grips are set, use your hip extension to momentarily lift your hips, creating a slight gap, then drive your hips *into* their torso while simultaneously pulling your elbows down and back. This combined force is more effective than raw strength.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Lapel Choke, and what if my opponent is defending well?

    The optimal time is when your opponent's posture is broken, and they are close to your chest, making it difficult for them to create space. If they are defending well, don't force the choke. Instead, use their defensive posture to transition to other attacks, such as an armbar or triangle choke, by subtly shifting your hips and leg positioning. Maintaining control of their upper body while they defend is key.

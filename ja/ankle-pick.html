Ankle Pick: The Fundamental BJJ Takedown for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This technique initiates from an open stance, aiming to disrupt the opponent's base and secure a takedown.

White belts often struggle by over-relying on upper body strength, leading to poor balance and failed attempts.

The key is to use your hips and the opponent's own momentum against them, creating a lever with your leg.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Secure a collar grip with your right hand (if right-handed) and a right wrist grip with your left hand. The collar grip should be at the opponent's sternal notch.
    2. Stance: Adopt a staggered stance, left foot forward, right foot back, shoulder-width apart. Your hips should be slightly lower than the opponent's.
    3. Penetration Step: Step your left foot forward, placing it outside the opponent's right foot. Your knee should track towards the opponent's right knee, not inwards.
    4. Hip Angle: Simultaneously drop your hips by bending both knees, creating a strong base. Your right hip should align with the opponent's right hip.
    5. Weight Transfer: Drive your weight forward and slightly to your left, using your collar grip to pull the opponent's upper body towards you.
    6. Ankle Grab: As you step, reach with your right hand to secure a deep grip on the opponent's right ankle, just above the Achilles tendon.
    7. Leverage and Sweep: Extend your left leg straight back and use your right hand on the ankle to sweep it upwards and to your right. Your pulling motion with the collar grip should continue.
    8. Finish: As the opponent's base is broken, drive through with your hips and chest, maintaining the ankle grip until they are on the mat. Your right knee should be close to the mat for stability.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Knee Drive: Driving your knee directly inwards instead of slightly forward towards the opponent's knee can torque your knee joint, leading to ACL or MCL tears.
    • Jerking with the Arms: Pulling solely with your arms without hip engagement strains your shoulder and bicep tendons, risking tears.
    • Over-extending the Back: Arching your back excessively to pull the opponent down can lead to lumbar disc compression and strains.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Footwork and Grip Practice: Practice the penetration step and hip drop motion with imaginary opponents, focusing on correct foot placement and knee angle. (50 reps)
    2. Grip and Ankle Grab Dummy Drill: With a training partner standing, practice securing the collar and wrist grip, then the ankle grab, without any movement. (30 reps each side)
    3. Controlled Penetration and Grab: Partner stands still. Perform the penetration step, hip drop, and ankle grab, holding the position for 3 seconds. (20 reps each side, 25% resistance)
    4. Assisted Ankle Pick: Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to execute the step, grab, and sweep. Focus on controlled movement and balance. (30 reps each side, 50% resistance)
    5. Live Ankle Pick Attempts: In a controlled rolling scenario, attempt the ankle pick when the opportunity arises. Focus on proper execution, not just completion. (5 rounds, 75% resistance)
    6. Full Rolling with Ankle Pick Focus: During live rolling, actively look for opportunities to use the ankle pick, aiming for a successful takedown. (5 rounds, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is posturing up slightly from your guard.
    • When the opponent is circling away from your strong side.
    • When you have a strong grip on their collar and can control their posture.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Sprawl and Posture Up: As the opponent attempts the penetration step, immediately sprawl your hips back and drive your chest up, breaking their grip and regaining your base.
    • Whizzer (Overhook): As the opponent reaches for your ankle, secure an overhook on their arm with your opposite arm and pull them forward, disrupting their balance and preventing the sweep.
    • Leg Trap (e.g., De La Riva Guard): If you are in a guard position that allows, use your leg to hook their ankle or thigh as they penetrate, creating a frame and preventing them from securing the grip.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt after trying the Ankle Pick?

    Wrist pain often occurs because you're gripping the opponent's ankle too tightly with an extended, unsupported wrist. Instead, try to cup the ankle with your palm and fingers, keeping your wrist in a neutral or slightly flexed position. Imagine you're trying to 'hug' their ankle with your hand, not just 'grab' it. This distributes the force across your hand and forearm, not just your wrist joint.

    Q: How can I Ankle Pick a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, the key is to leverage their weight and momentum. Instead of trying to pull them down, focus on stepping in deep and dropping your hips lower than theirs. Use your collar grip to initiate a slight pull as you penetrate, but the main force comes from your hip drive and sweeping motion with the ankle grab. Think of it as using their mass to help you.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt an Ankle Pick in BJJ?

    The ideal time is when the opponent is slightly off-balance, perhaps leaning forward or shifting their weight. This is common when they are trying to pass your guard or when you are creating distance. If they have a solid, wide base and are perfectly balanced, the ankle pick is much harder. Look for those brief moments of instability.

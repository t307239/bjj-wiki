Ankle Pick Takedown for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This takedown initiates from a standing position, aiming to disrupt your opponent's balance and bring them to the mat by attacking their ankle.

White belts often struggle with this due to a lack of coordinated hip and leg movement, leading to ineffective attempts or self-injury.

The key mechanical insight is using your hips to drive forward and down while your pulling arm creates a lever against their ankle.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Stance: Assume a staggered stance, left foot forward, right foot back, with hips squared to your opponent's hips.
    2. Grip: Secure a "collar and elbow" grip on your opponent's right side (left hand on their right collar, right hand on their right elbow joint).
    3. Head Position: Place your head on their chest, slightly to their left, creating a strong connection.
    4. Entry: Step your right foot forward, slightly outside your opponent's right foot, ensuring your right knee is bent and your right hip is low.
    5. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your bent right leg, driving your hips forward and down.
    6. Ankle Control: With your left hand, reach down and secure a "cupped hand" grip on the outside of your opponent's right ankle, just above the Achilles tendon.
    7. Pull and Drive: Simultaneously, drive your hips forward and down while pulling their ankle upwards and slightly across your body with your left hand. Your right arm remains connected to their elbow, guiding their upper body down.
    8. Completion: As they fall, maintain control of the ankle and follow them to the mat, ideally landing in a dominant position like side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Knee Drive: Driving your knee directly into the opponent's shin or ankle can hyperextend your own knee or cause a ligament tear in their ankle.
    • Jerking Motion: A sudden, jerky pull on the ankle without hip drive can lead to a rotator cuff injury in your shoulder or a muscle strain in your back.
    • Lack of Hip Connection: Trying to lift the ankle with only your arm, without driving your hips forward, puts immense strain on your lower back and can result in a herniated disc.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling: Practice the footwork, hip movement, and arm placement without a partner. (5 reps per side)
    2. Partnered Static Drilling (No Resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the grip, entry, and hip drive. Focus on feeling the connection. (10 reps per side)
    3. Partnered Light Resistance (50%): Partner resists slightly by not collapsing. Focus on generating enough hip drive to break their balance. (15 reps per side)
    4. Partnered Controlled Entry (75%): Partner offers a slight push or pull to simulate a live entry. Focus on maintaining your base and driving through. (20 reps per side)
    5. Simulated Takedown (90%): Partner allows the takedown if executed correctly, but offers some resistance to the finish. Focus on smooth transitions to the ground. (10 reps per side)
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Attempt the ankle pick during live rolling, focusing on timing and proper execution under pressure. (As opportunities arise)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent stands up from your guard, creating space.
    • When your opponent is reaching for your collar or sleeves from a distance.
    • When your opponent takes a step with the leg you intend to attack, and you can reach their ankle.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Defensive Footwork: As the attacker steps in, quickly step your attacked leg backward and widen your base, preventing the ankle grab.
    • Posting the Hip: If the ankle is grabbed, immediately post your free hand on the attacker's hip and drive your hips away, creating space and preventing the pull.
    • Whizzer: If the attacker has a collar grip and is driving in, secure a "whizzer" (overhook with your arm around their neck/shoulder) and use your body weight to prevent their hip drive.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try to Ankle Pick?

    Your ankle likely hurts because you're not driving your hips forward and down correctly. Instead, you're relying solely on your arm to pull their ankle. This puts excessive torque on your own ankle joint. To fix this, focus on using your entire body: bend your knees, drive your hips forward and down, and use your arm as a lever to guide their ankle, not to lift it.

    Q: How can I Ankle Pick a bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are crucial. Focus on getting your head and shoulder to their chest to create a strong connection and disrupt their base. Your hip drive must be powerful and low. Instead of trying to lift their ankle, aim to use your hips to drive *through* their leg as you pull their ankle across your body, forcing them to fall forward.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt an Ankle Pick in a BJJ match?

    The ideal time is when your opponent is actively trying to establish a base or move forward, especially if they've just taken a step. Look for opportunities when they are slightly off-balance or reaching. Avoid attempting it when they are perfectly squared up or actively defending by keeping their weight low and base wide, as this makes them much harder to off-balance.

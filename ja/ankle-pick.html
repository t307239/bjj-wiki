Ankle Pick: The Essential BJJ Takedown for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Ankle Pick is a dynamic takedown initiated from a standing or open guard position, aiming to secure a single leg and disrupt the opponent's base.

Many white belts struggle due to hesitation or attempting the move with excessive upper body force rather than leg drive.

The key is a low center of gravity, a strong hip connection, and precise timing of the lift and pull.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Facing an opponent in a staggered stance, establish a Gable Grip (both hands interlacing fingers) around the opponent's ankle, specifically targeting the Achilles tendon area.
    2. Simultaneously, drive your lead knee (same side as the gripped ankle) towards the mat, creating a low base and shortening the distance.
    3. Shift your weight onto your rear leg, keeping your hips low and chest close to the opponent's thigh.
    4. Initiate the lift by extending your rear leg and driving your hips forward and slightly upward, pulling the gripped ankle towards your chest.
    5. As the opponent's base is compromised, use your head to pressure their hip on the opposite side of the leg you are attacking.
    6. Maintain hip contact and continue the forward hip drive to complete the takedown, ensuring your body remains low and controlled.
    7. Once the opponent is on the mat, transition immediately to a controlling position like side control or knee-on-belly, keeping the leg secured.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly gripping too high on the calf can lead to the opponent pulling their leg free and a loss of balance. This can strain the wrist and elbow joints of the attacker due to compensatory movements.
    • Lunging with your upper body instead of driving with your legs results in a weak attempt and can cause knee hyperextension if the opponent steps back, potentially damaging the ACL.
    • Releasing the grip prematurely or attempting to stand straight up before the takedown is complete can lead to a loss of control and a compromised position, opening up opportunities for sweeps or submissions.
    • Failing to maintain a low center of gravity can make you susceptible to being tripped or taken down yourself if the opponent reacts effectively.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Drive Drill: Practice the hip extension and forward drive motion without a partner, focusing on generating power from the legs and hips. (50 reps)
    2. Gable Grip and Low Base: Stand with a partner, practice establishing the Gable Grip on their ankle and sinking into a low, stable base. (30 reps per leg)
    3. Ankle Pick Movement (No Resistance): Partner stands passively. Practice the full Ankle Pick motion, focusing on grip, low drive, hip extension, and controlled lift. (20 reps per leg)
    4. Light Resistance Drill: Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the timing of the grip, drive, and lift. Focus on maintaining control. (5 reps per leg, 25% resistance)
    5. Moderate Resistance Drill: Partner actively tries to defend by stepping back or pulling their leg. Focus on completing the takedown despite slight resistance. (5 reps per leg, 50% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling (Controlled): Attempt the Ankle Pick during live rolling, focusing on timing and execution rather than scoring. Stop and reset if the technique is not clean. (5 attempts per round, 75% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is in an open guard and has their legs extended, creating an opportunity to isolate an ankle.
    • When the opponent is in a staggered stance and takes a weight-bearing step, leaving one ankle exposed.
    • During transitions from standing to guard, or vice versa, where the opponent's base might be temporarily unstable.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Pull and Base Maintain: If the attacker grips your ankle, immediately pull your leg towards your body and widen your base by stepping your other foot out, maintaining your balance.
    • Hips Away and Circle: As the Ankle Pick is initiated, drive your hips away from the attacker and begin circling your body in the direction of the attacked leg, disrupting their pulling force.
    • Post and Grip Break: If the attacker's head is pressuring your hip, use your free hand to post on their head or shoulder and simultaneously try to break their grip on your ankle by rotating your foot outwards.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Ankle Pick?

    Wrist pain often occurs if you are gripping too tightly with the fingers alone or if your wrist is bending at an extreme angle. Ensure your grip is a solid Gable Grip with your palms and fingers interlaced, and keep your wrists relatively straight and neutral as you drive your hips forward. The power should come from your legs and core, not just your grip strength.

    Q: How do I Ankle Pick a bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, the Ankle Pick requires even more emphasis on biomechanics than brute strength. Focus on getting your head to their hip to off-balance them and use your entire body weight to drive forward. A low, explosive hip drive is crucial to lift their leg and disrupt their superior base. Don't try to muscle it; use leverage and timing.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt an Ankle Pick in BJJ?

    The optimal time is when the opponent is actively moving or transitioning, creating momentary instability in their base. This includes when they are stepping forward to close distance, stepping back to create space, or shifting their weight between legs. Look for a clear, isolated ankle that you can grip securely before they can fully re-establish their base.

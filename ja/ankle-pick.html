The Ankle Pick is a fundamental wrestling-style takedown initiated from a standing position, aiming to disrupt an opponent's base and bring them to the mat.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute force or poor weight distribution, leading to missed opportunities and potential injury.

The key mechanical insight is to use your body's leverage and forward momentum to scoop and control the opponent's ankle, not just pull it.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting stance: Opponent faces you, feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent. Establish a staggered stance, left foot slightly forward.
    2. Right hand grip: Secure a deep collar grip with your right hand, pulling their head down and slightly towards your right.
    3. Left hand grip: Reach your left hand down to the outside of their right ankle, securing a firm grip around the Achilles tendon or lower calf.
    4. Hip insertion: Simultaneously, lower your hips by bending your knees and angling your pelvis forward, driving your left shoulder towards their right hip.
    5. Weight transfer: Shift your weight from your back foot (right) to your front foot (left), creating forward momentum.
    6. Scooping action: As your hips drive forward and down, use your left arm to scoop the opponent's right ankle upwards and towards your body.
    7. Completion: Continue driving forward, pulling the ankle with your left hand and controlling their posture with your right collar grip, to bring them to the mat.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect ankle grip: Grabbing the foot or shin instead of the lower calf/Achilles tendon. This can lead to a dropped grip and loss of control, or worse, hyper-extension of your own wrist or elbow if the opponent resists forcefully.
    • Lunging with the head: Leaning forward with your upper body and neck exposed while reaching for the ankle. This puts excessive strain on your cervical spine, risking a neck injury, and reduces your base and power.
    • Pulling the ankle horizontally: Attempting to pull the ankle sideways instead of scooping it upwards. This creates an inefficient lever arm and can cause your shoulder or elbow to hyperextend if the opponent posts their foot.
    • Insufficient hip drive: Relying solely on arm strength to lift the ankle. This fails to utilize your body's leverage, making the takedown weak and straining your back muscles, potentially leading to a lumbar strain.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo shadow drilling: Practice the entire movement pattern without a partner, focusing on hip angle, grip acquisition, and weight transfer. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    2. Partnered static drilling: With a compliant partner, practice the grips and the initial hip insertion and scooping motion. Partner stands still. (5 minutes, 25% resistance)
    3. Partnered controlled movement: Partner offers slight resistance by not posting their foot fully. Focus on completing the scoop and bringing them down gently. (5 minutes, 50% resistance)
    4. Controlled live attempts: In a controlled rolling scenario, attempt the Ankle Pick when the opportunity arises, with the partner allowed to defend minimally. Focus on technique over success. (5 minutes, 75% resistance)
    5. Live rolling attempts: Integrate the Ankle Pick into live rolling. Prioritize correct execution over scoring the takedown. Accept if the takedown is defended. (5 minutes, 90% resistance)
    6. Live rolling with goal: Focus on successfully executing the Ankle Pick during live rolling. Aim to score the takedown with proper technique. (5 minutes, 100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent takes a staggered stance, exposing one leg.
    • When the opponent over-commits to a forward movement, creating an opening.
    • When you are in close proximity and can secure the ankle grip before they can react.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Posting the foot: If the opponent attempts the Ankle Pick, immediately post your free foot to widen your base and prevent them from scooping your ankle.
    • Whizzer (overhook): As they attempt to grip your ankle, secure an overhook on their far arm and use your chest to drive into them, disrupting their balance and forward momentum.
    • Exploding forward: Instead of resisting the pull, explode forward through the Ankle Pick attempt, driving your hips into them and potentially transitioning to a dominant position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try to Ankle Pick?

    Your ankle likely hurts because you are gripping too high on their leg, or you are pulling their ankle horizontally instead of scooping it upwards. When gripping, aim for the lower calf or Achilles tendon area. The scooping motion uses your body's leverage to lift their leg, minimizing strain on your own ankle and wrist. Ensure your hips are driving forward and down, not just your arms pulling.

    Q: How can I Ankle Pick against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a bigger opponent, leverage and timing are paramount. You must get lower than them, driving your shoulder into their hip to disrupt their base. Secure a tight collar grip to control their posture, preventing them from posturing up. The key is to use their size and momentum against them by initiating the scoop as they step or lean forward, making them fall into your trap.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt an Ankle Pick in a BJJ match?

    The Ankle Pick is best attempted when your opponent is moving forward or has a staggered stance. Look for openings when they are initiating a forward movement, like stepping into you or attempting a guard pass. Avoid attempting it when they are perfectly balanced or have a wide, stable base. A well-timed Ankle Pick capitalizes on their movement, making it significantly more effective.

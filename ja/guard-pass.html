Beginner's Guide to BJJ Guard Pass

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

This technique initiates from a standing or kneeling position against an opponent's closed guard. It aims to break their leg entanglement and establish a dominant side control or mount position.

White belts often struggle due to poor base and uncontrolled hip movement, leading to resets or submission attempts against them.

The key insight is to use your body weight and leverage to create a stable base, driving forward to break the guard rather than relying solely on strength.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a same-side sleeve grip with your left hand. Your opponent is in closed guard.
    2. Weight Distribution: Shift your weight onto your left hip, angling your torso slightly to your right. Your knees are wider than shoulder-width for base.
    3. Guard Break: Extend your right leg forward, placing your foot flat on the mat just outside your opponent's left hip. This creates a wedge.
    4. Hip Drive: Simultaneously drive your hips forward and down, using your right leg as a pivot point. Your left knee should track towards your opponent's left hip.
    5. Break Grip: Apply controlled pressure with your right hand on the collar, pulling their head slightly towards your left shoulder. This loosens their guard.
    6. Leg Separation: As their guard opens, pull your right leg back sharply while continuing to drive your hips forward. Your left knee slides under their hip.
    7. Establish Position: Move your left knee towards your opponent's torso, establishing a knee-on-belly or side control by bringing your chest to their chest and securing a body triangle or cross-face grip.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Injury: Attempting to forcefully pry open a closed guard with straight legs and no hip drive can hyperextend the knee joint, damaging the ACL or PCL. The correct alternative is to drive hips forward and use your leg as a wedge to create space.
    • Ankle Sprain: Lunging forward with an unstable base and twisting the ankle while the opponent's legs are still entangled can lead to ligament tears. The correct alternative is to maintain a wide base and controlled hip movement.
    • Lower Back Strain: Rounding your back and attempting to lift or force the opponent's legs open without proper hip engagement can cause lumbar disc compression. The correct alternative is to keep your back straight and use your hips to drive.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling: Practice the entire movement sequence without a partner, focusing on hip angles and leg placement. (10 reps each side, 0% resistance).
    2. Partnered Static Guard Break: Partner lies on their back with legs closed. You practice grip acquisition, hip drive, and leg separation without resistance. (10 reps each side, 10% resistance).
    3. Partnered Controlled Guard Break: Partner maintains a loose closed guard. You practice the guard break and initial hip drive. Partner offers minimal resistance. (10 reps each side, 25% resistance).
    4. Partnered Guard Pass Attempt: Partner maintains a more active closed guard. You execute the guard break and attempt to transition to side control. Partner defends passively. (5 reps each side, 50% resistance).
    5. Live Rolling - Controlled: Spar with a partner who is focused on defending passively, allowing you to practice the pass under light pressure. (3 x 2-minute rounds, 75% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling - Full: Spar with a partner using your full range of movement and technique against their active defense. (5 x 3-minute rounds, 100% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent has secured a tight closed-guard and you are in a stable kneeling or standing position.
    • When your opponent attempts to transition to a less dominant guard like butterfly-guard or half-guard, leaving an opening.
    • When you have established a strong cross-collar grip and can control their posture.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Reguarding: If the pass begins, the opponent can immediately re-establish their guard by bringing their knees back together and creating space with their hips. This requires quick hip movement and leg extension.
    • Shrimping and Hip Escape: The opponent can shrimp their hips away to create distance, break the passer's base, and potentially attempt a sweep or transition to a different guard.
    • Underhook/Control: The opponent can secure an underhook on the passer's arm or shoulder, allowing them to off-balance the passer and prevent the forward drive.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to pass the guard in BJJ?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are trying to force the guard open with straight legs and no hip drive. This puts excessive hyperextension pressure on the knee joint, risking ACL/PCL damage. To fix this, focus on bending your knees, driving your hips forward like a wedge, and using your leg as a lever to create space, rather than a rigid barrier.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, strength is your enemy. Focus on biomechanical leverage and speed. Instead of trying to muscle their legs apart, create a stable base with wide knees, angle your body, and use your hip drive to create a small opening. Once the guard is broken, prioritize establishing a dominant position quickly before they can use their size to re-establish control.

    Q: The best time to attempt a guard pass is when your opponent's posture is broken, or they are transitioning between guards, creating a momentary opening. If you fail, the most important thing is to immediately recover your base and posture. Do not stay in a compromised position; revert to a defensive stance, reset your grips, and look for the next opportunity rather than forcing a failed attempt.

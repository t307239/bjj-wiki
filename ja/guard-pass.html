This guide focuses on the fundamental concept of passing the guard, aiming to establish side control or mount from your opponent's guard.

White belts often struggle by focusing on brute force rather than leverage, leading to ineffective passes and potential injury.

The key insight is to control the opponent's hips and legs with your body weight and structure, creating openings to move around them.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting Position: Opponent is in Closed Guard. Grip opponent's right pant leg at the ankle with your left hand (pant grip).
    2. Opposite Grip: Grip opponent's right sleeve near the wrist with your right hand (sleeve grip).
    3. Hip Angle: Drive your right hip forward and slightly down, angling your body 45 degrees to your right relative to the opponent.
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your right leg, keeping your left leg slightly bent to maintain balance.
    5. Knee Movement: Bring your left knee towards your chest, tucking it to protect your own legs.
    6. Leg Release: Use your left hand to peel the opponent's right leg off your right leg by pulling the pant grip towards your chest.
    7. Pressure and Advance: As the leg is released, drive your right shoulder into the opponent's chest, using your right hand on their sleeve to maintain distance and control their posture.
    8. Passing Motion: Step your left foot back and slightly out, then step your right foot forward, clearing the opponent's hips and moving into side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Knee Drive: Driving your knee directly into the opponent's hip with a straight leg can hyperextend your knee, damaging the ACL or PCL. Always maintain a slight bend in your knee.
    • Overly Aggressive Hip Push: Pushing directly into the opponent's closed guard with your hips without proper structure can cause your lower back to round, leading to a lumbar strain. Maintain a neutral spine and use your shoulders and arms for control.
    • Ignoring Posture Control: Attempting to pass without controlling the opponent's upper body allows them to maintain a strong guard and potentially sweep you, risking ankle sprains from uncontrolled twists. Always maintain an upright posture and control their sleeves or collar.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Mobility Drill: Practice the hip angle and weight transfer without a partner. Focus on hip rotation and weight shifting. (5 minutes, 0% resistance).
    2. Static Grip and Control: With a partner lying on their back, practice establishing the pant and sleeve grips and holding the 45-degree hip angle. (10 reps per side, 10% resistance).
    3. Controlled Leg Release: Partner maintains closed guard. Practice peeling one leg off while maintaining grips and hip angle. Partner offers minimal resistance. (10 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Pass Completion Drill: Partner in closed guard. Execute the full pass sequence, focusing on clearing the leg and advancing to side control. Partner offers light resistance to their legs. (15 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Guard Recovery Drill: Partner attempts to recover guard after the initial pass attempt. You focus on maintaining control and re-establishing the pass. (20 reps per side, 75% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling (Guard Pass Focus): Roll with a partner, dedicating the round to attempting guard passes. Focus on technique over winning. (1 round, 90-100% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When opponent has established Closed Guard and you are in a stable base.
    • When opponent attempts to create distance to open their guard, creating an opportunity.
    • When opponent's hips are slightly angled away, creating a natural opening.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Leg Pummel: If the opponent tries to bring their leg back, use your own leg to block and create space, then re-establish your passing structure. This involves actively pushing your shin against their thigh.
    • Hip Escape and Re-grip: If the opponent flattens your base, quickly hip escape to create space, re-establish your grips, and re-angle your body. This involves a rapid lateral movement of your hips.
    • Tuck and Protect: If the opponent attempts a sweep or submission, immediately tuck your chin, protect your arms, and flatten your base to neutralize the immediate threat before re-engaging the pass.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: BJJガードパスで手首が痛むのはなぜですか？

    白帯がガードパス中に手首を痛めるのは、相手のガードを掴む際に手首を反りすぎていることが原因です。手首はまっすぐに保ち、指先で「フック」するように握るのではなく、手のひら全体で相手の袖やパンツを包み込むように握ることで、手首への負担を軽減できます。また、腕全体でコントロールし、手首だけで力をかけないように意識してください。

    Q: 大きな相手のBJJガードパスはどうすればいいですか？

    大きな相手のガードパスでは、力任せにパスしようとすると逆に押し返されてしまいます。相手の重心をコントロールし、その重心を崩すことに集中してください。相手の足の動きを制限するために、膝と足首を正確にコントロールし、自分の体重を相手の腰に正確に乗せることで、相手のバランスを崩し、パスのスペースを作り出します。相手の体を「押す」のではなく、相手の「重心」を「ずらす」イメージが重要です。

    Q: BJJガードパスで相手の足を越えられません。

    相手の足を越えられないのは、パスの際に相手の腰をしっかりコントロールできていないことが原因です。相手の片足を足でコントロールし、もう片方の足で相手の腰を自分の胸に引きつけるようにして、相手の股関節を「ロック」します。このロックを維持したまま、自分の腰を相手の腰にぶつけるようにして体を回転させ、相手の足を越えていきます。相手の足に「触れる」だけでなく、相手の腰の動きを「止める」ことが重要です。

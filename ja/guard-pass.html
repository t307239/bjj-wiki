Beginner's Guide to BJJ Guard Pass

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

This technique starts from your opponent controlling your legs in their closed guard, aiming to establish side control or mount.

White belts often fail by attempting to muscle through, ignoring the opponent's hip structure and frames.

The key is to break the opponent's posture and create angles with your hips, not just force your way forward.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Secure a deep cross-collar grip with your right hand on their left collar and left hand on their right collar. This is a strong, symmetrical grip.
    2. Posture Break: Drive your chest down towards their chest, simultaneously pulling down on the collars. Keep your elbows tucked to your ribs to prevent them from creating space.
    3. Hip Angle: As you break their posture, step your right foot forward and wide, angling your hips to your right (your left hip moves towards their left hip).
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your right foot, keeping your base wide and stable. Your left leg remains slightly back for balance.
    5. Knee Drive: Drive your left knee forward and across their body, aiming to place it on the mat near their hip crease. Your right knee should be bent, with your right foot planted firmly.
    6. Frame Creation: Use your right forearm to apply downward pressure on their hip or thigh, preventing them from re-establishing guard. Your left arm is still on the collar grip.
    7. Pass: As their hips shift to defend your knee drive, step your right leg through, bringing your hips closer to the mat. Release the collar grip and secure a cross-face or underhook.
    8. Control: Establish side control by posting your left hand on the mat, driving your left shoulder into their neck/chin, and bringing your right knee to the mat.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Posture Break: Trying to stand up straight and pull the opponent off your legs. This can lead to knee ligament tears (ACL/MCL) as your knees get trapped and twisted under their pulling force. The correct movement involves driving your chest down and pulling the collars to break their posture forward.
    • Over-Extending Knees: Driving your knees directly into the opponent's hips without proper angulation. This can cause hyperextension injuries to the knee joint, potentially tearing cartilage or ligaments. The correct movement involves angling your knee across their body.
    • Unstable Base: Having feet too close together or not distributing weight correctly while attempting to pass. This makes you susceptible to sweeps and can lead to ankle sprains or falls, potentially causing wrist fractures from falling onto an outstretched hand. Maintain a wide, stable base with weight balanced over your feet.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Grip & Posture Break Drill: Practice the collar grips and the downward chest drive without a partner. (50 reps each side, 0% resistance).
    2. Solo Hip Angle & Knee Drive Drill: Practice stepping out and driving the knee without a partner, focusing on hip movement. (50 reps each side, 0% resistance).
    3. Partnered Posture Break & Frame: Work with a partner (inactive) to practice breaking their posture and establishing the frame. (20 reps each side, 25% resistance).
    4. Partnered Hip Angle & Knee Drive: With an inactive partner, practice the hip angulation and knee drive to create space. (20 reps each side, 25% resistance).
    5. Controlled Pass Attempt: With a cooperative partner, practice the full sequence of the guard pass, allowing them to resist lightly. (10 reps each side, 50% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling Guard Pass: Attempt the guard pass during live rolling, focusing on applying the learned mechanics. (5 minutes, 75-90% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent has established a closed guard and is attempting to control your posture.
    • When the opponent's hips are elevated, creating an opportunity to drive past.
    • When you have successfully broken the opponent's posture and they are struggling to maintain their guard.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Re-establishment: If the opponent manages to bring their knees back to your hips, immediately reset your base and attempt to create space again or switch to a different pass.
    • Hip Escape: If you are being stacked or pressured excessively, perform a hip escape to create distance and regain a more advantageous position.
    • Knee Shield: If the opponent creates a strong knee shield, adjust your angulation and pressure, or transition to a leg-based pass.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to grab the collar for a Guard Pass?

    Wrist pain often stems from gripping too tightly with your fingers extended or gripping the fabric of the gi too high up the neck. This strains the extensor muscles and ligaments. Instead, use a "hook" grip with your fingers curled around the collar and your thumb on the outside, keeping your wrist neutral and the grip lower on the collar, closer to the sternum for better leverage and less strain.

    Q: How can I Guard Pass against a much bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, brute strength is your enemy. Focus on biomechanical advantages: break their posture aggressively by pulling their collar down towards your chest, forcing their head down. Then, use hip angulation and precise knee placement to cut through their base rather than trying to push through their mass. Your weight distribution and angles are your primary weapons.

    Q: The ideal time to attempt a Guard Pass is when the opponent's posture is broken, meaning their head is below their hips, and their frames (arms and legs) are not actively pushing you away. This moment of imbalance, often after you've secured a strong grip and pulled them forward, is when their guard is weakest and most vulnerable to being passed.

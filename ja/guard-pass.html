Fundamental BJJ Guard Pass Technique for White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

This technique starts from your opponent's closed guard and aims to establish a dominant side control position.

White belts often fail by relying on brute strength or by not understanding weight distribution, leading to stalled progress or injury.

The key insight is to control the opponent's hips and create a stable base to drive forward, breaking their posture and leg control.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grips: Secure a collar grip with your right hand on the opponent's left lapel and a sleeve grip with your left hand on their right sleeve.
    2. Posture Breakdown: Pull the collar grip towards your chest while simultaneously pushing the sleeve grip away, breaking their posture and creating space.
    3. Hip Control: Step your right foot forward, placing it outside the opponent's right hip, keeping your knee bent and foot flat.
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your right foot, driving your right hip towards the opponent's left hip.
    5. Base Widening: Widen your base by stepping your left foot back, creating a stable, athletic stance with knees bent.
    6. Head and Shoulder Pressure: Lower your head and drive your left shoulder into the opponent's chest, maintaining forward pressure.
    7. Hip Angle and Drive: Angle your hips so they are perpendicular to the opponent's body, then drive forward, pushing their legs away with your chest and hips.
    8. Side Control Establishment: Once the legs are cleared, lower your hips to the mat and secure a side control grip (e.g., cross-face and underhook).

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Injury: Applying excessive, uncontrolled inward pressure with your knee when trying to break open the guard can hyperextend or sprain the medial collateral ligament (MCL) of your own knee. Instead, focus on driving your hip forward and creating a space for your knee to slide through, rather than forcing it.
    • Lower Back Strain: Attempting to 'muscle' through the guard by arching your lower back and lifting the opponent can lead to acute lumbar strain. Maintain a neutral spine and use hip-driven power and a stable base to advance.
    • Wrist and Elbow Sprains: Gripping too tightly with fingers and then having the opponent resist with their body weight can cause hyperextension or torsion injuries to the wrist or elbow. Focus on secure, controlled grips and use your body's structure to maintain position, not just finger strength.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Control & Base Drill: Practice stepping into the guard, controlling the hip, and widening your base. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Solo Posture Breakdown & Hip Drive: Practice the collar and sleeve grip, breaking posture, and driving your hip forward. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Partnered Static Guard Pass: Your partner lies in closed guard. You practice the grips, posture breakdown, hip control, and driving pressure without them resisting. (20 reps each side, 10% resistance)
    4. Partnered Light Resistance: Your partner offers light resistance to posture breakdown and hip control. Focus on maintaining your base and driving forward. (30 reps each side, 25% resistance)
    5. Controlled Guard Pass with Passive Partner: Your partner remains passive but actively tries to maintain their guard. Focus on executing the steps smoothly and breaking their guard. (40 reps each side, 50% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling - Focused Guard Pass: During rolling, focus specifically on executing this guard pass. Your partner can defend normally. (5 minutes, 75% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent has their legs locked in a closed-guard.
    • Opponent's hips are within your striking distance.
    • You have established a stable base and are not in immediate danger of being swept.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): If the passer drives forward too aggressively, the defender can hip escape away, creating space to re-establish guard or transition.
    • Guard Recovery (Knee Shield): As the passer attempts to drive their shoulder in, the defender can insert a knee between their chest and hips to create a frame and block forward pressure.
    • Stacking: If the passer leaves their head and neck exposed, the defender can stack them forward by pulling their hips and pushing their head to break the passer's base and potentially escape.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to break the guard in BJJ?

    Wrist pain often occurs from gripping too tightly with just your fingers and having the opponent resist with their body weight, causing hyperextension. Instead, use a full grip with your thumb wrapped, and focus on using your forearm and shoulder to break posture, not just finger strength. Ensure your wrist remains neutral and aligned with your forearm.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, brute strength is less effective. Focus on precise biomechanics: control one leg at a time and use hip pressure. Instead of a direct head-on push, aim to create an angle by stepping your lead foot outside their hip, then drive your own hip into theirs to off-balance them and create space to pass.

    Q: When should I use the Guard Pass and when should I try something else?

    This Guard Pass is ideal when your opponent is in **closed-guard** and you have a stable base. If they are actively using a dynamic open guard like **spider-guard** or **de-la-riva-guard**, other passing strategies like the **torreano-pass** or **leg-drag-pass** might be more effective due to their ability to deal with leg entanglements.

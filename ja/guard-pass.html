This technique focuses on breaking an opponent's closed guard to establish a dominant side-control position.

White belts often fail by relying on brute force rather than precise body mechanics, leading to wasted energy and injury.

The core principle is to create a stable base and use your hips and shoulder to break the leg entanglement, not just pull.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting from opponent's closed guard: Secure both of opponent's ankles with your hands (forearms inside opponent's thighs, hands gripping opponent's ankles). Maintain a squared base, hips close to opponent's hips.
    2. Hip angle: Rotate your hips 45 degrees to your right (if passing to your right side).
    3. Weight transfer: Drive your left shoulder into opponent's right hip bone, transferring weight forward and down.
    4. Leg break: Simultaneously, extend your right leg back and drive your left knee forward, creating a wedge effect with your shin against opponent's right thigh.
    5. Grip release: As the leg entanglement loosens, release your grip on the right ankle and reach your right hand to grip opponent's right hip or waistline.
    6. Base adjustment: Maintain your squared base, keeping your hips low and close, ensuring opponent cannot re-establish guard.
    7. Shoulder pressure: Continue driving your left shoulder into opponent's right hip, preventing them from bridging or turning in.
    8. Pass to side control: Step your right leg around opponent's body, bringing your right hip to the mat and securing side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect knee drive: Forcing your knee directly into opponent's groin can cause severe groin strains and ligament damage to your own knee by forcing it into an unnatural valgus position.
    • Over-extension of arms: Pulling opponent's ankles with locked elbows and excessive force stresses the elbow joint, risking hyperextension injuries (ligament tears).
    • Relying on pulling: Yanking opponent's legs without hip pressure can lead to lower back pain as your spine is compressed and strained.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Isolation (0%): Practice the hip rotation and shoulder drive motion without a partner. Focus on the feeling of weight transfer. (20 reps)
    2. Partner Resistance (25%): Partner lies on their back with legs in closed guard. You practice the initial grip and hip angle adjustment, then the leg break motion without full pressure. (15 reps)
    3. Controlled Pass (50%): Partner maintains a loose closed guard. You execute the entire pass sequence, focusing on grip breaking and hip pressure. Partner offers minimal resistance. (10 reps per side)
    4. Gradual Increase (75%): Partner actively tries to maintain guard. You apply controlled pressure and execute the pass. (8 reps per side)
    5. Simulated Rolling (90%): Partner attempts to defend and re-guard. You focus on maintaining base and pressure, adapting to their reactions. (5 reps per side)
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Attempt the guard pass during live rolling, prioritizing technique over speed. (3-5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When opponent has established a tight closed guard and is preventing forward pressure.
    • When opponent attempts to open guard and you have a brief window to break the connection.
    • When opponent is attempting a submission from closed guard and you need to create space to defend.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Knee-to-chest defense: When opponent drives their knee into your chest, immediately block with your forearms, creating a frame and widening your base to prevent them from driving forward.
    • Hip escape and re-guard: If they start to break your guard, immediately shrimp your hips away and re-establish your guard by bringing your knees between you and your opponent.
    • Leg Pummeling: If they try to pass your legs, actively pummel your legs to the outside or inside to prevent them from isolating one leg or achieving a dominant position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my elbow hurt when I try to break closed guard by pulling their ankles?

    When you pull an opponent's ankles with locked elbows, you are directly stressing the ligaments and joint capsule of your elbow. Instead of pulling, keep a slight bend in your elbow and use your shoulder and hip to drive forward, breaking their legs open with your body's momentum.

    Q: How do I guard pass against a much larger opponent who has strong closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, brute force is ineffective and dangerous. Focus on creating a strong, stable base with your hips low. Use precise hip angles to create leverage, and drive your shoulder into their hip bone to break their posture and control their base before attempting to open their guard.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a guard pass from closed guard?

    The optimal time is when the opponent is slightly off-balance or their posture is compromised. Look for moments when they are reaching for a grip, shifting their weight, or attempting to open your guard. These brief windows allow for a more efficient and less resisted pass.

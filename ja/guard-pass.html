BJJ Guard Pass: The Definitive White Belt Guide

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

Guard Pass is a fundamental BJJ category focused on escaping the opponent's legs to establish a dominant control position like side control or mount.

White belts often struggle with guard passing due to a lack of understanding of leverage, weight distribution, and opponent's frames, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key mechanical insight is to break the opponent's base and posture using controlled pressure and precise body positioning, rather than brute force.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting Position: Opponent is in closed guard, hips are connected.
    2. Grip: Secure a "collar and sleeve" grip on the opponent's gi, one hand on the lapel near the neck, the other on the opposite sleeve at the wrist.
    3. Posture Break: Drive your chest into the opponent's chest, simultaneously pulling their collar grip down and their sleeve grip towards your opposite hip.
    4. Knee Angle: Angle your hips to 45 degrees relative to the opponent's hips, creating space.
    5. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight forward onto your toes, driving your hips down and forward.
    6. Leg Separation: Use your knee to "step" your lead leg between the opponent's legs, aiming to break their closed guard.
    7. Base Adjustment: Establish a wide, stable base with your feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, maintaining forward pressure.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Posture Break: Arching your back excessively while pulling the collar and sleeve. This can strain your lumbar spine and lead to disc compression.
    • Correct Movement: Maintain a neutral spine, driving forward with your hips and chest, using your arms to control leverage.
    • Knee Impact without Control: Driving your knee directly into the opponent's groin or stomach without proper framing or hip angle. This can cause severe internal injury to you or the opponent.
    • Correct Movement: Ensure your knee is placed between their legs, and your hips are angled, creating a controlled separation.
    • Standing Too Tall: Remaining upright with your base narrow and weight on your heels. This makes you unstable and susceptible to sweeps, and can lead to ankle or knee injuries from being pulled off balance.
    • Correct Movement: Keep your base wide, knees bent, and weight distributed over the balls of your feet, maintaining forward pressure.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Escape & Framing: Practice hip escapes and creating frames with your forearms against an imaginary opponent for 2 minutes, 5 sets (0% resistance).
    2. Static Guard Break: From closed guard, have a partner hold a static closed guard. Practice the posture break and hip angle described in biomechanics for 30 seconds, 5 reps (10% resistance).
    3. Controlled Guard Break & Step: Partner holds static closed guard. Practice breaking posture, creating hip angle, and stepping your knee between their legs. Partner offers minimal resistance to the step. 1 minute, 4 reps (25% resistance).
    4. Guard Pass Entry: Partner holds static guard. Practice breaking posture, creating hip angle, stepping your knee, and establishing a basic side control frame (e.g., head and arm control). Partner actively resists the step but doesn't actively guard pass. 2 minutes, 3 reps (50% resistance).
    5. Flow Rolling - Guard Pass Focus: Partner starts in closed guard. You attempt guard passes (focusing on the described technique). Partner defends by closing guard again or attempting a simple sweep. 5 minutes, 2 rounds (75% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling - Guard Pass Attempts: Full live rolling. You prioritize attempting the guard pass when the opportunity arises. Partner defends and attempts submissions/sweeps. 5 minutes, 3 rounds (100% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent initiates a closed guard, connecting their heels.
    • Opponent attempts to "stack" you, bringing their hips close to yours.
    • Opponent tries to "sit up" and gain an advantageous base while in guard.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Closed Guard: The opponent will attempt to re-establish their guard by bringing their legs back together. To counter this, maintain forward pressure, ensure your hips are angled, and drive your knee firmly between their legs before they can fully close the guard.
    • Hip Bump Sweep: If the opponent tries to hip bump you by pushing your hip away, immediately widen your base, drop your weight onto your toes, and use your gripping arm to pull their shoulder down, re-establishing your base and pressure.
    • Arm Drag: If the opponent attempts an arm drag to pull you into their guard, keep your elbow tight to your body and use your collar grip to prevent them from isolating your arm. Use your free hand to establish a grip on their far hip or pants to maintain control.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to pass the guard?

    Your lower back pain likely stems from hyperextending your spine to break your opponent's posture. Instead of arching, maintain a neutral spine and drive your hips forward and down, using your lats and chest to create pressure. Imagine you are trying to "sit" on their hips while keeping your back straight.

    Q: How can I pass a much bigger opponent's guard as a smaller white belt?

    Against a larger opponent, brute force is ineffective and dangerous. Focus on precise leverage and speed. Break their posture with a strong collar and sleeve grip, driving your chest into theirs. Then, quickly create an angle with your hips and drive your knee between their legs before they can use their weight to smother you. Mobility and timing are your greatest assets.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a guard pass, and should I always try to break their guard open?

    The best time to attempt a guard pass is when your opponent has lost their posture or is unbalanced. You don't always need to "break" their guard open forcefully; often, creating a small opening by angling your hips and driving a knee through is enough to initiate the pass. Focus on disrupting their structure and creating space to move.

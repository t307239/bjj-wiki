Triangle Choke: The Essential BJJ Technique for White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

The Triangle Choke is a submission applied from closed guard, aiming to cut off blood flow to the opponent's brain. It leverages the legs to create a vise around the neck and one arm.

White belts often struggle by rushing the setup or failing to secure the correct angles, leading to ineffective attempts and vulnerability. Proper weight distribution is crucial for success.

The core mechanical principle is to create a perpendicular angle between your hips and the opponent's torso, trapping their arm and neck simultaneously.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish a cross-collar grip with your right hand on the opponent's left collar, and a sleeve grip with your left hand on their right sleeve.
    2. Kick your right leg over the opponent's head, ensuring your right shin crosses the midline of their neck, left of their trachea.
    3. Simultaneously, pull the opponent's right arm across their body with your left hand, and use your right hand to pull their head down towards your chest.
    4. Shift your hips to your left, creating a 45-degree angle between your torso and the opponent's torso. Your left hip should be elevated.
    5. Bring your left leg up and hook your left foot onto your right shin, creating a locked triangle shape with your legs around the opponent's head and right arm.
    6. Squeeze your knees together forcefully, pushing your hips forward and upward. This compresses the carotid arteries.
    7. Adjust your posture by leaning back slightly, keeping your spine straight, to maximize the pressure. Ensure your right knee is not pushing directly into your own shin.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending your leg to lock the triangle: This can lead to hyperextension of your own knee, damaging the ACL or MCL ligaments. Always ensure your knee is bent and the pressure is controlled.
    • Tucking your chin on the wrong side when the opponent is posturing up: This can expose your neck to a guillotine choke or allow the opponent to stack you, potentially injuring your spine. Keep your chin tucked towards your shoulder on the side of the trapped arm.
    • Gripping too loosely on the collar or sleeve during the setup: This allows the opponent to easily escape the initial control and posture up, preventing the triangle setup. Maintain firm, consistent grips throughout the setup.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg movement and hip shift without a partner. Focus on the feeling of trapping the imaginary arm and neck. (10 reps)
    2. Partner drilling (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, execute the entire triangle setup and lock, focusing on precise grip and body positioning. (5 reps per side)
    3. Partner drilling (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to focus on executing the steps cleanly and feeling the pressure. (5 reps per side)
    4. Partner drilling (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to prevent the setup but does not defend the choke itself. Focus on overcoming minor resistance. (5 reps per side)
    5. Live rolling (75% resistance): Apply the triangle choke in a rolling scenario, but with the understanding that your partner will defend actively. Focus on timing and commitment. (2 rounds)
    6. Live rolling (100% resistance): Integrate the triangle choke into your regular rolling practice, aiming for submission against a fully resisting opponent. (3 rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured up in your closed guard, creating space to bring your leg over their head.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard and their arm becomes accessible for trapping.
    • When you have established control of their head and one arm from the guard.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The "Elbow Escape" (Hip Escape): If the opponent's leg is not fully locked, shift your hips away from the side of the trapped leg and drive your elbow into the mat to create space and escape.
    • "Stacking": The opponent can drive their weight forward, stacking you and potentially freeing their head. To counter, ensure your hips are tight and your back is straight, or transition to a different submission.
    • "Knee Slide Pass Defense": If the opponent is trying to pass by sliding their knee inside, you can often use the trapped arm to maintain frame and then transition to a sweep or another submission.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt after trying a Triangle Choke?

    Neck pain typically arises from improper head positioning or insufficient hip pressure. If you're not trapping the opponent's arm correctly, you might be squeezing your own neck or relying on brute force. Ensure your chin is tucked towards your shoulder on the side of the trapped arm and focus on driving your hips forward and upward to create a powerful, anatomical choke.

    Q: How can I Triangle Choke a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, you must prioritize precise body mechanics and angle. Focus on securing their arm across their body early and creating a strong frame with your hips. Instead of just squeezing, aim to elevate your hips and drive them forward to cut off blood flow, rather than just crushing their neck. A tight triangle is more effective than a loose one.

    Q: When is the best time to start setting up a Triangle Choke from closed guard?

    The optimal time to initiate a Triangle Choke setup from closed guard is when the opponent postures up, creating space between their head and shoulders. This allows you to swing your leg over their head. Avoid attempting when they are already low and tight, as it will be difficult to secure the necessary trapping mechanics.

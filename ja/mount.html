BJJ Mount: The Foundation of Control

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Mount is achieved when you are on top of your opponent, chest-to-chest, with both knees and shins in contact with the mat, controlling their hips and shoulders.

White belts often struggle with maintaining the position due to an incorrect base, allowing the opponent to escape or counter easily.

The key to a strong Mount is a tight, low base with your hips driving forward, preventing the opponent from creating space or bridging effectively.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a successful guard pass (e.g., knee-slice-pass), transition your hips forward and lower them towards the opponent's hips.
    2. Place your right shin across the opponent's abdomen, angling your right hip slightly towards their left shoulder.
    3. Simultaneously, place your left knee on the mat next to their left hip, creating a wide, stable base.
    4. Grip the opponent's collar with your left hand, pulling their head slightly towards your chest.
    5. Grip their left sleeve or wrist with your right hand, keeping your elbow tucked to your side.
    6. Drive your hips forward and down, compressing their chest and abdomen with your pelvis.
    7. Ensure your weight is distributed over their chest and shoulders, not on your knees or feet.
    8. Keep your back straight and your head up, maintaining a strong posture.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Placing knees too far apart or too high: This creates an unstable base and allows the opponent to easily shrimp and escape. The knee joint is subjected to shear forces, potentially damaging the ACL or meniscus.
    • Leaning back or sitting on your heels: This removes weight from the opponent's chest and allows them to bridge or roll. The lumbar spine can be hyperextended, risking disc injury.
    • Gripping opponent's arms too loosely or extending elbows: This gives the opponent leverage to escape. The shoulder joint can be hyperextended or dislocated if force is applied incorrectly.
    • Not driving hips forward: This allows the opponent to create space and potentially buck you off. The hip flexors and lower back can be strained from poor posture.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Drive and Base Drill: Practice entering the Mount position from a hypothetical guard pass, focusing on hip placement and base width. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    2. Partnered Mount Entry (No Resistance): Your partner lies on their back. Practice transitioning from a guard pass to Mount, focusing on correct hip and knee placement. (5 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    3. Mount Hold Drill: Achieve Mount position on a compliant partner and hold for 30 seconds, focusing on maintaining hip pressure and base width. (3 sets, 50% resistance)
    4. Mount Control and Stabilization: Enter Mount, then have your partner lightly resist escapes (e.g., bridging). Focus on maintaining your base and hip pressure. (5 minutes, 75% resistance)
    5. Mount to Submission Transition Drill: From Mount, practice transitioning to a common submission like an armbar or cross-collar choke. (5 reps per side, 90% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling (Focus on Mount): During rolling, prioritize achieving and maintaining Mount position. Focus on correct mechanics rather than immediate submission. (3 rounds, 100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • After successfully passing your opponent's guard.
    • When your opponent attempts a sweep from guard and you can reverse the position.
    • When your opponent is on their back after a takedown.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Bridging: The opponent attempts to lift you off by pushing their hips up and forward. To counter, maintain a low base, drive your hips forward, and use your grips to control their head and posture.
    • Shrimping (Hip Escape): The opponent tries to create space by moving their hips away. To counter, keep your knees tight to their body and your hips low, preventing them from generating space.
    • Elbow Escape: The opponent tries to bring their elbow inside your knee to create space. To counter, maintain tight control of their hips and prevent them from bringing their elbow through.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to hold Mount in BJJ?

    Lower back pain in Mount often stems from leaning back or sitting on your heels instead of driving your hips forward. This hyperextends your lumbar spine. To fix this, focus on keeping your back straight, driving your pelvis into your opponent's chest, and maintaining a wide, stable base with your knees. This engages your core and glutes, supporting your spine.

    Q: How can I maintain Mount against a much bigger BJJ opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise mechanics are crucial. Instead of trying to 'hang on', focus on driving your hips forward and down to compress their chest and diaphragm, making it difficult for them to breathe or bridge. Use your grips to control their head and posture, preventing them from posturing up. A tighter, lower base is more effective than a wide, loose one.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from Mount to a submission in BJJ?

    Transition from Mount to a submission when you have established solid control and your opponent is reacting defensively. For example, if they are trying to frame against your face, it's a good opportunity to attack an armbar. If they are posturing up to defend a choke, you can often transition to a kimura or Americana. Wait for their reaction to create the opening, rather than forcing a transition.

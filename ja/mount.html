BJJ Mount: The Ultimate White Belt Biomechanics Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Mount is a dominant top position where you control your opponent from on top, typically facing their head. It allows for submission opportunities and control.

White belts often struggle with Mount due to poor weight distribution and insufficient base, leading to them being easily swept or losing control.

The key mechanical insight is maintaining a stable base by driving your hips down and forward, creating a strong connection to your opponent's center of mass.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side control or knee-on-belly, transition your hips towards your opponent's hips. Your chest should be in contact with their chest.
    2. Bring your knee closest to their head across their body, aiming to place your foot on the mat slightly behind their hip line.
    3. Drive your hips down and forward, creating a strong connection. Your weight should be distributed over their hips and chest, not on your knees.
    4. Bring your second knee to the mat on the opposite side, ensuring your feet are flexed and pointed away from your opponent's legs.
    5. Your shins should be perpendicular to the mat, creating a "grapevine" effect by pressing your knees slightly outwards.
    6. Maintain a wide base by keeping your knees wider than your hips. Your chest should remain tight to their chest.
    7. Grip your opponent's far arm (the one not trapped between your legs) with your near-side hand around their bicep, and your far-side hand gripping their wrist or sleeve for control.
    8. Your other arm can secure a collar grip or frame on their hip for stability, ensuring your elbows are tucked.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Placing weight directly on your knees: This can cause hyperextension of the knee joint, potentially leading to ACL or meniscus tears. The correct action is to drive your hips down and forward, distributing weight onto your opponent's hips and chest.
    • Wide base with knees too far apart: This compromises your stability and makes you vulnerable to leg sweeps. Your knees should be wide enough for a stable base but not so wide that your hips are elevated. Your shins should press slightly outwards for a "grapevine" effect.
    • Leaning back or resting on your heels: This removes your weight from your opponent's center of mass, making you easy to escape or sweep. Maintain a forward pressure by driving your hips down, keeping your chest tight to their chest.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Mount positioning drill: Practice transitioning from a simulated side control to Mount, focusing on hip and leg placement. (5 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Mount with a partner holding passive resistance: Focus on maintaining hip pressure and base without allowing them to escape. (10 reps, 25% resistance)
    3. Mount with partner attempting light escapes: Practice holding Mount and re-establishing base as they try minor movements. (5 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    4. Mount with partner actively defending: Work on maintaining Mount against an opponent who is trying to bridge or shrimp. (3 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    5. Controlled Mount exchanges: Take turns attempting to establish and maintain Mount, with the defender focusing on surviving for a set time. (2 minutes per person, 90% resistance)
    6. Live rolling with focus on Mount: Aim to achieve Mount and hold it for a minimum of 15 seconds before attacking or transitioning. (5 minutes, 100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • After a successful guard pass where you end up on top of your opponent's hips.
    • When your opponent attempts a sweep from guard and you are able to stabilize on top.
    • Following a submission attempt where you transition to a dominant top position.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS (Defenses against Mount):

    • Shrimp (Hip Escape): When the opponent establishes Mount, immediately drive your hips away from their weight. This creates space to bring your knees to your chest and re-establish guard.
    • Bridge (Upa): If the opponent's weight is too far forward, explosively bridge your hips upwards, lifting their weight off the mat. This allows you to roll them over or create space to escape.
    • Technical Stand-up: If you manage to create significant space, immediately drive your feet into the mat and stand up while keeping your hips low, creating a barrier with your arms.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to Mount in BJJ?

    Lower back pain during Mount often stems from an anterior pelvic tilt, meaning your hips are not tucked under your body. This occurs when you're trying to force your chest down without engaging your core and glutes. To fix this, focus on driving your hips down and forward, actively squeezing your glutes and tucking your tailbone slightly. This creates a neutral pelvic position, alleviating pressure on your lower back.

    Q: How can I maintain Mount against a much bigger and stronger BJJ opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, your base and connection are paramount. Instead of trying to control them with your arms alone, drive your hips down and forward with maximum force, pinning their hips to the mat. Ensure your shins are tight to their body and your feet are flexed, creating a "grapevine" that prevents them from bridging effectively. Focus on keeping your chest glued to their chest.

    Q: When should I transition from side control to Mount in BJJ?

    The ideal time to transition to Mount from side control is when your opponent's hips are squared up to you, meaning they are not actively trying to create space or shrimp away. Look for an opening where their hips are accessible and you can quickly drive your knee across their body and establish your base. Don't rush the transition; wait for the right moment to ensure a secure establishment.

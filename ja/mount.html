Mount is a dominant top position in BJJ, achieved when your hips are on top of your opponent's torso, controlling their upper body.

White belts often struggle with Mount by becoming too static or losing their base, leading to easy escapes.

The key is to use your hip weight and knee pressure to create a strong, immovable connection, not just sit on your opponent.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side control, place your knee closest to the opponent's head onto the mat, driving it forward.
    2. Simultaneously, bring your other knee up and over the opponent's torso, aiming to trap their hips.
    3. Your hips should be low, creating a flat base and driving your weight directly down through their pelvis.
    4. Ensure your shins are perpendicular to the mat, creating a strong frame against their hips.
    5. Grip the opponent's far side collar with your near hand, and their sleeve (opposite side) with your far hand for control.
    6. Maintain a slight forward lean, driving your chest towards their chest to prevent them from bridging.
    7. Adjust your knee angle to maintain pressure, ensuring your weight is distributed over their sternum and hips.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Lunging forward with your head and chest: This hyperextends the cervical spine, risking neck injury (whiplash).

    Correct: Maintain a controlled forward lean, driving weight through your hips and chest connection.

    • Spreading knees wide apart when entering Mount: This compromises your base and allows the opponent to create space and potentially escape.

    Correct: Keep knees relatively close, driving them into the opponent's hips and torso to maintain control.

    • Gripping too low on the opponent's body (e.g., their thighs): This offers no upper body control and allows them to post their arms and escape.

    Correct: Grip the collar and sleeve to control their upper body posture and prevent them from bridging effectively.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice transitioning from side control to Mount, focusing on hip and knee movement. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Opponent lies flat. Practice Mount entry and maintaining position. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Opponent lightly resists the transition. Focus on maintaining base and control. (5 minutes, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled Mount entry: Start from side control. Your partner offers minimal resistance to your Mount entry. (5 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Mount maintenance drill: Once in Mount, your partner attempts to escape for 30 seconds. You focus on maintaining position. (5 rounds, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Focus solely on achieving and maintaining Mount for the duration of the round. (1 round, 100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT

    • When you have successfully passed the opponent's guard and are in a dominant top position like side control or knee-on-belly.
    • When the opponent is on their back, creating an opportunity to secure the Mount by climbing over their body.
    • After a successful sweep, transitioning directly into Mount if the opponent's defense is weak.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS

    • Bridging (Shrimping): The opponent generates upward force with their hips and legs, attempting to lift you off and create space to escape. This is initiated by driving knees towards their chest and pushing off the mat.
    • Elbow Escape (Forward Roll): The opponent uses their arms to create a frame and rolls forward, often in conjunction with a bridge, to disengage and create space to get their guard back.
    • Technical Stand-up: If you lose Mount and the opponent scrambles to their knees, they can attempt a technical stand-up to create distance and get back to their feet or guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my back hurt when I try to Mount in BJJ?

    Your back pain likely stems from an anterior pelvic tilt and an overextended lumbar spine. When you try to Mount, if your hips are too high and your chest is too far forward, you're putting excessive strain on your lower back. Focus on keeping your hips low and driving your weight directly down onto your opponent's torso, creating a posterior pelvic tilt and a neutral spine alignment.

    Q: How do I maintain Mount against a much bigger BJJ opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, static weight distribution is less effective. Instead, focus on dynamic pressure and control. Drive your knees into their hips and sternum, using your shins as levers. Grip their collar and sleeve tightly to control their posture and prevent them from creating space to bridge or shrimp. Maintain a constant forward pressure with your chest, making it difficult for them to generate upward momentum.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from Side Control to Mount in BJJ?

    The optimal time to transition from side control to Mount is when your opponent's hips are slightly elevated or their base is compromised, creating a momentary opening. Look for opportunities when they are trying to shrimp or bridge, as these movements can disrupt their defensive structure. A common trigger is when their hips are turned away from you, allowing you to 'climb' your knees over their body.

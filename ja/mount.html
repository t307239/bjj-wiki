Mount is a dominant top position in BJJ where your opponent is on their back, and you are sitting astride their hips with your knees on the mat.

White belts often struggle with Mount by presenting an easy target for sweeps or submissions due to poor weight distribution and uncontrolled hip movement.

The key mechanical insight is to create a heavy, stable base by controlling your opponent's hips and maintaining pressure through your hips and chest, not just your knees.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side control, transition your hips to sit on your opponent's sternum, facing their head. Your opponent's hips should be directly beneath yours.
    2. Secure your opponent's opposite arm with your same-side hand (e.g., right arm with right hand) in a "collar grip" around their upper bicep, pulling it across their body.
    3. Place your other hand on their far shoulder or collar for stability and control of their upper body rotation.
    4. Drive your hips down and forward, ensuring your pelvis is lower than your opponent's hips. Your knees should be wide enough to prevent them from tucking their legs but narrow enough for control.
    5. Shift your weight forward, pressing your sternum into their sternum. Your body should form a "T" shape, with your weight distributed over their chest and hips.
    6. Maintain a "grapevined" leg position by hooking your feet under your opponent's hips, preventing them from bridging effectively.
    7. Continuously adjust your hip angle and pressure to maintain control and prevent them from creating space or bridging.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • When transitioning to Mount, a white belt might push their knees too far apart, allowing the opponent to "butterfly sweep" by trapping one knee and bridging. This can lead to knee ligament tears (ACL/MCL) from excessive lateral force.
    • Instead of driving hips down, a white belt might simply sit back, creating a "holey" base. This allows the opponent to "bridge and roll" by creating upward momentum, potentially causing lower back hyperextension or facet joint sprains.
    • Grabbing the opponent's gi pants too low (ankles) without controlling their upper body allows them to "shrimp" away and escape Mount. This uncontrolled movement can strain hip flexors or cause groin pulls from the sudden separation.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Mobility Drill: Practice hip shrimping and bridging motions on your own for 30 seconds, focusing on controlled movement. (0% resistance)
    2. Mount Transition Shadow Drill: From a simulated side control position on the mat, practice transitioning to Mount, focusing on hip placement and weight distribution. 10 repetitions each side. (0% resistance)
    3. Mount Stability Drill (No Resistance): Partner lies on their back. You start in Mount. Focus on maintaining tight hips, wide knees, and chest pressure for 15 seconds. Partner remains passive. 5 repetitions. (0% resistance)
    4. Mount Stability Drill (Light Resistance): Same as above, but partner makes small movements to try and dislodge you. Focus on maintaining your base. 10 repetitions, 30 seconds each. (25% resistance)
    5. Mount Transition and Hold Drill: Start in side control. Transition to Mount and hold for 10 seconds. Partner attempts a basic bridge. You focus on maintaining Mount. 5 repetitions each side. (50% resistance)
    6. Controlled Mount Application: Start in side control. Work to establish Mount. Partner actively defends by trying to prevent Mount and escape. You focus on securing and maintaining Mount for 1 minute. (75% resistance)
    7. Live Rolling (Focus on Mount): During live rolling, actively seek to achieve and maintain Mount. Don't focus on submissions yet, just positional control. 3 rounds. (100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When successfully passing the opponent's guard and achieving a dominant top position like side control or knee-on-belly.
    • When your opponent attempts a guard recovery and you can transition your hips effectively to land on top.
    • When you have secured a strong grip on an arm or the collar and can use it to pull yourself into a superior position.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS (Defenses against Mount):

    • Bridge and Roll: When the opponent establishes Mount, immediately drive your hips upwards with a powerful bridge, simultaneously attempting to roll them to their side. This breaks their base and creates an opportunity to escape.
    • Shrimping and Hip Escape: If the opponent's weight is not fully settled, use your feet to push off the mat and shrimp your hips away, creating space to tuck your knees and begin to recover guard.
    • Elbow Escape (Technical Stand-up): If the opponent is slightly off-balance, place your hands on the mat, drive your hips back, and perform a technical stand-up to create distance and escape the position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why do my knees hurt when I try to do Mount in BJJ?

    Knee pain in Mount often stems from incorrect weight distribution and excessive pressure on the patella. Instead of driving your knees directly into the mat, focus on driving your hips down and forward, keeping your knees wider than hip-width apart. This disperses your weight more evenly across your femurs and shins, reducing direct pressure on your knee joints.

    Q: How can I get to Mount against a bigger BJJ opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise hip placement are crucial. Instead of relying solely on strength, use their weight against them. When transitioning from side control, ensure your hips are lower than theirs, and drive your chest into their sternum. Use your forearms to control their hips and prevent them from bridging effectively, then "climb" your hips up their body to secure Mount.

    Q: When should I transition to Mount in BJJ, and is it always the best position?

    Mount is ideal when you have successfully passed the guard and have a stable top position. It offers excellent control for submission attempts like armbars and chokes. However, it's not always the best position if your opponent is very skilled at escaping Mount or if you are fatigued, as maintaining it requires significant energy and precise body mechanics.

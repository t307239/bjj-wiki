BJJ Mount Position: The Ultimate White Belt Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Mount is a dominant top position achieved after successfully passing your opponent's guard. It allows for significant control and facilitates striking or submission attacks.

White belts often struggle with Mount due to a lack of understanding of weight distribution and base, leading to them being easily swept or losing the position.

The key to maintaining Mount is establishing a strong, low base with your hips, creating a stable platform that is difficult for your opponent to unbalance.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side control or knee-on-belly, transition your knee closest to the opponent's hips over their body.
    2. As your knee clears their hip line, simultaneously drive your other knee to the mat on the opposite side, creating a "shrimp-like" motion.
    3. Your hips should be low, making contact with your opponent's torso, specifically the upper abdomen or chest area.
    4. Your shins should be perpendicular to the opponent's body, forming a "figure-four" shape with your legs, with your feet "gripping" the mat.
    5. Distribute your weight forward, leaning your chest onto their sternum, with your center of mass directly over their hips.
    6. Maintain a wide base with your knees, preventing them from coming together, which would make you vulnerable to a leg entanglement.
    7. Secure grips on the opponent's collar (e.g., cross-collar grip) or sleeves to prevent them from creating frames or bridging effectively.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Placing knees too high on the opponent's hips and leaning back: This creates an unstable base and can lead to hyperextension of the opponent's lumbar spine, causing lower back injury.
    • Allowing knees to touch or cross: This dramatically narrows your base, making it easy for the opponent to "bridge" and unseat you, potentially causing knee or ankle sprains from awkward landings.
    • Gripping the opponent's arms too loosely and not driving weight forward: This allows the opponent to create frames with their arms, preventing proper weight transfer and leading to a loss of control, potentially causing shoulder impingement if you try to force a submission without control.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo: Practice the hip-over-hip movement to get to Mount from a simulated side control position. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Solo: Practice establishing the "figure-four" leg position and low base without a partner. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Partner Drill (no resistance): Have a partner lie on their back. Practice moving from side control to Mount, focusing on hip and knee placement. (20 reps, 0% resistance)
    4. Partner Drill (light resistance): Partner resists slightly by pushing with their arms. Focus on driving your weight forward and maintaining hip control. (20 reps, 25% resistance)
    5. Partner Drill (moderate resistance): Partner attempts to bridge or shrimp. Focus on maintaining your base and re-establishing Mount. (15 reps, 50% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling: Apply Mount during rolling, prioritizing maintaining the position and re-establishing it if lost. (5-minute rounds, 75-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • After a successful guard pass, when you have established side control or knee-on-belly and can transition.
    • When the opponent attempts to escape their guard but leaves their hips exposed.
    • After a successful sweep where you land in a dominant top position.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Bridge and Roll: The opponent simultaneously bridges their hips and rolls their body to unbalance you, aiming to get you "over" them and escape.
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): The opponent uses their legs to create space by shrimping their hips away, then attempts to bring their knee inside your legs to recover guard.
    • Elbow Escape: The opponent inserts their elbow between your hips and their torso, creating a frame to push you away and create space to recover guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get to Mount in BJJ?

    Your knee might hurt because you are not properly distributing your weight. If your knee is the primary point of contact with the mat and you are leaning heavily on it, it can cause undue pressure and strain. Ensure your hips are low and your weight is distributed through your entire base, with your shins perpendicular to your opponent's body, not directly bearing your full weight.

    Q: How do I maintain Mount against a much bigger BJJ opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on controlling their hips and maintaining a low, wide base. Drive your chest into their sternum to prevent them from bridging effectively. Use your shins to "grip" the mat and keep your knees apart. If they try to create space, immediately adjust your hips and drive forward to smother their movement.

    Q: When should I transition from Side Control to Mount in BJJ?

    The ideal time to transition from side control to Mount is when your opponent is reacting to your side control pressure, perhaps by turning away or attempting to shrimp. This creates an opening. Look for moments when their hips are relatively flat and they are not actively framing against you, allowing you to quickly drive a knee over their body and secure the Mount.

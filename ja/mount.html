Mount is achieved when you are on top of your opponent, controlling their torso with your hips and knees.

It's a dominant position offering significant submission and control opportunities.

White belts often struggle with maintaining base and proper weight distribution, leading to escape or injury.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Secure a double-unders grip on your opponent's hips or lower back, fingers interlocked.
    2. Drive your hips down, creating a tight connection and making your opponent's base unstable.
    3. Position your knees wide, just outside your opponent's hip line, forming a stable base.
    4. Keep your chest pressed against your opponent's sternum, preventing them from creating space.
    5. Shift your weight forward by slightly arching your back and extending your hips towards their feet.
    6. Maintain a narrow base with your feet, tucked close to their hips, to prevent them from shrimping out.
    7. If they attempt to bridge, absorb the upward force by bending your knees and keeping your hips heavy.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Standing on opponent's hips with feet wide apart: This creates a wide, unstable base. The opponent's hips can easily drive into your shins, causing shin bone fractures or severe bruising.
    • Leaning too far forward without hip pressure: This allows the opponent to bridge and roll you. Your lumbar spine can hyperextend, leading to lower back pain or disc injury.
    • Gripping opponent's collar without hip control: This offers little control over their body. If they bridge, you can lose your base and fall forward, risking neck or shoulder injury.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice transitioning from side control to mount on a dummy, focusing on hip pressure and knee placement. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): From side control, have a partner lie flat. Practice mounting, holding for 10 seconds, then returning to side control. (5 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Partner resists slightly by trying to keep hips heavy. Practice mounting and maintaining for 15 seconds. (5 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled transition drilling: Start in side control. Partner attempts minimal defense. You mount, hold for 20 seconds. (5 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Simulated roll (positional sparring): Start in side control. Goal is to achieve and hold mount for 30 seconds without submitting. (3 rounds of 2 minutes, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Focus on achieving mount from any position. Once mounted, prioritize control over immediate submission attempts. (5 minutes, 90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • After a successful guard pass, especially knee-slice or stack passes.
    • When your opponent is on their hands and knees (turtle position).
    • During scrambles where you gain top control.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): If the opponent's knees are too close together and chest is too high, drive your hips away from their knees to create space and escape.
    • Bridging: If the opponent's weight is too far forward and they lack hip pressure, drive your hips upwards to unbalance them and escape.
    • Technical Stand-up: If the opponent has weak control and you can create space, use a technical stand-up to return to guard or a neutral position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt so much when I try to hold Mount in BJJ?

    Your lower back pain is likely due to excessive arching without sufficient hip pressure. Instead of just leaning your chest on your opponent, focus on driving your hips down and forward. This uses your core and leg muscles for stability, reducing strain on your lumbar spine. Imagine your hips are heavy anchors.

    Q: How can I maintain Mount against a much bigger and stronger BJJ opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, your base and hip pressure are paramount. Ensure your knees are wide enough to prevent them from bridging effectively, and keep your feet tucked close to their hips. Drive your chest into their sternum and slightly forward, making it difficult for them to create upward momentum. Focus on control, not just position.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from Mount to a submission in BJJ?

    Transition to a submission when your opponent is showing signs of being stuck or unable to improve their position. This often happens when you have secured good hip pressure and chest-to-chest contact, and they are expending energy trying to escape. Look for openings like exposed arms for an armbar or collar for a choke.

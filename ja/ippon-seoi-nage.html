Ippon Seoi Nage is a dynamic shoulder throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to unbalance and take your opponent to the mat.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength instead of precise leverage and hip rotation.

The key mechanical insight is using your opponent's forward momentum against them by breaking their posture and driving through their center of gravity.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a strong collar grip with your right hand, reaching across your opponent's chest to the lapel opposite your side. Grip firmly on the fabric, not the neck.
    2. Simultaneously, secure a sleeve grip with your left hand on your opponent's right sleeve, just above the elbow. Maintain a firm, connected grip.
    3. Step your left foot forward, angling your body approximately 45 degrees to your opponent's right. Your left knee should point slightly forward, and your right foot should be behind your left heel.
    4. Lower your hips by bending your knees, sinking your weight below your opponent's center of gravity. Your back should be straight, creating a stable base.
    5. Execute a strong pulling motion with your right collar grip, drawing your opponent forward and slightly to your left. This breaks their posture and initiates their forward lean.
    6. Simultaneously, drive your left shoulder into your opponent's chest, just below their sternum. This creates a pivot point and leverage.
    7. Rotate your hips forcefully to your left while maintaining the collar pull and shoulder drive. Your right hip should swing underneath your opponent, lifting them onto your shoulder.
    8. As your opponent's weight is on your shoulder, extend your legs and continue the hip rotation, driving them forward and over your shoulder to the mat.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect grip on the sleeve: Gripping the wrist or forearm too loosely can cause your opponent to easily escape, leading to you over-extending and potentially hyperextending your elbow joint when they resist.
    • Poor posture during the throw: Rounding your back and lifting with your arms instead of driving with your hips can strain your lumbar spine and cause disc compression.
    • Insufficient hip rotation: Trying to force the throw with upper body strength alone, without a proper hip turn, can result in your opponent falling awkwardly, leading to knee or ankle sprains for either party.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo grip and posture drill: Practice establishing collar and sleeve grips, then breaking posture with a pulling motion. 10 reps per side, 0% resistance.
    2. Shadow Ippon Seoi Nage: Execute the entire Ippon Seoi Nage motion without a partner, focusing on hip rotation and weight transfer. 10 reps per side, 0% resistance.
    3. Partnered static drill: With a cooperative partner, practice the grip, posture break, and initial hip rotation. Partner stands still. 10 reps per side, 25% resistance.
    4. Controlled Ippon Seoi Nage: Execute the throw with a partner who offers light resistance and helps guide the movement. Focus on completing the throw safely. 10 reps per side, 50% resistance.
    5. Live grip fighting and entry: Practice establishing grips and entering for Ippon Seoi Nage against a resisting opponent. Focus on the initial setup and balance disruption. 5 minutes, 75% resistance.
    6. Live rolling with Ippon Seoi Nage attempts: Integrate Ippon Seoi Nage into live rolling, focusing on timing and commitment to the throw. 5 minutes, 90% resistance.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is leaning forward, creating a good opportunity to break their posture.
    • When you have secured a strong collar and sleeve grip, allowing for effective control and leverage.
    • When your opponent attempts a forward-moving technique, such as a push or a lunge, which you can redirect.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • If your opponent attempts Ippon Seoi Nage and you feel the shoulder drive: Immediately widen your base, drop your hips lower than theirs, and create a strong frame with your arms to prevent them from getting under you.
    • If your opponent is about to lift you: Maintain a strong base, keep your weight distributed evenly, and try to sprawl your legs back to disrupt their balance and connection.
    • If your opponent initiates the pull and shoulder drive but fails to complete the throw: Quickly transition to a guard pass or a different offensive opportunity, such as a leg entanglement or a sweep.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try Ippon Seoi Nage as a white belt?

    Your shoulder likely hurts because you are not properly engaging your hips and core to generate the lifting power. Instead, you're relying on your arm and shoulder muscles to force the throw. This puts excessive strain on your rotator cuff and surrounding ligaments. To fix this, focus on sinking your hips low, rotating them forcefully, and using your opponent's forward momentum to assist the throw.

    Q: How can I effectively use Ippon Seoi Nage against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, you must prioritize breaking their posture and disrupting their base before attempting the throw. Use a strong collar grip to pull their head down and forward, and a sleeve grip to control their arm and limit their ability to post. Then, sink your hips extremely low, almost to a squat, to get underneath their center of gravity. The throw relies on leverage, not brute strength, so a deep entry and aggressive hip rotation are crucial.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt Ippon Seoi Nage during a BJJ match?

    The optimal time to attempt Ippon Seoi Nage is when your opponent is actively moving forward or leaning in, creating an opportunity for you to catch their momentum. This often occurs during transitions, when they are trying to advance their position, or when they are reacting to your pressure. Avoid attempting it when your opponent is stable and rooted, as this makes it much harder to unbalance them effectively.

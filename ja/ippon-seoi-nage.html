Ippon Seoi Nage is a powerful one-arm shoulder throw initiated from a standing position, typically when the opponent is in a closed guard or standing grip fighting.

It aims to unbalance the opponent and throw them to the mat with significant force.

White belts often fail due to poor weight distribution and insufficient hip engagement, leading to ineffective throws and potential injury.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Secure a standard lapel grip with your dominant hand (e.g., right hand on opponent's left lapel, thumb inside).
    2. Grip: Simultaneously, establish a sleeve grip with your non-dominant hand on the opponent's opposite sleeve (e.g., left hand on opponent's right sleeve).
    3. Entry: Step your non-dominant foot (left) forward, slightly angling your body to your dominant side (right), creating a 45-degree angle relative to the opponent.
    4. Hip Insertion: Drop your hips by bending your knees and lowering your center of gravity, inserting your dominant side hip (right) deeply under the opponent's armpit.
    5. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your dominant leg (right), which is now your pivot point, while keeping your non-dominant leg (left) slightly behind for base.
    6. Pull and Pivot: Pull the opponent's lapel and sleeve towards your chest and pivot your body counter-clockwise (if right-handed) to unbalance them forward and over your hip.
    7. Throw: As the opponent's weight shifts forward, continue the pivot and use your hips and back to elevate them, driving through with your legs to complete the throw.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Sleeve Grip: Gripping the opponent's sleeve too high on the forearm can lead to wrist hyperextension. The ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) in the wrist is strained and can tear, causing pain and instability. Always grip the sleeve near the elbow or mid-forearm.
    • Jerking Motion: Yanking the arm upwards and outwards without proper hip insertion and weight transfer strains the rotator cuff and shoulder joint. This can cause a rotator cuff tear or labral tear due to excessive anterior translation of the humerus. Focus on driving your hip and pivoting your torso.
    • Over-Extending Legs: Trying to throw by solely relying on leg extension without hip engagement can lead to knee hyperextension and ACL/MCL tears. The knee joint is forced beyond its natural range of motion. Ensure your hips are under the opponent before attempting the throw.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Grip and Stance Practice: Practice gripping and assuming the 45-degree angle with no movement. (10 reps x 0% resistance).
    2. Solo Hip Insertion and Pivot: Practice dropping hips and pivoting without a partner, focusing on the feeling of the hip under the armpit. (10 reps x 0% resistance).
    3. Partner Assisted Entry: With a cooperative partner, practice the step, hip insertion, and pull motion, with the partner helping to simulate weight. (15 reps x 25% resistance).
    4. Controlled Throw Practice: With a partner offering minimal resistance, execute the full Ippon Seoi Nage, focusing on proper weight transfer and body mechanics. (20 reps x 50% resistance).
    5. Live Grip Fighting to Throw: Engage in live grip fighting, and when the opportunity arises, attempt the Ippon Seoi Nage against a resisting opponent. (25 reps x 75% resistance).
    6. Full Live Rolling Application: Integrate Ippon Seoi Nage into live rolling scenarios, attempting the throw when the timing and positioning are optimal. (5 minutes x 90-100% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is posturing up from closed guard, creating a slight distance for entry.
    • During standing grip fighting when you can secure a strong lapel and sleeve grip and break their posture.
    • When the opponent overcommits to a forward movement, creating an opportunity for a counter-throw.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Fleeing the Throw: If the opponent attempts to insert their hip, immediately step your hips back and widen your base to prevent them from getting under you. This keeps your center of gravity higher.
    • Breaking the Grip: As the opponent attempts to grip your lapel and sleeve, focus on breaking their grip by twisting your wrists outwards or stepping out of the line of attack. This disrupts their setup.
    • Counter-Grappling: If the opponent commits to the hip insertion, immediately shift your weight to your back leg and execute a forward roll or a duck under to escape the throw and transition to a better position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do Ippon Seoi Nage?

    Wrist pain during Ippon Seoi Nage often stems from an improper sleeve grip. If you are gripping too high on the opponent's forearm or yanking their arm upwards instead of pulling them into your body, you can overextend your own wrist. Ensure your grip is firm but controlled, and focus on pulling the opponent's elbow towards your chest as you pivot, rather than just pulling their hand.

    Q: How can I perform Ippon Seoi Nage against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paramount. Focus on breaking their posture first with your lapel grip, then secure a strong sleeve grip. The key is to get your hip *under* their center of gravity by dropping your hips significantly and driving your body forward. Use your entire body's momentum, not just arm strength, to unbalance them before the throw.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt Ippon Seoi Nage from standing in BJJ?

    The optimal time to attempt Ippon Seoi Nage from standing is when you have established dominant grips and can break the opponent's posture. Look for moments when they are slightly off-balance, leaning forward, or have committed to a specific movement. A slight forward lean from the opponent creates the necessary opening for your hip insertion and subsequent throw.

Ippon Seoi Nage: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Ippon Seoi Nage originates from a standing clinch, aiming to throw your opponent over your shoulder. White belts often fail by relying on brute strength instead of leverage.

The key to success is understanding how to create a strong pulling and guiding force with your arms while simultaneously dropping your hips.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Right hand secures opponent's left lapel, thumb inside the fabric, approximately at the sternoclavicular joint level. Left hand grips opponent's right sleeve, just above the wrist joint, with your palm facing upwards.
    2. Entry: Step your left foot forward, placing it slightly wider than shoulder-width, angled at approximately 45 degrees to your opponent. Simultaneously, your right foot steps diagonally behind your left foot.
    3. Posture Break: Pull down on the lapel grip with your right arm, driving your elbow towards your own hip. Simultaneously, pull the sleeve grip across your body with your left arm, keeping your elbow tucked.
    4. Hips In: As you break their posture, rotate your hips inward, bringing your right hip towards your opponent's left hip. Your pelvis should be angled slightly downwards.
    5. Drop and Pivot: Lower your center of gravity by bending your knees and dropping your hips below your opponent's hips. Pivot on your left foot, turning your body 90 degrees counter-clockwise.
    6. Shoulder Placement: Drive your right shoulder into your opponent's upper chest/clavicle area, using it as a fulcrum. Your back should be relatively straight, not rounded.
    7. Throwing Action: Continue the rotation and hip drive, using your pulling arms to guide your opponent over your shoulder. Extend your legs slightly to assist in the upward lift and rotation.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Elbow Extension: Yanking the sleeve grip with a fully extended arm can hyperextend your own elbow, damaging the ulnar collateral ligament (UCL).
    • Correct: Maintain a slight bend in your elbow throughout the pull, leading with your forearm and wrist.
    • Over-Rotation without Hip Engagement: Spinning your body without driving your hips forward and down can cause your lower back to twist excessively.
    • Correct: Focus on dropping your hips low and driving them forward to create a stable base for the throw.
    • Standing Too Tall: Attempting the throw while remaining upright prevents effective hip engagement and leverage.
    • Correct: Bend your knees deeply and actively drive your hips into the opponent's.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling: Practice the footwork, body rotation, and arm movements without a partner. Focus on hip angle and weight transfer. (50 reps, 0% resistance).
    2. Partner Static Grip and Step: With a compliant partner, practice establishing grips and taking the initial steps, focusing on breaking posture. (20 reps per side, 10% resistance).
    3. Partner Partial Hip Engagement: Partner offers slight resistance to hip entry. Focus on driving your hips in and getting under their center of gravity. (20 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Controlled Entry and Drop: Partner stands passively. You execute the full entry, hip drive, and shoulder placement, but without the final throwing motion. (30 reps per side, 30% resistance).
    5. Controlled Throw with Light Resistance: Partner provides minimal resistance, allowing you to complete the throw with controlled movement. Focus on smooth execution. (15 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling Introduction: Attempt Ippon Seoi Nage during live rolling, prioritizing technique over success. Focus on recognizing opportunities. (10 attempts, 75% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up after you've established a strong collar and sleeve grip.
    • When your opponent is leaning forward slightly, creating an opening for your entry.
    • When you have successfully pulled your opponent into your guard and are looking to transition to a standing exchange or throw.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee Trap and Base Out: If the opponent attempts the entry, immediately bring your lead knee (the one closest to their hip) between their legs and widen your base to prevent them from getting under your hips.
    • Postural Resistance and Grip Break: Maintain a strong upright posture, actively resisting their pull. Simultaneously, attempt to break their grip on your sleeve or collar by pushing their hand away.
    • Counter Rotation and Footwork: As they attempt to pivot, step your lead foot out and away, creating distance and disrupting their rotational force. Simultaneously, use your free hand to push their gripping arm.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try Ippon Seoi Nage?

    Shoulder pain often arises from over-extension or improper leverage. If you're trying to 'pull' your opponent over with arm strength alone, you're likely extending your shoulder joint too far. Ensure you're using your hips to generate the primary lifting and rotating force, and keep your pulling arm at a controlled angle, not fully locked out.

    Q: How do I Ippon Seoi Nage against a much bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus intensely on breaking their posture *before* you enter. Use a strong lapel pull to bring their weight forward and down, then quickly drive your hips in low. Your goal is to get your hips *below* their center of gravity, making their size irrelevant to the throw's mechanics.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt Ippon Seoi Nage from guard?

    From closed guard, you'd typically aim for Ippon Seoi Nage when you've successfully created space and are ready to stand up. This involves breaking their posture, opening the guard, and then using your grips to pull them forward as you stand, allowing you to enter the throw. It's less about 'guard' and more about transitioning to a standing clinch from guard.

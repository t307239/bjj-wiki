Ippon Seoi Nage: The Essential BJJ Takedown for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This technique is a powerful shoulder throw, typically initiated from a standing position with a lapel and sleeve grip.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of coordinated hip and leg drive, relying too much on upper body strength.

The core principle is utilizing your body's rotation and weight to unbalance and then throw your opponent over your shoulder.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a strong right lapel grip (tsukuri) with your left hand, fingers digging into the fabric near the opponent's collarbone.
    2. Secure a right sleeve grip (tsukuri) with your right hand, gripping the opponent's gi sleeve just above the wrist bone.
    3. Step your left foot forward, angling your body approximately 45 degrees to your opponent's right side, creating a stable base.
    4. Simultaneously, begin to drop your hips by bending your knees, lowering your center of gravity below your opponent's.
    5. Pivot your right foot inward, turning your hips towards your opponent's center line as you begin to rotate your torso counter-clockwise.
    6. Drive your right shoulder into the opponent's chest, specifically targeting the sternum area, while keeping your posture upright.
    7. Execute the throw by pulling with your left hand on the lapel and pushing with your right arm on the sleeve, extending your hips and rotating your body to complete the arc over your shoulder.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly gripping the lapel too high or too low can lead to an unstable throw and potential shoulder impingement on your own shoulder.
    • Rushing the hip drop and rotation without establishing a solid base can cause knee hyperextension and ligament tears (ACL/MCL).
    • Forcing the throw with arm strength alone, rather than using hip and body rotation, can strain your lower back and cause disc compression.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo grip and stance practice: Practice establishing the lapel and sleeve grips and taking the correct 45-degree stance 20 times (0% resistance).
    2. Hip drop and pivot drill: Practice the hip drop and body pivot motion without a partner, focusing on the feeling of lowering your center of gravity 20 times (0% resistance).
    3. Shadow Ippon Seoi Nage: Perform the full technique motion in the air, visualizing an opponent, focusing on grip, stance, hip drop, and rotation 20 times (0% resistance).
    4. Grip fighting and entry drill: With a partner, focus only on establishing the correct grips and taking the initial stance, with the partner offering light resistance to grip fighting 20 reps per side (25% resistance).
    5. Controlled entry and hip drive: Practice the entry, hip drop, and initial torso rotation. The partner maintains a stable base but doesn't actively resist the throw, allowing you to feel the mechanics 20 reps per side (50% resistance).
    6. Full technique with light resistance: Execute the full Ippon Seoi Nage with a partner who offers minimal resistance and a controlled fall 20 reps per side (75% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up from your guard, creating an opportunity for a standing grip battle.
    • During a break in the action where you can establish dominant grips and control their posture.
    • When your opponent overcommits to a forward movement or a weak guard pass attempt.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Opponent steps their hips back and widens their base to prevent the entry, creating a strong defensive posture.
    • Opponent uses a strong collar tie with their opposite arm to prevent your head from getting under their chin, breaking your posture.
    • Opponent grips your sleeve and posts their hand on your hip, creating a strong frame to resist the hip drive and rotation.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try Ippon Seoi Nage?

    Shoulder pain often stems from trying to throw with arm strength alone instead of using your body's rotation and hip drive. When you fail to drop your hips and pivot correctly, your shoulder takes the brunt of the force trying to lift and throw the opponent. Focus on sinking your hips low and rotating your torso around your opponent; your shoulder should act as a pivot point, not the primary lifting mechanism.

    Q: How can I do Ippon Seoi Nage against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, the key is emphasizing leverage and timing over brute strength. Ensure your lapel grip is deep and your sleeve grip is tight to control their posture. The most critical element is a significant hip drop and a powerful body rotation to get their weight over your center of gravity. Don't try to lift them; instead, use your body's weight and momentum to unbalance and throw them.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate Ippon Seoi Nage from the guard?

    Ippon Seoi Nage is rarely initiated directly from the closed guard. It's primarily a standing technique. However, you can set it up by breaking your opponent's posture when they are standing over you in guard. Focus on achieving strong lapel and sleeve grips, then using your legs to create space and stand up, transitioning into the standing Ippon Seoi Nage entry. The best timing is when they are slightly off-balance or reaching for a pass.

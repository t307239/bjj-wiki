Butterfly Guard starts from a seated or supine position, aiming to control an opponent's base and create sweep opportunities.

White belts often struggle by focusing on pulling instead of lifting and unbalancing, leading to ineffective control.

The core principle is using your shins to elevate and hook the opponent's thighs, creating space to attack.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a seated guard, sit back slightly, bringing your knees towards your chest.
    2. Place both feet flat on the mat, shins vertical, toes pointing forward.
    3. Hook your shins *underneath* the opponent's thighs, just above the knees.
    4. Maintain a slight forward lean, driving your hips towards the opponent's hips.
    5. Secure grips: either both collar grips, or one collar and one sleeve grip.
    6. Keep your elbows tucked to your sides to maintain connection and prevent arm isolation.
    7. Actively drive your knees upwards and slightly outwards to create pressure and lift.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Placing feet too far apart and extending shins: This over-extends the knee joint, risking ACL or MCL tears as the opponent's weight can force the knee inwards.

    Correct: Keep feet shoulder-width apart and shins bent, hooking *under* the opponent's thighs.

    • Gripping opponent's pants too low (ankles): This allows the opponent to easily step out of the guard and provides no leverage for sweeps.

    Correct: Grip the opponent's thighs or hips, or secure collar and sleeve grips for control.

    • Leaning back too far without hip pressure: This creates a weak frame and allows the opponent to establish a strong pass, risking spinal hyperextension.

    Correct: Maintain forward hip pressure and drive your hips towards the opponent's hips.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice sitting up, hooking your own shins under your thighs, and driving hips forward (50 reps).
    2. Solo drill: Practice placing your feet under imaginary opponent thighs and driving hips forward (50 reps).
    3. Partner drill (0% resistance): Partner stands, you sit in Butterfly Guard, hook under thighs, drive hips. Partner just stands there (10 reps per side).
    4. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner gently tries to step forward. You maintain hooks and hip pressure, preventing them from advancing (10 reps per side).
    5. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner attempts a basic knee-slice pass. You focus on maintaining hooks and hip pressure, preventing the pass (5 reps per side).
    6. Live rolling (75% resistance): Focus on establishing Butterfly Guard and attempting sweeps, prioritizing control and hip pressure over speed.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured up and has their weight forward.
    • When the opponent is kneeling in front of you.
    • When the opponent is attempting to pass your closed guard or half guard.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee-slice pass defense: Maintain strong hip pressure, keep your knees wide, and use your outside leg to hook the opponent's hip to prevent them from sliding their knee across.
    • Stack pass defense: Drive your hips into the opponent, creating a strong base and preventing them from stacking you. Use your arms to frame against their chest or shoulders.
    • Leg drag pass defense: Keep your knees tight to your body and use your hooks to prevent the opponent from isolating and dragging your leg. Drive your hips forward to maintain distance.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why do my knees hurt when I try to get into Butterfly Guard?

    Knee pain often stems from improper hook placement. You might be extending your legs too much or placing your shins too far forward. Ensure your shins are bent and actively hooking *under* the opponent's thighs, just above the knee. Drive your hips forward to create a stable base, which takes pressure off your knee joints.

    Q: How do I use Butterfly Guard effectively against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on leverage over strength. Your goal is to elevate their hips and unbalance them. Use your shins to create a strong, upward lifting force on their thighs. Maintain tight hooks and constant hip pressure to prevent them from settling their weight. Once you create an opening, drive your hips and use your grips to initiate a sweep.

    Q: When is the best time to attack with Butterfly Guard, and when should I switch guards?

    The ideal time to attack from Butterfly Guard is when the opponent is slightly off-balance or leaning forward. If they have strong posture and are pressing down heavily, it's difficult to generate sweep power. If the opponent successfully defends your sweep attempt and stabilizes their base, it's often better to transition to another guard, like closed guard or half guard, to reset and look for a new opportunity.

Butterfly Guard: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide to BJJ

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

Butterfly Guard is a dynamic guard position initiated from the guard or half guard, aiming to create sweeps and submissions by controlling the opponent's base and mobility.

White belts often struggle by being too rigid, allowing their opponent to establish heavy pressure and bypass the guard's core mechanics.

The key is creating a stable platform with your hips elevated and using your knees as levers to disrupt their base and initiate movement.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting from a seated guard or half guard, establish a standard closed guard grip on the opponent's hips or belt line.
    2. Sit up, bringing your knees towards your chest and allowing your feet to rest on the mat, toes pointed.
    3. Place your feet inside the opponent's thighs, approximately shoulder-width apart, with your knees flared outwards.
    4. Lift your hips off the mat, creating a 'butterfly' shape with your legs, ensuring your shins are parallel to the mat or slightly angled upwards.
    5. Secure your grips: a common setup is to grip both of the opponent's pant legs just above the knee, or one pant leg and one sleeve.
    6. To initiate a sweep, drive one hip up and towards the opponent's centerline while simultaneously extending the leg on that side.
    7. Simultaneously, pull the opponent's corresponding arm forward and down, creating an off-balance situation and opening a path for your sweep.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to 'pull hard' with straight arms: This can lead to elbow hyperextension, damaging the ulnar collateral ligament (UCL).
    • Allowing knees to collapse inward: This places excessive stress on the medial collateral ligament (MCL) of the knee and can lead to meniscus tears.
    • Keeping hips flat on the mat: This prevents the creation of leverage and can result in your opponent easily passing, potentially leading to knee or ankle injuries when trying to recover.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo: Practice the hip lift and leg framing motion for 30 seconds, focusing on hip height and knee angle. (0% resistance)
    2. Solo: Practice grip placement on a training dummy or a willing partner's hips/pants. (0% resistance)
    3. Partner Drill: With a partner lying flat, practice entering Butterfly Guard and establishing the hip-up position for 5 reps per side. (25% resistance)
    4. Partner Drill: Practice the hip lift and leg extension sweep initiation without full commitment to the sweep. 5 reps per side. (50% resistance)
    5. Partner Drill: Execute the full sweep with a compliant partner, focusing on timing and weight transfer. 5 reps per side. (75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling: Attempt Butterfly Guard entries and sweeps against a resisting opponent, focusing on maintaining the guard structure. (90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is in your guard and has their weight distributed evenly.
    • Opponent is attempting a guard pass and is within your leg reach.
    • You have successfully established a seated guard or half guard position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee-to-Chest Defense: If the opponent tries to smash your Butterfly Guard, bring your knee to your chest and use your shin to create a frame, preventing their weight from collapsing your base.
    • Hip Escape and Re-Guard: If your opponent is posturing up and you can't establish leverage, perform a hip escape and transition to a closed guard or other suitable guard.
    • Leg Pummeling: If the opponent tries to step over your legs, actively pummel your legs to keep your feet inside their thighs, maintaining the Butterfly Guard structure.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to get into Butterfly Guard?

    Lower back pain often stems from trying to lift your hips too high with your back muscles instead of your glutes and hamstrings. Ensure your knees are bent and flared out, and focus on driving your hips up using your core and leg engagement. Relax your shoulders and avoid arching your back excessively.

    Q: How can I use Butterfly Guard effectively against a much larger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on creating frames and disrupting their base. Use your shin and knee to wedge between their legs, preventing them from establishing a strong base. Prioritize sweeping by using their forward momentum against them, rather than trying to lift them directly. Secure grips on their pants and pull them forward as you drive your hips up.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from Butterfly Guard to other positions like sweeps or submissions?

    The ideal time to transition is when you've successfully disrupted your opponent's balance and created an opening. This is typically after you've established a strong hip-up position and have them leaning forward or to one side. Look for moments where their weight is committed, allowing you to execute a sweep or set up a submission like a triangle choke or armbar.

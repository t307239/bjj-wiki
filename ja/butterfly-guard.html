Butterfly Guard: A White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

Butterfly Guard starts from the bottom position, aiming to disrupt your opponent's base and off-balance them for sweeps.

Many white belts struggle by trying to muscle through, neglecting the core mechanical advantage of the position.

The key is using your legs to create hooks and your hips to generate upward lifting and forward-driving force.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From bottom half-guard or closed guard, sit up with your back straight. Place your knees inside your opponent's thighs, feet flat on the mat.
    2. Your shins should be parallel to each other, forming a V-shape with your knees pointing slightly outwards. This creates a stable base.
    3. Secure a double-underhook on your opponent's torso, with your forearms wrapped around their waist. Your palms face upwards.
    4. Alternatively, secure one underhook and one collar grip (same-side lapel). Your thumb should be inside the lapel for a strong grip.
    5. Drive your hips forward and upwards, lifting your opponent's base off the mat. Think of pushing your pelvis into their groin.
    6. Simultaneously, extend one leg, keeping your foot hooked behind their knee or ankle. This leg acts as a lever.
    7. Use your underhooks to pull your opponent forward and slightly to the side you are extending your leg towards.
    8. As your opponent's weight shifts, initiate a scooping motion with your hooked leg, driving them forward and over your hips.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Trying to 'pull hard' with underhooks without hip elevation: This strains your lower back and shoulders, risking lumbar disc compression and rotator cuff tears. Instead, drive your hips up first to create space and leverage.
    • Extending your legs straight out to 'kick' your opponent: This can hyperextend your knees, damaging the ACL and meniscus. Instead, keep a slight bend in your knee and use a scooping motion with your foot hooked.
    • Letting your opponent stack you by not driving hips forward: This compresses your spine and can lead to facet joint injury. Instead, actively drive your pelvis into their groin area.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Sit up, establish butterfly hooks, practice hip elevation and forward drive. 50 repetitions.
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): Partner lies on their back. You establish butterfly hooks and practice hip lift and forward push. 20 repetitions.
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner adds slight weight. Practice establishing hooks and a gentle hip lift. 20 repetitions.
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner tries to maintain base. Practice hip lift and a single leg sweep attempt. 10 repetitions per side.
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner actively tries to prevent the sweep. Focus on timing the hip lift with the leg extension. 5 repetitions per side.
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Attempt butterfly guard sweeps in a controlled roll. Focus on execution, not winning. 3 rounds.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up and has their weight on their toes.
    • When you have successfully defended a guard pass and are in a scrambled position.
    • When you are in bottom half-guard and can secure underhooks.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stacking: Opponent drives their weight directly down your spine. Counter by actively driving your hips forward and creating space with your legs.
    • Knee Slice Pass: Opponent attempts to drive a knee across your body. Counter by switching to closed guard or a strong butterfly hook to prevent the pass.
    • Hip Escape and Base Reset: Opponent attempts to create space and stand up. Counter by maintaining tight hooks and driving your hips forward to keep them close.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to sweep from Butterfly Guard?

    This is likely due to trying to lift your opponent solely with your arms and back muscles, rather than using your hips. Ensure you are actively driving your pelvis forward and upwards into your opponent's groin. This uses the larger gluteal and hamstring muscles and creates a biomechanical advantage, reducing strain on your lumbar spine.

    Q: How can I use Butterfly Guard effectively against a much larger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on the 'lifting' aspect of the sweep rather than brute force. Use your underhooks to slightly pull them forward, then drive your hips up and forward aggressively. Your legs act as levers to off-balance their superior mass. Prioritize securing tight hooks and maintaining a stable base under their weight.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from Butterfly Guard to another guard if the sweep isn't working?

    If your opponent successfully defends your initial sweep attempt and stabilizes their base, it's time to transition. Look for opportunities to switch to closed guard by bringing your feet together, or to a technical stand-up if they create significant space. Avoid lingering in a compromised butterfly guard position for too long.

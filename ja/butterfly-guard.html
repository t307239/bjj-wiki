バタフライガード：白帯のためのBJJ生体力学ガイド

Butterfly Guard is a sweeping and submission position initiated from the guard, aiming to disrupt the opponent's base and off-balance them.

White belts often struggle by trying to muscle through, neglecting the precise body mechanics needed to elevate and control.

The key insight is using your hips to create leverage, not just brute force, to control your opponent's weight distribution.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a seated guard position, place both feet inside the opponent's thighs, knees wide, forming a 'butterfly' shape.
    2. Secure a standard "collar grip" on the opponent's gi, pulling them slightly forward and down, creating slack in their posture.
    3. Simultaneously, establish a "sleeve grip" on the opponent's opposite arm, using your forearm to create a frame against their bicep.
    4. With your hips, drive your left knee upwards and slightly towards your left shoulder, tucking it under the opponent's right thigh.
    5. Your right leg hooks behind the opponent's right knee, creating a "hook" that prevents them from easily stepping out.
    6. Shift your weight onto your left hip, angling your torso to your left, creating a strong base for leverage.
    7. Execute a "hip lift" by extending your hips upwards and forwards, using your hooked right leg to drive the opponent's weight over their base.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • When attempting to lift, white belts often straighten their legs completely, causing their knees to hyperextend. This can lead to ACL or meniscus tears. The correct movement is to drive the hips upwards while maintaining a slight bend in the knees.
    • In gripping, white belts may grab the opponent's sleeves too tightly or with a flat palm, stressing the wrist joint. This can cause wrist sprains or tendonitis. The correct grip is with fingers wrapped around the sleeve, thumb over, creating a secure but flexible hold.
    • When framing with the arms, white belts often push directly into the opponent's chest, allowing the opponent to stack. This can lead to shoulder impingement or neck strain. The correct frame uses the forearm against the bicep, angled to push the opponent's weight away and off-center.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip lift and hook placement without resistance. Focus on the feeling of elevating your hips. (20 reps)
    2. Static partner drill: With a compliant partner, practice entering Butterfly Guard and executing the hip lift and hook. Partner remains still. (15 reps per side)
    3. Light pressure drill: Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the sweep mechanics. Focus on maintaining your hooks and hip angle. (25% resistance, 10 reps)
    4. Controlled sweep drill: Partner actively tries to defend but allows for sweep attempts. Focus on transitioning to a dominant position after the sweep. (50% resistance, 15 reps)
    5. Dynamic guard retention drill: Partner attempts to pass your Butterfly Guard. Focus on re-establishing hooks and hip control when they try to break your structure. (75% resistance, 20 reps)
    6. Live rolling: Attempt Butterfly Guard sweeps and submissions in a rolling scenario, prioritizing safety and technique over outcome. (90-100% resistance, 5 minutes per round)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is posturing up and you have space to insert your hooks.
    • When the opponent is leaning heavily forward, making them susceptible to an off-balance sweep.
    • When the opponent is trying to pass your guard and you can intercept their leg.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • "Stack Pass Defense": If the opponent tries to stack, drive your hips into their thighs, creating a frame with your forearms against their hips to prevent them from fully compressing you.
    • "Knee Slice Defense": If the opponent attempts a knee slice, immediately bring your knees together and drive your hips back, creating a tight "shell" to prevent them from getting their knee across.
    • "Leg Drag Defense": If the opponent attempts a leg drag, shrimp your hips away and re-establish your hooks, aiming to bring your knees back to a closed guard or transition to another guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my back hurt when I try to do the Butterfly Guard sweep?

    Your back pain likely stems from an over-reliance on lumbar flexion rather than hip extension for the sweep. When you try to lift the opponent by arching your back, you put excessive strain on your spine. Instead, focus on driving your hips upwards and forwards from your glutes and hamstrings, maintaining a neutral spine.

    Q: How can I effectively use Butterfly Guard against a much larger and heavier opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, your hip lift must be explosive and timed precisely to disrupt their base. Focus on securing tight collar and sleeve grips to pull them forward, creating a momentary imbalance. Use your hooks to elevate their weight over their supporting leg, and don't be afraid to transition to a sweep that uses more momentum, like a pendulum sweep, if the direct hip lift isn't sufficient.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a sweep from Butterfly Guard versus going for a submission?

    The optimal time to sweep is when you have successfully disrupted the opponent's base and weight distribution, making them unstable. You can attempt submissions like a butterfly guard triangle choke or armbar when you have dominant control and the opponent is postured up or reaching, creating an opening. Prioritize the sweep if their base is compromised, as a successful sweep often leads to even better submission opportunities.

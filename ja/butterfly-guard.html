Butterfly Guard: A Biomechanically Precise Guide for BJJ White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

Butterfly Guard is initiated from a seated or supine position, aiming to create off-balancing opportunities and sweep or transition. White belts often struggle by tensing up or allowing their opponent to establish a dominant base. The key is to use your shins as hooks and hips as levers for controlled elevation and movement.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From supine, sit up and place both feet inside opponent's thighs, knees bent at 90 degrees, feet flexed. Your shins should be parallel to the mat, creating a "butterfly" shape.
    2. Grip both of opponent's sleeves with your hands, palms facing away from you, thumbs inside. This is a standard "Sleeve Grip".
    3. Angle your hips slightly to one side, creating a 45-degree angle between your torso and opponent's center line.
    4. Drive your left knee towards the mat while simultaneously lifting your right hip off the mat, transferring weight slightly to your right side.
    5. Use your left shin hook to elevate opponent's right leg, pulling their sleeve towards their opposite hip.
    6. As opponent's weight shifts, drive your right hip forward and under their elevated leg, using your right shin to scoop their ankle.
    7. Continue the hip drive and sleeve pull to complete the sweep, rotating your body to land in a dominant top position.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly framing with forearms against opponent's hips: This can lead to wrist hyperextension or carpal tunnel syndrome if opponent applies pressure. The correct alternative is to use your shins and hips to control distance and create leverage.
    • Extending knees straight to "push" opponent away: This places excessive strain on knee ligaments (ACL/MCL) and offers no leverage. Instead, keep knees bent and use shin hooks to lift and off-balance.
    • Tensing the entire body and "pulling" with arms: This leads to shoulder impingement and elbow hyperextension. Focus on hip-driven movement and precise sleeve grips for controlled pulling.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Elevation Drill: Practice lifting one hip off the mat while keeping shins hooked and sleeves gripped. 50 reps per side.
    2. Static Butterfly Guard Setup: Partner sits passively. Practice entering the guard with correct foot and shin placement. 20 repetitions.
    3. Controlled Sweep Entry (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance. Practice the sweep motion from guard entry to hip elevation. 10 sweeps per side.
    4. Butterfly Guard Sweep (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to defend but does not pass. Focus on executing the sweep mechanics. 15 sweeps per side.
    5. Butterfly Guard Sweep (75% resistance): Partner attempts to defend and apply pressure. Work on maintaining your structure and executing the sweep. 10 sweeps per side.
    6. Live Rolling (90% intensity): Incorporate Butterfly Guard sweeps into rolling with a focus on timing and execution. 3 rounds.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When opponent is in your guard and attempting to pass, especially with a wide base.
    • When opponent is kneeling or in a "dog fight" position.
    • When you have established strong sleeve grips and can control opponent's arms.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee-to-Elbow Defense: If opponent tries to pass by driving knees to the mat, bring your knees to your elbows to create a tight frame and prevent them from flattening you out.
    • Hip Escape to Open Guard: If opponent manages to break your hooks, immediately hip escape and transition to an open guard like Closed Guard or Spider Guard to re-establish control.
    • Guard Retention with Frames: If opponent is posturing up, use your forearms as frames against their hips to prevent them from collapsing your guard and maintaining a strong base.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do Butterfly Guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs from gripping too tightly with the entire hand or allowing the opponent to twist your wrist. Ensure you are using a proper sleeve grip with your thumb inside the sleeve, and focus on pulling with your arms as levers driven by your core, not just your wrist. Keep your wrists straight and avoid excessive flexion or extension.

    Q: How can I effectively use Butterfly Guard against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on precise hip elevation and angling. Instead of trying to lift their entire weight, aim to unbalance them by elevating one leg with your shin hook and driving your hip underneath. This creates a tipping point, allowing you to sweep even with a significant weight disadvantage.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from Butterfly Guard to another guard in BJJ?

    You should consider switching guards when your opponent effectively defends your sweep attempt or establishes a strong base that negates your leverage. If they consistently stop your hip elevation, transition to a guard that allows for different attack angles, such as Spider Guard by pushing their arms away, or Closed Guard if they are too close.

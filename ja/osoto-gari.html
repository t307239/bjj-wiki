Osoto Gari: The Essential BJJ Takedown for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Osoto Gari is a major outer reaping throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to off-balance and sweep an opponent's leg to achieve a takedown. White belts often struggle due to incorrect weight transfer and poor grip fighting, leading to failed attempts and potential injury. The key is to create a strong pulling and pushing dynamic, using your hips and base to control their balance before the sweep.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grips: Establish a right collar grip (thumb in) and a right sleeve grip (at the wrist bone). Your left hand secures their left sleeve at the elbow.
    2. Stance: Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, creating a stable base. Your weight is centered.
    3. Initial Movement: Step your left foot forward, slightly to the outside of their right foot, to establish a dominant angle.
    4. Pulling Action: Pull their right sleeve firmly towards your left hip while simultaneously pushing their left collar towards your right shoulder. This breaks their posture and shifts their weight.
    5. Hips: Sink your hips down and slightly back, aligning your right hip with their right hip. Your right knee should point slightly inward.
    6. Reaping Leg: As their weight shifts forward onto their left leg, extend your right leg outwards, sweeping their right leg behind their ankle with the sole of your foot.
    7. Completion: Continue the pulling and pushing motion as your right leg sweeps, driving your hips forward to complete the throw and land in a dominant top position.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Valgus Collapse: Attempting to sweep with the leg while keeping the knee straight and pushing outwards. This can cause the medial collateral ligament (MCL) or anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) to tear due to excessive stress on the knee joint. The correct movement involves bending the knee and using the calf and foot to sweep, keeping the knee aligned with the foot.
    • Lower Back Hyperextension: Leaning back excessively to pull the opponent while their weight is still on both legs. This strains the lumbar spine, potentially leading to disc injury or muscle sprains. Instead, sink your hips and maintain an upright, stable torso.
    • Ankle/Foot Twisting: Stepping incorrectly or applying the sweep with the wrong part of the foot, causing the opponent's ankle to twist unnaturally. This can result in ligament sprains or fractures. Ensure your sweeping foot contacts their lower leg just above the ankle bone with the sole or instep.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Footwork: Practice stepping into the Osoto Gari angle and sweeping motion without a partner. Focus on hip movement and leg extension. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Grip Fighting & Stance: Practice establishing grips and maintaining a stable base. (25 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Controlled Sweep (No Resistance): Partner stands passively. Practice the full motion, focusing on precise biomechanics and weight transfer. (20 reps, 0% resistance)
    4. Light Resistance: Partner offers minimal resistance to grips and posture. Focus on breaking their posture and executing the sweep. (15 reps, 25% resistance)
    5. Moderate Resistance: Partner actively defends grips and posture, but doesn't actively counter the throw. Focus on timing and power generation. (10 reps, 50% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling: Integrate Osoto Gari into sparring, looking for opportunities to apply the technique. (5-10 attempts per round, 75-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is leaning forward, creating an over-extended posture.
    • When you have secured strong collar and sleeve grips that allow you to break their posture.
    • When you have established a dominant side-on position, with your hips slightly forward of theirs.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Inside Trip (Uchi Mata): If the opponent attempts Osoto Gari, step your left foot in and lift their right leg with your left thigh while driving your hips forward and pulling their collar.
    • Snap Down: If the opponent is reaching for your collar, use a strong downward pull on their sleeve and a simultaneous head-down push to break their balance and potentially transition to a single leg.
    • Guard Pull: If the opponent initiates the throw aggressively, you can choose to pull guard by dropping to your back and attempting to establish a closed guard or open guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my back hurt when I try to do Osoto Gari in BJJ?

    Your back likely hurts because you are leaning backward too much to pull your opponent. This hyperextends your lumbar spine. Instead of pulling with your back, focus on sinking your hips down and using your legs and core to create a stable base, while your arms create the pulling and pushing force to break their posture.

    Q: How can I do Osoto Gari against a much bigger and stronger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on grip fighting to control their posture and create angles. Utilize the inside reap of their leg with your sole or instep, rather than trying to lift. Your hip must be firmly connected to their hip to leverage their weight against them. Small, precise movements are more effective than brute force.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt Osoto Gari in BJJ? Should I wait for them to attack first?

    The ideal time to attempt Osoto Gari is when you have already established dominant grips (collar and sleeve) and have broken their posture, causing them to lean forward. You can initiate the setup by stepping in and creating an angle. Waiting for them to attack first can be reactive; proactive setup with strong grips is more effective.

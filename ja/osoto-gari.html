Osoto Gari: The Fundamental BJJ Takedown for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Osoto Gari, or Major Outer Reaping, is a fundamental standing takedown initiated from a clinch. It aims to unbalance your opponent and sweep their leg to score points or transition to the ground.

White belts often fail by relying on brute strength or incomplete body mechanics, leading to poor execution and potential injury. Focusing on the opponent's structure is key.

The core principle is to use your body's leverage to create a dominant angle, then use a specific leg sweep motion to harvest their balance, not just pure pulling.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Opponent is in a standard clinch. Grip their right lapel with your left hand, fingers inside the lapel, thumb outside. Grip their right sleeve with your right hand at the wrist bone.
    2. Position your body at a 45-degree angle to your opponent's right side. Your right hip should be slightly forward of their center of mass.
    3. Step your left foot forward, placing it beside or slightly ahead of their left foot. Your weight is primarily on your left leg.
    4. Turn your hips inward, driving your right hip towards their left hip. Simultaneously, pull their right sleeve downwards and slightly across their body.
    5. As you pivot on your left foot, bring your right leg back and slightly outward, keeping it straight but not locked. Your right foot should be parallel to the mat.
    6. Sweep your right leg in a wide arc, making contact with the back of their left thigh or calf, just above the knee. The sweep should be a continuous motion, not a kick.
    7. As your right leg sweeps, continue driving your hips forward and pull their lapel grip across their chest. This combined motion unbalances them backwards.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to pull the opponent directly forward with arm strength alone. This can strain the elbow and shoulder joints (hyperextension or dislocation) if the opponent resists strongly. The correct action is to use hip drive and leverage to create the angle.
    • Sweeping the leg with a bent knee or a chopping motion. This puts excessive stress on the knee joint (ligament tears like ACL/MCL) and can lead to ankle sprains. The sweep must be a controlled, straight-leg motion targeting the thigh/calf.
    • Stepping too close or too far with the lead foot. Stepping too close can cause you to lose balance or get swept yourself. Stepping too far can prevent proper hip engagement. Maintain a base width that allows for hip rotation and forward drive.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the footwork and hip rotation motion without a partner. Focus on the 45-degree angle and hip drive. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Stand in clinch, practice the grip and body positioning. Execute the sweep motion slowly. (25 reps)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Opponent offers slight resistance. Focus on maintaining the angle and generating the sweep with hip movement. (25 reps)
    4. Shadow drilling with movement: Imagine an opponent and execute the Osoto Gari, simulating their reaction. (50 reps)
    5. Controlled sparring (standing): Attempt Osoto Gari against a resisting opponent. Focus on execution and timing, not scoring. (5 minutes, 25% resistance)
    6. Live rolling (standing start): Integrate Osoto Gari into live rolling. Focus on feeling the opponent's balance and committing to the throw. (10 minutes, 50% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is leaning forward, creating an easy-to-exploit forward weight distribution.
    • When you have established a strong grip on their lapel and sleeve, controlling their posture.
    • When you can successfully break their posture by pulling their collar grip down and across their body.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Uchimata (Inner Thigh Throw): If the opponent attempts Osoto Gari, step your right foot slightly behind their sweeping leg and drive your left hip into their right hip while lifting their right leg with your arms. This uses their momentum against them.
    • Counter-sweep with the other leg: If your leg is threatened, quickly bring your leg back and step it in front of their sweeping leg, establishing a strong base and driving your hips forward to push them off balance.
    • Snap Down Transition: If the Osoto Gari attempt is telegraphed, instead of committing to the sweep, snap their head and collar down forcefully. This can break their posture and create an opportunity for a follow-up attack or takedown.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do Osoto Gari?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are either bending your sweeping leg too much during the sweep, or you are 'kicking' rather than 'sweeping' their leg. This puts unnatural stress on the knee joint ligaments. Ensure your sweeping leg is kept straight but not locked, and the motion is a controlled arc from the hip, targeting the back of their thigh or calf.

    Q: How can I perform Osoto Gari against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a bigger opponent, rely more on timing and leverage than brute force. Focus on establishing a strong lapel and sleeve grip to break their posture and pull them slightly forward. Your body angle and hip drive are crucial. Instead of a powerful sweep, aim to subtly harvest their balance by sweeping the back of their thigh or calf as you drive your hips into them.

    Q: When is the best time to use Osoto Gari in a BJJ match?

    Osoto Gari is most effective when your opponent is slightly off-balance, leaning forward, or has committed to a forward movement. It's ideal when you have established good grips, specifically a strong lapel grip that allows you to pull their upper body forward and create a space to execute the sweep. It's also a good option when you want to avoid a prolonged wrestling exchange and aim for a swift takedown.

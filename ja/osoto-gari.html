大外刈り: 初めてのBJJテイクダウン

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック

Osoto Gari is a major outer reaping throw from a standing clinch, aiming to unbalance and sweep an opponent's leg. White belts often fail by relying on brute force instead of proper weight transfer and leverage.

The key mechanical insight is using your body's rotation and hip extension to create a powerful lever against their standing leg.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grips: Right-handed attacker: High collar grip with left hand, deep sleeve grip on opponent's right sleeve with right hand. Left-handed attacker: Mirror grips.
    2. Stance: Feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, maintaining a strong base.
    3. Initial Pull: Pull opponent's collar towards your left shoulder (for right-handed attacker) while simultaneously pulling their right sleeve towards your right hip.
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your left leg, bending your left knee and extending your right leg back and slightly out.
    5. Hip Rotation: Rotate your hips counter-clockwise (for right-handed attacker), driving your left hip towards the opponent's right hip.
    6. Reaping Action: Extend your right leg, sweeping behind the opponent's right leg at the ankle or calf level, applying pressure downwards and outwards.
    7. Completion: As their leg is reaped, drive forward and slightly down with your upper body, completing the throw as their base collapses.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Valgus Collapse: Attempting to sweep with a straight, locked leg and excessive outward rotation of the knee can cause the medial collateral ligament (MCL) to tear or sprain. The correct movement involves a bent knee and a sweeping motion originating from the hip.
    • Ankle Sprain/Fracture: If the opponent's foot is planted firmly and you forcefully drive your reaping leg into it at an awkward angle, you risk spraining or fracturing your own ankle. Ensure your reaping leg moves fluidly behind their foot, not directly into it.
    • Lower Back Strain: Pulling with only your arms and twisting your spine without engaging your core and hips can lead to lumbar strain or disc injury. Use your entire body's rotation, initiating from the hips and transferring power through your core.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling (0%): Practice the footwork, hip rotation, and arm movement without a partner. Focus on the sequence and body mechanics. 20 repetitions.
    2. Resistance Band Drilling (25%): Attach a resistance band to a stable object and practice the pulling and rotation motion against light resistance. 15 repetitions.
    3. Partner Static Grip Drilling (50%): With a compliant partner, establish grips and practice the initial pull and weight transfer without any leg movement. 10 repetitions per side.
    4. Partner Step-Through Drilling (75%): Partner stands still. You execute the full Osoto Gari motion, with the partner offering minimal resistance to the sweep. Focus on the sweep timing. 10 repetitions per side.
    5. Controlled Entry Drilling (90%): Partner offers light resistance and slight movement. You practice entering and executing the Osoto Gari, aiming for a controlled sweep. 5 repetitions per side.
    6. Live Takedown Situational Sparring (100%): In a controlled grappling scenario, focus on entering for Osoto Gari when the opportunity arises. Partner attempts to defend. 5 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • Opponent's Weight Forward: When your opponent leans too far forward, their base is compromised and susceptible to being reaped.
    • Opponent's Foot Planted: If your opponent plants their foot firmly to resist a forward pull, their leg becomes a stable pivot point for the sweep.
    • During Grip Fighting: When you secure a strong collar and sleeve grip and can initiate a slight pull to break their posture.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Switching Base: If you feel the sweep coming, quickly shift your weight to your other leg and widen your base to resist the outward sweep.
    • Posting the Leg: If your opponent attempts to reap, immediately lift your reaping leg and post it on their hip or shoulder to create a frame and prevent the sweep.
    • Counter-Throw: If the opponent's entry is slow or off-balance, you can transition into a counter-throw like an ankle pick or a forward roll.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try Osoto Gari?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are extending your leg too straight and rotating your knee joint outwards excessively, creating a valgus stress. To avoid this, keep your reaping knee slightly bent throughout the motion and ensure the sweep originates from your hip rotation, not a forceful knee extension. Focus on sweeping behind the ankle with a fluid motion.

    Q: How can I use Osoto Gari against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paramount. You need to break their posture and unbalance them significantly before attempting the sweep. Focus on securing strong, high grips and pulling them forward and slightly off-balance. Then, use your hip rotation and a sharp reaping motion to their ankle. Do not try to overpower them; use their size against them by making them commit their weight.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt Osoto Gari during a BJJ match?

    The optimal time is when your opponent is actively moving forward, trying to pass your guard, or has just committed their weight in a forward direction. Look for moments when their posture is compromised or when they plant a foot firmly to establish a base. Avoid attempting it when they are stable, balanced, or actively retreating, as this will significantly reduce your chances of success and increase your risk of injury.

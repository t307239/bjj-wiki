Osoto Gari is a fundamental BJJ takedown initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to sweep your opponent's leg and bring them to the mat.

Many white belts struggle due to a lack of coordinated body movement and relying solely on upper body strength.

The key insight is using your hip as a pivot and your leg as a sweeping lever, driven by your core rotation.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a strong right collar grip (right hand on opponent's left lapel, thumb inside) and a right sleeve grip (right hand on opponent's left sleeve, just above the elbow).
    2. Step your right foot forward, slightly angled outside your opponent's right foot, creating a base width approximately shoulder-width apart.
    3. Sink your hips by bending your knees and lowering your center of gravity, ensuring your hips are lower than your opponent's.
    4. Rotate your torso counter-clockwise, pulling your opponent slightly forward and to your left with your collar and sleeve grips.
    5. As your opponent's weight shifts forward, pivot on the ball of your right foot, turning your right hip towards your opponent's right hip.
    6. Swing your left leg in a wide arc behind your opponent's right leg, aiming to make contact with the back of their calf or Achilles tendon.
    7. Continue the counter-clockwise torso rotation and hip drive, extending your left leg to sweep their right leg out from under them, while simultaneously stepping your right foot back to maintain balance.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to lift your opponent with your arms alone without proper hip engagement: This can strain your lower back (lumbar spine) and shoulder joints (glenohumeral joint) due to excessive axial loading.
    • Sweeping with a straight, stiff leg: This can hyperextend your knee joint, potentially damaging the anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) or meniscus.
    • Stepping too far forward with your right foot, creating a narrow base: This can lead to loss of balance and a fall, risking ankle sprains (lateral ligamentous complex) or knee injuries if you land awkwardly.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the footwork, hip pivot, and leg sweep motion without a partner. Focus on body coordination. (10 reps, 0% resistance).
    2. Partner drill (static): Assume the starting grip, practice the hip turn and leg sweep motion against a non-resisting partner. (10 reps, 10% resistance).
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers slight resistance by standing firm. Execute the sweep, focusing on breaking their balance. (10 reps, 25% resistance).
    4. Partner drill (controlled): Partner offers moderate resistance. Focus on timing the sweep with their forward pressure. (10 reps, 50% resistance).
    5. Live drilling (takedown focus): Attempt Osoto Gari during live rolling, with the goal of scoring a takedown. Partner defends realistically. (5 attempts, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling (integration): Use Osoto Gari as part of your offensive strategy during sparring. Focus on smooth transitions after the sweep. (5 attempts, 90% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is leaning forward, creating an opportunity for a forward sweep.
    • When you have established a strong collar and sleeve grip, allowing you to control their posture.
    • When you are positioned slightly to the side of your opponent, enabling a wider sweep.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Front roll (Uke): If you feel the sweep coming, immediately perform a forward roll by tucking your chin to your chest and rolling over your shoulder, landing on your feet or knees.
    • Step back and posture up (Uke): As the leg sweeps, quickly step your supporting leg back and simultaneously pull up on the collar grip to regain upright posture.
    • Whizzer counter (Uke): If the opponent's hip comes in, apply a 'whizzer' grip (arm over their back, hand on their hip) and use your hips to drive into them, disrupting their sweep attempt.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try Osoto Gari?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are either sweeping with a straight, locked leg or you are not properly rotating your hips. When sweeping, keep a slight bend in your knee and focus on driving your own hip into your opponent's leg. This ensures the force is distributed through your hip and not hyperextending your knee joint.

    Q: How can I perform Osoto Gari against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a bigger opponent, you must prioritize lowering your center of gravity significantly by bending your knees deeply. Use your hip as a pivot point rather than trying to lift them. Focus on off-balancing them by pulling them forward and to your side, then executing the sweep with core rotation and hip drive.

    Q: I can't seem to sweep my opponent's leg with Osoto Gari, what am I doing wrong?

    You are likely not creating enough leverage or off-balancing your opponent. Ensure your initial step is angled and your hips are lower than theirs. Rotate your torso sharply and pull them forward as you pivot your hip. The sweep comes from the combined motion of hip rotation and leg extension, not just pushing your leg forward.

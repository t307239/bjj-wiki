Osoto Gari: The Essential BJJ Takedown for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Osoto Gari starts from standing, aiming to off-balance and sweep an opponent's leg to achieve a takedown. White belts often lack the coordinated body mechanics necessary for a clean execution.

The key insight is generating rotational force through hip extension and a controlled leg sweep, not brute strength.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grips: Establish a strong right collar grip (lapel near the collarbone) and a left sleeve grip (wrist or forearm).
    2. Stance: Adopt a staggered stance, left foot forward, feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent.
    3. Weight Shift (Initial): Transfer 70% of your weight onto your right leg, slightly bending your right knee.
    4. Pull/Push: Pull down sharply on the opponent's left sleeve with your left hand while simultaneously pushing their collar/shoulder area forward with your right hand.
    5. Hip Turn: Rotate your hips 45 degrees counter-clockwise, tucking your right hip towards the opponent's left hip.
    6. Leg Sweep: As your hips turn, extend your right leg backward, sweeping the opponent's left leg at the ankle or calf.
    7. Body Follow-Through: Drive your body forward and down, maintaining contact with your opponent's center of gravity as they fall.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Hyperextending Knee: Attempting to sweep with a locked-out right leg can cause knee ligament tears (ACL/MCL) due to excessive valgus stress. The correct movement involves a bent knee sweep, allowing for controlled force.
    • Over-Reaching with Arm: Grabbing the sleeve too far up the arm and pulling with a straight arm can lead to shoulder dislocation or biceps tendon tears. Maintain a bent elbow and use your body to create the pull.
    • Stiff-Legged Sweep: Sweeping with a straight, rigid right leg creates a pivot point for the opponent to counter or for your own ankle/knee to twist. The sweep should be a fluid motion originating from hip rotation and a bent knee.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Footwork: Practice the hip turn and leg sweep motion without a partner. 10 repetitions per side.
    2. Solo with Resistance Band: Attach a resistance band to a sturdy object and practice the pulling and sweeping motion. 10 repetitions.
    3. Partnered Static Grips & Movement: Hold static grips with a compliant partner and practice the weight shift, pull/push, and hip turn. 5 repetitions.
    4. Partnered Controlled Sweep (0% Resistance): With a compliant partner, execute Osoto Gari with minimal resistance, focusing on timing the sweep with the hip turn. 10 repetitions.
    5. Partnered Controlled Sweep (25% Resistance): Partner offers slight resistance to the pull and sweep. 10 repetitions.
    6. Live Rolling (Controlled): Attempt Osoto Gari during live rolling with a focus on execution, not necessarily completion. 3-5 attempts per roll.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is leaning forward, creating an open posture.
    • When the opponent takes a step with their left leg, exposing their base.
    • When you have established strong collar and sleeve control, allowing you to break their posture.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • The "Uchi Mata" Counter: When the opponent attempts Osoto Gari, step your left foot slightly back and inside their sweeping leg. Use your right hand to push their collar away and your left hand to block their sweeping leg with your shin, then counter with an Uchi Mata (inner thigh reap).
    • The "Ankle Pick" Counter: If the opponent commits to the sweep and their base is unstable, quickly drop your level and secure their right ankle with both hands, lifting it to disrupt their balance and potentially score an ankle pick.
    • The "Post and Base" Defense: When the opponent initiates the sweep, maintain a wide base and resist the pull by keeping your weight low. Use your right arm to block their pulling arm and your left hand to push their hip away, preventing them from turning.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my right knee hurt when I try to Osoto Gari?

    This is likely due to a stiff-legged sweep. When you extend your right leg straight to sweep, your knee joint becomes a rigid lever. If your opponent resists or shifts their weight unexpectedly, your knee can twist or hyperextend, damaging ligaments like the ACL or MCL. Always ensure your sweeping knee remains slightly bent to absorb impact and maintain flexibility.

    Q: How can I Osoto Gari against a much bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing become paramount. Focus on breaking their posture down *before* initiating the sweep by pulling their collar down and their sleeve across their body. Then, use your hip rotation and a well-timed leg sweep to off-balance them. Do not try to overpower them; instead, use their size against them by redirecting their weight.

    Q: I keep getting my Osoto Gari blocked or countered. When is the best time to attempt it?

    The optimal time for Osoto Gari is when your opponent is actively moving forward or adjusting their stance, creating a moment of imbalance. Look for them to step with the leg you intend to sweep, or when they are leaning forward to attack. If they are static and well-balanced, your chances of success are significantly lower. Focus on creating the opening through grip fighting and posture control first.

Osoto Gari is a powerful foot sweep initiated from a standing position, aiming to unbalance and throw your opponent to the mat. It is often initiated from a collar-and-sleeve grip or a lapel-and-sleeve grip.

White belts often struggle because they rely on brute force instead of proper body mechanics and timing, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The core principle is to use your body as a lever, disrupting your opponent's base by sweeping their leg while simultaneously pulling and pushing them off balance.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a strong collar grip with your right hand, pulling their lapel just below the clavicle. Your left hand secures their right sleeve at the wrist joint, keeping their arm extended.
    2. Position your body slightly to your opponent's right side, creating a 45-degree angle between your hips and theirs. Your feet should be shoulder-width apart, maintaining a stable base.
    3. Take a small step forward with your left foot, placing it slightly ahead and to the outside of your opponent's right foot. Keep your knees slightly bent.
    4. Simultaneously, begin to pull your opponent's right sleeve towards your left hip while pushing their upper back/shoulder area with your right hand. This initiates their forward lean.
    5. As they lean forward, pivot your right foot outwards approximately 90 degrees, so your toe points away from your opponent. Your right heel should now be aligned with your opponent's right ankle.
    6. Swing your right leg behind your opponent's right leg, making contact with the back of their calf/shin area. Your leg should be straight but not locked.
    7. Drive your hips forward and down, rotating your torso counter-clockwise. This motion, combined with the leg sweep and grip pressure, will unbalance them.
    8. Continue the pulling and pushing action with your grips as their base is compromised, guiding them to fall to their right side.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to sweep by lifting your leg high and snapping it outwards: This can strain your hamstring and knee ligaments due to excessive extension and rotational force, especially if your opponent resists by stepping their leg back. The correct movement involves a sweeping motion low to the ground.
    • Driving forward with a straight knee when initiating the hip drive: This can hyperextend your knee and damage the ACL or meniscus, particularly if the opponent pivots or resists unexpectedly. Maintain a slight bend in your standing knee.
    • Gripping too loosely on the sleeve and pulling with only your arm strength: This allows the opponent to easily pull their arm out and can lead to wrist sprains or dislocations for both parties. Maintain a firm grip and use your body's rotational power to control their arm.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo movement drill: Practice the footwork and body rotation without a partner. Focus on the hip angle, stepping, and leg swing. (10 repetitions)
    2. Shadow drilling with resistance band: Attach a light resistance band to your wrist and the opponent's sleeve. Practice the pulling and pushing motion against the band's resistance. (10 repetitions)
    3. Partner drill (no resistance): Stand with a compliant partner. Execute the Osoto Gari motion, focusing on the timing of the step, hip drive, and leg sweep. Partner offers minimal resistance. (20 repetitions)
    4. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers slight resistance by not fully committing to the forward lean. Focus on generating your own momentum. (30 repetitions)
    5. Controlled sparring (0% resistance): Partner attempts to defend actively but does not initiate aggressive counters. Focus on clean execution. (5 minutes)
    6. Live rolling (75% resistance): Apply Osoto Gari in a live rolling situation, focusing on timing and commitment. Accept that some attempts will fail. (5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent has a stable, wide base and you are facing them directly, making them susceptible to a linear sweep.
    • When you have established a strong collar and sleeve grip, giving you control over their posture and balance.
    • During transitions or when your opponent is posturing up, creating an opening to disrupt their base.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Leg catch: If the opponent attempts the sweep, immediately bring your leg back and step your other leg forward, creating a stable base. Simultaneously, use your free hand to push their hip away to create space.
    • Inside reap/sweeper's leg: As the opponent commits to the sweep, step your leg inside their sweeping leg and use your body weight to drive them over their own leg. This is a high-level counter requiring good timing.
    • Takedown defense: If the sweep is initiated, focus on maintaining your base and using your hands to prevent the grip from controlling your posture. You can then attempt a counter-takedown like a double-leg.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try Osoto Gari?

    Knee pain during Osoto Gari often stems from hyperextending your standing leg or forcing the sweep with a locked knee. Ensure your standing knee is always slightly bent to absorb impact and allow for natural movement. Also, avoid swinging your sweeping leg too high; keep it low and close to the opponent's leg to minimize strain on your knee and hip.

    Q: How can I perform Osoto Gari against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, you must prioritize disrupting their base before committing to the sweep. Focus on creating a strong pulling and pushing motion to make them lean forward, reducing their base significantly. Use your hips to drive into them, not just your legs. A strong collar grip that pulls their head down is crucial to breaking their posture and leverage.

    Q: When should I NOT attempt Osoto Gari in BJJ?

    Avoid Osoto Gari when your opponent has a very solid, wide base and is actively resisting your grips, as you will likely expend a lot of energy without success. It's also ill-advised if you are off-balance or if your own base is compromised. Wait for an opening where their weight is shifting or their posture is broken.

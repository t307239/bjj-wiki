Osoto Gari is a major outer reaping throw initiated from standing, aiming to unbalance and take your opponent to the mat.

White belts often struggle because they lack the coordinated weight transfer and hip positioning required for leverage.

The core concept is to create a dominant, off-balance situation by controlling their posture and using your body as a lever.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Establish a strong right collar grip with your right hand, pulling their lapel towards your sternum. Simultaneously, secure a left sleeve grip with your left hand, controlling their wrist joint.
    2. Stance: Adopt a staggered stance, left foot forward, right foot back, with knees slightly bent and hips lowered to a stable base.
    3. Break Posture: Use your collar grip to pull their weight forward and down, collapsing their upper body towards you. Your right hip should be close to their left hip.
    4. Foot Placement: Step your right foot in front of their left foot, angling your body so your right hip is slightly behind their left hip.
    5. Reaping Action: Extend your right leg straight back, aiming to sweep the back of their left ankle/lower calf with the back of your calf.
    6. Weight Transfer: Simultaneously, pivot on your left foot, driving your hips forward and slightly up, while your right leg acts as a fulcrum.
    7. Completion: As your right leg sweeps, continue the forward hip drive, pulling them over your right leg and guiding them to the mat.
    8. Follow-Through: Maintain your grips and posture as they fall, ready to transition to a dominant ground position.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Hyperextending the Knee: Attempting to block their leg with a stiff, straight knee can lead to knee ligament tears (ACL/MCL). Instead, keep your supporting leg slightly bent and mobile.
    • Twisting the Ankle: Sweeping with the front of your foot or ankle can cause ankle sprains if their foot is trapped. Use the back of your calf to sweep their lower leg, not their foot.
    • Over-Reaching with the Back: Throwing your upper body backward to make the sweep happen strains the lower back. Keep your core engaged and drive with your hips, not your torso.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Movement (0%): Practice the footwork and hip rotation without a partner. Focus on the staggered stance, stepping in, and the reaping leg motion. (50 reps)
    2. Shadow Drilling (25%): Visualize an opponent. Practice the grips, posture break, and the sweeping motion against air. (50 reps)
    3. Partner Static Drill (50%): With a compliant partner standing still, practice the entire Osoto Gari sequence, focusing on correct grip, posture break, and foot placement. Partner offers minimal resistance. (20 reps per side)
    4. Controlled Entry (75%): Partner offers light resistance to your entry. Focus on breaking their posture effectively before initiating the sweep. (15 reps per side)
    5. Live Takedown Entry (90%): In a controlled sparring situation, focus on setting up and executing Osoto Gari. Opponent actively defends but allows for successful execution. (10 reps per side)
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Attempt Osoto Gari during live rolling. Focus on timing and commitment to the technique. (As opportunities arise).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is leaning forward, creating a posture break opportunity.
    • When you have established a strong collar and sleeve grip, controlling their upper body.
    • When your opponent takes a large step forward with one leg, leaving it exposed for a sweep.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Single Leg Takedown: If they commit to the sweep and their base is compromised, you can often transition to a double-leg or single-leg takedown by dropping your level and securing a grip on their legs.
    • Foot Block/Sweep Defense: If they attempt the sweep, quickly bring your sweeping leg back and establish a strong base, potentially stepping in to counter their movement with your own.
    • Posture Recovery: If you feel the sweep coming, immediately try to regain your posture by driving your hips forward and away, and potentially stepping out to regain balance.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try Osoto Gari?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are hyperextending it when you try to block your opponent's leg. Instead of a straight, stiff leg, keep your supporting leg (the one on the ground) slightly bent and mobile. This allows for better shock absorption and protects your knee joint from excessive force during the sweep.

    Q: How can I use Osoto Gari against a much bigger and heavier opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus intensely on breaking their posture and lowering your center of gravity. Use your grips to pull their weight forward and down, making them feel lighter. Your hip needs to get *behind* their hip and you must drive with your hips, not just your leg, to generate leverage. Timing is crucial; wait for their slight imbalance.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt Osoto Gari in a BJJ match?

    Osoto Gari is best attempted when your opponent is actively trying to advance their position or is postured up. Look for moments when they take a step forward, creating an opening. It's also effective when they are trying to create distance, as you can use their forward momentum against them by pulling them into the sweep.

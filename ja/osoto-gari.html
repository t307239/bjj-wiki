Osoto Gari: The Essential BJJ Takedown for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Osoto Gari is a major outer reaping throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to unbalance and sweep an opponent's leg to achieve a takedown.

White belts often struggle due to insufficient control, poor weight transfer, and a fear of committing to the throw, leading to ineffective attempts.

The key insight is to use your body's mass and leverage to create an off-balance point, then drive through the opponent's base with your hips and legs.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Assume a staggered stance, opponent's right leg forward. Grip opponent's left lapel with your right hand, thumb inside, and opponent's left sleeve (near the elbow) with your left hand.
    2. Step your left foot forward, slightly behind and to the outside of the opponent's left foot, establishing a base width approximately shoulder-width apart.
    3. Rotate your hips to your right, aligning your pelvis with the opponent's left hip line. Your weight should be primarily on your right foot.
    4. Begin to pull the opponent's lapel and sleeve towards your right hip, creating a pulling tension that draws their weight forward and onto their left leg.
    5. As you pull, step your right foot forward and slightly across the opponent's body, bringing your right knee towards the mat outside their left knee.
    6. Simultaneously, drive your hips forward and down, sweeping your left leg outwards and backwards in a wide arc, targeting the back of the opponent's left ankle/lower calf.
    7. Maintain continuous pulling pressure on the lapel and sleeve as your left leg sweeps, ensuring their weight is committed to their supporting leg.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to lift the opponent with arm strength alone: This can cause shoulder impingement and rotator cuff tears in the thrower, and hyperextension injuries to the opponent's shoulder if they fall awkwardly. Maintain a strong base and use hip power.
    • Sweeping with the knee or shin instead of the foot/lower leg: This puts excessive torque on the knee joint, risking meniscus tears or ACL/PCL sprains. Sweep with the heel of your foot, targeting the outside of the opponent's ankle.
    • Not committing to the hip rotation and drive: This results in a weak sweep and can cause the thrower to stumble forward, risking a knee hyperextension on their own leg. Drive your hips through the opponent's center of gravity.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the footwork, hip rotation, and leg sweep motion without a partner. Focus on body mechanics. (10 repetitions, 0% resistance)
    2. Shadow drill with resistance band: Attach a resistance band to a sturdy object and simulate the pulling action of the lapel and sleeve. (10 repetitions, 0% resistance)
    3. Partner drill (static): Opponent stands passively. Practice the full Osoto Gari sequence, focusing on correct grips, foot placement, and hip drive. (5 repetitions per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner drill (light movement): Opponent offers slight resistance to grip breaking and movement. Focus on initiating the pull and executing the sweep. (5 repetitions per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Partner drill (controlled): Opponent actively tries to resist and maintain balance. Focus on breaking their posture and committing to the sweep. (5 repetitions per side, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling (limited): Attempt Osoto Gari during live rolling, focusing on timing and execution. Do not force the technique if the opportunity is not ideal. (5 attempts per round, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is leaning forward, presenting an unbalanced posture.
    • When you have secured a strong lapel and sleeve grip and can control their upper body.
    • When the opponent takes a step with their leg that you intend to sweep.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Uchi Mata (Inner Thigh Throw): If the opponent initiates Osoto Gari against you, step your right leg inside their sweeping leg and use your right hip to block and lift their inner thigh as you drive forward.
    • Tai Otoshi (Body Drop): As the opponent attempts to sweep your leg, step your right foot forward and place your right hip against their right hip, using your arm to block their pulling arm and simultaneously push their body forward and down.
    • Foot Sweep Defense: If the opponent's leg sweep is initiated, quickly pivot your hips and bring your sweeping leg back, stepping it behind your standing leg to avoid being reaped. Simultaneously, use your grips to pull them off-balance in a different direction.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try Osoto Gari as a white belt?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are not using your hips and core to generate the sweeping force. Instead, you might be trying to 'kick' the opponent's leg with your own, placing excessive rotational stress on your knee joint. Ensure your sweep originates from a strong hip drive, with your leg moving in a controlled arc, and focus on using your heel to contact the opponent's ankle.

    Q: How can I perform Osoto Gari effectively against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paramount. Do not try to match their strength. Focus on breaking their posture by pulling them forward with your lapel and sleeve grips. Use your hips to get offline and create an angle, then drive your hips into their base as you execute the sweep. Your smaller mass can be an advantage if used biomechanically correct to unbalance them.

    Q: I keep getting my Osoto Gari attempt stuffed. What am I doing wrong?

    Getting stuffed often means your opponent is anticipating the move or you are not creating a strong enough off-balance. Ensure your lapel grip is deep and you are pulling them forward before initiating the sweep. Your hip rotation needs to be decisive, and your sweeping leg should be the last thing to move, timed with your hip drive. Don't telegraph your intention; make the pull and the sweep simultaneous.

Sマウント BJJ: 白帯のための生体力学ガイド

The S-Mount is a dominant attacking position in BJJ, achieved from the mount. It allows for superior control and access to submissions.

White belts often struggle with S-Mount due to a lack of body awareness and a tendency to use brute force instead of leverage.

The key to a successful S-Mount is understanding hip rotation and creating a stable base by connecting your knee to the mat.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From mount, secure opponent's far arm with your near arm (e.g., opponent's left arm with your right arm) using a gable grip or palm-to-palm grip. Your chest should be chest-to-chest with the opponent.
    2. Place your left knee on the mat, directly beside the opponent's left hip. Your left foot should be tucked behind your own left shin.
    3. Rotate your hips 90 degrees counter-clockwise. Your weight should shift forward onto your hands and your right knee, which remains elevated.
    4. Drive your right knee forward, aiming to place it on the mat inside the opponent's armpit, creating a "figure-four" leg structure.
    5. Simultaneously, extend your left leg back, creating a stable base. Your left hip should be low, almost touching the mat.
    6. Maintain chest-to-chest pressure. Your right elbow should be tucked, preventing the opponent from bridging effectively.
    7. Ensure your left shin is perpendicular to the opponent's body, and your right shin is parallel to their spine. This creates an "S" shape with your legs.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly lifting your hips too high while transitioning to S-Mount can cause your opponent to bridge and escape, leading to a loss of position.
    • Forcing your knee into the opponent's armpit without proper hip rotation can strain your knee ligaments (ACL/MCL) due to shear forces.
    • Hanging your weight too far back instead of driving forward onto your hands and knee can allow the opponent to shrimp and create space, risking limb entrapment and shoulder injuries.
    • Gripping the opponent's collar too tightly without establishing base can lead to wrist and elbow hyperextension if they resist forcefully.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip rotation and leg placement from mount to S-Mount without resistance. Focus on the "S" shape. (20 reps)
    2. Partner drill: With a compliant partner, practice transitioning from mount to S-Mount. Focus on hip connection and weight transfer. (10 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill: Introduce light resistance. Partner attempts to prevent the transition. Focus on maintaining pressure. (15 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner drill: Increase resistance. Partner actively defends the transition. Focus on securing the position. (15 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Live rolling: Attempt S-Mount transitions from mount in rolling situations. Focus on executing the mechanics correctly under pressure. (5 minutes, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Integrate S-Mount into your game, looking for submission opportunities. Focus on maintaining control and transitioning smoothly. (5 minutes, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have established a strong mount position with good hip control.
    • When the opponent attempts to shrimp or create space from mount.
    • When transitioning from a loose mount to a more dominant attacking position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • When the attacker attempts to transition to S-Mount, the defender can "stack" by driving their hips forward and away, compressing the attacker's base.
    • The defender can "bridge" by driving their hips upwards and towards the attacker's head, attempting to dislodge the attacker's weight and create space.
    • The defender can "turn their back" slightly, tucking their chin and rolling towards their own shoulder to relieve pressure and potentially escape the position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get into S-Mount from mount in BJJ?

    Knee pain during S-Mount transitions often occurs because you're forcing your knee into the opponent's armpit without proper hip rotation. This creates a shearing force on your knee ligaments. To fix this, focus on rotating your hips 90 degrees first, then driving your knee forward. Ensure your shin is perpendicular to their body, not directly pushing into their armpit.

    Q: How can I get to S-Mount against a much bigger BJJ opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on using their weight against them. Instead of trying to lift them, drive your hips into them as you rotate. Use your near arm to "pull" their far shoulder into your chest, creating a wedge. Then, drive your knee forward, using your base leg to push off the mat and elevate yourself slightly to get your knee inside.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from mount to S-Mount in BJJ?

    The optimal time to transition to S-Mount is when the opponent is actively trying to escape your mount, usually by shrimping or bridging. Their defensive movements create openings. If they are completely flat and controlled, it's harder to transition. Look for moments when their hips are moving away from you, allowing you to "cut the corner" with your own hips.

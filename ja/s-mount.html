Sマウント: 白帯のための必須BJJポジションガイド

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate

The S-Mount is a dominant top position in BJJ, achieved when one knee is placed on the opponent's hip and the other crosses their torso, forming an 'S' shape. It offers superior control and sets up numerous submissions and positional advancements.

White belts often struggle because they lack the body awareness to maintain the correct angles and weight distribution, leading to them being easily swept or losing control.

The key insight is to use your hips to create a strong base and control the opponent's center of gravity, preventing them from bridging or turning into you.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side-control, establish a cross-collar grip with your far hand and a sleeve grip (or wrist control) with your near hand.
    2. Shift your weight forward, lowering your hips towards the mat while keeping your chest close to the opponent's upper body.
    3. Drive your knee closest to the opponent's head (your 'outside' knee) onto their hip. Ensure your shin is perpendicular to their torso.
    4. Simultaneously, bring your other knee (your 'inside' knee) across their torso, tucking it behind their far armpit or shoulder blade.
    5. Your hip on the side of the 'inside' knee should be angled slightly forward, digging into their ribs.
    6. Your supporting leg's foot (the one on the mat) should be planted wide, providing a stable base. Your knee should point slightly outward.
    7. Transfer your weight onto your hips and knees, lifting your chest off the opponent's body. Your weight should be centered over their hips, not their shoulders.
    8. Maintain head pressure and keep your elbows tucked to prevent your arms from being attacked.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly driving your knee onto the opponent's stomach instead of their hip: This can cause internal organ damage or severe abdominal pain due to direct pressure.
    • Extending your supporting leg straight or having your foot too close to your body: This creates a weak base, making you vulnerable to sweeps. Your knee and ankle joints are at high risk of hyperextension or ligament tears.
    • Letting your chest collapse onto the opponent's face or neck: This can restrict their breathing and lead to positional stalling, but more importantly, it compromises your posture and makes you prone to being pushed off balance or having your arms isolated.
    • Arching your back excessively to stay on top: This puts immense strain on your lumbar spine and can lead to disc issues. Maintain a neutral spine and use your hips for control.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice transitioning from side-control to S-Mount on a dummy or mat, focusing on hip and knee placement. (10 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Have a partner lie still. Practice the S-Mount entry and maintaining the position, focusing on hip angle and weight distribution. (10 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to focus on maintaining control as you transition. (5 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner drill (controlled movement): Your partner actively tries to defend but without aggressive counters. Focus on stabilizing the position once achieved. (5 reps, 50% resistance)
    5. Live rolling (positional sparring): Start from S-Mount and try to maintain it against a resisting opponent for a set time. (3 rounds, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling (full sparring): Integrate S-Mount transitions and control into your regular sparring sessions. (5 minutes rounds, 90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When transitioning from side-control and the opponent is attempting to shrimp away or create space.
    • When you've successfully passed the opponent's guard and are looking for a dominant top position.
    • When the opponent is turtled and you can secure a grip to drive into the S-Mount.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): As the attacker's knee comes across, actively shrimp your hips away to create space and break their base. Your goal is to return to guard or half guard.
    • Bridge and Roll: If the attacker's weight is too far forward, bridge your hips upward and attempt to roll them over, aiming to land in a dominant position or reset.
    • Knee Shield/Posting: If the attacker's knee is on your hip, use your shin (your 'knee shield') to push against their knee and create distance, preventing them from fully securing the S-Mount.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get into S-Mount?

    Knee pain in S-Mount often stems from improper alignment. If your knee is angled inward or you're driving it directly into your opponent's abdomen instead of their hip, you're putting excessive shear force on the knee joint. Ensure your knee is perpendicular to their torso and your shin is aligned with their body. Always aim to have your shin bone apply pressure on their hip bone, not the soft tissue.

    Q: How can I maintain S-Mount against a bigger opponent who keeps pushing me off?

    Against a larger opponent, your weight distribution and hip control become paramount. Instead of relying on brute strength, use your hips to anchor yourself. Dig your hip on the side of your 'inside' knee into their ribs, and keep your supporting leg's foot planted wide. Maintain constant chest-to-chest pressure, and use your head to guide their face away from your chest, preventing them from creating space to push you off.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from side-control to S-Mount?

    The optimal time to transition to S-Mount from side-control is when your opponent attempts to create space by shrimping or bridging. As they move, their weight shifts, creating an opening. Simultaneously, as you begin to drive your knee onto their hip, begin to bring your other knee across. This timing exploits their defensive movement to secure your dominant position.

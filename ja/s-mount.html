S-Mount: The Essential BJJ Position Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The S-Mount is a dominant attacking position in BJJ, transitioning from mount to isolate an opponent's arm for submissions or to transition to other attacks.

White belts often struggle because they focus on 'holding' rather than controlling the opponent's structure and weight distribution.

The key mechanical insight is to use your hip pressure and knee angle to create a stable base while preventing your opponent from bridging or turning in.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Start from a standard mount position, knees framing the opponent's torso.
    2. Place your right knee firmly on the mat beside the opponent's right hip, creating a 90-degree angle at your knee.
    3. Drive your left knee forward, aiming to place it slightly past the opponent's left shoulder, creating an 'S' shape with your legs.
    4. Shift your weight forward onto your chest and shoulders, pinning the opponent's upper body.
    5. Ensure your left foot is flexed, toes pointing towards the opponent's head, providing a stable base.
    6. Your right hip should be low and tight against the opponent's right hip, preventing them from turning in.
    7. Maintain head control by placing your head on the opponent's chest or shoulder, looking forward.
    8. Grip the opponent's far-side collar with your left hand, and their near-side sleeve (or wrist) with your right hand for control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Extension Risk: Extending your knee fully to try and 'hook' your opponent's arm can hyperextend your knee joint, potentially tearing the ACL or PCL if the opponent bridges suddenly.
    • Correct Movement: Maintain a bent knee, creating a strong base and frame with your shin and foot.
    • Hip Disconnection Risk: Lifting your hips too high to 'sit up' or 'lean back' disconnects your base from the opponent's hips, allowing them to shrimp out or bridge effectively.
    • Correct Movement: Keep your hips low and heavy, driving forward to maintain pressure.
    • Neck Extension Risk: Tilting your head back to look up while in S-Mount can strain your neck and make you vulnerable to attacks if the opponent manages to turn.
    • Correct Movement: Keep your head down, looking forward or slightly towards the opponent's chest.
    • Foot Placement Error: Leaving your trailing foot (the one by the shoulder) flat on the mat without flexing your toes makes it easier for the opponent to push your leg away or create space.
    • Correct Movement: Flex your toes and use the ball of your foot as a point of contact.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Mobility Drill: Practice transitioning from mount to S-Mount position without a partner, focusing on hip and knee angles. (10 reps each side, 0% resistance)
    2. Mirror Drill: Perform the S-Mount transition while facing a mirror, focusing on the 'S' shape of your legs and hip placement. (20 reps each side, 0% resistance)
    3. Partner Isolation Drill: With a compliant partner, start in mount. Practice the S-Mount transition and hold for 5 seconds, then return to mount. Partner provides minimal resistance. (5 reps each side, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled Transition Drill: From mount, transition to S-Mount. Your partner attempts to perform a basic bridge or shrimp escape. You focus on maintaining the position. (10 reps each side, 50% resistance)
    5. Submission Setup Drill: In S-Mount, practice setting up a common submission like an armbar or americana. Partner defends passively. (5 reps each side, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling Scenario: Start from mount and work towards achieving S-Mount. Your partner actively defends. (3 rounds, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When you have established a strong mount position and your opponent is trying to escape by turning into you.
    • After successfully defending an opponent's escape attempt from mount, creating an opportunity to transition.
    • When your opponent is turtled or framing poorly, allowing you to flatten them and transition to S-Mount.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • The Bridge and Roll: When the attacker transitions to S-Mount, immediately bridge your hips upwards and to the side they are not controlling. As they lose base, try to roll them over and escape the position.
    • The Shrimp Escape: If the attacker's hips are not tight, shrimp your hips away and try to create space to bring your knee back between you and the attacker, returning to guard.
    • The Elbow Escape: If the attacker has good hip pressure, focus on driving your elbow into the mat and shrimping your hips away to create a small pocket of space. Use this to bring your knee back into play.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get into S-Mount?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are hyperextending it or not using your foot correctly. When transitioning, ensure your knee maintains a bent angle and your foot is flexed, with the ball of your foot pressing into the mat. This creates a stable base and prevents excessive stress on the knee joint.

    Q: How can I maintain S-Mount against a bigger opponent who keeps bridging?

    Against a larger opponent, your hip pressure and forward weight distribution are paramount. Instead of just sitting up, drive your chest into their chest and use your hips to anchor their hips. Maintain a tight frame with your leg closest to their shoulder, using your foot to create a strong base.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from mount to S-Mount in BJJ?

    The optimal time to transition to S-Mount is when your opponent attempts to bridge or turn into you from mount. This movement creates an opening for you to adjust your leg positioning and secure the dominant S-Mount. It's also effective if they are turtled or present a flat back.

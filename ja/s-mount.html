The S-Mount is a dominant top position in BJJ, typically achieved after a successful guard pass. It allows for superior control and sets up devastating submissions.

White belts often struggle with S-Mount due to a lack of understanding of weight distribution and hip pressure, leading to them being easily swept or losing the position.

The core principle of S-Mount is maintaining a strong base by driving your hips forward, creating a "figure four" leg entanglement that pins the opponent's hips and prevents them from bridging or shrimping away.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a successful guard pass (e.g., Knee Slice Pass), establish a cross-face grip with your left hand on the opponent's right collar and your right hand on their right hip.
    2. Drive your left knee across the opponent's chest, aiming it towards their left shoulder, while simultaneously lowering your hips to the mat.
    3. Shift your weight forward, ensuring your chest is in contact with their chest, and your hips are lower than theirs.
    4. Bring your right leg up and over the opponent's torso, placing your right foot on the mat on their right side, slightly behind their hip.
    5. Your left leg should now be bent, with your left foot tucked under the opponent's right armpit, creating a "figure four" with your legs.
    6. Your right knee should be pointing towards the opponent's head, and your left knee towards their feet, pinning their hips.
    7. Maintain a strong cross-face grip and use your hips to apply forward pressure, denying space for the opponent to escape.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect knee placement: Driving your knee directly into the opponent's stomach or solar plexus can cause rib fractures or internal organ damage. Always aim your knee towards their shoulder/head.
    • Hyperextending the knee: When transitioning, if your knee buckles outwards or inwards beyond its natural range of motion, you risk ACL or MCL tears. Keep your knee joint stable and aligned with your hip.
    • Dropping hips too low: Allowing your hips to sag to the mat without forward pressure can lead to the opponent bridging and escaping, and can also put undue stress on your lower back if you're not properly braced.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg and hip movements of the S-Mount transition from a stationary position on the mat. Focus on the "figure four" leg lock and hip drive. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the full transition from a basic guard pass to S-Mount, focusing on correct grip and weight distribution. (25 reps per side)
    3. Light resistance drill: Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the pressure and control of the S-Mount. Focus on maintaining the pin. (5 rounds, 1 minute each)
    4. Controlled sparring (positional): Start from the guard pass and work specifically to achieve and hold S-Mount against a partner who is actively trying to defend but not counter-attack aggressively. (5 rounds, 2 minutes each)
    5. Live sparring (focus on position): During live rolling, prioritize achieving the S-Mount position after a pass. If you achieve it, work to maintain it for at least 10 seconds before continuing. (5 rounds, 5 minutes each)
    6. Full live sparring: Implement S-Mount as a natural part of your game during regular sparring, aiming to transition to submissions or maintain control. (Unlimited rounds, 5 minutes each)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • After successfully passing the opponent's guard, particularly when they are attempting to recover guard.
    • When the opponent is trying to shrimp away from a side control or knee-on-belly position.
    • When you have established a strong cross-face and your opponent's hips are elevated off the mat.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Bridge and roll: The opponent bridges their hips to the ceiling and attempts to roll you over their head, often combined with a hip escape.
    • Back step and shrimp: The opponent "steps" their hips away from your pressure and shrimps their hips to create space to re-establish guard.
    • Leg pummel and frame: The opponent attempts to "pummel" their legs inside your leg entanglement and create frames with their arms to push your weight away.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get into S-Mount?

    Knee pain in S-Mount often stems from improper alignment and excessive torque. Ensure your hips are driving forward, not just your knee. Your knee should track over your ankle, and you should avoid forcing it into an unnatural angle. If you feel pain, immediately adjust your hip pressure and ensure your supporting leg is stable, not twisted.

    Q: How can I get into S-Mount against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise mechanics are even more critical. Focus on off-balancing them during your pass, creating a small window to drive your hips forward. Use your grips to control their upper body and prevent them from using their size to re-establish guard. Prioritize keeping your chest glued to their chest to minimize their ability to create space.

    Q: When is the best time to attack a submission from S-Mount?

    The ideal time to attack from S-Mount is immediately after establishing the position and securing your opponent's hips. Look for opportunities to attack the armbar or triangle choke as they react to your pressure and try to escape. If they defend aggressively, maintain control and look for transitions to other submissions like the kimura or Americana.

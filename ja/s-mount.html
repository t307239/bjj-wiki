The S-Mount is a dominant grappling position achieved from side control, where one knee is placed near the opponent's hip and the other near their head, creating an 'S' shape with your legs. It offers superior control and sets up powerful submission opportunities.

White belts often struggle with S-Mount because they lack the proprioception and body awareness to maintain the correct angles and weight distribution, leading to them being easily swept or losing control.

The key insight is to use your shin and instep as levers, creating a strong base and preventing your opponent from bridging or shrimping effectively by controlling their hip and shoulder.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side control, establish a cross-face grip with your right hand on the opponent's left collar and a secondary grip with your left hand on their left sleeve or armpit.
    2. Shift your weight forward, driving your left knee across the opponent's chest towards their left hip.
    3. As your left knee lands near the hip, begin to pivot your hips counter-clockwise, bringing your right knee off the mat.
    4. Drive your right knee forward, aiming to place your right instep on the mat on the opponent's left side, near their hip bone.
    5. Simultaneously, bring your left shin across the opponent's body, parallel to their spine, with your left foot tucked under their right armpit.
    6. Your right leg should now be bent, with your right instep providing base on the mat, and your left leg extended, creating the 'S' shape.
    7. Distribute your weight by driving your hips down and slightly forward, ensuring your chest is connected to their chest, and your left knee is tight to their hip.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Placing your knee directly on the opponent's hip bone with excessive force can cause hip impingement. The correct movement is to aim for the area slightly above the hip bone, allowing your shin to rest on their torso.
    • Arching your lower back excessively to maintain chest-to-chest connection can strain your lumbar spine. Instead, engage your core and keep your pelvis tucked slightly.
    • Allowing your opponent to create space by shrimping out: If your knee is not tight to the hip, they can easily create space. Keep your left knee glued to their hip and use your left foot to frame against their body.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice transitioning from side control to S-Mount on your own, focusing on the leg and hip movements. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): With a willing partner, practice the transition from side control to S-Mount, focusing on correct body positioning and weight distribution. (25 reps, 10% resistance)
    3. Static hold drill: Once in S-Mount, have your partner remain still while you practice adjusting your weight and grips for 30 seconds. (10 reps, 15% resistance)
    4. Controlled transition drill: Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to complete the transition and hold the position for 10 seconds. (20 reps, 30% resistance)
    5. Simulated submission drill: Practice transitioning to S-Mount and then attempting a basic submission like an armbar, with your partner offering light resistance to the submission. (15 reps, 50% resistance)
    6. Live rolling (positional sparring): Focus on achieving S-Mount from side control during live rolling, aiming to hold it for at least 10 seconds before moving to another technique or submission. (5 rounds, 75% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When you have established dominant side control and your opponent is attempting to escape by bridging or shrimping.
    • As a transition from a failed guard pass where you've secured a dominant side control position.
    • When your opponent is turtled and you have established a strong cross-face grip from side control.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): If the attacker's knee is not tight to your hip, create space by shrimping your hips away, thus breaking their S-Mount structure.
    • Bridge and Roll: If the attacker's weight is too far forward and their base is weak, bridge your hips upwards and attempt to roll them over, aiming to regain guard or end up in a better position.
    • Leg Peel/Clearance: If the attacker's leg is trapped, attempt to peel their leg off your hip or use your foot to push their shin away, creating space to escape.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get into S-Mount from side control?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are placing it directly on your opponent's hip bone with excessive force, or your knee is bending at an unnatural angle. Ensure your knee lands slightly above the hip bone, on their torso, and that your shin is parallel to their spine. Distribute your weight through your hips and core, not solely through your knee joint.

    Q: How can I get into S-Mount against a bigger opponent who is strong in side control?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on using your hips and core to create angles rather than brute strength. Initiate the S-Mount transition by driving your knee towards their hip with a slight upward motion, forcing them to react. Once the knee is in place, immediately pivot your hips and drive your weight down, using your instep as a solid base to prevent them from shrimping out.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to S-Mount, and should I always try for it?

    The S-Mount is best attempted when you have established solid side control and your opponent is actively trying to escape by shrimping or bridging. It is not a mandatory transition; focus on maintaining your current dominant position first. If the opportunity arises naturally from their escape attempt, then execute the S-Mount for a stronger position.

S-Mount is a dominant attacking position achieved from mount, aiming to isolate limbs for submissions. White belts often struggle due to poor weight distribution and uncontrolled hip movement.

The key insight is to use your hips to drive your shin across the opponent's body, creating a powerful, stable base.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From mount, secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip on the opponent's left arm with your left hand.
    2. Shift your weight onto your left hip, lowering it towards the mat.
    3. Drive your right knee forward, creating an 'S' shape with your legs, placing your right shin across the opponent's sternum.
    4. Your left foot should be flat on the mat, acting as a base, with your left knee pointing towards the opponent's left armpit.
    5. Extend your right leg to create a frame, preventing the opponent from bridging.
    6. Transfer your weight forward so your chest is directly over the opponent's chest, maintaining pressure.
    7. Adjust your hip angle to be perpendicular to the opponent's shoulders, maximizing control and minimizing space.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee hyperextension: Attempting to force the shin across by straightening the knee prematurely can lead to hyperextension of the knee joint, damaging the ACL and PCL. The correct movement involves driving the hip forward to bring the shin across.
    • Ankle inversion/eversion: Placing the foot directly onto the opponent's hip or thigh without proper leg alignment can cause the ankle to twist inward or outward, leading to ligament sprains. Ensure the foot is flat or the shin is securely across the body.
    • Lower back strain: Arching the lower back excessively to create an 'S' shape puts immense stress on the lumbar spine, risking disc compression or muscle strains. Maintain a neutral spine and use hip drive for the transition.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Swivels (0% resistance): Practice hip swiveling motions on your back, mimicking the hip drive for S-Mount. 10 reps per side.
    2. Shin-Across Drill (0% resistance): On your hands and knees, practice bringing one shin across your body as if it were an opponent's chest, focusing on hip movement. 10 reps per side.
    3. Mount to S-Mount Transition (25% resistance): Start in mount on a cooperative partner. Practice the hip shift and shin placement without pressure. 5 reps per side.
    4. Controlled S-Mount (50% resistance): From mount, transition to S-Mount and hold for 10 seconds, focusing on maintaining weight and base. Partner offers minimal resistance. 5 reps per side.
    5. Submission Setup (75% resistance): Transition to S-Mount and practice establishing a basic submission grip (e.g., for an armbar). Partner attempts to escape passively. 5 reps per side.
    6. Live Rolling (90% resistance): During rolling, actively look for opportunities to transition to S-Mount from mount. Focus on correct mechanics over immediate submission. 3 rounds.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have established a stable mount position and the opponent is defending by turning their hips away.
    • When the opponent attempts to bridge or shrimp, creating an opening to shift your hips.
    • When you have secured a strong grip on an opponent's arm or collar, allowing you to control their posture and initiate the transition.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape and Underhook: As the S-Mount transitions, if the opponent escapes their hips and secures an underhook on the side of the attacking leg, they can bridge and roll to escape. The counter is to maintain posture and prevent the underhook.
    • Knee-to-Chest Defense: If the opponent's shin is across the chest but not securely locked, the defender can bring their knee to their chest to block the shin and create space to shrimp out.
    • Framing with Forearm: The defender can place their forearm between their own chest and the opponent's shin, creating a frame to prevent the shin from fully crossing and to aid in escaping.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get into S-Mount?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are forcing the shin across by straightening your leg, leading to hyperextension or twisting. The correct biomechanical movement involves driving your hip forward and pivoting your shin across the opponent's body, keeping your knee slightly bent. Focus on hip mobility and core engagement rather than leg extension.

    Q: How can I achieve S-Mount against a much bigger opponent who is strong in mount?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on precise weight transfer and hip angle. Instead of trying to muscle your way through, use their weight against them. As they try to maintain pressure, shift your weight onto one hip and quickly bring your shin across their solar plexus. Maintain a strong chest-to-chest connection to prevent them from creating space.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to S-Mount from mount in BJJ?

    The optimal time to transition to S-Mount is when the opponent is actively trying to escape mount by turning their hips or bridging. These movements create slight openings and shifts in their weight. Look for moments when their hips are not directly underneath them, allowing you to quickly drive your shin across and secure the dominant position.

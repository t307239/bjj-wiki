Snap Down: The Essential BJJ Takedown for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Snap Down is initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to break your opponent's posture and create an opening for a takedown or sweep.

White belts often fail by pulling their opponent's head directly down, leading to a loss of balance or injury.

The key is to create a sudden, controlled downward pull on the head and neck, while simultaneously stepping in to disrupt their base.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grips: Right hand secures a two-on-one grip on the opponent's left wrist (forearm and wrist). Left hand secures a collar grip on the opponent's gi, approximately at the sternal notch.
    2. Stance: Maintain a staggered stance, left foot forward, right foot back, with knees slightly bent. Base width should be approximately shoulder-width.
    3. Weight Transfer (Initiation): Shift your weight onto your lead (left) foot. Simultaneously, drive your hips forward and slightly down, angling your pelvis towards your opponent's right side.
    4. Pulling Action: With your right hand, pull the opponent's left wrist forward and down, creating tension in their arm and shoulder. Your left hand (collar grip) pulls the collar down and across their chest.
    5. Head Movement: As you pull, slightly tuck your chin and drive your forehead into the opponent's head or shoulder, creating a subtle downward pressure and forward momentum.
    6. Footwork: Step your right foot forward and slightly to the outside of your opponent's right foot as you execute the pull. This disrupts their base.
    7. Completion: The combined action of the wrist pull, collar pull, hip drive, and stepping foot should cause the opponent to lose their balance and their head to snap down.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Wrist Pull: Pulling the opponent's wrist directly down without forward momentum can hyperextend the opponent's elbow (risk of UCL/MCL tear) or shoulder (risk of rotator cuff tear). The correct movement involves a forward and downward arc.
    • Jerking the Head: Yanking the opponent's head directly down without controlling their posture can cause neck strain or whiplash for both parties. Focus on a controlled pull on the collar and head pressure.
    • Collapsing Base: Bending your knees too deeply or bringing your feet too close together during the pull will make you unstable and susceptible to sweeps. Maintain a solid, staggered base with bent knees.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Grip and Body Movement: Practice the grip acquisition and hip drive motion without a partner. Focus on the feel of the weight transfer and angling the hips. (50 reps each side)
    2. Shadow Snap Down: Perform the entire Snap Down motion against an imaginary opponent, emphasizing the timing of grips, pulls, and footwork. (25 reps each side)
    3. Partner Resistance (Light): With a partner standing, practice the Snap Down with 25% resistance. Focus on the feel of their posture breaking. (10 reps each side)
    4. Partner Resistance (Medium): Increase partner resistance to 50%. Work on driving through their attempts to maintain posture. (10 reps each side)
    5. Partner Resistance (Heavy): Increase partner resistance to 75%. Focus on finishing the takedown. (5 reps each side)
    6. Live Rolling (Takedown Focus): Attempt the Snap Down during live rolling with a focus on securing the takedown. Allow 90-100% resistance. (5-minute rounds, 3 rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is posturing up aggressively from a standing clinch.
    • Opponent is reaching for your collar or sleeves in a standing clinch.
    • Opponent is attempting a grip fight that leaves their head exposed.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stance Adjustment and Grip Release: If the opponent attempts a Snap Down, immediately widen your base, lower your hips, and break their collar grip by stepping your lead foot back and pulling your head away. Simultaneously, release your wrist grip.
    • Sprawl and Drive: If the opponent drives forward with the Snap Down, quickly sprawl your legs back and drive your hips into their chest, creating a strong base and preventing them from pulling you down.
    • Counter-Pull: If the opponent initiates a Snap Down, instead of resisting directly, meet their pull with a controlled counter-pull to your own collar while stepping in with your lead leg to maintain your balance. This can sometimes transition into your own takedown.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the BJJ Snap Down?

    Wrist pain often occurs if you are pulling the opponent's wrist directly downwards without incorporating forward momentum and hip drive. This can lead to hyperextension. Ensure your grip is on the forearm and wrist, and your pull is a forward-and-downward arc, synchronized with your hip drive, to protect your wrist and effectively break their posture.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, leverage is key. Focus on a strong collar grip and a secure wrist grip (two-on-one). Instead of trying to muscle them down, create a sharp, sudden downward pull on the head and arm while simultaneously driving your hips forward and angling your body. The goal is to use their size against them by disrupting their base and balance, rather than overpowering them.

    Q: When is the best time to use the Snap Down in BJJ, and should I always aim for a takedown?

    The Snap Down is most effective when your opponent is postured up in a standing clinch, or when they are reaching for grips. You don't always need to aim for a takedown; a successful Snap Down can break their posture enough to set up a sweep from guard, or even create an opportunity to pass their guard if they fall to their knees. The immediate goal is to disrupt their control and create an advantage.

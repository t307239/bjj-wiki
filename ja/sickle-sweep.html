This sweep originates from closed guard, aiming to invert your opponent and take top position.

White belts often rush, sacrificing control for speed, leading to ineffective attempts and injury.

The core principle is using your hip as a fulcrum to drive their weight past your body's center of mass.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish Collar Grip with your right hand, thumb inside the lapel, and Sleeve Grip with your left hand on their left sleeve, just above the elbow.
    2. Sit up, bringing your hips forward, and place your right foot on their left hip, knee angled slightly towards their chest.
    3. Simultaneously, bring your left leg up, hooking their left thigh from the outside with your shin, heel towards their glute.
    4. Initiate a Hip Thrust: Drive your hips upwards and forward, creating space and leverage.
    5. Pull and Push: With your right hand, pull their collar towards your left shoulder; with your left hand, push their left sleeve away from your body.
    6. Ankle Pivot: As their weight shifts, pivot on your right foot, turning your body to the right.
    7. Sweep Motion: Continue the hip thrust and pulling/pushing motion, guiding their weight over your right hip and off-balance.
    8. Recover Guard: As they fall, bring your right leg back to establish closed guard or immediately transition to a better position.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hip Extension: Trying to lift opponent with arms alone, hyperextending lower back, damaging lumbar discs.

    *Correct:* Initiate sweep with a powerful, controlled hip thrust, using legs and core.

    • Jerking Sleeve Grip: Yanking the sleeve without coordinated hip movement, straining rotator cuff and elbow ligaments.

    *Correct:* Pull the sleeve with a controlled motion synchronized with hip extension.

    • Knee Valgus Collapse: Letting the hooking leg's knee cave inwards during the sweep, stressing the MCL and ACL.

    *Correct:* Maintain outward pressure with the hooking leg's knee, keeping it aligned with the ankle.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Thrust and Sweep Motion: Practice the hip thrust and pulling/pushing motion without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Against Light Resistance (No Partner): Use a dummy or rolled-up mat to simulate resistance for the hip thrust and pull/push. (50 reps)
    3. Partner Assisted Sweep (0% Resistance): Partner lies on their back, you practice grips and hip movement, partner offers no resistance. (20 reps each side)
    4. Partner Assisted Sweep (25% Resistance): Partner gently resists hip and arm movements, focusing on grip control. (20 reps each side)
    5. Controlled Sweep (50% Resistance): Partner actively tries to defend, focusing on maintaining grips and executing the sweep motion. (15 reps each side)
    6. Live Rolling (75% Resistance): Apply the Sickle Sweep during rolling with a compliant partner. (5 attempts)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When opponent is posturing up, creating a gap between your guards.
    • When opponent is leaning heavily forward, making their weight easy to shift.
    • When opponent's arm is extended, providing a clear sleeve grip opportunity.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Posture Defense: Opponent brings their hooking leg back and posts it flat on the mat, preventing hip elevation.
    • Base Shift: Opponent shifts their weight to their base leg, making it difficult to unbalance them over the hip.
    • Grip Break: Opponent breaks your collar or sleeve grip before you can initiate the sweep motion.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Sickle Sweep?

    Wrist pain often stems from gripping too tightly with the fingers alone, or from the sleeve grip being too far down the arm. Ensure your grip is firm but not strained, and try to grip closer to the elbow for better leverage. Also, synchronize the pull with your hip thrust to avoid isolating the wrist as the primary force generator.

    Q: How can I Sickle Sweep a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paramount. Focus on creating a strong frame with your legs and hips to prevent them from collapsing onto you. Prioritize a deep collar grip and a secure sleeve grip. The key is to drive your hips *up* and *forward* forcefully, using their weight against them by pulling their upper body towards your left shoulder as you sweep.

    Q: The optimal time is when the opponent postures up, creating space between their upper body and your hips. If they are leaning forward and their weight is on their toes, you have a prime opportunity. Avoid attempting when they are flat on their back or have their hips very close to yours, as this limits your hip's ability to create leverage.

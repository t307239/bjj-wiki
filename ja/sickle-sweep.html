The Sickle Sweep is initiated from closed guard and aims to disrupt your opponent's base, allowing you to reverse the position and end up on top.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength rather than leverage, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The core principle is to create a strong off-balancing angle by controlling one leg and using your hips to drive the sweep.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish closed guard with both hooks securely around the opponent's hips. Grip their right sleeve with your left hand (standard grip).
    2. With your right hand, secure a deep grip on the opponent's right pants, just above the knee, creating a strong pulling frame.
    3. Shift your hips slightly to your left, creating an angle. Your left knee should be slightly forward of your right knee.
    4. Begin to pull the opponent's right sleeve towards your chest with your left hand while simultaneously arching your back slightly.
    5. As you pull the sleeve, drive your right hand down the opponent's right thigh, creating a strong upward leverage point.
    6. Execute a powerful hip thrust upwards and to your left, simultaneously straightening your left leg to clear the opponent's hips.
    7. As the opponent is off-balanced, use your right hand to push their right leg outwards and away, completing the sweep motion.
    8. Immediately transition to your feet or drive into a top position like side control as they fall.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Knee Extension: Extending the sweeping leg (right leg in the example) straight out towards the opponent's hips can hyperextend the knee joint, leading to ACL or MCL tears if the opponent resists or shifts weight unexpectedly.
    • Neck Strain from Over-Pulling: Grabbing the opponent's collar and pulling with excessive force without proper hip drive can lead to cervical spine strain or disc injury due to the uncontrolled flexion of the neck.
    • Hip Impingement from Poor Angling: Failing to create a sufficient hip angle and instead driving straight forward can cause the opponent's hip to jam into your own hip joint, leading to labral tears or impingement.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip thrust and leg extension motion without a partner, focusing on the range of motion and coordination (50 reps).
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the grip establishment and the hip thrust/leg extension motion, focusing on feel and timing (10 reps per side).
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner remains mostly passive, offering minimal resistance to the sweep, allowing you to feel the leverage points (10 reps per side).
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to maintain their base but doesn't resist the sweep mechanics directly; focus on breaking their balance (15 reps per side).
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner actively defends by trying to keep their base strong and avoid being swept, requiring more precise timing and leverage (20 reps per side).
    6. Live rolling (90-100% resistance): Attempt the Sickle Sweep during sparring, focusing on applying the learned mechanics and adapting to resistance (5 attempts per round).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is posturing forward, creating a gap in their base.
    • When the opponent's weight is forward, making them susceptible to off-balancing.
    • When you have secured a strong sleeve grip and good pant control.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stalling the Sweep: If the opponent tries to sweep, they must immediately plant their sweeping foot on the mat and widen their base, preventing the hip thrust from creating leverage.
    • Knee Shield Defense: The opponent can bring their free leg (left leg in the example) up and across your body, creating a strong knee shield to prevent your hip from driving through and completing the sweep.
    • Posturing Up: The opponent can quickly posture up and bring their weight back, negating the off-balancing effect and making the sweep attempt ineffective.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try the Sickle Sweep?

    Your lower back pain likely stems from over-arching your spine and using your back muscles as the primary driver, instead of your hips. To fix this, focus on generating power from your glutes and hamstrings with a strong hip thrust. Ensure your core is engaged to support your spine and transfer the force efficiently.

    Q: How can I perform the Sickle Sweep against a much larger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes even more critical. Focus on securing your grips tightly on the sleeve and pants, creating a strong pulling frame. Your hip escape and drive must be explosive and perfectly timed to disrupt their superior weight and base. Prioritize breaking their posture before initiating the sweep.

    Q: Is it okay to grab the opponent's collar for the Sickle Sweep?

    While a collar grip can add some control, it's biomechanically less efficient and carries a higher risk of neck injury for white belts. A strong sleeve and pant grip provides better leverage for the sweep itself. Focusing on the sleeve and pant control allows for a more controlled and powerful hip drive, minimizing reliance on neck flexion.

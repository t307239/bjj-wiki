The Sickle Sweep originates from closed guard, aiming to unbalance your opponent and come to the top position. It's a fundamental sweep for gaining control.

Many white belts struggle with this sweep due to a lack of understanding of weight distribution and leverage, often resorting to brute force.

The key insight is creating a rotational force by using your hips and the opponent's own weight against them, rather than simply lifting.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a strong closed guard, securing both of your opponent's arms with your legs, knees touching.
    2. Grip your opponent's right sleeve with your left hand and their left collar with your right hand.
    3. Contract your abdominal muscles and slightly arch your lower back, creating a posterior pelvic tilt.
    4. With your right hand (collar grip), pull your opponent diagonally towards your left side.
    5. Simultaneously, extend your left leg (sleeve grip side) straight, driving your heel towards the mat.
    6. Rotate your hips to the left, pushing your right hip towards the mat and bringing your right knee forward.
    7. As your opponent falls to their left, drive your left foot forward and begin to stand up on your right knee, bringing your left leg through to secure mount.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Trying to lift your opponent with your arms alone: This can lead to shoulder impingement or rotator cuff tears due to excessive strain on the joint capsule.
    • Extending your supporting leg too forcefully before hip rotation: This can cause knee hyperextension, risking ACL or meniscus tears.
    • Allowing your hips to remain flat on the mat: This prevents effective rotation, potentially leading to lower back strain or disc compression.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip rotation and leg extension movements without a partner (50 reps).
    2. Mirror drilling: Mirror the movements of a partner who is standing still, focusing on grip and body positioning (25 reps per side).
    3. Static partner drill: With a compliant partner standing, practice the sweep mechanics with no resistance (10 reps per side).
    4. Light resistance drill: Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the timing of the sweep (5 reps per side).
    5. Semi-live drill: Your partner attempts to defend lightly, focusing on maintaining good structure (5 reps per side).
    6. Live rolling: Apply the Sickle Sweep in a rolling scenario with full resistance (3 attempts per round).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up in your closed guard, creating an opportunity for leverage.
    • When your opponent's weight is leaning slightly to one side, making them easier to unbalance.
    • When you have secured both a sleeve and collar grip, providing control and pulling mechanics.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Grip break and posture up: If the sleeve and collar grips are broken, immediately widen your base and stand up to prevent the sweep.
    • Hip escape and frame: If the sweep is initiated, immediately hip escape to the side and create a frame with your forearm to stop the rotation.
    • Base out and crossface: If the opponent is rotating, quickly bring your knee across their body and establish a crossface to prevent them from coming to the top.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my right shoulder hurt when I try the Sickle Sweep from closed guard?

    This pain often occurs when you're pulling too hard with your collar grip without proper body mechanics. Your shoulder joint is being overextended. Focus on rotating your hips and using your legs to drive the sweep, allowing your collar arm to act as a pivot point rather than a primary pulling force.

    Q: How can I perform the Sickle Sweep effectively against a much larger and heavier opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, brute strength is ineffective. Emphasize creating a strong rotational angle with your hips and using their weight against them. Ensure your leg extension is sharp and coordinated with your hip drive; the sweep comes from leverage, not lifting power.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Sickle Sweep, and what if my opponent's weight is perfectly balanced?

    The ideal time is when your opponent is slightly off-balance or posturing up. If their weight is perfectly balanced, the sweep will be very difficult. Instead, focus on breaking their posture first with a hip bump or creating an angle by opening your guard and transitioning to a different sweep or submission setup.

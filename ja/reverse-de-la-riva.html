リバース・デ・ラ・リヴァ ガード：白帯向け生体力学ガイド

The Reverse De La Riva guard is an open guard position where you attack from an inverted stance, hooking your opponent's leg from the outside. It aims to disrupt their balance and create sweep or submission opportunities.

White belts often struggle because they lack the nuanced body control and framing necessary to maintain this inverted position safely. They tend to over-rely on grip strength instead of leverage.

The key insight is understanding how to use your hooked leg as a lever to control your opponent's hip and knee line, creating off-balancing angles with your torso positioning.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting Position: Opponent is in your guard, feet between their legs. Your right leg is positioned to hook their left leg.
    2. The Hook Grip: Place your right foot's sole on the outside of their left shin, just above the ankle bone. Your right shin should be angled slightly inward.
    3. Left Leg Positioning: Your left leg remains free, ready to establish frames or transition. Keep it bent at the knee, foot flexed.
    4. Hip Angle: Rotate your hips to your right, creating a slight angle away from your opponent. Your pelvis should be perpendicular to their centerline.
    5. Torso Lean: Lean your upper body slightly forward and to your left, creating a base and preventing them from easily stepping over your hooked leg.
    6. Weight Distribution: Your weight is distributed primarily onto your left hip and shoulder, with your right foot providing the hooking leverage.
    7. Arm Grips (Optional but Recommended): Secure a sleeve grip (e.g., opponent's left sleeve) with your left hand to control their posture and prevent them from posturing up.
    8. Opponent's Knee Line: The goal is to keep their knee line elevated and off-balance. Your hooked leg prevents them from easily driving forward.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hooking Angle: Placing your right foot too high on their thigh or too far forward on their shin can lead to your knee hyperextending when they drive. This can damage the ACL or MCL. Instead, aim for the outside of the shin, just above the ankle, with your shin angled slightly inward.
    • Falling Back Too Far: Leaning back excessively without maintaining a strong frame with your free leg and torso can cause your opponent to collapse onto your legs. This can result in knee or ankle sprains from the unexpected weight. Maintain a controlled lean forward and use your free leg to create space.
    • Allowing Opponent to Step Over: If your hooking leg is too loose or your hip angle is wrong, your opponent can easily step their leg over your hook. This can lead to a knee-on-belly or side control transition, and if you resist by twisting your knee incorrectly, you risk an ACL tear. Keep your hook tight and your hips angled away.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hook Placement: Practice placing your right foot in the Reverse De La Riva hook position against a wall or dummy, focusing on the shin angle. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    2. Solo Body Positioning: From the hook, practice rotating your hips and leaning your torso to find the correct off-balancing angle without an opponent. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    3. Partnered Static Hook: With a partner lying on their back, practice establishing the hook and maintaining the correct hip angle and torso lean. Partner offers no resistance. (5 reps per side, 5 minutes)
    4. Partnered Controlled Movement: Partner gently tries to step out or drive forward. You practice maintaining the hook and adjusting your body to prevent them. (5 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    5. Basic Sweep Drill: Practice a simple sweep from Reverse De La Riva (e.g., an outside hook sweep). Partner offers minimal resistance. (5 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling Application: Attempt Reverse De La Riva during live rolling, focusing on establishing the position and maintaining control. (3 rounds, 75% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is posturing up and you need to disrupt their base.
    • When they try to pass your closed guard and you can transition to an open guard.
    • When you've been swept or are in a transitional guard and can secure the hook.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Opponent Steps Over: If they step their leg over your hook, immediately frame with your free leg (right leg if left leg hooked) and push their hip away, creating space to reset or transition.
    • Opponent Drives Forward: If they drive forward with weight, use your hooked leg to elevate their knee line and your free leg to push their hip. Simultaneously, use your torso lean to create an angle and prevent them from flattening you.
    • Opponent Stays High: If they maintain a high posture, use your sleeve grip to pull them forward and down, creating an opening to attack their legs or sweep.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do Reverse De La Riva?

    Knee pain often stems from improper hooking mechanics. If your foot is too high on their thigh or your shin is not angled correctly, it can lead to hyperextension or twisting forces when your opponent applies pressure. Ensure your hook is on their shin, just above the ankle bone, with your shin slightly angled inward, and maintain a strong frame with your free leg.

    Q: How can I use Reverse De La Riva against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes even more critical. Focus on their knee line and hip. Use your hooked leg to elevate their knee, making it difficult for them to drive forward. Simultaneously, use your torso lean and free leg frame to create angles and off-balance them. Do not try to muscle them; instead, use their weight against them by creating dynamic lever points.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from De La Riva to Reverse De La Riva?

    A common transition point is when your opponent tries to pressure forward heavily or step around your standard De La Riva hook. As they commit to a pass, you can actively withdraw your hooking leg and re-establish it on the outside of their opposite leg, simultaneously rotating your hips to create the Reverse De La Riva angle. This often happens as they attempt a knee-slice or torreado pass.

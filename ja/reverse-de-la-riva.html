Reverse De La Riva Guard for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Reverse De La Riva is an open guard position where you control your opponent's leg with your foot on their hip and the other leg behind their knee, enabling sweeps and submissions from unexpected angles.

White belts often struggle by overextending their hips or neglecting proper framing, leading to a loss of control and vulnerability to passes.

The core principle is to use your leg hooks to create off-balancing angles while maintaining hip connection and framing with your arms.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting Position: From a seated guard, establish control of your opponent's lead leg with your right leg (hooking behind their left knee). Your left leg is extended, with your left foot planted on their left hip bone.
    2. Grip: Secure your opponent's right ankle with your right hand (e.g., a "gobbler grip" or "palm-to-shin grip"). Simultaneously, grip their left collar with your left hand, pulling them slightly forward.
    3. Hip Angle: Angle your hips approximately 45 degrees to your opponent's right. This creates a strong base and prevents them from easily stacking you.
    4. Weight Distribution: Keep your weight low and centered on your hips. Your left leg's foot on their hip acts as a pivot and frame.
    5. Frame Creation: Use your left forearm to create a frame against your opponent's right bicep or shoulder, preventing them from posturing up directly.
    6. Leg Hook Engagement: Dig your right hook (behind their knee) into their hamstring, creating tension and controlling their mobility.
    7. Initiating Movement: To initiate a sweep, shift your weight slightly onto your left hip while simultaneously pulling their ankle towards you and pushing their hip away with your left foot.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Hyperextending Knee: If you allow your opponent to drive forward while your knee is fully extended and vulnerable, you risk a knee hyperextension or ligament tear (ACL/MCL).
    • Correct Action: Maintain a slight bend in your hooking knee and use your hips to absorb forward pressure, framing with your free arm.
    • Over-Rotating Hips: When attempting to sweep, if you rotate your hips too far past 90 degrees without a strong base, you can strain your lower back or hip flexors.
    • Correct Action: Keep your hip angle controlled around 45 degrees and use your foot on the hip for leverage, not just rotation.
    • Dropping Base: If you lose connection with your opponent's hip and allow your base to collapse, you risk your opponent passing your guard or injuring your ankle/foot.
    • Correct Action: Maintain constant pressure with your left foot on the hip and keep your hips connected to your opponent's base.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip and Hook Placement: Practice placing your left foot on the hip and your right hook behind the knee without resistance. (50 reps each side)
    2. Solo Gripping and Framing: Practice establishing the ankle and collar grips while maintaining the correct hip angle and arm frame. (50 reps each side)
    3. Partner Resistance (0%): With a compliant partner, practice the initial setup: hooking the leg, placing the foot on the hip, and establishing grips. (10 reps)
    4. Partner Resistance (25%): Partner gently resists your initial control. Focus on maintaining your frames and hip angle as they try to straighten up. (10 reps)
    5. Partner Resistance (50%): Partner begins to resist more actively, attempting to break grips and posture up. Practice maintaining your structure and initiating small movements. (10 reps)
    6. Live Rolling (75%): Begin light sparring, focusing on applying the Reverse De La Riva setup and attempting a sweep or transition. (3 rounds, focus on setup)
    7. Live Rolling (90%): Integrate Reverse De La Riva more naturally into sparring, focusing on successful sweeps and transitions. (5 rounds)
    8. Live Rolling (100%): Full sparring, using Reverse De La Riva as a primary guard option. (5 rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard from an open or half-guard position.
    • When your opponent is posturing up from your guard and you can isolate a leg.
    • When you are in a closed guard and want to transition to a more dynamic open guard.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass Defense: If your opponent tries to stack you, immediately widen your base and drive your left foot into their hip, simultaneously pulling their ankle towards you to create space and prevent them from collapsing on you.
    • Leg Drag Pass Defense: If your opponent attempts to peel your hooking leg off and drag it across, use your free hand to grip their far collar and pull them forward, while driving your left foot deeper into their hip to maintain your framing structure.
    • Knee Slice Pass Defense: As they attempt to bring their knee across, use your right leg (the hook) to elevate their hips slightly and create an angle to shrimp out or transition to a different guard like X-guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try to do Reverse De La Riva?

    Ankle pain in Reverse De La Riva often stems from gripping the opponent's ankle with your hand in a way that forces your own ankle into a twisted or hyperextended position. You might also be allowing your opponent to drive their weight directly onto your ankle. To fix this, focus on a "gobbler grip" or a palm-to-shin grip where your hand supports your own ankle. Maintain a slight bend in your ankle and keep your hips engaged to absorb their weight, rather than letting it bear directly down on your foot.

    Q: How do I use Reverse De La Riva against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, your leverage and frames become even more critical. Focus on keeping your left foot firmly planted on their hip bone to create a strong, stable base and prevent them from collapsing on you. Use your collar grip to pull them forward and off-balance, making their weight advantage less effective. Prioritize hip connection; the closer your hips are to their base, the harder it is for them to generate passing pressure.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from De La Riva to Reverse De La Riva?

    The transition from De La Riva to Reverse De La Riva is often initiated when your opponent attempts to pass by stepping their leg outside of your De La Riva hook. Instead of letting them pass, you can use that moment to bring your hooking leg across their body and behind their knee, establishing the Reverse De La Riva position. This is a reactive adjustment that capitalizes on their movement to gain a dominant control.

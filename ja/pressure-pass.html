Pressure Pass Technique for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Pressure Pass is a fundamental guard passing technique initiated from a standing or kneeling position against an opponent's guard, aiming to secure side control.

White belts often struggle by focusing on brute force rather than precise weight distribution and structure, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key insight is to use your body weight and structure to create an inescapable pressure that forces the opponent to concede position.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (or wrist grip) with your left hand on the opponent's left sleeve.
    2. Lower your base by bending your knees, keeping your hips lower than your opponent's hips.
    3. Drive your left shoulder into the opponent's left hip, angling your body 45 degrees to their left.
    4. Transfer your weight forward onto your left leg, driving your left knee towards the mat behind their hip.
    5. With your right hand, pull their collar towards your left shoulder, creating a strong pulling angle.
    6. Simultaneously, use your left arm to control their sleeve, preventing them from establishing grips or framing.
    7. Step your right leg across their body, placing your right foot outside their right hip, maintaining a wide base.
    8. Drive your hips down and forward, collapsing their guard and pinning their legs with your own legs and hips.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Knee Drive: Driving your knee directly into the opponent's hip joint with excessive force can hyperextend their knee, risking ACL or MCL tears.
    • Correct Alternative: Drive your knee *behind* their hip, using it to off-balance and create space for your leg to step across.
    • Excessive Head/Neck Pressure: Arching your back and driving your head into the opponent's chest can strain your cervical spine, leading to whiplash.
    • Correct Alternative: Keep your head up and look forward, using your chest and shoulders to apply pressure to their torso.
    • Standing Base Collapse: Attempting to force the pass from too upright a position with a narrow base makes you vulnerable to sweeps and can lead to ankle or knee injuries if you lose balance.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Pressure Drill: Practice driving your shoulder into a mat or heavy bag, focusing on hip angle and weight transfer (10 reps, 0% resistance).
    2. Grip and Drive Simulation: With a partner lying on their back, practice establishing grips and driving your shoulder into their hip, focusing on body alignment (10 reps per side, 0% resistance).
    3. Knee Slice Motion Practice: From a kneeling position, practice the motion of driving your knee behind the opponent's hip and stepping your other leg across without full engagement (10 reps, 0% resistance).
    4. Controlled Pass with Light Resistance: Partner lies on their back, no active guard retention. You execute the pressure pass, focusing on each biomechanical step (10 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    5. Guard Retention Resistance: Partner actively tries to maintain guard, but you focus on breaking grips and driving through. They offer minimal resistance to the pass itself (10 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling Application: Attempt the pressure pass during live rolling, focusing on applying the learned biomechanics and adapting to their resistance (5-minute rounds, 75-100% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is in closed guard and you have established a solid cross-collar and sleeve grip.
    • When the opponent has an open guard that allows for a clear path to drive your shoulder into their hip.
    • When the opponent is attempting a submission from their guard, creating an opening for a pass.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee Shield and Hip Escape: If the opponent creates a knee shield with their leg, use your foot to push their knee away while simultaneously hip escaping to create space and reset.
    • Guillotine or Arm Triangle Setup: If the opponent frames with their arms and tries to pull your head down, use the pressure to set up a guillotine choke or transition to an arm triangle choke.
    • Spider Guard Re-Guard: If they manage to get a grip on your sleeve and create a spider guard hook, use your free hand to break their grip and step back to re-establish your base.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Pressure Pass?

    Wrist pain during the Pressure Pass often occurs from gripping too tightly with your fingers or gripping the sleeve too low on the forearm. Ensure you are using a firm but not death-like grip, and try to grip closer to the opponent's wrist. Focus on using your entire arm and shoulder to create the pulling pressure, not just your wrist.

    Q: How can I effectively Pressure Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise weight distribution become even more critical. Instead of trying to drive straight through, focus on creating angles and using their size against them. Your goal is to collapse their base and off-balance them, not overpower them. Maintain a low base and ensure your hips are always lower than theirs.

    Q: When should I give up on a Pressure Pass attempt and reset?

    You should abandon a Pressure Pass attempt if you lose your base, your opponent secures a dominant submission, or you find yourself in a compromised position where you cannot maintain structure. If you feel your hips rising too high or your base becoming too narrow, it's better to disengage, reset to a safe position (like standing or a stable kneeling base), and re-evaluate.

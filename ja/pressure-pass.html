Pressure Pass: The Biomechanically Sound BJJ Technique Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Pressure Pass is initiated from a standing or kneeling position against an opponent's guard, aiming to break their grip and advance to side control.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute force rather than controlled weight distribution and leverage.

The key insight is using your body's mass and skeletal structure to compress and control the opponent's hips, not just their legs.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Start in a low, kneeling stance with your hips below your opponent's hips. Place your knees slightly wider than shoulder-width for a stable base.
    2. Secure a grip on your opponent's collar (e.g., cross-collar grip) with one hand and their opposite sleeve or pants (e.g., pant grip at the knee) with the other.
    3. Drive your lead shoulder into your opponent's chest or sternum, creating a downward and forward force. Keep your head up and looking forward.
    4. Angle your hips 45 degrees away from your opponent's legs, aligning your body's long axis with their hip line.
    5. Transfer your weight directly onto your lead shoulder and chest, driving forward to compress their hips against the mat.
    6. As your opponent reacts, step your trailing leg forward, bringing your knees closer together to maintain a tight base.
    7. Continue to drive your hips forward and down, using your skeletal structure to pin their hips and prevent them from creating space.
    8. Once their hips are flattened, advance your chest to their chest and begin to establish side control by controlling their upper body.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Grasping sleeves with a bent wrist: This can lead to a hyperextended wrist joint, damaging the carpal ligaments. The correct grip is a firm, neutral wrist with the thumb wrapped around the opponent's forearm.
    • Driving forward with a rounded back: This puts immense stress on the lumbar spine, risking disc herniation. Maintain a neutral spine and drive with your hips and legs.
    • Attempting to force legs apart with straight arms: This can cause shoulder impingement or rotator cuff tears. Instead, use your chest and hips to compress their guard closed, then create space with a controlled leg-over motion.
    • Standing up too quickly from a closed guard: This can lead to knee hyperextension if the opponent pulls guard closed tightly. Maintain a low base and control their hips before standing.

    ドリル段階

    1. Static Hip Compression Drill: Partner lies on their back. You assume the low kneeling stance and practice driving your shoulder and hips into their hips without moving your feet. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Controlled Weight Transfer Drill: Partner lies on their back. Practice the grip and weight transfer sequence, focusing on hip angle and driving force, without trying to pass. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Partner Applies Light Resistance: Partner lies on their back and offers minimal resistance to your pressure. Focus on maintaining your base and driving force. (30 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Opponent Tries to Close Guard: Partner starts with legs open and tries to close guard as you apply pressure. You focus on breaking the closed guard and maintaining hip control. (30 reps, 50% resistance)
    5. Opponent Tries to Maintain Guard: Partner actively tries to maintain their guard against your pressure pass. Focus on maintaining posture and driving force. (20 reps, 75% resistance)
    6. Light Sparring (Positional): Start from a closed guard position. Focus solely on executing the Pressure Pass against light resistance. (5 rounds x 2 minutes, 90% resistance)
    7. Live Rolling: Integrate the Pressure Pass into your regular rolling. (1-2 rounds x 5 minutes, 100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent has a tight closed guard and you are in a low, stable base.
    • When your opponent is attempting to use their legs to create frames and prevent you from advancing.
    • When you have established control of their hips and are looking to break their posture and control.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): If the passer drives too far forward, the guard player can shrimp their hips away to create space and re-establish guard. The passer must maintain constant hip pressure and forward drive.
    • Uppercut/Frame Creation: The guard player can use their forearms to frame against the passer's neck or shoulders, creating space to push away. The passer must keep their head tight and drive their shoulder into the opponent's upper chest.
    • Re-Guard: If the passer's pressure falters, the guard player can use their feet to hook the passer's hips and pull them back into guard. The passer must maintain a tight base and continuously drive their hips forward.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try the Pressure Pass?

    Your shoulder is likely hurting because you are rounding your back and driving with your upper body, rather than using your hips and legs. This puts excessive strain on the shoulder joint. Ensure your spine is neutral and your drive comes from your hips pushing your body forward, compressing the opponent's hips.

    Q: How can I Pressure Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are even more critical than raw strength. Focus on getting your hips lower than theirs and driving your shoulder into their sternum to break their posture and flatten their hips. Use their weight against them by angling your body and controlling their hip line, preventing them from using their mass effectively.

    Q: The Pressure Pass is most effective against a tight, closed guard where the opponent is actively trying to keep their knees together and prevent you from advancing. The Knee Slice Pass is generally better when the guard is more open or when you have already started to create space and are looking to slice your knee through.

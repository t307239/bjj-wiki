Pressure Pass Technique for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Pressure Pass starts from a standing or kneeling position, aiming to collapse the opponent's guard and establish side control. White belts often struggle because they focus on brute strength rather than precise body mechanics.

The key is to use your body weight and structure to create constant, unyielding pressure, preventing the opponent from creating frames or space.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Grab opponent's right pant leg at the ankle with your left hand. Grab opponent's left pant leg at the ankle with your right hand.
    2. Stance: Adopt a low, wide base, knees bent, hips below your opponent's hips.
    3. Weight Transfer: Drive your left shoulder into the opponent's right hip, pushing it downwards and slightly away.
    4. Leg Action: Simultaneously, step your right foot forward, placing it just outside the opponent's right knee line.
    5. Hip Angle: Angle your hips to your left, creating a 45-degree angle relative to your opponent.
    6. Head Position: Keep your head up and drive it into the opponent's chest or shoulder, forcing them to absorb your weight.
    7. Leg Sweep: As you drive forward, use your right foot to sweep the opponent's right leg outwards and across their body.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Error: Attempting to force the pass by pushing the opponent's hips straight back with your hands. Risk: Shoulder impingement or rotator cuff tear in your shoulder joint due to improper leverage and excessive force against a stable base. Correction: Drive your shoulder into the hip and use your legs to create forward momentum and angle.
    • Error: Standing too upright and trying to break the guard with arm strength. Risk: Knee joint hyperextension or ligament strain from unstable base and falling backward. Correction: Maintain a deep, wide base with bent knees, keeping your hips low.
    • Error: Neglecting to control the hips and instead focusing solely on leg grips. Risk: Opponent can easily shrimp or create frames, leading to wrist sprains or hyperextended elbows if you resist their movement incorrectly. Correction: Use your body weight and shoulder to pin the hip while securing leg grips.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Drive Drill: Practice driving your shoulder into a heavy bag or mat, focusing on hip angle and forward drive. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Solo Leg Grip and Drive: Practice gripping your own ankles and driving your shoulder into a stable object, simulating the pressure. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Partner Mobility Drill: With a partner lying on their back, practice grip placement and shoulder drive, allowing them to resist slightly with their hips. (20 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner Guard Collapse Drill: From closed guard, have your partner open their guard. Practice gripping pant legs and driving shoulder into hip, aiming to collapse their legs. (20 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Controlled Pass Attempt: From your partner's open guard, attempt the Pressure Pass with controlled resistance. Focus on maintaining pressure and hip angle. (10 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling: Apply the Pressure Pass in live rolling situations, focusing on maintaining pressure and achieving side control. (5 minutes, 90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent has their guard closed and you are in a kneeling or standing position.
    • When the opponent attempts to open their guard but maintains a weak base or frames.
    • When you have established a strong cross-collar grip and can use it to help collapse their posture.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Shrimping: If the opponent attempts to shrimp away, maintain your shoulder pressure and drive your hips forward, ensuring your base remains wide.
    • Framing with Forearms: If the opponent tries to frame with their forearms, use your body weight to absorb and push through the frames, keeping your head low.
    • Re-guarding: If the opponent tries to bring their legs back into guard, use your sweeping leg to push their leg further away and maintain hip control.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Pressure Pass?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you grip too tightly with your fingers alone and try to pull the pant legs. Instead, use your entire forearm and hand to grip the pant leg, and focus on driving your shoulder into the opponent's hip. The pressure should come from your body weight and structure, not from yanking the legs with your wrists.

    Q: How do I do the Pressure Pass against a bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, biomechanical leverage becomes even more critical. Focus on getting your hips lower than theirs and maintaining a very wide base to absorb their weight. Drive your shoulder into their hip with maximum force, and use your leg to sweep their base outwards, creating an angle to pass. Do not try to match their strength; instead, disrupt their structure.

    Q: When is the best time to use the Pressure Pass, and should I always try to get side control?

    The Pressure Pass is most effective when the opponent's guard is somewhat compromised, such as when they are trying to open it or their frames are weak. While side control is the primary goal, sometimes collapsing their guard and establishing a dominant knee-on-belly or even transitioning to mount is a more immediate outcome. Focus on maintaining pressure and controlling their hips, adapting your next move based on their reaction.

Omoplata: A Biomechanically Precise BJJ Technique Guide for White Belts

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

The Omoplata is a shoulder lock initiated from various guard positions, aiming to hyperextend the opponent's shoulder joint. It's a powerful submission that can also lead to sweeps if applied correctly.

White belts often struggle with the Omoplata due to a lack of understanding of body mechanics, leading to uncontrolled movements and potential injury. They might try to force the submission rather than using leverage.

The key mechanical insight is using your hips to drive forward and your leg as a fulcrum, creating a rotational force on the opponent's shoulder, not just brute strength.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From Closed Guard, secure your opponent's far-side arm by placing your left knee inside their armpit and your right knee over their shoulder, trapping the arm.
    2. Grip their left wrist with your right hand, ensuring your thumb is pointing upwards (a "baseball grip").
    3. Simultaneously, grip your own right shin with your left hand, creating a "figure-four" grip around their trapped arm.
    4. Untuck your left leg and swing it over their back, positioning your left shin across their upper back, perpendicular to their spine.
    5. Rotate your hips to face the opponent's head, angling your pelvis towards their left hip.
    6. Drive your hips forward and slightly upward, using your right leg as a lever and your left shin as a fulcrum point.
    7. Maintain your grip on their wrist and shin, and continue to drive your hips forward, applying pressure to their shoulder joint.
    8. As the pressure increases, their shoulder will externally rotate and abduct, leading to the submission.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to "pull" the opponent's arm across by arching your back: This places immense strain on your own lower back ligaments and can cause a lumbar sprain. The correct movement is to drive your hips forward.
    • Letting your head drop to the mat while trying to finish: This compromises your base and can lead to your opponent flattening you out, potentially injuring your neck or shoulder from the uncontrolled fall. Maintain your head up and connected to their body.
    • Grabbing the opponent's elbow instead of their wrist: This grip is much weaker and can lead to the opponent easily posturing up or escaping. Always grip the wrist for maximum control and leverage.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip rotation and leg swing motion without a partner. Focus on the smooth transition from closed guard to the Omoplata position. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Partner lies on their back. Practice securing the arm, transitioning your legs, and achieving the Omoplata position. Focus on grip integrity. (25 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to practice the hip drive and body rotation. Focus on feeling the leverage. (15 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner drill (medium resistance): Partner attempts to defend by posturing up or shrimping. Practice maintaining your position and adjusting your hip angle. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Live rolling (controlled): Attempt the Omoplata from closed guard during controlled sparring. Focus on executing the steps precisely, even if the submission isn't achieved. (5 attempts per round, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling (open): Attempt the Omoplata in regular sparring. Focus on recognizing the opportunities and applying the technique with proper body mechanics. (100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent establishes a tight closed guard and you have control of their posture.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard and you can trap an arm in the process.
    • When you have successfully transitioned to a strong open guard position like spider or lasso guard, and can isolate an arm.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Posturing Up: If the Omoplata is not fully locked in, the opponent can posture up by bringing their hips closer to you and raising their upper body. This relieves pressure on the shoulder.
    • Rolling Through: The opponent can attempt to roll forward in the direction of the Omoplata. This is a dangerous counter and requires precise timing; if successful, it can relieve pressure and potentially transition to a better position for them.
    • "Stacking": The opponent can try to "stack" you by driving their weight and hips directly onto your shoulders and head. This negates your leverage and can lead to a dangerous pressure situation.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to do the Omoplata?

    Your shoulder likely hurts because you are trying to force the submission with pure strength or by pulling your opponent's arm directly. The Omoplata is a leverage-based technique. Ensure you are using your hips to drive forward and your leg as a fulcrum, creating a rotational force rather than a direct pull, and that your grip is on the wrist, not the elbow.

    Q: How can I Omoplata against a much bigger and heavier opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, precise hip placement and body mechanics are even more critical. Focus on getting your hips underneath their trapped shoulder and driving them forward at a steep angle. Do not try to lift them; instead, use their weight against them by creating a strong rotational fulcrum with your legs and hips. Ensure your leg is across their upper back, not just their torso.

    Q: I keep losing the Omoplata because my opponent escapes by rolling. How do I prevent this?

    The opponent's roll is countered by maintaining a strong connection to their body and controlling their hips. As they attempt to roll, drive your hips forward more aggressively and keep your chest tight to their back. Your left shin should remain firmly across their upper back, preventing them from completing the roll. If they commit to the roll, you may need to transition to a sweep or another submission.

Omoplata: The Essential BJJ Technique for White Belts

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

The Omoplata is a shoulder lock initiated from guard, aiming to isolate the opponent's arm and apply leverage for a tap.

White belts often rush this, neglecting foundational body control and leading to compromised positions or injury.

Success hinges on controlling the opponent's weight distribution and creating a strong mechanical advantage with your hips and legs.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From Closed Guard: Secure a 'Sleeve Grip' on the opponent's gi sleeve with your left hand, and a 'Collar Grip' on the same side's gi lapel with your right hand.
    2. Break the Guard: Use your grips to pull the opponent's upper body forward and slightly to your left, opening your guard.
    3. Initiate the Sweep: As the opponent's weight shifts forward, pivot your hips to the left, bringing your right leg over the opponent's head.
    4. Secure the Leg: Immediately trap the opponent's right arm between your legs, ensuring your right shin is across their shoulder blade.
    5. Hip Angle: Drive your hips down and slightly forward, creating a 90-degree angle between your torso and the opponent's shoulder.
    6. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your hips, pressing your left knee towards the mat to prevent them from sitting up.
    7. Lock Formation: Your right thigh should be pressing into the opponent's upper back, and your left ankle should be secured by your right hand (Ankle Grip).
    8. Finish: Arch your back and push your hips forward, extending your legs to apply pressure to the opponent's shoulder joint.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to 'pull' the opponent's arm over your head without proper hip rotation: This strains the shoulder joint and can cause rotator cuff tears or dislocations.

    Correct: Focus on pivoting your hips and driving them forward to create the angle.

    • Letting your legs fall flat after trapping the arm: This allows the opponent to escape by posturing up or rolling, potentially spraining your knee or ankle.

    Correct: Maintain a tight leg trap, keeping your right shin across their shoulder blade and your left foot secured.

    • Overextending your own arm to finish the lock: This puts excessive strain on your elbow and shoulder, risking injury.

    Correct: Use your hips and body weight to apply pressure, not just your arm strength.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling of hip rotation and leg over the head movement with no resistance (10 reps each side).
    2. Partner drilling of grip acquisition and guard break with 0% resistance.
    3. Partner drilling of the full Omoplata setup and leg trap with 25% resistance (focus on movement, not pressure).
    4. Drilling the Omoplata finish with a compliant partner, stopping before full extension (50% resistance, 5 reps each side).
    5. Controlled drilling of the Omoplata entry and control phase against a partner actively trying to defend (75% resistance, 3 reps each side).
    6. Live rolling scenario: Attempting the Omoplata from closed guard when the opportunity arises (90-100% resistance, focus on application).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent posts their arm on the mat to prevent a sweep from closed guard.
    • Opponent is posturing up heavily from closed guard, creating an opportunity to trap an arm.
    • Opponent attempts to pass your guard and leaves an arm exposed in a vulnerable position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Rolling to your back: If the Omoplata is being applied incorrectly, the opponent can roll their shoulders to relieve pressure and escape.
    • Posturing up and 'stacking': The opponent can stand up and drive their weight down onto the attacker's hips, breaking the lock.
    • Escaping the leg trap by stepping over: If the attacker's leg is not securely trapping the arm, the opponent can step their leg over to untangle.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to do the Omoplata from closed guard?

    Your shoulder likely hurts because you are trying to force the opponent's arm over your head without creating the correct biomechanical angle. Instead of pulling, focus on breaking their posture, pivoting your hips to your left, and driving your hips forward as your right leg comes over their head to trap the arm. This creates a lever with your hips and legs, not just your arm strength.

    Q: How can I Omoplata against a much bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, the key is timing and precision. You must break their posture effectively to create the opening. Once you trap their arm, immediately drive your hips down and forward to establish a strong base and prevent them from 'stacking' you. Focus on controlling their weight distribution with your legs and hips, rather than trying to overpower them with pure strength.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt an Omoplata from closed guard?

    The ideal time is when the opponent is posturing up, reaching forward, or trying to pass your guard. Look for moments when their weight is forward and one of their arms is extended within reach. Avoid attempting it when they are low and heavy, as this makes it harder to break their posture and secure the necessary angles for the submission.

Omoplata: A Biomechanically Precise Guide for BJJ White Belts

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

This technique originates from closed guard, aiming to isolate an opponent's arm for a shoulder lock. It's a powerful submission that can also lead to dominant positions.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength or poor body positioning, leading to missed submissions or injury. Understanding the mechanics is crucial for success.

The core principle is using your hips and legs to create leverage, trapping the opponent's arm while rotating your body to apply pressure.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From Closed Guard, secure a Collar Grip with your left hand on the opponent's gi lapel, and your right hand on their Right Sleeve at the wrist.
    2. Hip out to your left, creating a 45-degree angle between your pelvis and the opponent's torso. Your knees should remain tight to their hips.
    3. Swing your right leg over the opponent's head, aiming to place your right shin across their upper back, just below the neck.
    4. As your right shin crosses, drop your hips down and back, pulling the opponent's right arm across your chest. Your left leg remains tight to their hip.
    5. Secure a S-Grip (or Gable Grip) with your hands around your own right shin, pulling it towards your chest to tighten the trap.
    6. Rotate your torso counter-clockwise, driving your hips forward and slightly upward, while keeping the opponent's right arm pinned.
    7. Maintain pressure by keeping your right shin tight to their upper back and your left leg controlling their hip. The submission is applied by continuing the rotation and hip drive.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to simply 'pull' the opponent over without hip movement: This can hyperextend the opponent's shoulder or elbow, causing ligamentous damage (rotator cuff, UCL) in the opponent, and leaves you in a compromised position.
    • Forcing the leg over the head with straight knees: This risks knee hyperextension or ligament tears (ACL, MCL) in yourself, and can also dangerously torque the opponent's neck.
    • Not controlling the opponent's hip with the free leg: This allows the opponent to posture up or escape by bridging, potentially leading to your own arm being trapped or a sweep, and can cause shoulder impingement if the arm is not properly secured.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Escape and Leg Swing (0% resistance): Practice hip escaping and swinging your leg over your own head, focusing on the motion and knee bend. 10 repetitions per side.
    2. Partner Assisted Hip Escape and Leg Swing (25% resistance): Your partner lies on their back. You practice the hip escape and leg swing, with your partner lightly resisting the movement. 5 repetitions per side.
    3. Partner Assisted Omoplata Setup (50% resistance): From closed guard, practice the initial grips and hip escape. Your partner offers minimal resistance as you bring your leg over. Focus on the initial leg placement. 5 repetitions per side.
    4. Omoplata Control Drill (75% resistance): Your partner allows you to get the leg over and secure the S-grip. They offer light pressure to help you feel the control, but do not actively defend. Focus on maintaining the trap. 3 repetitions per side.
    5. Omoplata Submission Drill (90% resistance): Your partner allows the full Omoplata setup and you attempt the submission. They tap immediately upon feeling pressure, simulating a controlled tap. 3 repetitions per side.
    6. Live Rolling Scenario (100% resistance): Integrate the Omoplata setup into controlled sparring rounds, attempting it only when the positional cues are present. Focus on execution, not just completion. 2-minute rounds, 3 rounds.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent attempts to pass your closed guard by posturing up significantly.
    • Opponent is attempting a guard pass where their arm is extended away from their body.
    • You have secured a strong grip on their sleeve and are able to control their posture.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The "Roll Through" Defense: If the opponent's leg is trapped but they haven't secured the S-grip, the defender can attempt to roll forward towards their own feet, bringing their hips underneath them to relieve pressure.
    • The "Escape to Turtle" Defense: If the opponent has the leg over but their weight is not heavy, the defender can attempt to drop to their knees and turtle up, creating space to potentially crawl out or transition.
    • The "Stack Pass" Counter: If the opponent is slow to commit their weight, the defender can attempt a heavy stack pass, driving their weight into the opponent's upper back to break the hold and pass.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to do the Omoplata from closed guard?

    Your shoulder is likely hurting because you are not properly using your hips to create the angle for the Omoplata. Instead of hip escaping, you might be trying to 'muscle' the arm over. This forces your shoulder into an awkward, extended position. Focus on a sharp hip escape to your left, bringing your right shin across their upper back, and then dropping your hips to create the leverage.

    Q: How can I do the Omoplata against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is even more critical. Ensure your initial grips are tight (sleeve and collar). When you hip out, drive your hips much further away to create space, then swing your leg over. Crucially, as you secure the S-grip on your shin, pull your own shin towards your chest with significant force, keeping your elbow tucked. This traps their shoulder more effectively, allowing your smaller frame to apply the submission.

    Q: I keep getting swept when I try to set up the Omoplata, what am I doing wrong?

    You are likely not maintaining control of your opponent's hips with your free leg. As you swing your leg over for the Omoplata, your free (left) leg should remain tight to your opponent's hip, preventing them from posturing up or bridging effectively. If you allow your left leg to drift away, they can use that space to generate momentum for a sweep. Keep that leg glued to their hip throughout the setup.

Omoplata: The Biomechanically Sound BJJ Technique for White Belts

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

The Omoplata is a shoulder lock applied from a grappling guard position, typically closed guard or open guard, aiming to hyperextend the opponent's shoulder joint.

White belts often struggle with the Omoplata due to a lack of understanding of rotational mechanics and hip pressure, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key to a successful Omoplata lies in controlling the opponent's posture and creating a stable base to isolate and manipulate the shoulder joint's rotation.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure a 'Sleeve Grip' on the opponent's far arm with your near hand, and a 'Collar Grip' on their same-side lapel with your far hand.
    2. Untuck your near leg and swing it over the opponent's shoulder, positioning your shin across their upper back, perpendicular to their spine.
    3. Simultaneously, drive your hips forward and to the side, creating a 90-degree angle between your torso and the opponent's torso.
    4. Secure your 'Grapevine Grip' by interlocking your hands behind the opponent's trapped arm, ensuring your knuckles are facing upwards.
    5. Shift your weight onto your hips, creating a stable base, and extend your legs slightly to create a fulcrum point.
    6. Drive your chest into the opponent's back, forcing their shoulder into a compromised position against your shin.
    7. Apply pressure by extending your hips and pulling your opponent's trapped arm towards your chest, creating a lever arm that hyperextends the shoulder.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly swinging the leg over: This can cause the opponent to fall awkwardly, leading to potential knee hyperextension or ACL tears if the white belt's leg is not positioned correctly.
    • Insufficient hip drive: Failing to drive hips forward and sideways creates a weak base, allowing the opponent to easily stack or posture up, risking shoulder impingement or rotator cuff strain.
    • Gripping too loosely: A loose 'Grapevine Grip' allows the opponent's arm to slip out, and in an attempt to compensate, the white belt might twist their own shoulder or elbow awkwardly, risking a sprain.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling of hip and leg movement without a partner (50 reps).
    2. Partner drilling of the initial leg over and hip angle with zero resistance (25% resistance, 10 reps per side).
    3. Partner drilling of the 'Sleeve Grip' and 'Collar Grip' exchange and 'Grapevine Grip' setup (50% resistance, 10 reps per side).
    4. Partner drilling of the full Omoplata entry and hip drive, stopping before submission pressure (75% resistance, 5 reps per side).
    5. Controlled Omoplata submission attempts with verbal cues for tap (90% resistance, 5 reps per side).
    6. Live rolling with a focus on attempting the Omoplata from a stable guard position (100% resistance, 3 attempts per round).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent posts their arm to prevent a sweep from closed guard.
    • Opponent is postured up in your open guard, creating an opportunity to trap an arm.
    • During a failed sweep attempt where you can isolate an opponent's arm.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stacking: Opponent drives their weight forward onto your hips, attempting to crush your base and relieve pressure on the shoulder.
    • Rolling through: Opponent attempts to roll their body in the direction of the Omoplata, using their momentum to escape the lock.
    • Re-guarding: Opponent attempts to bring their legs back between you and them, or to create space to regain their guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to do an Omoplata?

    Your shoulder likely hurts because you are not controlling the opponent's posture and creating a strong base. When you lack hip pressure, you tend to use your arms to pull, which can strain your own shoulder joint. Focus on driving your hips forward and sideways to create a stable platform, and use your legs to control the opponent's trapping arm, not just your grip.

    Q: How can I Omoplata a much bigger opponent?

    Against a bigger opponent, leverage and precise body mechanics are paramount. Ensure your shin is positioned high on their back, and your hips are driven aggressively forward to break their posture. Your 'Grapevine Grip' must be tight to prevent them from posturing up. The key is to create a strong lever arm with your legs and hips, rather than trying to overpower them with upper body strength.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to an Omoplata from closed guard?

    The ideal time to transition to an Omoplata from closed guard is when the opponent attempts to break your guard by posting their hands on your hips or shins, or when they try to posture up significantly. This is when their arm is often extended and vulnerable. Look for the moment they commit to a posture change, creating the opening to swing your leg over.

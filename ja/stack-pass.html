The Stack Pass is initiated from closed guard or open guard, aiming to break the opponent's guard and establish side control or knee-on-belly.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength or incorrect body positioning, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key insight is using your hips to generate upward pressure, creating space to pass the legs, rather than simply pushing the opponent's hips.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Opponent is in closed guard. Grip opponent's gi pants at the ankles with your hands, thumbs inside.
    2. Place your knees inside the opponent's thighs, maintaining a wide base with your feet on the mat.
    3. Simultaneously, drive your hips forward and slightly upward, creating a pressing motion into the opponent's pelvis.
    4. As you press, begin to arch your back, lifting your hips off the mat and driving your chest towards your opponent's chest.
    5. Maintain a strong grip on the pants and use your legs to create a wedge between their thighs.
    6. Once sufficient upward pressure is applied and their legs start to separate, drive your knees further apart.
    7. Shift your weight to one side, bringing your hip down towards the mat, and step your foot outside their leg to begin the pass to side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Pushing directly on the opponent's hips with your hands while their legs are still locked: This can hyperextend the opponent's spine or damage your own wrists if they resist forcefully by driving their hips back.
    • Attempting to stand up prematurely with locked legs: This can lead to knee ligament tears (ACL/MCL) for you or your opponent, as uncontrolled twisting forces are applied.
    • Allowing the opponent to maintain a tight closed guard and driving straight forward: This can result in your own spine being compressed excessively, leading to lumbar disc issues or nerve impingement.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling of hip drive and arch: Practice the hip lift and back arch motion without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drilling with no resistance: Focus on the exact grip and hip pressure to create space. (25 reps per side)
    3. Partner drilling with light resistance (0%): Opponent offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the hip drive and leg separation. (25 reps per side)
    4. Partner drilling with 25% resistance: Opponent actively tries to maintain guard, but your drive should still be dominant. (20 reps per side)
    5. Partner drilling with 50% resistance: Opponent uses more active defense, requiring precise weight shifts and hip pressure. (15 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the Stack Pass as a primary option against closed guard, starting with 75% resistance. (5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent secures a tight closed guard and is posturing up slightly.
    • Opponent attempts to open their guard and create space for sweeps.
    • You are stuck in closed guard and need a definitive way to break posture and pass.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • HIP ESCAPE AND RE-GUARD: If the stacker drives too hard, escape your hips laterally and immediately bring your knees back to form guard again.
    • KNEE-TO-ELBOW FRAME: If they are stacking, bring your knee to your elbow on the side they are driving into, creating a strong frame to prevent their chest-to-chest pressure and create space to recover guard.
    • UNDERHOOK AND WALK AWAY: If they are stacking and you can get an underhook on their arm, use it to create separation and walk your hips away to regain guard or transition to a different position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try the Stack Pass from closed guard?

    Your lower back pain likely stems from hyperextending your spine rather than generating power from your hips. Instead of arching your back excessively, focus on driving your hips forward and upward. Engage your glutes and core to lift your pelvis, then use your chest to apply pressure. This shifts the force generation to your posterior chain and core, protecting your lumbar spine.

    Q: How can I effectively Stack Pass a significantly larger opponent who closes their guard tightly?

    Against a larger opponent, brute force is less effective and riskier. Focus on precise biomechanics: ensure your hips are directly under their hips when you drive upwards. Use your arms to grip their gi pants at the ankles, creating leverage. The key is to create a slight lifting motion with your hips, forcing their base to become unstable, then using your knees to pry their legs apart, not just pushing their hips.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Stack Pass, and what if they transition to an open guard?

    The Stack Pass is most effective when the opponent is committed to maintaining a tight closed guard and their hips are somewhat anchored. If they transition to an open guard (e.g., spider guard, lasso guard), the Stack Pass becomes much less viable and potentially dangerous. In open guard scenarios, you should transition to a different passing strategy like a knee-slice or torreando pass, depending on their leg positioning.

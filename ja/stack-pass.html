Stack Pass BJJ: A Biomechanically Precise Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Stack Pass begins from a stable base when your opponent is in your guard, aiming to break their leg entanglement and establish side control.

White belts often struggle because they lack precise weight distribution and rely on brute force, leading to ineffective passes and injury.

The key mechanical insight is to use your hips and base to drive vertically into their center of mass, collapsing their structure.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a same-side sleeve grip with your left hand.
    2. Place your left knee on the mat, perpendicular to your opponent's hips, creating a stable base.
    3. Drive your right hip forward and down, angling your pelvis towards your opponent's left hip socket.
    4. Simultaneously, pull their collar and sleeve, creating upward tension on their upper body.
    5. Shift your weight onto your left knee and right foot, driving vertically through their torso.
    6. Keep your head up and chest tight to their body, preventing them from bridging or shrimping.
    7. Continue driving forward, aiming to displace their hips and clear their legs to achieve side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly driving your hips too far forward without proper base: This can hyperextend your knee joint (ACL/MCL tear) if your opponent hip escapes. The correct movement is to drive vertically through their center of mass while maintaining a strong base with your supporting leg.
    • Grabbing the collar too high and pulling straight up: This can strain your shoulder joint (rotator cuff tear) and offers little leverage. Instead, grip the collar lower and pull with your body's momentum, driving your hips forward.
    • Letting your opponent's legs entangle your torso: This can lead to your own spine being compressed or twisted, risking disc injury. Maintain a tight frame with your chest and arms to prevent this entanglement.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the grip setup, knee placement, hip angle, and weight transfer without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (static): Your partner lies on their back, allowing you to practice the entire Stack Pass motion without resistance. (25 reps)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the pressure and adjustments. (50 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner drill (medium resistance): Partner actively tries to maintain guard, forcing you to apply the biomechanics correctly. (50 reps, 50% resistance)
    5. Flow rolling (guard pass focus): Focus on executing the Stack Pass against a resisting opponent in a controlled rolling scenario. (5 minutes, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the Stack Pass into your regular rolling sessions, attempting it when the opportunity arises. (10 minutes, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent has established a closed guard and is not actively threatening with sweeps.
    • When your opponent attempts an open guard but their leg structure is somewhat collapsed or predictable.
    • When you have a stable base and are looking to transition from a defensive position to an offensive one.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): As the Stack Passer drives forward, the defender quickly shrims their hips away, creating space to re-establish guard or escape. The key is timing the escape as the weight transfers.
    • Scissor Sweep Preparation: If the Stack Pass is initiated with a high collar grip, the defender can use their free arm to block the passer's head and attempt a scissor sweep by extending one leg and pulling the other.
    • Knee Shield/Frame: If the Stack Pass is initiated too slowly or with insufficient pressure, the defender can place their knee or forearm between their hips and the passer's chest to create a frame and prevent forward pressure.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try the Stack Pass?

    This often occurs because you are rounding your lower back and hyperextending it when driving forward, rather than using your glutes and core to create a stable, neutral spine. Ensure your hips are driving forward and down, maintaining a slight anterior tilt of your pelvis, and keep your chest tight to your opponent's body to avoid arching your back.

    Q: How do I Stack Pass against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on precision and leverage over strength. Establish a strong cross-collar grip and use your hip angle to drive vertically into their center of mass, aiming to collapse their structure. Do not try to muscle through; instead, use their weight against them by driving your hips forward and down, clearing their legs with your own body's momentum.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Stack Pass from closed guard?

    The optimal time is when your opponent has established a closed guard but is not actively threatening with a submission or sweep. Look for moments where their hips are slightly off-center or their leg entanglement isn't fully locked down. A stable base and a clear intention to pass are crucial before initiating the move.

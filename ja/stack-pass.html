スタックパス BJJ: 白帯のための生体力学ガイド

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate

The Stack Pass is a guard pass initiated from a top position, aiming to break the opponent's guard by driving forward and upwards.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of weight distribution and body angles, leading to ineffective pressure.

The key is using your hips to drive into the opponent's hips while maintaining a strong base and controlling their legs.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a strong top-side control, either in side control or knee-on-belly, with your chest low and tight to the opponent's torso. Your opponent's knees should be bent, attempting to form their guard.
    2. Grip the opponent's pants at the ankle or shin with both hands, ensuring your thumbs are outside the fabric for maximum control. Your arms should be straight but not locked.
    3. Place your knee closest to the opponent's hip on the mat, forming a stable tripod with your feet. Your other leg remains tucked or extended for base.
    4. Drive your hips forward and slightly downward, aiming to compress the opponent's hip line. Your body should form a slight 'V' shape with your hips lower than your shoulders.
    5. Simultaneously, use your leg grips to pull the opponent's legs towards you, creating an upward 'stacking' motion. This forces their hips off the mat.
    6. As their hips lift, step your non-gripping leg (the one not on the mat) towards their head. Your weight should now be entirely on your front leg and the knee on the mat.
    7. Drive your chest into their torso and continue to apply upward pressure with your leg grips. Your goal is to clear their legs to secure side control or mount.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly driving hips too low: This can cause hyperextension of the lumbar spine, leading to lower back strain or disc injury. Always aim to drive hips forward and slightly down, not solely downwards.
    • Overextending arms when gripping pants: This can lead to elbow hyperextension and potential ligament damage. Maintain a slight bend in your elbows, keeping your core engaged.
    • Allowing the opponent to turn into you: If your base is too narrow or your weight distribution is off, the opponent can use your momentum to shrimp and escape. Maintain a wide, stable base and keep your hips driving forward.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip drive and leg grip motion without a partner. Focus on the body mechanics of stepping and driving. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice step 1-3 of the biomechanics. Focus on establishing the correct base and initial hip drive. (20 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Have a partner gently resist your leg grips. Focus on generating upward pressure and maintaining your base. (30 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (medium resistance): The partner actively tries to maintain their guard. Focus on the full sequence of the pass, driving through their resistance. (25 reps per side)
    5. Live rolling (controlled): Attempt the Stack Pass during controlled rolling. The goal is to execute the technique, not necessarily to finish. (5 minutes, 50% resistance)
    6. Live rolling (full intensity): Integrate the Stack Pass into your regular rolling sessions. Focus on timing and reacting to the opponent's movements. (10 minutes, 90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is attempting to close guard or has a loose closed guard, presenting an opportunity to break their legs apart.
    • From knee-on-belly, when the opponent tries to shrimp away or create space, making their legs vulnerable to being stacked.
    • During scrambles where you briefly achieve a dominant top position and the opponent's legs are not yet secured.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): If the Stack Passer's base is too narrow or their hips are not driving forward effectively, the defender can shrimp their hips away to create space and re-establish guard.
    • Knee Shield: The defender can bring their knee up between their own hips and the attacker's chest, creating a frame and preventing the forward hip drive. This allows for re-guarding or sweeping.
    • Guard Recovery (Inside Heel Hook setup): As the attacker drives forward, the defender can attempt to hook the attacker's leg with their own and turn into them, potentially setting up leg entanglement or sweep opportunities.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try the Stack Pass?

    This often happens when you're driving your hips too far down instead of forward and slightly down. The lumbar spine is designed for forward flexion and extension, not excessive downward compression. Ensure your chest is driving into the opponent's torso and your hips are leading the forward motion to maintain a neutral spine.

    Q: How do I Stack Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on leverage and precise timing rather than raw strength. Use your grips to control their legs and drive your hips forward to create an upward lift. Your weight distribution is key; aim to get your hips lower than theirs to initiate the stack. Explosive hip drives are more effective than sustained pushing.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Stack Pass in BJJ?

    The Stack Pass is most effective when the opponent's guard is somewhat open or transitioning. If they have a tight closed guard, it's usually better to focus on breaking it first. Look for opportunities when they are trying to create space, shrimp away, or when you have a strong top-side control like knee-on-belly where their legs are exposed.

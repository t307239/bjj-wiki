Stack Pass BJJ: The Biomechanically Sound Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Stack Pass is a guard passing technique initiated from a standing or kneeling position against an opponent's closed guard. Its objective is to break the opponent's guard and secure a dominant top position like side control.

White belts often struggle because they rely on raw strength or incomplete understanding of leverage, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The core mechanical insight is creating a controlled, vertical "stack" of the opponent's body by driving your hips forward and downward, compressing their base.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Opponent is in closed guard. Assume a staggered, kneeling stance with knees outside opponent's hips.
    2. Grip opponent's collar with your right hand, palm facing up, thumb inside. Grip opponent's left sleeve with your left hand, palm facing down, fingers inside.
    3. Drive your right hip forward and slightly down, as if trying to sit your hip into opponent's left hip.
    4. Simultaneously, pull opponent's left sleeve towards your left hip, breaking their posture forward.
    5. Shift your weight to your right knee, keeping your left leg planted for base.
    6. Drive your forehead into opponent's right shoulder, creating a forward-leaning pressure.
    7. With your right hand still controlling the collar, use your left hand to push opponent's left elbow away from their body, creating space to pass.
    8. Step your right leg back and to the side, pivoting your hips to face the opponent's side, as your left knee slides forward to establish side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly driving forward without hip pressure: This can hyperextend the cervical spine (neck) if the opponent pushes back, causing whiplash-like injuries. Always maintain a stable base and drive with your hips.
    • Gripping too tightly on sleeves without breaking posture: This can lead to wrist sprains or tears from the opponent's counter-leverage. Focus on a controlled pull that breaks their posture.
    • Allowing opponent to maintain a strong frame with their arms: This can result in shoulder impingement or rotator cuff tears if you try to force through a solid frame. Focus on breaking the frame by pushing the elbow away.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the grip acquisition and hip drive motion without a partner. Focus on feeling the weight transfer. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner drill: Opponent maintains a loose closed guard. Execute grips and hip drive, focusing on breaking posture. (10 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    3. Partner drill: Opponent actively tries to maintain closed guard. Practice breaking posture and stepping to the side. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    4. Partner drill: Opponent attempts to resist the pass with leg pressure. Focus on driving hips and sliding knee. (10 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    5. Controlled rolling: Attempt the Stack Pass from closed guard. Your partner offers mild resistance to guard retention. (3 minutes, 90% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the Stack Pass during regular sparring. Focus on applying the learned mechanics. (5 minutes, 100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent's closed guard is tight but their posture is slightly compromised.
    • Opponent is attempting to hip escape, creating a momentary gap.
    • You have secured strong grips on the collar and sleeve, controlling their upper body.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape & Re-Guard: If the opponent starts to stack, immediately hip escape to create space and re-establish guard. Do not allow your hips to be flattened.
    • Shrimp and Frame: Use your legs to shrimp away and create frames with your forearms against the opponent's shoulders and biceps to prevent them from flattening you.
    • Sweep/Re-Guard: If the stack is deep, try to use your legs to hook their hips and sweep them over, or immediately transition to a different guard like half guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to Stack Pass from closed guard?

    Wrist pain during the Stack Pass often stems from gripping the opponent's sleeve too tightly and pulling with your wrist instead of your entire arm and shoulder. The opponent may be resisting this pull with their own arm strength. To fix this, focus on a controlled, sweeping motion with your arm to break their posture, rather than a rigid pull. Ensure your grip is firm but not tense, and use your body's leverage, not just your wrist.

    Q: How can I effectively Stack Pass against a much bigger and stronger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, raw strength is not your ally. Focus intensely on the biomechanics of the Stack Pass: precise grip placement on the collar and sleeve to control their upper body, and a deep, driving hip pressure to compress their base vertically. Prioritize breaking their posture before initiating the drive. Use their weight against them by creating a controlled 'stack' rather than trying to force your way through their mass.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Stack Pass, and what if my opponent immediately tries to invert?

    The Stack Pass is most effective when the opponent's hips are somewhat elevated or when you can create an immediate imbalance. If your opponent attempts to invert (e.g., to enter a leg entanglement) as you initiate, do not chase them blindly. Instead, maintain your forward hip pressure, and if they commit to the inversion, you can often secure a dominant position by continuing to drive forward and sideways, potentially isolating a limb or securing side control.

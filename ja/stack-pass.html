Stack Pass BJJ: The White Belt's Biomechanic Blueprint

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Stack Pass is a guard passing technique designed to break the opponent's closed guard by vertically collapsing their base, creating an opening to advance to side control.

White belts often fail by focusing on brute force instead of precise body mechanics, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The core principle is to control the opponent's hips and use your shoulder to drive into their pelvis, forcing them to stand or fall, thereby breaking their guard structure.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Opponent is in closed guard. Establish a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (e.g., left sleeve) with your left hand.
    2. Walk your knees towards the opponent's hips, maintaining a wide base with your feet hip-width apart.
    3. Drive your right shoulder into the opponent's left hip bone, angling your torso slightly towards their right side.
    4. Simultaneously, use your left hand to pull their left sleeve towards your right shoulder, creating a pulling tension.
    5. Shift your weight forward, driving your hips down and slightly back, while your right shoulder continues to push into their hip.
    6. Your left leg will begin to slide forward, aiming to get your knee past their hip.
    7. As their guard breaks, adjust your grip on the collar to a cross-face grip and drive your shoulder into their neck/upper chest to secure side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying excessive upward force on the opponent's legs while they are in closed guard can hyperextend the opponent's knees, potentially damaging the ACL and PCL ligaments.
    • Incorrectly driving your shoulder into the opponent's mid-section instead of their hip can cause their spine to arch excessively, risking lumbar disc injury.
    • Attempting to stand up with your hips too high and not driving into the opponent's base can lead to the opponent sweeping you, causing a fall that could injure your tailbone or lower back.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice walking your knees to the hips and establishing grips without a partner (5 reps per side).
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): Partner lies on their back, you practice establishing grips and driving the shoulder into the hip, breaking the guard (10 reps per side).
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner maintains a loose guard, you practice the full Stack Pass motion, focusing on hip angle and weight transfer (10 reps per side).
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to maintain guard but does not actively resist the pass, you focus on clean execution (10 reps per side).
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner actively attempts to keep guard closed and postured, you apply the Stack Pass with controlled pressure (10 reps per side).
    6. Live rolling (90-100% resistance): Apply the Stack Pass during sparring, prioritizing technique over speed (3 minutes, 3 rounds).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent's knees are not tightly locked around your hips, allowing space for your hips to get closer.
    • When the opponent attempts to create distance by pushing your hips away with their feet.
    • When the opponent is postured up, making their base less stable.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): If the passer drives their shoulder into your hip, immediately shrimp your hips away to create space and prevent them from collapsing your base.
    • Framing with Forearms: As the passer drives forward, create a frame with your forearms between their shoulder and your hip to block their forward momentum and maintain your base.
    • Standing Posture: If the passer attempts to Stack Pass, immediately try to stand up with your hips low and back, bringing your knees towards your chest to defend the collapse.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try the Stack Pass?

    Your lower back pain is likely due to hyperextending your lumbar spine. Instead of arching your back, focus on driving your hips DOWN towards the mat and slightly BACK, using your shoulder to push into the opponent's hip bone. This engages your glutes and core for a more stable, less injurious spinal alignment.

    Q: How can I Stack Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is key. Focus on controlling their hips and breaking their base with precision, not strength. Ensure your shoulder is firmly planted on their hip bone and use your grip to pull their leg across your body as you drive forward. Think of it as a controlled collapse of their structure, not a strength contest.

    Q: When should I stop trying the Stack Pass and switch to another pass?

    If the opponent consistently defends by keeping their knees tightly locked and their hips heavy, making it impossible for you to get your shoulder to their hip, it's time to switch. Also, if they successfully create space and are able to hip escape or push you away effectively, abandon the Stack Pass and transition to a pass like the Torreando or Knee Slice.

Spider Guard BJJ: A Precise Biomechanical Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Spider Guard starts from the closed guard, aiming to control the opponent's sleeves and create space for sweeps and submissions.

White belts often struggle by overextending, losing posture, or allowing the opponent to establish a strong base.

The core principle is using your legs as flexible, dynamic frames to off-balance and dictate the opponent's movement.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, place the soles of your feet on the opponent's biceps, approximately 2-3 inches from the shoulder joint.
    2. Grip both of the opponent's sleeves with your hands, thumbs inside the cuffs, ensuring a secure grip.
    3. Maintain a slight bend in your knees, creating a posterior pelvic tilt to keep your hips close to the mat.
    4. Extend one leg slightly, pushing against the opponent's bicep to create space and break their posture forward.
    5. Simultaneously, use the other leg to hook behind the opponent's arm, preventing them from posturing up or passing.
    6. Shift your weight slightly to your hip on the side of the hooked leg, preparing to initiate a sweep.
    7. Apply a controlled push and pull motion with your legs, utilizing your hips and core to generate leverage.
    8. Transition to a sweep by extending the leg on the bicep while pulling the hooked arm across your body.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Gripping too low on the sleeve (near the wrist) allows the opponent to easily pull their arm free and pass. This can lead to elbow hyperextension if the opponent forcefully pulls, damaging the ulnar collateral ligament. Always grip at the cuff.
    • Overextending your legs without a strong grip can cause your knees to lock out, risking anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) tears if the opponent drives forward. Maintain a slight bend in your knees and keep your hips connected to the mat.
    • Allowing the opponent to establish heavy pressure through your hips by not maintaining a posterior pelvic tilt can lead to back compression injuries and make it impossible to create space. Actively tilt your pelvis backward to create a frame with your shins.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice opening and closing your guard, placing feet on biceps, and gripping sleeves 20 times per side (0% resistance).
    2. Solo drill: Practice the leg extension and hook motion, simulating the push-pull of Spider Guard without an opponent (30 reps).
    3. Partner drill: With a compliant partner, practice establishing Spider Guard grips and maintaining the frame for 30 seconds, focusing on posture control (25% resistance).
    4. Partner drill: Practice the basic sweep from Spider Guard (e.g., scissor sweep) with a compliant partner 5 times per side (50% resistance).
    5. Flow drill: Practice transitioning between Spider Guard and closed guard, and initiating sweeps with a partner who offers light resistance (75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Attempt to implement Spider Guard against a resisting opponent, focusing on maintaining grips and frames, and attempting sweeps (90-100% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured up and trying to pass your closed guard.
    • When you have established good grips on the opponent's sleeves.
    • When you have created sufficient space to introduce your feet between your hips and the opponent's torso.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Opponent Posture Control: Maintain a posterior pelvic tilt and keep your hips close to the mat. Use your shins as frames on the opponent's biceps to prevent them from collapsing your guard.
    • Sleeve Grip Defense: If the opponent tries to break your sleeve grips, immediately switch to a secondary grip (e.g., wrist or forearm) and readjust your foot placement on the biceps.
    • Passing Pressure: If the opponent drives forward with heavy pressure, use your legs to create space by extending one leg and shrimping your hips away, then re-establishing your frames.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I do Spider Guard?

    Wrist pain in Spider Guard often stems from gripping the sleeve too low, near the opponent's wrist, and allowing them to pull their arm forcefully. This puts a shearing force on your wrist joint. To fix this, ensure your grip is at the very end of the sleeve cuff, and use your thumb to secure it. When the opponent pulls, absorb the force by bending your elbow slightly and using your entire arm, not just your wrist.

    Q: How do I use Spider Guard against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, your Spider Guard's effectiveness relies on maximizing leverage and minimizing their weight advantage. Focus on creating significant space with your leg extensions, forcing them to reach and break their posture. Use the hooked leg to control their arm and prevent them from driving their weight into you. Prioritize sweeps that utilize hip movement and momentum, rather than pure strength.

    Q: The optimal time to transition from closed guard to Spider Guard is when the opponent is attempting to break your closed guard by posturing up or driving forward. This is when they are most vulnerable to having their posture compromised. Look for opportunities where they have committed to a forward movement and their base is slightly unstable, allowing you to insert your feet onto their biceps.

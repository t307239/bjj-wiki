Spider Guard: The Ultimate BJJ Technique Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Spider Guard is an open guard position where you control opponent's sleeves with your feet, creating frames and attacking angles.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of precise body control and fear of giving up position.

Success hinges on maintaining constant tension through your legs and hips, creating an unpassable barrier.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, break opponent's posture by pulling their collar grips down with your hands while bridging hips slightly.
    2. Insert the sole of your right foot onto opponent's left bicep, maintaining a straight leg and flexed foot.
    3. Simultaneously, insert the sole of your left foot onto opponent's right bicep, mirroring the right leg's position.
    4. Maintain a wide base with your hips, keeping them elevated off the mat to prevent easy posture recovery.
    5. Keep your knees slightly bent, not locked, to absorb opponent's pressure and maintain flexibility.
    6. Use your feet to actively push and pull on opponent's sleeves, creating leverage and controlling their upper body.
    7. Angle your hips slightly to your left to set up sweeps like the scissor sweep or triangle choke.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Bending knees excessively and allowing hips to drop to the mat: This compromises your base, allowing opponent to easily pass your guard by driving forward.
    • Damaged Joint/Ligament: Knee valgus stress (knees caving inward) can injure ACL/MCL.
    • Correct Alternative: Keep hips elevated and actively push away with your feet, maintaining a stable frame.
    • Gripping opponent's sleeves with flat feet: This offers little control and allows opponent to easily break your grips.
    • Damaged Joint/Ligament: Wrist hyperextension or flexion can cause sprains or carpal tunnel irritation.
    • Correct Alternative: Flex your feet, gripping the sleeves with the balls of your feet and toes, creating a hook.
    • Overextending legs and losing core engagement: This makes you vulnerable to sweeps and takedowns.
    • Damaged Joint/Ligament: Lumbar spine hyperextension can lead to back pain or disc issues.
    • Correct Alternative: Engage your core, keeping your back relatively straight and your hips active.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice inserting foot grips and extending legs with no opponent (5 minutes, 0% resistance).
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Practice entering Spider Guard from closed guard, focusing on grip placement and hip elevation (10 reps per side, 0% resistance).
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Opponent maintains a neutral posture. Practice maintaining Spider Guard and creating frames (10 reps, 25% resistance).
    4. Sweep drilling: Practice one specific sweep (e.g., scissor sweep) from Spider Guard with a compliant partner (5 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Guard retention drilling: Partner attempts to pass. Focus on maintaining grips and frames to prevent the pass (3 minutes, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Attempt Spider Guard entries and sweeps against a resisting opponent (5 minutes, 90-100% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When opponent is posturing up from your closed guard, creating space to transition.
    • When opponent attempts a knee-slice pass or pressure pass, and you can intercept their sleeves.
    • When opponent's grips are loose, allowing you to establish your foot grips on their sleeves.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Pass (e.g., Torreando Pass): If opponent disengages one sleeve, use your free leg to push their hip away and shrimp out, creating space to re-guard or transition.
    • Posture Control: If opponent tries to stack you, use your foot frames to push their weight away and maintain your hip elevation.
    • Grip Break: If opponent breaks your foot grip, immediately transition to a different guard or attempt a sweep before they establish a strong pass.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to grip their sleeves in Spider Guard?

    Your wrist pain likely stems from gripping with a flat, unsupported hand. When you use Spider Guard, the power comes from your legs and hips, not your grip strength alone. Ensure you are flexing your foot and using the balls of your feet to hook the sleeves, with your hands providing secondary control. This allows your legs to absorb the primary force and protects your wrists from overextension or flexion.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise angles are paramount. Focus on keeping your hips elevated and active, using your legs to constantly push and frame against their biceps. Your goal is not to overpower them, but to create off-balancing angles. Use hip movement and leg extension to disrupt their base and create openings for sweeps like the tripod sweep or triangle choke. Never allow your hips to sink to the mat, as this negates your leverage advantage.

    Q: Is Spider Guard too advanced for white belts, or is it okay to start learning it?

    Spider Guard is an excellent position to begin exploring early, even as a white belt, provided you focus on the foundational biomechanics and safety. The key is to prioritize precise foot placement, hip elevation, and controlled movements over speed or power. By drilling the fundamental frames and understanding how to maintain your base, you'll build a strong understanding of open guard principles without risking injury. Focus on the basics of control and posture disruption.

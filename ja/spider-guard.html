Spider Guard BJJ: A White Belt's Biomechanically Accurate Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Spider Guard is an open guard position where you control your opponent's sleeves with your feet, creating frames and leverage.

White belts often struggle by overextending or losing posture, negating the control and leading to sweeps or passes.

The core principle is maintaining consistent tension and angles, using your legs as flexible, powerful frames to dictate distance and opponent posture.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a seated open guard, establish two 'Spider Hooks' by placing the soles of your feet on your opponent's biceps, just above the elbow joint.
    2. Your heels should be slightly tucked towards your hips, creating a slight bend in your knees, NOT fully extended.
    3. Maintain a 'frame' by pushing your hips slightly away from your opponent, keeping your back relatively flat on the mat.
    4. Grip both of your opponent's sleeves with your hands, ideally at the wrist or forearm area, using a strong 'figure-four' grip (thumb inside).
    5. Keep your elbows tucked close to your body, creating a stable base and preventing them from being easily attacked.
    6. To initiate a sweep, extend one leg to push your opponent's hip away while simultaneously pulling their sleeve across their body with the corresponding hand.
    7. As they shift weight to defend, adjust your hip angle and use your other leg to create a sweeping motion, aiming to unbalance them to the side.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Extending legs fully and locking knees: This hyper-extends the knee joint, potentially tearing the ACL or PCL, and removes your ability to absorb impact. Always maintain a slight bend in the knees.
    • Gripping too high on the sleeve (near the shoulder): This allows the opponent to easily 'hide' their arm and reduces your leverage, making them harder to control. Grip lower on the forearm or wrist.
    • Allowing opponent to 'stack' you (driving weight directly down): This collapses your frames and puts immense pressure on your spine and neck, risking disc injury. Maintain hip distance and use leg frames to push them away.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice establishing Spider Hooks and sleeve grips on a stationary object (e.g., a large pillow or dummy) for 10 repetitions per side.
    2. Controlled partner drilling (0% resistance): Partner lies on their back, you establish Spider Guard. Practice framing and slight leg extensions for 1 minute.
    3. Controlled partner drilling (25% resistance): Partner actively tries to break grips and improve posture. Focus on maintaining frames and control for 2 minutes.
    4. Controlled partner drilling (50% resistance): Partner attempts basic guard passes. Focus on preventing them from passing by adjusting frames and hip position for 3 minutes.
    5. Controlled partner drilling (75% resistance): Partner attempts guard passes with more intent. Focus on initiating a sweep attempt from Spider Guard. 4 minutes.
    6. Live rolling (90-100% resistance): Implement Spider Guard strategically during rolling, focusing on maintaining position and attempting sweeps. 5 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is postured up and within your sweeping range.
    • When your opponent is attempting to pass your guard from a standing or kneeling position.
    • When you have established strong sleeve grips and can control their arm extension.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: If the opponent aggressively drives weight down, immediately widen your leg base and push their hips away with both feet while driving your hips forward and slightly to the side.
    • Grip Break: If they break your sleeve grip, immediately switch to controlling their wrist or elbow with your hand, or transition to a different guard like Closed Guard.
    • Knee Slice Pass: If they attempt to drive a knee through your guard, use your Spider Hooks to push their knee away and simultaneously bring your other leg up to create a frame, preventing them from establishing the pass.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I do Spider Guard?

    Wrist pain in Spider Guard usually stems from an improper grip and excessive outward rotation of the forearm. Ensure your grips are at the wrist or forearm, with your thumb inside the grip (figure-four). Avoid extending your arms fully; keep a slight bend in your elbows to absorb force and maintain control without straining your wrist joints. This allows your bones to bear weight, not just your soft tissues.

    Q: How can I use Spider Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and angles are paramount in Spider Guard. Focus on using your legs to create a wide, stable base and push their weight away to prevent them from 'stacking' you. Your Spider Hooks on their biceps are crucial for controlling their arm extension and preventing them from posturing up. Use subtle hip movements to create off-balancing opportunities, rather than trying to overpower them.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from Spider Guard to another guard?

    The optimal time to switch from Spider Guard is when your opponent successfully breaks one of your sleeve grips, or when they manage to significantly close the distance and 'hug' your legs. If they achieve a strong grip on your pants or hips that you cannot break, it's also a sign to transition. Recognizing these moments allows you to avoid getting swept or controlled and move to a more advantageous position.

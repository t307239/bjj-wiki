The Monoplata is a joint lock applied from closed guard, aiming to isolate the opponent's shoulder and arm for a submission.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of hip mechanics and framing, leading to uncontrolled movements and potential injury.

The core principle is using your hip as a fulcrum to create leverage, rotating your body to finish the lock, not just brute force.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish a grip on the opponent's far wrist with your near hand (e.g., opponent's left wrist with your right hand).
    2. Secure a grip on the opponent's collar or shoulder with your far hand (e.g., opponent's left collar with your left hand).
    3. Kick your near leg (e.g., your right leg) up and over the opponent's far shoulder, aiming to place your shin across their upper back.
    4. Simultaneously, drive your hips forward and slightly to your side, creating a strong base.
    5. Trap the opponent's arm between your leg and your torso, ensuring your shin is firmly against their shoulder joint.
    6. Rotate your hips towards the trapped arm, bringing your chest closer to the opponent's shoulder.
    7. Extend your hips and push your opponent's shoulder away with your shin to create the finishing pressure on the shoulder joint.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to force the leg over the shoulder without proper hip movement can strain the knee or hip flexor, causing ligament tears.
    • Failing to maintain a tight frame and allowing the opponent to stack your hips can lead to spinal compression or neck injury.
    • Over-extending the finishing motion without controlled hip rotation can hyperextend the opponent's shoulder, risking dislocation or rotator cuff tears.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg over and hip rotation motion without a partner. (50 reps per side)
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the entire sequence from closed guard, focusing on correct grip and hip placement. (10 reps)
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the control points and hip movement. (10 reps)
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to defend or posture up slightly, requiring you to adjust your frames and hip pressure. (10 reps)
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner applies more active defense, simulating a training roll scenario. Focus on maintaining control. (5 reps)
    6. Live rolling (90% intensity): Attempt the Monoplata in a controlled rolling situation, prioritizing technique over submission. (2 rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent postures up significantly from closed guard, creating space for your leg to enter.
    • When the opponent attempts a guard pass and their arm is extended, leaving it vulnerable.
    • When you have established a strong hip connection and can control their posture.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stacking: The opponent drives their weight forward, attempting to crush your hips and break the lock. Maintain hip pressure and a tight frame.
    • Arm-out defense: The opponent pulls their arm free by posturing up and rotating their shoulder. Use your leg and hip to prevent this rotation.
    • Sweeping to side control: If the Monoplata fails, transition to a sweep or pass to a dominant position instead of forcing the submission.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to finish the Monoplata?

    Your shoulder might hurt because you are not using your hips as the primary lever. Instead of rotating your hips to finish, you may be trying to pull the opponent's arm with your arms, which puts excessive strain on the shoulder joint. Focus on driving your hips forward and rotating your torso, using your shin as a fulcrum.

    Q: How do I apply the Monoplata against a much bigger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paramount. Ensure your leg fully encircles their shoulder and your shin is firmly planted on their upper back. Drive your hips forward aggressively to create the angle, and use your far hand to control their head or upper body to prevent them from posturing up and crushing you. Focus on creating a tight trap before attempting the rotation.

    Q: What is the main difference between a Monoplata and an Omoplata?

    The main difference lies in the starting position and the mechanics of the arm isolation. The Monoplata is typically initiated from guard (especially closed guard) and involves trapping the opponent's arm with your leg across their back. The Omoplata is usually initiated from a standing or side-control position, where you use your legs to wrap around the opponent's arm and hip, creating a similar shoulder lock.

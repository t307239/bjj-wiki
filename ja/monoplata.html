Monoplata BJJ: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Monoplata is a joint lock submission typically initiated from closed guard, aiming to isolate and hyperextend the opponent's shoulder.

White belts often struggle due to uncontrolled hip movement and insufficient frame creation, leading to escapes or injury.

The core principle is to use your leg as a fulcrum to lever their arm into a compromising position, controlling their posture with your hips.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure your opponent's right arm by placing your right leg's shin across their upper chest, just below the collarbone, with your right foot hooking behind their left shoulder blade. Your left leg remains hooked under their right armpit, knee pointing towards the ceiling.
    2. Grip your own right shin with your left hand. Simultaneously, use your right hand to grip your opponent's left wrist, ensuring your palm faces away from you and your thumb is up.
    3. Rotate your hips 45 degrees to your left. Your right hip should drive into their chest, creating a strong frame and preventing them from posturing up.
    4. Extend your left leg straight, pulling your opponent's right arm across their body. This straightens their arm and begins the shoulder lock.
    5. Shift your weight onto your left hip, keeping your right hip firmly planted against their chest. Your body should form an 'L' shape with your opponent's right arm.
    6. Raise your right leg off the mat, using your left hand to pull your right shin towards your chest. This increases the leverage on their shoulder joint.
    7. Apply pressure by continuing to pull your right shin towards your chest while maintaining hip pressure. The submission occurs when their shoulder is hyperextended.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to "pull" the opponent's arm across with your hands alone without hip pressure: This risks tearing the rotator cuff due to uncontrolled shearing forces on the shoulder joint. The correct movement is to drive with the hips and use the legs as levers.
    • Letting your hips fall backward while applying the lock: This releases the frame and allows the opponent to escape, potentially leading to your own arm being trapped or twisted in an awkward position. Maintain constant hip pressure into their chest.
    • Overextending your own back while trying to finish: This can lead to lumbar strain or disc injury. The power comes from hip drive and leg extension, not spinal flexion. Keep your core engaged and back relatively neutral.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the leg placement, hip rotation, and grip transitions without a partner. Focus on smooth movement. (10 reps each side)
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the entire sequence from closed guard. Focus on correct hip angle and leg leverage. (5 reps each side)
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to posture or arm movement. Focus on maintaining control and hip pressure. (5 reps each side)
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner attempts to defend by posturing up or pulling their arm out. Work on maintaining the frame and adjusting your position. (5 reps each side)
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner actively defends with more intensity. Focus on finishing the submission if the opportunity arises. (3 reps each side)
    6. Live rolling (90% intensity): Integrate the Monoplata into sparring, looking for opportunities. Do not force the submission if the position is not ideal. (5 minutes rolling focus)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent attempts to posture up from your closed guard, exposing their arm.
    • When your opponent's arm is trapped between your legs during a guard pass attempt.
    • When you have established a strong closed guard and can control their posture.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape and Frame: If the opponent starts to fall backwards, immediately hip escape to your left and use your right hand to frame against their shoulder, breaking the lock. Then re-establish guard.
    • Arm Pull-Out: If the opponent's hip pressure is weak, they may be able to pull their arm through. As they attempt this, drive your hips forward and use your legs to keep their arm trapped, transitioning to a sweep or another submission.
    • Stacking Defense: If the opponent attempts to stack you, keep your hips tight to their chest and drive your left leg up, creating space to either escape or transition to a different guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to do the Monoplata?

    Your shoulder is likely hurting because you are not creating a proper frame with your hips. When you pull the arm across, if your hips are not driving into your opponent's chest, the force is applied directly to your shoulder joint. Ensure your right hip is firmly against their chest throughout the movement, using your legs as levers, not just your arms.

    Q: How do I finish the Monoplata against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on controlling their posture by keeping your hips glued to their chest. Use your left leg to extend and pull their arm across their body, straightening it. Then, instead of just pulling your shin, drive your hips forward and up, using your body's weight to create the necessary hyperextension.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Monoplata from closed guard?

    The ideal time is when your opponent tries to posture up and break your guard. As they extend their torso, their arm becomes more accessible. You can also bait them by slightly opening your guard or faking a sweep, then quickly transitioning to the Monoplata as they react and commit their weight.

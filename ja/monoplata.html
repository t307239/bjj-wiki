The Monoplata is a shoulder joint lock initiated from open guard positions, aiming to hyperextend the opponent's arm. White belts often rush, neglecting crucial control and creating openings for escape or injury.

The core insight is using your leg as a lever and your body weight to control the shoulder joint's rotation, not brute strength.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard or open guard, establish a cross-collar grip with your left hand and control the opponent's right sleeve with your right hand.
    2. Swing your right leg over the opponent's head and shoulder, aiming to place your shin across their upper back, close to the neck.
    3. Simultaneously, drive your hips forward and slightly to your left, creating a tight angle and ensuring your right shin is perpendicular to their spine.
    4. Grip your own right shin with your left hand, creating a secure "figure-four" lock around their neck and shoulder.
    5. Maintain a strong frame with your left arm, pushing against their far shoulder or bicep to prevent them from posturing up or bridging.
    6. Control their posture by pulling their collar grip down and away from their body, isolating their shoulder.
    7. To finish, extend your hips back and slightly upward while simultaneously driving your left shoulder into their upper back, creating a fulcrum at their shoulder joint.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly driving hips forward without leg control: This can lead to the opponent shucking their arm out or bridging, potentially stressing your knee or hip.
    • Forcing the submission with arm strength alone: This can cause rotator cuff tears or shoulder impingement for you and your opponent if not properly applied.
    • Allowing the opponent to flatten out: If they flatten their base, the angle for the shoulder lock is lost, and you risk your own posture being compromised, leading to potential back exposure or sweeps.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the leg swing and shin placement on a dummy or pillow, focusing on hip angle and leg positioning. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance).
    2. Solo drill: Practice the grip fighting and pulling the collar down, simulating the initial setup. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance).
    3. Partner drill: With a compliant partner, practice the full sequence, focusing on precise body positioning and hip drive without any pressure. (5 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Partner drill: Introduce light pressure, allowing the partner to resist slightly but not actively defend. Focus on maintaining control. (5 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Live drilling: Attempt the Monoplata from a guard position against a resisting opponent, focusing on executing the setup and hip movement. (5 attempts, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the Monoplata into rolling. Focus on creating the opportunity and executing the submission attempt, accepting that it won't always be successful. (Max 2 attempts per roll, 90% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is postured up in your guard, creating a clear line for your leg to swing over.
    • When the opponent is attempting to pass your guard and their weight is distributed forward, making them vulnerable.
    • When you have secured a strong collar and sleeve grip from open guard, allowing for the initial setup.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape and Leg Release: If the opponent is slow to secure the shin-over-back, immediately hip escape to your right and swing your leg back, creating space to disengage.
    • Posture Up and Base Out: If the opponent attempts to drive their hips into you before securing the lock, actively posture up and widen your base to prevent them from establishing the correct angle.
    • Bridge and Roll: If the opponent secures the Monoplata but doesn't have strong hip control, you can attempt to bridge into them and roll towards your free side to relieve pressure.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try the Monoplata, even if I don't finish it?

    Your shoulder is likely hurting due to insufficient control of the opponent's upper body and posture before initiating the leg mechanics. If their body is too mobile, your attempt to create the shoulder lock leverage becomes a direct force on your own shoulder joint. Focus on a tight collar grip and pulling their posture down before swinging your leg; this stabilizes their upper body and makes the lock biomechanically sound.

    Q: How can I use the Monoplata against a much bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, the Monoplata relies less on raw strength and more on precise positioning and timing. Ensure your leg is placed high on their back, close to the neck, to disrupt their base. Use your hips to drive *into* them, not just forward, to off-balance their weight. Control their collar and sleeve with extreme precision to prevent them from using their size to simply power out.

    Q: When should I switch from a triangle choke attempt to a Monoplata?

    You should consider switching to a Monoplata when you've attempted a triangle choke but the opponent has successfully defended by posturing up and relieving pressure on your chest. If their head is still somewhat trapped, but they've created space by standing or leaning back, the angle for a triangle is lost. This is the perfect moment to adjust your leg from around their neck to over their shoulder and transition into the Monoplata setup.

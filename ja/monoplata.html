モノプラタ: 白帯のためのバイオメカニクス戦略

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック

The Monoplata is a joint lock originating from the guard, aiming to isolate and attack the opponent's shoulder joint.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of rotational forces and proper body positioning, leading to ineffective attempts or injury.

The core principle is to use your hip's internal rotation to create a stable fulcrum, generating leverage through your legs and torso.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure your opponent's arm by placing your shin across their upper chest, just below the clavicle, with your foot extended towards their far armpit.
    2. Simultaneously, grip your own shin with both hands, creating a strong frame around their arm with your forearm pressing against their bicep.
    3. Begin to open your guard, allowing your hips to pivot inwards towards your opponent's trapped arm.
    4. Drive your hips forward and slightly upwards, rotating your pelvis so your left hip (if trapping their right arm) is closer to their shoulder than your right hip.
    5. Extend your right leg, placing your foot on their hip to create a frame and prevent them from posturing up.
    6. With your left leg, drive your knee towards your opponent's opposite shoulder, keeping your ankle flexed and heel pointed towards the ceiling.
    7. Apply pressure by squeezing your knees together and driving your hips forward, creating a lever action that forces their shoulder into extension.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to 'pull' the opponent's arm across your body without hip rotation: This can strain the rotator cuff and labrum of your own shoulder due to excessive external rotation.
    • Letting your hips fall flat to the mat after opening guard: This removes the stable base and fulcrum, allowing the opponent to easily escape or posture up, potentially leading to knee hyperextension if you try to force the lock.
    • Overextending your locking leg's knee: Pushing your knee too far past 180 degrees while trying to finish can cause ligament damage in your own knee joint.
    • Not maintaining a strong frame with your arms around their trapped arm: This allows the opponent to straighten their arm, negating the lock and potentially leading to a wrist or elbow injury if they resist forcefully.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip pivot and leg placement mechanics without a partner. Focus on the smooth transition from closed guard to the Monoplata setup. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drilling (static): With a compliant partner, practice the entire sequence from guard to the locked position, focusing on precise grips and hip angles. Partner offers no resistance. (20 reps per side)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Partner attempts to prevent the lock by posturing slightly or shrugging their shoulders. Focus on maintaining control and adjusting your body. (25% resistance, 15 reps per side)
    4. Partner drilling (medium resistance): Partner actively tries to escape by posturing up or bridging. Focus on maintaining hip pressure and frame. (50% resistance, 10 reps per side)
    5. Live rolling (controlled): Integrate the Monoplata into positional sparring, aiming to set it up from guard. Opponent can defend normally but avoids aggressive counter-attacks. (75% resistance, 5 attempts per round)
    6. Live rolling (full intensity): Attempt the Monoplata during live rolling. Focus on recognizing opportunities and executing the mechanics under pressure. (100% resistance, focus on learning, not necessarily finishing)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up in your closed guard, presenting an arm that can be isolated.
    • When your opponent is attempting to pass your guard and their arm becomes accessible to your legs.
    • When you have secured an arm-drag and are transitioning to a leg-based submission.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape and Re-guard: If the Monoplata is being set up, the defender should immediately hip escape away from the attacking leg and attempt to re-establish guard or pass.
    • Posture Up and Straighten Arm: The defender can try to quickly posture up and pull their trapped arm straight, creating space to disengage or transition.
    • Knee-to-Elbow Defense: If the Monoplata is locked, the defender can bring their free knee to their trapped elbow, creating a strong defensive frame to prevent further rotation and potential injury.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try the Monoplata?

    Shoulder pain often occurs when you rely on pulling the opponent's arm rather than using your hips to generate rotation. Your shoulder joint is not designed for that type of direct leverage. Ensure your hips are driving forward and rotating inward, using your shin and ankle as a stable lever against their upper arm.

    Q: How can I finish the Monoplata against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on securing the arm deeply and using your hip's internal rotation to create the initial leverage. Drive your hips forward aggressively, and use your free leg's knee to push towards their opposite shoulder, creating a strong fulcrum. Your body weight and hip positioning are more important than brute strength.

    Q: The optimal time is when your opponent postures up and their arm is extended slightly, making it accessible to your legs. Look for moments where they are reaching in to break your guard or attempting to create space. Avoid trying to force it when they are tightly controlled or have their arms tucked.

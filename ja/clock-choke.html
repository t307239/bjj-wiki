The Clock Choke is a powerful submission applied from the closed guard, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain. It's a fundamental technique for beginners to understand.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of precise control and understanding of leverage, leading to ineffective attempts and potential self-injury. Rushing the steps is a common pitfall.

The key insight is using your opponent's posture and your body's rotation to create a perpendicular pressure across their carotid arteries, rather than just raw pulling force.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand on their left lapel, thumb inside. Your left hand secures their right sleeve near the wrist.
    2. Shift your hips slightly to your right, creating a small angle (approx. 30 degrees) between your torso and theirs.
    3. Bring your right knee across their chest, aiming towards their left shoulder.
    4. Your left leg remains anchored, with your foot on the mat or hooked behind their right leg, maintaining hip connection.
    5. As you begin to rotate your torso counter-clockwise, drive your right shoulder into their neck on the side of your cross-collar grip.
    6. Simultaneously, pull their left arm across your body with your left hand, creating a fulcrum point.
    7. Continue the counter-clockwise rotation and lean back slightly, using your hips to elevate their head and neck into your shoulder and forearm pressure.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly attempting to "pull" the choke with just arm strength can strain your own biceps and shoulders, as the primary force should come from body rotation. Instead, focus on driving your shoulder into their neck while rotating your hips.
    • Over-extending your neck backward to "force" the choke can lead to cervical spine strain or hyperextension. Maintain a neutral neck alignment and let the rotation do the work.
    • Grabbing too high or too low on the lapel can compromise the choke's effectiveness and put undue pressure on the trachea, which is painful and less effective. Secure the lapel firmly at the sternal notch level.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling of the grip acquisition and hip shift motion without a partner (20 reps).
    2. Partner drills: Instructor provides zero resistance (0%) for grip establishment and hip angle positioning (10 reps).
    3. Partner drills: Instructor offers minimal, static resistance (25%) for the knee-across-chest and shoulder drive (15 reps).
    4. Partner drills: Instructor offers slight resistance to the rotation (50%) allowing for timing practice of the arm pull and body lean (20 reps).
    5. Partner drills: Instructor actively resists but allows the submission to be completed (75%) to feel the pressure and adjustments (25 reps).
    6. Live rolling (100% resistance): Focus on applying the Clock Choke when the opportunity arises, without forcing it (3 rounds).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When opponent is posturing up in your closed guard, creating space to insert your knee.
    • When opponent is trying to stand up or pass your guard, offering an opportunity for a counter-attack.
    • When opponent keeps their head slightly tilted to one side, making the neck more accessible.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Escape the grip by shucking the attacking arm off and immediately posturing up or switching to a different guard. This requires immediate disengagement.
    • Turn into the choke: If the cross-collar grip is loose, the opponent can turn their head and body into the attacking arm, relieving pressure and potentially creating an escape route. This is done by tucking the chin and driving the shoulder.
    • "Stacking" defense: A strong opponent may try to "stack" their weight directly onto you, flattening you out. To counter this, you must maintain hip connection and use your legs to "frame" and prevent them from crushing you.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Clock Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often stems from gripping too hard on the sleeve or lapel with your fingers extended and then trying to force the choke with arm strength. Ensure a firm, relaxed grip on the sleeve near the wrist, and focus on using your body's rotation to apply the choke, not just your arms. Keep your wrist in a neutral or slightly flexed position.

    Q: How can I successfully apply the Clock Choke against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on precise hip angles and body rotation to create the choke. Ensure your cross-collar grip is deep and secure. Instead of trying to pull them down, use your legs to create space and "climb" your hips up their torso as you initiate the rotation. This allows your smaller frame to generate effective pressure.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Clock Choke, and how do I avoid giving up my guard?

    The Clock Choke is best attempted when your opponent's posture is upright and they are not yet in a strong guard passing position. As you initiate the setup, maintain tight hip connection and use your legs to control their base. If they begin to stack or pass aggressively, be ready to disengage and reset your guard, prioritizing your safety over forcing the submission.

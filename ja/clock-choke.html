クロックチョーク：白帯のための生体力学ガイド

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Clock Choke is typically initiated from the closed guard, aiming to submit the opponent by constricting their carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of precise angles and weight distribution, leading to ineffective attempts.

The key is leveraging your hips to create a tight angle and using your forearm as a fulcrum against their neck.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish a standard cross-collar grip with your right hand on the opponent's left lapel, thumb inside.
    2. Place your left hand on the opponent's right lapel, thumb inside, creating a framing grip.
    3. Arch your lower back slightly and begin to shrimp your hips to your left, creating a 45-degree angle relative to your opponent's torso.
    4. As you shrimp, simultaneously extend your right leg and pull your opponent's head towards your right shoulder, breaking their posture.
    5. Maintain your left hand's grip on the collar, using it to control their head's position and prevent them from turning into you.
    6. Swing your right arm underneath their chin, ensuring your forearm is flush against their trachea, not their jawbone.
    7. Your right bicep should be pressing against the side of their neck, and your right hand can reinforce the grip on their left lapel or frame against their shoulder.
    8. Drive your hips forward and slightly to your left, compressing your right forearm into their trachea and your right bicep into their carotid artery.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying excessive upward force with your hands instead of driving hips forward: This strains the neck and cervical spine, risking a whiplash injury.

    The correct movement is a hip-driven compression, not a pull.

    • Gripping the opponent's jawbone with your forearm: This can cause temporomandibular joint (TMJ) pain and damage.

    Your forearm should be positioned against the soft tissue of the trachea.

    • Allowing your opponent to turn into your arm: This can lead to shoulder joint impingement or rotator cuff strain.

    Maintain a tight angle and use your left hand to control their head's rotation.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip shrimp and angle creation without a partner, focusing on the body mechanics. (10 reps)
    2. Static partner drill: With a compliant partner on their back, practice the initial grips and angle creation, stopping before the choke is applied. (10 reps)
    3. Controlled grip and angle drill: Partner remains on their back, you execute the full sequence of grips and hip movement, partner offers minimal resistance. (10 reps)
    4. Partial resistance drill: Partner actively tries to defend by posturing up or turning, but allows you to establish the choke position. (5 reps)
    5. Live rolling with focus: Attempt the Clock Choke from closed guard, but can disengage if the position isn't optimal. (2 minutes, 50% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the Clock Choke attempt naturally within closed guard exchanges, aiming for submission. (3 minutes, 75% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is postured down inside your closed guard.
    • When the opponent attempts to stack you from closed guard.
    • When you have successfully isolated one of their arms and are looking to transition to a choke.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Posture Up and Grip Break: If the opponent starts the choke, immediately posture up by extending your legs and simultaneously using your free hand to break their collar grip.
    • Turn into the Choke: If the choking arm is on your right side, turn your body sharply to your left, facing your opponent and relieving pressure on your carotid artery.
    • Shrimp Away and Base Out: If you feel the choke being applied, quickly shrimp your hips away from the choking arm and establish a solid base by placing your hand on the mat to prevent them from completing the angle.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Clock Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs if you're gripping the lapel too tightly with your fingers extended and not utilizing the thumb-inside grip effectively. Ensure your forearm is the primary pressure point against the trachea, not your wrist, and that your hand is supporting the collar grip without excessive tension. Re-evaluate your grip on the lapel and focus on forearm-to-trachea pressure.

    Q: How can I effectively Clock Choke a much larger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and angle are paramount. Focus intensely on creating a significant hip angle (at least 45 degrees) by shrimping your hips. Your goal is to get your body perpendicular to their torso, making their size less of a factor. Use your legs to break their posture and pull their head down to create the necessary space for your choking arm.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from a failed Clock Choke attempt?

    If your opponent successfully defends the Clock Choke by posturing up or turning into you, do not force the submission. Immediately transition to a sweep like the scissor sweep by extending your legs and using their defensive posture against them, or attempt a guard pass if you've created enough space. The key is to flow into another BJJ technique rather than getting stuck in a compromised position.

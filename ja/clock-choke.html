Clock Choke BJJ Technique Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Clock Choke is a powerful submission initiated from closed guard, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body control and understanding of leverage points.

The key insight is using your opponent's posture and your hip rotation to create a tight, inescapable choke.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish a cross-collar grip with your right hand on their left collar and your left hand on their right collar. Ensure fingers are deep within the fabric.
    2. Simultaneously, secure a sleeve grip with your left hand on their right sleeve, near the wrist. This prevents them from posturing up.
    3. Drive your hips to your left, creating a 90-degree angle relative to your opponent's spine. This shifts their weight off balance.
    4. Use your right knee to hook their right hip, preventing them from squaring up. Your left leg remains draped over their back.
    5. Arch your back and pull your opponent's head towards your chest, creating tension in the collar grip.
    6. Rotate your hips counter-clockwise, driving your right knee forward and pulling the sleeve grip towards your left hip.
    7. Maintain a strong base by keeping your feet locked and your core engaged. The choke is applied by the pressure of the collar and the arm.
    8. As you rotate, your left elbow should drive down into their neck, further constricting blood flow.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to crank the head with your arms instead of rotating your hips: This can strain the cervical spine and neck ligaments, leading to whiplash or disc injury. The correct movement is a hip-driven rotation, not arm strength.
    • Overextending your own arm to grab the sleeve: This can lead to a shoulder dislocation or labral tear if the opponent resists forcefully. Maintain a strong frame and secure the grip close to the wrist without excessive reach.
    • Allowing your opponent to flatten your guard by not controlling their hips: This can lead to your knees being pushed open, potentially causing knee ligament sprains or meniscus tears. Actively hook their hip and maintain a tight guard.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip rotation and grip changes without a partner. Focus on the body mechanics. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Practice the full sequence with a compliant partner, focusing on precise body positioning and grip application. (10 rounds x 30 seconds)
    3. Light resistance drilling: Partner offers mild resistance to posture and hip movement. Focus on maintaining control and executing the choke. (10 rounds x 30 seconds, 25% resistance)
    4. Flow drilling: Practice the Clock Choke as part of a sequence from closed guard, with the partner attempting to defend or escape. (10 rounds x 1 minute, 50% resistance)
    5. Live rolling (controlled): Attempt the Clock Choke during positional sparring, with specific focus on this technique. (5 rounds x 2 minutes, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the Clock Choke into regular sparring, aiming for submission. (5 rounds x 3 minutes, 90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up slightly in your closed guard, creating space between your bodies.
    • When your opponent tries to stack you by pushing their hips forward into your guard.
    • When your opponent attempts to open your guard by posturing up and driving their knees in.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Posture Up and Drive Forward: The opponent straightens their posture and drives their chest into your face, flattening your guard and relieving pressure on the collar. This breaks the choke angle.
    • Hip Escape and Square Up: The opponent uses their hands to push off your hips and hips escape to their right, squaring their body to yours. This disrupts the rotational mechanics of the choke.
    • Grip Break: The opponent uses their free hand to peel your choking hand off their collar or uses their sleeve-hand to break your sleeve grip. Precise grip fighting is key to defending.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try the Clock Choke from closed guard?

    Neck pain often occurs when you rely on arm strength to constrict the neck rather than using your hips and body rotation. The choke is applied by the pressure of the collar on the carotid artery, amplified by your hip rotation and the opponent's posture. Focus on driving your hips to your left and rotating your torso, allowing the collar to create the choke rather than muscling it.

    Q: How can a white belt use the Clock Choke effectively against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on precise hip angles and maintaining a tight, closed guard to prevent them from using their weight advantage. Ensure your cross-collar grip is deep and secure, and use your sleeve grip to control their posture. The key is to use their weight against them by rotating your hips and pulling them into the choke, rather than trying to overpower them.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to the Clock Choke from closed guard?

    The ideal moment is when your opponent attempts to posture up or create space to pass your guard. If they lean forward, you have an opening to drive your hips to the side and initiate the rotation. If they try to stack you, their forward momentum can be used to your advantage as you shift your hips and apply the choke. Look for the moment their weight is shifting away from a stable base.

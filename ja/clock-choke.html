クロックチョーク: 白帯のための生体力学ガイド

白★★☆☆☆中級
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Clock Choke is initiated from the closed guard, aiming to submit your opponent by compressing their carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of precise body control and understanding of leverage points.

Success hinges on establishing a tight cross-collar grip and using your hip extension to create the choke's rotational force.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure a deep cross-collar grip with your right hand on the opponent's left lapel, thumb inside.
    2. Simultaneously, secure your left hand on the opponent's right lapel, thumb inside, creating a "figure-four" grip.
    3. Arch your back, lifting your hips slightly off the mat, positioning your body as if you were a clock's minute hand at the 12 o'clock position.
    4. Drive your left shoulder into the opponent's right shoulder, preventing them from posturing up.
    5. Rotate your hips counter-clockwise, moving your body from 12 o'clock towards 9 o'clock, while keeping your head down and driving your left shoulder forward.
    6. Extend your legs slightly to create space and leverage, but maintain hip contact with the opponent's torso.
    7. As you rotate, pull your right arm across your body and push your left arm away, creating a strong squeezing pressure on the neck.
    8. Maintain a stable base with your knees tucked towards your chest and your hips actively engaged in the rotation.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly pulling your opponent's head down with your arms can strain your own shoulders and elbows, leading to rotator cuff tears or bicep tendonitis. The correct action is to use your shoulder and chest to control their posture while your hips drive the choke.
    • Attempting to "muscle" the choke by simply squeezing with arm strength can lead to wrist sprains or carpal tunnel syndrome due to excessive, uncontrolled force. Focus on hip rotation and body leverage instead of brute arm strength.
    • Overextending your neck to try and get a better grip can cause cervical strain or whiplash. Keep your head stable and tucked slightly, allowing your body mechanics to create the pressure.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip rotation and grip setting motion without a partner, focusing on smooth transitions (50 reps).
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the full sequence, focusing on precise grip placement and hip movement (10 reps each side).
    3. Partner drill (light resistance - 25%): Your partner offers minimal resistance to your initial setup, allowing you to practice breaking their posture and establishing grips.
    4. Partner drill (50%): Your partner attempts to defend by posturing up slightly or shrugging their shoulders, requiring you to adjust your body mechanics.
    5. Partner drill (75%): Your partner actively tries to escape the guard and defend the choke, forcing you to maintain tight control and execute the rotation precisely.
    6. Live rolling (100%): Attempt the Clock Choke during live rolling, focusing on recognizing opportunities and executing the technique with full intention.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is postured up in your closed guard, creating an opening for grip fighting.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard and you can establish a strong closed guard grip.
    • When you have successfully controlled your opponent's posture and they are unable to create significant space.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard retention: If the opponent establishes a strong grip, immediately break the grip by shucking their arm across your body and creating space with your hips.
    • Posture control: If the opponent is attempting to choke, focus on breaking their grip and establishing a strong defensive posture, creating distance with your knees.
    • Escape to side control: If the choke is being applied effectively, attempt to turn your hips towards their armpit and drive them over, creating a scramble for a better position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Clock Choke?

    Wrist pain often stems from using your wrist as the primary lever to apply the choke. Instead of relying on wrist flexion, focus on using the rotation of your hips and the extension of your entire arm, driven by your shoulder. Ensure your cross-collar grip is deep and your thumb is inside the lapel for better leverage and less strain on the wrist.

    Q: How can I apply the Clock Choke against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus intensely on establishing a deep cross-collar grip and using your hips to create the "clock" rotation. Drive your shoulder into their chest to prevent them from using their weight to flatten you. Your body rotation, rather than pure strength, will generate the choking pressure.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Clock Choke from closed guard?

    The ideal time is when your opponent has postured up slightly, creating a gap between their head and shoulders, and you can secure a strong cross-collar grip. This allows you to "lock" their posture and initiate the rotational movement effectively. Avoid attempting it when they are in a very tight, flattened guard pass position.

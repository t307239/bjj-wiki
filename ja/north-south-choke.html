The North-South Choke is a powerful submission initiated from the North-South position, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain. White belts often struggle due to a lack of positional control and understanding of leverage.

The key to this choke is creating a secure frame and driving your opponent's head into your forearm using your body's weight and structure, not just arm strength.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish North-South: From side control, transition your hips towards your opponent's head, so your head is near their armpit and your hips are near their head. Your chest should be perpendicular to their torso.
    2. Secure the Collar Grip: Reach your left arm under your opponent's head and grip their gi collar with your left hand, thumb inside. Your grip should be deep, close to the neck seam.
    3. Grab the Far Shoulder/Lapel: With your right hand, reach across their chest and grip their far gi lapel or shoulder. This stabilizes their upper body and prevents them from turning.
    4. Hip Angle and Weight Transfer: Angle your hips slightly towards your opponent's head. Drive your weight forward, pressing your chest into their upper chest.
    5. Forearm Placement: Slide your left forearm (the one gripping the collar) across their throat, ensuring the gi material is taut against their carotid artery.
    6. Head and Neck Pressure: Tuck your chin down and slightly to your right. Use your head to apply downward pressure, compressing their neck against your forearm.
    7. Drive and Squeeze: Drive your hips forward and slightly down, while simultaneously squeezing your elbows together and tightening your collar grip. Your body weight should be the primary force.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Elbow Placement: Attempting to grip too low on the collar or not bringing the choking forearm across the throat directly can lead to the radial nerve in the forearm being compressed, causing temporary numbness or tingling.
    • Excessive Neck Rotation: Forcing the head to turn to create the choke can strain the cervical spine ligaments, potentially causing whiplash-like injuries.
    • Lack of Hip Drive: Relying solely on arm strength to squeeze the choke without proper hip drive and weight transfer can lead to shoulder impingement as you over-extend your arms.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Grip and Frame Practice: Practice the collar grip and forearm placement on a dummy or by yourself, focusing on the feel of the gi and the pressure. (10 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Static Position Drilling: With a partner lying on their back, practice transitioning to North-South and establishing the grips without any movement from the partner. (10 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Assisted Transition and Grip: Your partner remains passive. You practice transitioning to North-South and securing the grips, with them slightly resisting the transition. (10 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled Choke Application: Your partner offers minimal resistance. You apply the choke, focusing on hip drive and weight transfer. Your partner taps early. (10 reps, 50% resistance)
    5. Light Flow Rolling: Practice the North-South Choke during light flow rolling, looking for opportunities. Your partner is aware and offers light resistance to escapes. (3 minutes, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling Application: Attempt the North-South Choke in live rolling, focusing on positional control and timing. Your partner actively defends and escapes. (3 minutes, 90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is turtled and you have established North-South control.
    • After successfully passing the guard and landing in the North-South position.
    • When your opponent attempts to shrimp away from side control, creating an opportunity to transition to North-South.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape and Bridge: If your opponent attempts the choke, bridge your hips to the side of the choking arm and escape to guard. This breaks the frame and allows you to regain guard.
    • Frame with the Arm: If the choking arm is not fully across your throat, use your free arm to create a frame between your neck and their forearm, preventing the choke from fully engaging.
    • Turn to Your Side: Attempt to turn your body towards your opponent's choking arm. This can relieve pressure and create space to escape or transition to a better position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the North-South Choke?

    Wrist pain often occurs if you are gripping the collar too tightly with your fingers extended or if you are trying to pull the collar down with excessive wrist flexion. Ensure your grip is firm but relaxed, with your thumb inside the collar. Focus on driving your forearm across the neck using your body weight and hip pressure, rather than a forceful wrist pull.

    Q: How can I apply the North-South Choke against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise body positioning are crucial. Instead of relying on raw strength, focus on driving your weight directly into their upper chest and head, creating a strong frame. Angle your hips significantly towards their head and use your forearm to push their head into your other forearm, compressing the carotid artery. Ensure your grip is deep on the collar to maximize leverage.

    Q: When is the best time to try the North-South Choke in a round?

    The North-South Choke is most effective when your opponent is already in a compromised position, such as turtled or trying to escape side control. It's not a primary attack from a neutral side control. Look for opportunities immediately after a successful guard pass that lands you in North-South, or when your opponent exposes their neck by trying to shrimp away or turtle.

North-South Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanic Guide

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

The North-South Choke is a submission applied from the North-South position, aiming to constrict the carotid arteries. It is often initiated when the opponent is turtled or attempts to escape a dominant side control.

White belts often struggle because they rely on brute strength rather than precise leverage and body mechanics, leading to inefficient pressure and potential injury.

The key to this choke is creating a "frame" with your body and arms, forcing the opponent's head into a position of vulnerability against your shoulder.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Start in the North-South position, your chest directly over the opponent's torso, head facing their feet.
    2. Secure a right collar grip with your left hand, palm facing you, thumb inside the collar.
    3. Slide your right arm under the opponent's head, placing your right shoulder firmly against their jawline.
    4. Your right hand grabs your own left bicep, creating a "figure-four" grip with your arms.
    5. Adjust your hips so they are directly over the opponent's shoulders, creating a stable base.
    6. Drive your chest downwards, compressing their upper body and forcing their chin towards their chest.
    7. Angle your head slightly to the opponent's left, increasing the pressure on the right carotid artery.
    8. Maintain hip pressure and chest compression, ensuring your right shoulder stays locked against their jaw.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Neck Extension: A white belt might crane their neck backward trying to apply pressure. This can strain the cervical spine and ligaments in the neck, potentially leading to whiplash-like injuries. The correct movement is to drive your chest down and forward, using your shoulder and upper back to apply pressure.
    • Arm Entanglement Mishap: Trying to "pull" the head into the choke by flailing the free arm can result in your own arm getting trapped or bent awkwardly. This can sprain the elbow or wrist. Instead, focus on the secure figure-four grip and controlled body compression.
    • Uneven Weight Distribution: Leaning too far to one side or not keeping hips over the shoulders can lead to the opponent escaping or you losing balance. This can result in a knee or hip joint being hyperextended during the struggle. Maintain a stable, centered base with hips directly over the opponent's shoulders.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Grip and Frame Practice: Practice securing the collar grip and figure-four arm grip from a stationary position on a mat, focusing on the feel of the frame. (50 reps each side)
    2. Static Position Drilling: Your partner lies on their back, you assume North-South position and apply the choke mechanics without any movement from your partner. Focus on hip and chest pressure. (10 reps)
    3. Partner Resists Lightly: Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to practice the subtle adjustments of hip angle and chest drive. (20 reps)
    4. Controlled Movement Drill: Partner attempts to shrimp or bridge slightly, and you practice maintaining the choke by adjusting your base and pressure. (30 reps)
    5. Light Sparring (25% Resistance): Apply the choke in a live rolling scenario but with your partner actively defending and you focusing solely on executing the technique correctly. (3 rounds, 2 minutes each)
    6. Full Sparring (75% Resistance): Attempt the North-South Choke during regular sparring, allowing for realistic defense and counter-attacks. (5 rounds, 3 minutes each)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent turtles defensively from side control or mount.
    • When you achieve a clean North-South position and the opponent's head is accessible.
    • When the opponent attempts to shrimp away from your side control, transitioning them towards the North-South orientation.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Escape to Guard: The opponent can try to bridge and roll to their side, bringing their legs between you and creating a guard. This requires the attacker to maintain a strong base and not get rolled over.
    • Head and Arm Escape: The opponent can try to pull their head free by extending their neck and creating space, often by pushing off your shoulder. The attacker must keep their shoulder locked and maintain chest compression.
    • Stalemate/Reset: If the choke is not fully secured, the opponent can simply settle into a defensive posture, forcing a stalemate. The attacker must be ready to transition to another submission or maintain positional control.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the North-South Choke?

    Wrist pain often occurs when the grip is too tight or the wrist is bent at an unnatural angle. Ensure your left hand's thumb is inside the collar and your right hand is gripping your left bicep firmly but without excessive tension. Focus on driving your shoulder into the opponent's jaw and using your chest to compress their upper body, rather than relying on wrist strength for the choke.

    Q: How do I apply the North-South Choke against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and body positioning become even more critical. Ensure your hips are directly over their shoulders to maximize your base and prevent them from using their weight to crush you. Drive your chest down aggressively and use your right shoulder to dig into their jawline, forcing their head into the choke. Do not try to overpower them; instead, use their size against them by creating a tight, inescapable frame.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the North-South Choke, and what if I can't get the grip?

    The optimal time is when the opponent is turtled or you've transitioned to a clean North-South position after a sweep or pass. If you can't secure the collar grip, don't force it. You can transition to other submissions from North-South, such as an arm-triangle choke if their arm is exposed, or maintain positional control and wait for another opportunity. A missed grip can lead to losing position, so prioritize control.

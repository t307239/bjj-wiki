North-South Choke: A Precise BJJ Guide for Beginners

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

The North-South Choke is initiated from the North-South positional control, aiming to submit the opponent by constricting blood flow to the brain.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of precise body alignment and excessive force, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key to this choke is maintaining strong positional control while strategically driving your shoulder into the carotid artery, supported by your opponent's own shoulder.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish North-South position: Opponent is supine, your head is near their hip, feet near their head, chest to chest.
    2. Control opponent's head: Reach your right arm (if applying choke on their left side) across their neck, securing a deep grip on their left collar with your palm facing your face.
    3. Secure opponent's arm: With your left arm, reach under their right armpit and grip your own right wrist, forming a gable grip or palm-to-palm grip.
    4. Drive your shoulder: Lower your right shoulder and drive it into the opponent's left carotid artery, pressing it against their own shoulder.
    5. Hip angle and weight transfer: Angle your hips to your left, away from their head, and drive your weight forward through your shoulders, compressing their neck.
    6. Maintain head control: Keep your head tight to their head to prevent them from turning into you.
    7. Finish: Continue driving your shoulder into their neck while maintaining hip angle and weight forward to complete the choke.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying the choke by simply "squeezing" with your arms: This places undue stress on your own biceps and shoulders, risking bicep tendonitis or rotator cuff strain. The correct method is to drive your shoulder into the artery, using your arms for grip and alignment.
    • Releasing pressure too early or not establishing North-South control firmly: This can lead to the opponent escaping the position, creating a scramble where you might be vulnerable to sweeps or submissions. Always ensure solid positional control before attacking.
    • Allowing the opponent to create space under their chin with their shoulder: If their shoulder isn't pressed against their carotid, the choke will not be effective and you risk straining your own neck muscles trying to force it. Ensure their shoulder acts as a backstop for your driving shoulder.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill (0% resistance): Practice the grip changes and body alignment from a static position, focusing on the shoulder drive and hip angle. (20 reps)
    2. Partner drill with no resistance (0% resistance): Execute the full sequence with a compliant partner, focusing on smooth transitions and proper weight distribution. (15 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill with light resistance (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to practice establishing control and applying the choke with correct pressure. (10 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill with moderate resistance (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to defend by creating space or turning, forcing you to adjust your body mechanics and maintain control. (10 reps per side)
    5. Live rolling (75% resistance): Attempt the North-South Choke during rolling, focusing on recognizing the correct timing and positional setup. (3 attempts per round)
    6. Live rolling (90-100% resistance): Fully integrate the North-South Choke into your rolling game, aiming for submission against actively defending opponents. (Focus on opportunity)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Immediately after successfully transitioning to North-South control from a guard pass.
    • When your opponent attempts to shrimp away from side control, creating a North-South opportunity.
    • When your opponent tries to bridge or escape from bottom side control, exposing their neck.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Shrimp and turn: If the attacker drives too hard without proper setup, the defender can shrimp their hips away and turn into the attacker's base, creating a scramble.
    • Bridge and roll: A strong bridge from the defender, combined with turning their head away from the choke, can disrupt the attacker's weight distribution and allow for escape.
    • Frame with arm: The defender can try to create a frame with their free arm between their neck and the attacker's shoulder to alleviate pressure and buy time to escape.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try the North-South Choke?

    Your neck is likely hurting because you are forcing the choke with your arms and upper body strength instead of using your shoulder to drive into the carotid artery. This misalignment puts direct strain on your cervical spine and trapezius muscles. Ensure your shoulder is the primary point of contact and drive your weight forward through your hips and chest, not just your arms.

    Q: How can I apply the North-South Choke effectively against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, perfect positional control and leverage are paramount. Focus on driving your shoulder deep into their carotid artery and using your hips to create a strong angle, preventing them from using their size to power out. Secure a deep collar grip and use your non-choking arm to control their posture by gripping your own wrist, ensuring you don't allow them to create space.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to the North-South Choke from side control?

    The ideal time to transition to the North-South Choke from side control is when your opponent attempts to shrimp or bridge to escape. These movements often create an opening where their head is exposed and their body is momentarily out of position. Instead of following their escape directly, you can use their momentum to pivot your hips and establish the North-South position, then immediately attack the choke before they can recover their defense.

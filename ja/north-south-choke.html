North-South Choke: A Precise BJJ Technique Guide for White Belts

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

The North-South Choke is applied from the north-south position, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain via the carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of control and understanding of weight distribution, leading to ineffective attempts and potential self-injury.

The key to this choke is creating a strong rotational pressure with your hips and shoulders, effectively squeezing the opponent's neck.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From the north-south position, establish a cross-collar grip with your right hand on the opponent's left lapel, fingers inside the collar.
    2. Place your left hand on the opponent's right shoulder, palm facing down, fingers pointing towards their head.
    3. Angle your hips to the opponent's left, so your left hip is lower than your right hip, creating a slight rotation.
    4. Drive your chest into the opponent's chest, ensuring your weight is distributed over their sternum.
    5. Use your left arm to pull the opponent's shoulder towards you, while simultaneously driving your right elbow down and slightly back.
    6. Rotate your hips and torso counter-clockwise, continuing to drive your right elbow down and in.
    7. Apply sustained pressure by squeezing your elbows together and crunching your torso, maintaining hip angle and chest pressure.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying the choke by simply pulling your opponent's head down: This can strain your own neck and shoulder joints (cervical spine and glenohumeral joint) without effectively choking the opponent. The correct movement involves hip rotation and shoulder pressure, not just arm pulling.
    • Gripping too high or too low on the collar: This can lead to a weak grip and ineffective pressure, potentially causing your fingers or wrist to be hyperextended (metacarpophalangeal and radiocarpal joints) as you try to force the submission. Always aim for the base of the collar.
    • Losing hip control and leaning too far forward: This can cause your base to collapse and your opponent to escape, potentially leading to a knee or ankle injury (tibiofemoral or talocrural joint) if you fall awkwardly. Maintain a stable base with your hips angled.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip angle and shoulder positioning without a partner. Focus on smooth transitions. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Execute the entire sequence with a compliant partner, focusing on grip accuracy and weight distribution. (25 reps per side)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the pressure and adjust your hips and shoulders. (20 reps per side)
    4. Scenario drilling (defensive posture): Partner attempts to shrimp or bridge, and you maintain the north-south position and choke setup. (15 reps per side)
    5. Live rolling (controlled): Attempt the choke during controlled rolling, prioritizing technique over submission. (5 rounds)
    6. Live rolling (full resistance): Attempt the choke during full rolling, understanding that it may not always be available. (10 rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have successfully transitioned to the north-south position after a guard pass.
    • When your opponent is turtled and you can secure a strong cross-collar grip.
    • When your opponent attempts to bridge from side control and you can flatten them out into north-south.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Escape to Guard: If the attacker's hip angle is shallow, shrimp your hips away and bring your legs up to establish guard.
    • Bridge and Roll: If the attacker leans too far forward, bridge your hips forcefully towards their head and attempt to roll them over.
    • Defensive Grip Release: If the choke is being applied, focus on breaking the grip on your collar by prying with your opposite hand and turning your head away from the pressure.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the North-South Choke?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you grip too high on the collar or try to force the choke by pulling downwards with your arm. This hyperextends your wrist. Ensure your grip is at the base of the collar and focus on driving your elbow down while rotating your hips, rather than just yanking.

    Q: How do I apply the North-South Choke against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on maximizing your body weight and hip pressure. Ensure your hips are deeply angled to their side, preventing them from posturing up. Drive your chest into their sternum and use your shoulder to control their head while executing the rotational squeeze. Don't try to overpower them; use leverage.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the North-South Choke in a BJJ match?

    The North-South Choke is most effective when you've recently achieved the north-south position, especially after a successful guard pass where your opponent is still trying to recover. It's also a good opportunistic submission if your opponent turtles and you can secure a strong collar grip before they can fully defend.

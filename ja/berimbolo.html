The Berimbolo is a dynamic sweep and transition originating from open guard positions like De La Riva or Reverse De La Riva. It aims to quickly invert your body to secure a back take or a dominant sweep position.

White belts often struggle with the Berimbolo due to a lack of body awareness and fear of falling, leading to stiff movements and incorrect weight distribution.

The core insight is using your hips to generate momentum and your frames to control your opponent's base, creating an 'inversion' rather than a simple 'fall'.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting Position: Assume a De La Riva guard. Grip your opponent's far pant leg just above the ankle with your left hand (if right-handed). Grip their near sleeve with your right hand.
    2. Hip Engagement: Initiate a slight hip escape (shrimping) to your right, bringing your hips away from your opponent's center of gravity.
    3. Leg Hook Progression: As your hips move, sweep your right leg (the one not hooked) behind your opponent's lead leg, hooking it from the outside.
    4. Weight Transfer and Inversion: Simultaneously, drive your left knee towards the mat while transferring your weight onto your right hip. Your body should begin to invert.
    5. Frame and Push: Use your right arm (sleeve grip) to push against your opponent's hip or thigh, creating space and controlling their base.
    6. Mat Contact and Rotation: Your left hip will contact the mat. Rotate your torso, keeping your right leg hooked, to face your opponent's back.
    7. Securing the Position: As you rotate, bring your left leg over your opponent's back to establish a body triangle or secure hooks for a back take.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Knee Drive: Driving your knee directly into your opponent's hip with force without proper hip rotation. This can cause a hyperextension or twisting injury to the knee ligament (ACL/MCL) or meniscus.
    • Correct Alternative: Focus on hip escape and rotation, letting the knee drive be a consequence of the inversion, not the primary force.
    • Stiff Arm Posture: Grabbing the sleeve and rigidly holding your arm straight while inverting. This puts immense stress on the elbow joint, potentially leading to a valgus or varus strain.
    • Correct Alternative: Maintain a slight bend in your elbow, using it as a dynamic frame to push and control, not a rigid prop.
    • Premature Leg Release: Releasing the pant grip too early in the inversion. This causes your body to fall uncontrolled, risking ankle sprains or awkward landings on your back or shoulder.
    • Correct Alternative: Maintain a secure pant grip until your body is inverted and your hips are in position to transition to hooks.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Escape & Hook Placement (0% resistance): Practice shrimping, bringing your leg behind, and hooking without an opponent. 10 repetitions.
    2. Solo Inversion Drill (0% resistance): From a seated position, practice the hip escape, leg hook, and controlled inversion to your side. 10 repetitions.
    3. Partner Drill: Controlled Inversion (25% resistance): Partner kneels. You perform the Berimbolo movement, and they offer minimal resistance by simply staying in place. Focus on smooth transitions. 5 repetitions per side.
    4. Partner Drill: Hip Push and Rotation (50% resistance): Partner stands. You initiate the De La Riva, and they slightly push their hips back as you begin the inversion. Focus on using your frame to maintain control. 5 repetitions per side.
    5. Partner Drill: Sweep Attempt (75% resistance): Partner stands and offers light pressure. Execute the Berimbolo with the intention of sweeping. Partner reacts naturally but without full intensity. 5 repetitions per side.
    6. Live Rolling: Berimbolo Attempts (90% resistance): During sparring, actively look for opportunities to initiate the Berimbolo. Your partner defends naturally. Focus on completing the transition or recognizing when to disengage. 3-5 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up, creating space between your legs.
    • When your opponent is leaning heavily on one leg, making it unstable.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard by stepping their legs through.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The 'Knee-Block' Defense: As the Berimbolo attacker inverts, the defender drives their lead knee *into* the attacker's hip on the side of the inversion. This blocks the rotation and prevents the attacker from getting their leg over. Maintain a strong base and forward pressure.
    • The 'Stand-Up and Disengage': If the attacker commits to the inversion, the defender can quickly stand up and step their hips away, breaking the leg entanglement and creating distance. The attacker will be left in an awkward, often exposed, position.
    • The 'Leg-Drag Counter': As the attacker attempts to hook the far leg, the defender can execute a leg drag by grabbing the attacker's exposed ankle and pulling it across their body, simultaneously stepping their own hips back to establish a leg-drag position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to Berimbolo?

    Knee pain during the Berimbolo often stems from forcing the inversion with your knee instead of your hips. If you drive your knee directly into your opponent's hip without proper hip escape and rotation, you can torque your knee joint, straining ligaments like the ACL or MCL. Focus on shrimping your hips away first, then allowing your leg to hook and your body to invert naturally, using your arms as frames.

    Q: How can I Berimbolo against a much bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing become even more critical. Instead of relying on brute force, use their weight against them. As they lean forward, initiate a strong hip escape to create a gap. Use your grips to pull their weight slightly off-balance, then explosively invert. Your frames are crucial here; use your arms to push their hips away and create just enough space to get your leg over their back, then immediately drive your hips forward to secure position.

    Q: Is Berimbolo a sweep or a back take, and when should I prioritize one over the other?

    The Berimbolo is a transitional technique that can lead to either. If your opponent reacts by trying to sprawl or push their hips away to defend, you can often transition to a sweep. If they stay relatively square or try to follow your inversion, you have a prime opportunity to secure a back take. Pay attention to their body's reaction: if their hips move away, focus on sweeping; if they stay close, aim for the back.

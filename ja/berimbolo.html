Berimbolo BJJ: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

The Berimbolo is a dynamic guard transition originating from the closed guard, aiming to achieve a back-mount position. It involves a specific hip twist and leg entanglement to secure a dominant angle.

White belts often struggle with the Berimbolo due to a lack of body awareness and an overly aggressive, linear approach, leading to instability and injury.

The core mechanical insight is using your hips to create space and leverage, rather than brute force, to initiate the roll and secure the entanglement.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Start in closed guard with opponent in your guard, knees pinched, feet clasped behind their back. Grip both of their sleeves with a standard cross-grip (right hand on left sleeve, left hand on right sleeve).
    2. Angle your hips 45 degrees to your left, driving your left hip into your opponent's right hip. Simultaneously, drive your right knee forward, creating a wedge between your legs.
    3. Release your right sleeve grip. Grip your opponent's right pant leg just above the knee with your right hand. Your left hand maintains the left sleeve grip.
    4. Initiate a hip heist: Lift your hips off the mat, arching your back slightly and rotating your pelvis to the left. This disconnects your opponent's base.
    5. As your hips lift, pull your opponent's left arm across their body with your left hand and drive your right knee further into their right hip. This initiates a forward rotation.
    6. Simultaneously, swing your right leg under your opponent's right leg, aiming to hook their thigh or calf with your right shin. Your left leg remains hooked behind their back.
    7. Continue the rotation, using your left sleeve grip to pull them forward and over your right leg. Your hips should now be on the mat on your left side, facing your opponent's back.
    8. As you complete the roll, your right leg hooks their right leg from the inside (like a De La Riva hook), and your left leg snakes to secure their left leg. Your goal is to secure the back.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to force the roll by pulling straight up on the sleeves without hip rotation: This can lead to shoulder impingement or rotator cuff tears in the pulling arm as the opponent's weight is not properly leveraged.
    • Rolling directly onto your back without establishing the leg entanglement: This puts immense pressure on the lumbar spine, risking disc herniation or facet joint injury.
    • Grabbing the opponent's pants too low (e.g., ankle): This offers poor leverage and can lead to knee hyperextension injuries when you attempt to hook their leg, as your ankle is not braced.
    • Releasing the initial closed guard grip too early or without proper hip control: This can result in your opponent posturing up and stacking you, causing neck strain or spinal compression.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip heist and leg swing motion without a partner. Focus on hip rotation and leg placement. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the entire sequence from closed guard to securing the back-mount position. Focus on smooth transitions and correct body mechanics. (10 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers slight resistance to the hip heist. Focus on generating enough hip power to overcome minimal resistance. (10 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to prevent the hip heist and leg entanglement. Focus on maintaining control and adjusting your angles. (10 reps per side)
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner actively defends the Berimbolo by posturing up or trying to shrimp away. Focus on maintaining entanglement and adjusting your grip. (5 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Attempt the Berimbolo during sparring, focusing on timing and recognizing opportunities. Prioritize safety and technique over submission. (3 attempts per round)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up in your closed guard, creating space between your hips and theirs.
    • When your opponent's weight is shifted forward, making them susceptible to a forward roll.
    • When you feel your opponent's base is unstable, perhaps after a failed guard pass attempt.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: If the white belt attempts to roll forward, immediately drive your hips down and stack their upper body onto your hips. This prevents the entanglement and can lead to a pass.
    • Leg Pummeling: As they attempt to swing their leg, actively pummel your own leg to the inside or outside to prevent their hook from establishing.
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): If they are slow to initiate the roll, immediately shrimp your hips away to create distance and break their base before they can secure the entanglement.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to Berimbolo?

    Your knee pain likely stems from improper leg mechanics during the roll. If you're not swinging your leg *under* and *around* your opponent's leg, but rather pushing against it, you're creating shear forces on your knee joint. Ensure your rolling leg's shin acts as a hook, and your hips are rotating to facilitate the movement, not forcing the knee directly.

    Q: How can I Berimbolo against a much larger and heavier opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paramount. Focus on the hip heist to create a significant forward momentum. Use your sleeve grips to pull their weight forward and over your hip as you initiate the roll. The key is to make them feel like they are falling forward, allowing your body to roll with their momentum rather than trying to lift them.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Berimbolo from closed guard?

    The optimal time is when your opponent attempts to posture up or create space. As they push their hips away from yours to gain leverage, this is your cue to initiate the hip heist. If they are completely slumped over your legs, you lack the necessary leverage to generate the forward momentum required for a successful Berimbolo.

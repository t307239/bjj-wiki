The Berimbolo is a dynamic guard recovery and sweep that transitions from a closed guard or open guard position to gain back control.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of weight transfer and hip rotation, leading to predictable and easily countered movements.

The core mechanical insight is using your hip rotation to 'swing' your legs through, creating a lever to unbalance your opponent and secure a deep hook.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting Position: Opponent is in your closed guard, hips close. Grip opponent's right sleeve with your left hand (standard), and their left collar with your right hand (standard).
    2. Initiate Hip Escape: Execute a sharp hip escape to your left, creating space between your hips and theirs. Your right hip moves towards the mat.
    3. Leg Swing Initiation: As your hips escape, simultaneously swing your right leg (the one on the outside) over opponent's right hip. Your knee should point slightly towards the ceiling.
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your left hip, which is now your base. Your right leg swings like a pendulum, its momentum carrying it towards opponent's back.
    5. Hook Placement: As your right leg swings around, aim to place your right foot on opponent's left hip crease or flank. Your left leg remains controlling opponent's right leg from the inside.
    6. Core Engagement & Rotation: Engage your core and rotate your torso to your left, pulling opponent's collar grip to bring them closer and off-balance.
    7. Securing the Back: As opponent falls forward from the unbalancing, continue the hip rotation and bring your left leg across to secure the opponent's left hip/waist, completing the 'berimbolo' motion and establishing back mount.
    8. Final Grip: Once in back mount, secure your opponent's torso with your legs (body triangle) and transition to a rear-naked choke or other submission.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Hyperextension: Attempting to 'kick' the leg over the opponent's hip with a straight leg and insufficient hip rotation can cause hyperextension of the knee joint, potentially tearing the ACL or PCL. The correct movement involves a bent knee and a sweeping motion driven by hip rotation.
    • Ankle Sprain/Twist: Rushing the leg swing and failing to establish a stable hook on the hip can result in your foot getting caught or twisted awkwardly as the opponent shifts, leading to an ankle sprain. Ensure your foot is placed firmly on the hip crease or flank.
    • Lower Back Strain: Trying to force the rotation with upper body strength alone, without proper hip dissociation and weight transfer, can strain the lumbar spine. Focus on generating power from your hips and core, allowing your torso to follow.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Escape and Leg Swing (0% Resistance): Practice the hip escape and the initial leg swing motion without a partner. Focus on the feeling of hip rotation. 50 reps per side.
    2. Partnered Leg Swing Placement (25% Resistance): With a compliant partner, practice swinging your leg over their hip and placing your foot on their flank. Partner offers minimal resistance. 30 reps per leg.
    3. Controlled Berimbolo Entry (50% Resistance): Execute the hip escape and leg swing, aiming for the hook placement. Partner gently resists, allowing you to feel the unbalancing. 20 reps per side.
    4. Full Berimbolo Transition (75% Resistance): Perform the entire Berimbolo sequence, aiming to secure the back mount. Partner offers moderate resistance, allowing for adjustments. 15 reps per side.
    5. Live Rolling - Berimbolo Attempts (90% Resistance): Attempt the Berimbolo during live rolling, focusing on the mechanics and timing. Do not force if the opportunity isn't there. 5 minutes of rolling, focusing on the attempt.
    6. Live Rolling - Berimbolo Completion (100% Resistance): Execute the Berimbolo with full intention in live rolling. Aim to finish the technique by securing the back mount. 10 minutes of rolling, with the goal of executing the Berimbolo.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent attempts to pass your closed guard by stacking or pressuring forward.
    • When an opponent tries to create significant distance from your open guard, leaving their hips vulnerable.
    • When you have established a strong collar and sleeve grip and can control opponent's posture.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stacking Defense: As the Berimbolo begins, the opponent can perform a powerful stack pass. They should drive their weight directly onto your hips and shoulders, forcing your legs to the mat and preventing the swing.
    • Leg Peel: The opponent can use their hands to peel your hooking leg off their hip as you swing through. This requires immediate pressure and a strong grip on your leg.
    • Hip Escape Counter: If you anticipate the Berimbolo, the opponent can perform a sharp hip escape in the opposite direction, creating space and breaking the connection needed for the sweep.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to Berimbolo?

    Your knee is likely hurting because you are trying to 'kick' your leg over your opponent's hip with a straight leg and insufficient hip rotation. This puts excessive strain on the knee joint. Instead, focus on a bent knee and a swinging motion driven by your hip's dissociation and rotation, like a pendulum.

    Q: How do I Berimbolo against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, your hip escape needs to be more explosive to create the necessary space. Focus on a very sharp, angled hip escape to your side. Your leg swing should be a tight, controlled arc, and you must quickly establish the hook on their hip to prevent them from using their weight to crush you.

    Q: The ideal time to initiate a Berimbolo from closed guard is when your opponent is posturing up slightly and attempting to create space or advance their pass, especially if they are leaning forward. If they are completely flattened or have their weight heavily on top of you, it's not the optimal moment for the Berimbolo.

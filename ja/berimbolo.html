Berimbolo BJJ: A White Belt's Biomechanical Breakdown

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

The Berimbolo is a dynamic guard transition originating from open guard, aiming to secure a back-take. It requires precise hip movement and weight transfer to initiate. Many white belts fail due to a lack of proprioception and a tendency to muscle through, leading to inefficient and risky movements. The key mechanical insight is using your hips to 'swing' your legs around your opponent, not pushing or pulling with your arms.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: From open guard (e.g., de la Riva guard), secure a collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip on your opponent's left sleeve with your left hand. Your left foot hooks behind their right knee.
    2. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your right hip, creating a strong base. Your left hip should lift off the mat.
    3. Initiation: With your right leg, step your foot onto your opponent's left hip. Simultaneously, begin to 'swing' your left leg over your opponent's head by driving your hips upwards.
    4. Leg Over: As your left leg swings over, your right leg releases the hip and extends to hook behind your opponent's right leg, creating a 'hook' position.
    5. Hip Rotation: Continue rotating your hips, bringing your left leg to the mat on the other side of your opponent.
    6. Body Positioning: Your chest should be facing your opponent's back as you complete the rotation. Your hips are now underneath your opponent's.
    7. Secure Back Mount: Immediately transition to securing both hooks and establishing a dominant back-mount position, often by grabbing your opponent's hips or a belt grip.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Leg Swing: Swinging your leg out wide and laterally instead of in a tight, upward arc. This can hyperextend the knee joint of the swinging leg, risking ACL or MCL tears.
    • Jerking with Arms: Using your arms to pull your opponent's upper body in an attempt to generate momentum. This can lead to shoulder dislocations or rotator cuff tears if your opponent resists with their posture.
    • Landing Flat on Back: Failing to rotate your hips and body completely, resulting in landing flat on your back with your opponent on top. This exposes your spine to potential compression injuries or allows them to easily pass your guard.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Swings (0%): Practice the hip elevation and rotation movement without any partner. Focus on lifting your hips and driving them forward. 20 reps.
    2. Solo Leg Swing and Hook (0%): From a seated position, practice swinging one leg over an imaginary opponent's head and then hooking the opposite leg. 20 reps per side.
    3. Partnered Positional Drilling (25%): With a compliant partner lying on their back, practice the initial grips and weight transfer. Have them gently resist your hip movement. 5 reps per side.
    4. Partnered Berimbolo Initiation (50%): Practice the full Berimbolo sequence with a partner who understands the drill and provides minimal resistance, focusing on smooth transitions. 5 reps per side.
    5. Controlled Berimbolo with Resistance (75%): Drill with a partner who offers light resistance to your leg over and hip rotation. Focus on maintaining control and completing the back take. 3 reps per side.
    6. Live Rolling Application (90-100%): Attempt the Berimbolo in sparring situations, understanding that it may not always be successful. Focus on the execution rather than immediate success. 5 attempts per round.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent extends their leg away from you, creating space to hook their knee.
    • When your opponent is postured up, making it difficult for them to base out effectively.
    • When you have established a strong de la Riva or reverse de la Riva guard with good control of their sleeve and collar.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Standing Up and Posturing: If you feel the Berimbolo initiation, immediately stand up straight and pull your hips away, creating distance and preventing the leg over.
    • Stacking: As your opponent attempts to swing their leg over, drive your weight forward and down onto their hips, effectively 'stacking' them and collapsing the Berimbolo attempt.
    • Turning In: If they get their leg over, immediately turn your hips into their leg and try to face them, preventing them from establishing hooks and the back mount.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to Berimbolo?

    Knee pain during the Berimbolo is often caused by a lack of controlled hip rotation and excessive lateral force on the knee. Instead of swinging your leg outwards, focus on driving your hips up and over, keeping your knee bent and tucked. Ensure your hooking leg maintains a stable base, and avoid letting your knee buckle outwards.

    Q: How can I Berimbolo against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is key. Focus on using your hips to create space and 'swing' your legs around them, rather than trying to pull them. Secure a strong collar grip to help control their posture and guide their movement. Your weight transfer needs to be explosive but controlled, using your body's momentum to initiate the rotation.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Berimbolo in BJJ?

    The Berimbolo is best attempted when your opponent is extending a leg away from you, creating an opening for your hook. It's also effective when they are postured high, as this makes it harder for them to base out and defend. Avoid attempting it when they are already low and have a strong base, as this will make the transition much more difficult and risky.

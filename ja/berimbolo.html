The Berimbolo is a dynamic sweeping and back-taking technique initiated from open guard, typically De La Riva or Spider Guard. It aims to invert your body and secure a dominant back position.

White belts often struggle because they prioritize speed over precise body mechanics, leading to uncontrolled movements and wasted energy.

The core principle is using your hips to create space and leverage, rotating your body to pass your leg through and establish control.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From De La Riva guard, grip your opponent's left ankle with your right hand (standard leg). Your left hand grips their left pant leg at the knee.
    2. Hook your right foot around their left shin, applying pressure with the sole of your foot.
    3. Shift your weight onto your left hip, creating a slight angle away from your opponent.
    4. Use your right hand on their ankle to "pull" their leg towards your body, initiating a slight pull.
    5. Simultaneously, drive your hips forward and to your left, beginning your inversion.
    6. As your hips clear your opponent's leg, swing your left leg over their back, aiming to secure it.
    7. Rotate your torso, keeping your hips low, and establish a seatbelt grip on their back.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Injury (ACL/MCL Tears): Attempting to forcefully "kick" your leg over the opponent's body without proper hip rotation and weight transfer. This creates a valgus stress on the knee joint.
    • Correct Action: Focus on hip drive and torso rotation to guide the leg, not brute force.
    • Ankle Sprains: Gripping the opponent's ankle too tightly with a bent wrist and attempting to "pull" their leg with wrist strength alone. This hyperextends the wrist and strains the ankle.
    • Correct Action: Grip the ankle with your forearm and use your body's momentum to move their leg.
    • Lower Back Strain: Arching your back excessively during the inversion or failing to keep your core engaged, leading to hyperextension of the lumbar spine.
    • Correct Action: Maintain a neutral spine and engage your abdominal muscles throughout the movement.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Rotation Drill: Practice the hip rotation and body angling motion without a partner. Focus on smooth weight transfer. (50 reps)
    2. Solo Leg Swing Drill: Practice swinging your leg over your own hip as if it were an opponent's back, maintaining core engagement. (50 reps per side)
    3. Partnered Gripping and Hip Shift: With a compliant partner, practice the initial grips and hip shift. Focus on the feel of their weight distribution. (0% resistance, 10 reps)
    4. Partnered Inversion Drill: With a compliant partner, execute the full Berimbolo motion, focusing on the leg over and hip rotation. Stop before securing the back. (25% resistance, 5 reps)
    5. Controlled Berimbolo Execution: Execute the Berimbolo against a partner who offers light resistance, focusing on completing the sweep and securing a dominant position. (50% resistance, 3 reps)
    6. Live Rolling Berimbolo Attempts: Attempt the Berimbolo during live rolling, prioritizing correct mechanics over success. (75-90% resistance, as opportunities arise)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up from De La Riva or Spider Guard, creating an opening.
    • When your opponent extends their leg to pass your guard, offering a clear attachment point.
    • When you have established strong grips and control over your opponent's legs.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: If the Berimbolo attacker's hips are too high and they are not properly anchored, the defender can stack their weight forward, collapsing the Berimbolo attempt and potentially achieving a pass.
    • Leg Pummeling: The defender can actively pummel their free leg to the outside of the attacker's hooking leg, breaking the entanglement and regaining guard.
    • Hip Escape and Base: The defender can quickly hip escape to their right (if the attacker is going left) and establish a strong base, preventing the attacker from completing the inversion.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to Berimbolo and grip their ankle?

    Wrist pain during the Berimbolo grip often stems from using only wrist flexion to pull the opponent's ankle. This puts excessive strain on the carpal bones and ligaments. Instead, grip the ankle with your forearm and use your entire body's momentum, specifically your hips and torso rotation, to create the pulling action. Keep your wrist in a neutral or slightly extended position.

    Q: How can I Berimbolo against a much bigger and heavier opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, precise hip mechanics and timing are even more crucial. Focus on generating explosive hip drive to create the initial space, rather than relying on pure grip strength. Use your body weight to your advantage by keeping your hips low and your core tight during the inversion. The goal is to get your leg *under* their weight and use their mass against them as you rotate.

    Q: When should I stop trying to Berimbolo and just reset my guard?

    You should stop and reset if you feel your opponent's weight stack directly onto your hips without any hip angle, or if you lose control of their ankle and can't complete the leg over. Also, if your opponent has a strong grip on your leg or body that prevents your hip from moving, it's better to disengage and re-establish a safer guard position than to force a compromised Berimbolo attempt and risk injury.

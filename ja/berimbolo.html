Berimbolo BJJ: The Complete Beginner's Guide

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced

The Berimbolo is a dynamic sweep from open guard, aiming to transition into a dominant back-mount position. White belts often struggle due to a lack of foundational body mechanics and an eagerness to rush the movement.

The core mechanical insight is using your hips and core to create rotational momentum, not brute strength, to unbalance your opponent and secure the back.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Closed Guard (or Open Guard variation): Establish a strong grip on the opponent's right sleeve with your left hand (palm facing up, fingers gripping the fabric) and their right pants leg (around the ankle/shin) with your right hand.
    2. Hip Angle: Lower your hips by bending your knees, bringing them closer to your chest. Angle your pelvis slightly to your left.
    3. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your left hip, creating a base. Your right leg will extend slightly.
    4. Grip Release & Hook: Release the pants grip. Immediately swing your right leg over the opponent's hips, aiming to hook their left leg (around the ankle or calf).
    5. Body Rotation: Simultaneously, drive your left hip into the opponent's right hip and begin to rotate your torso to your left, pulling their right sleeve with your left hand.
    6. Leg Drive: Extend your left leg to push off the mat, assisting the rotation and lifting the opponent's right leg slightly.
    7. Back Take: As the opponent falls to their right, continue the rotation and bring your right leg (the hook) up to secure their waist or hips, transitioning to back control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Hyperextension: Swinging the leg too wide and fast without proper hip control can cause the knee to hyperextend, leading to ACL or MCL tears. The correct movement involves controlled rotation and hooking the leg *after* initiating hip movement.
    • Ankle/Foot Sprain: Attempting to hook the opponent's leg with an inflexible ankle or by 'stomping' the foot can result in ankle sprains or fractures. The hook should be a sweeping motion, with the foot remaining relatively relaxed and the ankle mobile.
    • Lower Back Strain: Trying to 'pull' the opponent over with upper body strength, rather than using hip rotation, puts excessive torque on the lower back, leading to muscle strains or disc issues. The power comes from the core and hip drive, not the arms.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Rotation Drill: Practice the hip angle and rotation movement without a partner, focusing on the feeling of creating momentum. (50 reps)
    2. Solo Leg Hook Drill: Practice swinging your leg over and hooking an imaginary opponent's leg. (50 reps)
    3. Partnered Grip and Hip Drill (0% Resistance): With a compliant partner, practice establishing grips and performing the initial hip angle and rotation. Partner offers no resistance. (10 reps per side)
    4. Partnered Sweep Drill (25% Resistance): Partner offers slight resistance to your hip movement. Focus on completing the sweep motion. (10 reps per side)
    5. Partnered Berimbolo to Back Mount (50% Resistance): Focus on transitioning smoothly from the sweep to securing back mount. Partner defends minimally. (10 reps per side)
    6. Live Rolling (75-90% Resistance): Attempt the Berimbolo in rolling situations, focusing on timing and execution against a resisting opponent. (5-10 attempts per roll)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured up in your closed guard, creating space between your hips and theirs.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard by stepping their legs wide, creating an opening.
    • When the opponent is leaning heavily on one side, off-balancing themselves.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Pummeling: If the opponent initiates a Berimbolo, immediately bring your free leg (the one not being hooked) to the inside of their hooking leg, creating a frame with your shin and pushing their leg away. This prevents the hook from securing.
    • Hip Escape & Base: As the Berimbolo attempt begins, immediately hip escape to your right (if they are Berimboloing to your left) and establish a strong base by planting your foot and driving your hips down. This disrupts their rotational momentum.
    • Grip Break & Posture Up: Focus on breaking the sleeve grip as early as possible. Simultaneously, drive your knees down and try to regain a more upright posture, making it harder for them to generate leverage.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Berimbolo?

    Wrist pain often stems from a weak or incorrect sleeve grip. Instead of gripping the fabric of the sleeve with your fingers, you might be gripping the opponent's wrist or forearm. Ensure your grip is on the fabric, with your palm facing up and fingers wrapped around the sleeve, and your wrist in a neutral or slightly extended position, not bent backward.

    Q: How can I do the Berimbolo against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paramount. Focus on using their weight against them by initiating your hip rotation *before* they can fully stabilize. Your core strength and the speed of your hip movement are more critical than brute force. Ensure your hook is tight and you transition to back control quickly to avoid being stacked.

    Q: When is the best time to start the Berimbolo from open guard?

    The Berimbolo is most effective when the opponent's weight is distributed unevenly, or when they create space by posturing up or stepping their legs wide. If the opponent is tightly controlled and their weight is centered, attempting a Berimbolo can be easily countered. Look for moments of instability.

The Berimbolo is a dynamic sweep and transition originating from open guard, aiming to secure a back-mount position. It requires precise timing and body control to avoid giving up dominant positions.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of fundamental body mechanics and a tendency to force movements rather than flow. This leads to predictable patterns and vulnerability.

The core insight is understanding hip dissociation and weight transfer; you're not just reaching, but actively repositioning your center of gravity to create angles and momentum.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Establish a strong 'spider hook' with your left foot on the opponent's right hip crease. Your right foot hooks behind the opponent's left knee pit, creating a frame.
    2. Grip: Grab the opponent's right sleeve with your left hand, and their left pants (near the knee) with your right hand. Maintain a consistent distance using your legs.
    3. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your right hip, initiating a slight hip escape to your left, creating a small space under your hips.
    4. Rotation: As you hip escape, simultaneously pull the opponent's right sleeve towards you and push their left knee away with your right foot. This breaks their posture.
    5. Hook Placement: While rotating your torso counter-clockwise, bring your left leg (initially on the hip) across the opponent's back, aiming to secure a body triangle or hook.
    6. Hip Elevation: Drive your hips upwards and forward, using your right leg's push and your left arm's pull to propel yourself forward and around.
    7. Transition: Continue the rotation, aiming to end up on your opponent's back with your legs securing a controlling position, often a body triangle or a strong leg entanglement.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Premature Hip Rotation: Attempting to rotate hips before establishing secure hooks or breaking posture can lead to the opponent stacking you. This can hyperextend the lumbar spine or cause knee valgus if the opponent falls incorrectly.
    • Over-Reaching with Arms: Grabbing the sleeve and pulling without accompanying hip movement or leg frame can dislocate the shoulder or tear the rotator cuff. Maintain a strong leg frame and use your hips to create leverage.
    • Losing Leg Connection: Releasing the hook on the knee pit or hip too early to 'reach' for the back allows the opponent to easily pass your guard or establish a dominant top position. Keep your feet connected and use your legs to control distance and angle.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Movement Drill: Practice the hip escape and rotation motion without any opponent. Focus on feeling the weight shift and core engagement. (50 reps, 0% resistance).
    2. Shadow Berimbolo: Mimic the technique against an imaginary opponent, focusing on grip placement and the sequence of leg and hip movements. (25 reps per side, 0% resistance).
    3. Partner Assisted Frame Drill: Your partner stands, providing minimal resistance. You practice establishing grips and executing the initial hip escape and pull, focusing on creating a small space. (15 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Controlled Sweep Drill: Your partner lies on their back, allowing you to secure grips and execute the full Berimbolo sweep motion, aiming to land in side control or mount. Partner offers slight resistance to maintain posture. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Live Guard Retention Drill: Start from closed guard. Your partner attempts to pass. You practice transitioning to an open guard setup for Berimbolo when appropriate, but are allowed to revert to closed guard or defend the pass. (5 rounds, 2 minutes each, 75% resistance).
    6. Live Berimbolo Attempt Drill: Start from open guard. You actively look for Berimbolo opportunities. Your partner defends the sweep and transition, but the goal is to complete the Berimbolo to back control or transition to a dominant position. (5 rounds, 2 minutes each, 90% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up from your closed guard, creating a space to enter open guard.
    • When your opponent is attempting a knee-cut pass and you have established a spider hook or de la riva hook.
    • When you have successfully isolated one of your opponent's legs in an open guard scenario (e.g., de la riva, reverse de la riva).
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The Stack: If your opponent attempts to stack you *before* you've fully rotated, maintain your leg frame and drive your hips into their chest, then pivot to escape to your feet or secure a sweep. Avoid letting your hips get trapped underneath them.
    • The Knee-on-Belly Transition: If your opponent successfully defends the rotation and you end up in a compromised position, immediately transition to securing knee-on-belly by driving your knee into their torso and establishing a strong cross-face grip. This salvages a dominant position.
    • The Stand-Up Defense: If your opponent anticipates the Berimbolo and tries to stand up, use your sleeve grip to pull them back down or immediately transition to a single leg takedown attempt by securing their leg and driving forward.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Berimbolo?

    Knee pain during the Berimbolo often stems from improper leg mechanics. If you're rotating your hips without maintaining a strong connection with your hooked foot behind the opponent's knee pit, your knee can collapse inwards (valgus stress). Ensure your hook is deep and actively used to control their leg's position, and that your hip rotation is initiated by your core and hip flexors, not just by forcing the leg through.

    Q: How can I do the Berimbolo against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing become paramount. Focus on getting your hooks *underneath* their weight and using your hips to create angles, rather than trying to overpower them. Prioritize breaking their posture with your grips and leg frame, then use your own body weight to 'fall' into the rotation, making their size work against them by pulling them off balance. Precise hip dissociation is key.

    Q: When should I transition from Berimbolo to taking the back?

    The transition to taking the back occurs when your left leg has successfully swung across the opponent's back and you have achieved a high hip position relative to theirs. At this point, your body should be rotating to face their back. If your leg is only at their waist or hip, you haven't completed the Berimbolo rotation and should focus on completing that movement before attempting to secure the back mount. You are looking for the moment your hips are higher than theirs and you can wrap your legs around their waist.

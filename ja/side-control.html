BJJ Side Control: The Essential Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Side control is a dominant grappling position where you control your opponent from their side, aiming to immobilize and set up submissions.

White belts often struggle by giving too much space, allowing their opponent to escape or create scrambles due to poor weight distribution.

The key is to establish a strong, tight connection with your opponent's body, controlling their hips and shoulders with your own structure.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a guard pass (e.g., knee-slice-pass), establish cross-collar grip with your right hand (palm facing up) and their left hip/waist grip with your left hand.
    2. Place your chest directly on their chest, creating a tight connection.
    3. Your right knee should be positioned on the mat, slightly forward of their hip, forming a base.
    4. Your left leg should be extended back, with your left foot flat on the mat for balance and weight distribution.
    5. Drive your weight down through your chest and hips, aiming your center of gravity over their torso.
    6. Keep your hips low and tight, preventing them from bridging or creating space.
    7. Maintain a wide base with your knees and feet to remain stable and prevent them from sweeping or escaping.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly posting your hand on the opponent's head: This can lead to neck hyperextension or whiplash if the opponent moves suddenly. The correct action is to grip their collar or shoulder.
    • Allowing your hips to rise too high: This creates a gap between your chest and their torso, enabling them to shrimp and escape. Keep your hips low and drive them into the opponent.
    • Extending your arms to push their head away: This can strain your shoulder or elbow and offers no control. Instead, use your chest and hips to maintain connection.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice transitioning from a pass to side control on an unresisting dummy or air (50 reps).
    2. Static partner drill: Partner lies on their back, you practice entering side control and holding for 10 seconds (20 reps per side).
    3. Light resistance drill: Partner actively resists movement but does not actively escape (25% resistance, hold for 20 seconds) (15 reps per side).
    4. Controlled movement drill: Partner attempts to escape, you maintain side control, focusing on weight distribution (50% resistance, 30 seconds) (10 reps per side).
    5. Live rolling (positional sparring): Start from side control, try to maintain for 1 minute (75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Full rolling with focus on maintaining and advancing from side control (100% resistance, 3 minutes).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • After successfully passing an opponent's guard.
    • When an opponent attempts a guard recovery and you can secure a dominant side position.
    • During scrambles where you gain top control from an advantageous angle.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Shrimp (Hip Escape): When the opponent's hips are too close, drive your hips into them, then shrimp your hips away to create space and recover guard.
    • Bridge: If the opponent's weight is too heavy and off-balance, generate upward force from your legs and hips to lift them and create an opportunity to escape.
    • Framing and Elbow Escape: Use your forearms to create frames against the opponent's hips or shoulders, then use your elbow to drive into the mat and shrimp your hips away.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to hold side control?

    This often happens when you're overextending your arm to create a frame or push your opponent's head away. Instead, keep your elbow tucked and use your chest and hips to maintain pressure. Focus on driving your weight downwards and forwards, keeping your shoulder in a stable, neutral position.

    Q: How can I maintain side control against a bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on tightening your connection and controlling their hips. Use your weight to drive their hips down, preventing them from using their size to bridge or shrimp. Secure a deep cross-collar grip and use your opposite arm to control their hip or far arm.

    Q: When should I switch from side control to another position?

    You should look to transition if your opponent successfully defends your submission attempts and starts to create space, or if they initiate a strong escape sequence. If they are actively defending your primary attack, consider transitioning to mount or north-south to maintain pressure and look for new opportunities.

Side Control: The Foundation of BJJ Dominance for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Side control is a dominant positional grappling hold in BJJ, achieved after a successful guard pass. It allows for control and the setup of submissions. White belts often struggle with maintaining this position due to a lack of understanding of weight distribution and framing.

The key to effective side control lies in creating a stable base and applying consistent, directed pressure, preventing your opponent from creating space or escaping.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a successful guard pass, establish a cross-body position with your chest against your opponent's side, aligning your hips with their hips.
    2. Place your left knee (if attacking from the right side) on the mat, angled slightly towards their head, creating a base.
    3. Your right knee should be bent, foot flat on the mat, providing a second point of contact for stability.
    4. Your left arm (underhook arm) penetrates deep to control their far hip, with your forearm pressing into their hip bone.
    5. Your right arm (crossface arm) secures a grip on their collar, just below the lapel, or on the side of their head, with your forearm applying pressure to their jawline.
    6. Distribute your weight by driving your chest into their chest and your hips down towards the mat, creating a "smash" pressure.
    7. Keep your base wide enough to prevent them from bridging effectively, with your knees and feet acting as anchors.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying excessive upward force with your arms to "lift" the opponent: This strains the rotator cuff and can lead to tears by forcing the humerus into the acromion.
    • Allowing your hips to lift off the mat: This creates a "hollow" space, allowing the opponent to bridge and escape, potentially hyperextending your opponent's spine if you maintain pressure.
    • Standing up straight to "hold" them: This removes your base and makes you vulnerable to sweeps, and can strain your lower back if the opponent aggressively pushes back.
    • Gripping too tightly on the collar without chest pressure: This allows the opponent to create space by "shrimping" away, leading to knee-on-belly or guard recovery.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice transitioning from a guard pass to side control, focusing on hip alignment and knee placement. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Opponent lies passively. Execute the transition and hold side control for 15 seconds, focusing on weight distribution. (5 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Opponent offers minimal resistance to the initial pass. Execute the transition and hold side control for 20 seconds. (5 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled positional sparring: Start in side control. Opponent attempts to escape for 30 seconds. Focus on maintaining control and pressure. (5 rounds, 50% resistance)
    5. Controlled positional sparring: Start from a neutral position. Focus on achieving side control after a pass and holding for 45 seconds. (5 rounds, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Focus on implementing side control after a successful pass and maintaining it for as long as possible. (5 minutes, 100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • After successfully passing your opponent's guard.
    • When your opponent attempts to recover guard from top position.
    • When you have established a dominant top position and can isolate a limb or the head.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Elbow Escape (Shrimping): When the opponent's hips are too close and their crossface is weak, create space by driving your hips away, tucking your knee, and shrimping out.
    • Bridge and Roll: If the opponent's base is too narrow and their pressure is not centered, bridge your hips up and roll them over, aiming to end up in guard or mount.
    • Guard Recovery: If the opponent's crossface is too loose and they are not pinning your hips, actively push their head away and bring your knees to your chest to re-establish guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my opponent keep escaping side control when I feel like I'm holding them?

    You are likely not applying pressure correctly. Instead of just "holding," focus on distributing your weight biomechanically. Your chest should be pressed into their chest, and your hips should be driven down towards the mat, creating a "smash" effect. Ensure your underhook arm's forearm is pressing into their hip bone and your crossface arm is pressuring their jawline or head, preventing them from creating space to shrimp.

    Q: How do I maintain side control against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, precise weight distribution and leverage are crucial. Use your hips and chest to create a "smash" pressure, making it difficult for them to move. Your underhook should be deep, controlling their hip, and your crossface should prevent their head from turning towards you. Think of using your entire body as a weight, not just your arms. Avoid trying to match their strength; instead, use angles and pressure.

    Q: Should I always try to submit from side control, or is it okay to just hold it?

    For white belts, the primary goal from side control is to maintain the position and learn to control your opponent. Submissions can be attempted, but it's more important to develop a solid understanding of the positional mechanics. Focusing on maintaining control and understanding how to apply pressure will naturally lead to submission opportunities as you progress. Don't rush submissions; prioritize solid control.

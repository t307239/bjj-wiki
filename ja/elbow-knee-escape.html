The Elbow-Knee Escape is a fundamental BJJ technique used to escape disadvantageous positions like side control or mount. It aims to create space and establish a better defensive or offensive posture.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body awareness, leading to stiff movements and increased injury risk. They tend to push or pull with brute force instead of using leverage.

The key mechanical insight is to use your elbow and knee as a fulcrum, bridging your hips to create a controlled explosion of space, allowing you to re-establish guard or return to your knees.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side control, establish a strong "frame" with your forearm against the opponent's neck or shoulder and your other hand on their hip (or bicep if hip is inaccessible).
    2. Drive your hips down and slightly back, creating a small gap.
    3. Place your elbow on the mat directly underneath your shoulder, creating a pivot point.
    4. Simultaneously, bring your knee towards your chest, aiming to place the knee cap on the mat in front of your elbow.
    5. This "elbow-knee post" should form a stable base.
    6. Bridge your hips upwards and backwards, pushing off the mat with your elbow-knee base.
    7. As your hips rise, drive your elbow forward and your knee backward to create maximum space, bringing your legs back to a closed guard or returning to your knees.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to "push" the opponent off with your hands alone: This can lead to shoulder impingement or rotator cuff tears if the opponent is significantly heavier or stronger, as your joint is not designed for static pushing against immense force.
    • Arching your back excessively to create space: This puts immense strain on the lumbar spine, potentially causing disc herniation or muscle strains, as the spine is not meant to be the primary lever.
    • Dropping your knee directly onto the mat without proper elbow support: This can lead to knee hyperextension injuries or patellar tendonitis if you land awkwardly, as the knee joint is vulnerable to direct impact and twisting forces.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip bridge and elbow-knee post motion without a partner. Focus on the feeling of creating space. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Have a partner lie passively in side control. Execute the elbow-knee escape, focusing on precise mechanics. (20 reps per side)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to create space and recover guard. (20 reps per side)
    4. Partner drilling (moderate resistance): Partner attempts to maintain control but allows you to work the escape. Focus on timing the bridge with space creation. (15 reps per side)
    5. Drilling with positional sparring: Start from side control and allow the partner to attempt passes. Execute the escape when the opportunity arises. (5 rounds, 1-minute rolls)
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the elbow-knee escape into your regular rolling sessions. Aim to escape from disadvantageous positions. (5 minutes, 100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent has established a strong side control and is beginning to flatten you out.
    • When you feel pressure from the mount and are losing your posture.
    • When the opponent is transitioning between positions and their weight distribution is momentarily unstable.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass Defense: If the opponent stacks you during your escape attempt, immediately tuck your chin and drive your hips into their chest, using your legs to push their hips away, preventing them from crushing you.
    • Knee-on-Belly Transition: If the opponent attempts to shift to knee-on-belly as you escape, quickly bring your elbow and knee together to create a tight frame, then use your legs to push their knee off your hip and re-establish guard.
    • Armbar Attempt: If the opponent tries to armbar you as you escape, keep your elbow tight to your body and use your free hand to defend your head, then bridge and shrimp to create space and break the hold, returning to guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my elbow hurt when I try the Elbow-Knee Escape in BJJ?

    Elbow pain often stems from the olecranon (the bony tip of the elbow) being directly pressed into a hard mat without adequate padding or support. Ensure you are creating a solid "post" with your entire forearm and elbow, distributing the pressure evenly. If the pain persists, consider using a mat with more cushioning or practicing on a softer surface.

    Q: How can I effectively use the Elbow-Knee Escape against a much larger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, raw strength is your enemy. Focus on timing and leverage. Instead of pushing, create a very small, controlled "pop" with your hips by driving off your elbow and knee simultaneously. Think of it as a spring uncoiling. Use your head to push against their chest or shoulder to create a subtle angle, then explode your hips to create the necessary space.

    Q: I keep getting stuck in the Elbow-Knee Escape and can't get my legs back. What am I doing wrong?

    This usually means you are not creating enough space with your hips. Your "bridge" needs to be more explosive and directed backwards, not just upwards. Ensure your elbow is firmly planted and your knee is driving forward as you bridge. Visualize pushing the opponent's weight over your head, then immediately focus on bringing your knees to your chest to re-establish guard.

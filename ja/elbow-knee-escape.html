BJJ Elbow-Knee Escape: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Elbow-Knee Escape is a fundamental defensive maneuver initiated from a compromised position, typically side-control or mount, aiming to create space and regain guard or a more neutral position.

White belts often fail by muscling the escape, leading to energy expenditure and increased vulnerability to submissions.

The core mechanical principle is creating a stable base with your hips and framing with your arms to "lift" your opponent's weight off you, allowing your knees to create space.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side-control, establish a "frame" by placing your left forearm (if opponent is on your right) across their neck/shoulder and your right hand on their hip bone.
    2. Your hips should be angled slightly towards your opponent, creating a "shrimp" ready position.
    3. Simultaneously, drive your left knee (the one closer to their head) towards your chest while pivoting your hips away.
    4. Your right knee (the one closer to their hips) should extend to create a wedge between your body and theirs, pushing their hip away.
    5. As you shrimp, maintain pressure on their neck/shoulder with your left forearm frame and their hip with your right hand.
    6. Your goal is to create a 90-degree angle between your torso and theirs, with your knees acting as bumpers.
    7. Once space is created, immediately bring your knees together to establish closed guard or push their hips further to transition to open guard.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to "push" the opponent away with straight arms: This can lead to hyperextension of the elbow joint and ligament damage (e.g., UCL tear) if the opponent is significantly stronger or applies pressure.
    • Jamming your knee directly into the opponent's hip bone with excessive force: This can cause patellar tendonitis or meniscal tears by creating shearing forces on the knee joint.
    • Not creating a stable hip base and "flopping" your hips out: This can lead to a strained lower back (lumbar sprain) as your core is not engaged to support the movement.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo: Practice the hip shrimping motion and knee placement without a partner. Focus on creating the 90-degree angle. (50 reps)
    2. Solo: Add the framing hand and hip grip. Practice maintaining the frame while shrimping. (50 reps)
    3. Partner drill (0% resistance): Partner lies passively. Practice initiating the escape and establishing guard. (10 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers slight resistance to your escape attempt. Focus on maintaining frames. (10 reps per side)
    5. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner applies light pressure. Practice shrimping against their weight and establishing guard. (10 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling (75-100% resistance): Implement the Elbow-Knee Escape when in a compromised position. Focus on successful guard recovery.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When trapped in side-control with the opponent's weight heavily on your chest or head.
    • When the opponent is posturing up in your guard, threatening to pass.
    • When you feel your guard is about to be passed and need to reset.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Opponent "stacking" you: Instead of shrimping, drive your hips forward and upward, using your arms to frame their shoulders and push them away, then transition to your knees.
    • Opponent "cutting" the angle: If they move their hips to face you, immediately switch to a "grapevine" with your legs and use your arms to push their head away, creating space to sit up.
    • Opponent "knee-on-belly" setup: If they transition to knee-on-belly, use your free arm to frame their hip and your shrimping motion to create space to bring your guard back in.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to Elbow-Knee Escape from side control?

    Your knee might be hurting because you are jamming it directly into your opponent's hip bone without proper alignment or creating a stable base. The correct biomechanics involve using your knee as a wedge to push their hip away while your hips are angled away from them. Ensure your hips are driving horizontally, not directly into the knee joint's path of resistance.

    Q: How can I Elbow-Knee Escape against a much larger and stronger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, brute force is ineffective and risky. Focus intensely on precise framing with your arms (forearm on neck/shoulder, hand on hip) to create leverage. Use your hips to generate the "shrimp" motion, not your upper body. The key is to create a small amount of space by lifting their weight slightly, then immediately closing the distance to regain guard, rather than trying to push them off entirely.

    Q: I can't seem to get my knees together to establish guard after the Elbow-Knee Escape, what am I doing wrong?

    This usually happens when you create space but don't transition quickly enough. After shrimping and using your knee to create the wedge, your immediate next action should be to bring your knees together. Think of it as "closing the door" behind you. If you hesitate, the opponent will re-establish pressure and prevent guard recovery. Prioritize closing the space with your knees before they can react.

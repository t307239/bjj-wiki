Stand In Base: The Foundation of BJJ Transitions for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Stand In Base is a transitional position, typically used to stabilize your body after a sweep or when transitioning from guard to a more dominant position. It’s crucial for maintaining control and preventing your opponent from recovering guard.

Many white belts struggle because they lack the proprioception to feel their base or they rely on brute strength instead of proper alignment. This leads to being easily swept or losing position.

The core mechanical insight is creating a stable, triangular base with your body, aligning your center of gravity over your support points to resist lateral or forward pressure.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a prone or kneeling position, establish a wide base by placing both knees on the mat, approximately shoulder-width apart.
    2. Distribute your weight evenly over your shins and knees, ensuring your hips are lower than your shoulders.
    3. Place your hands flat on the mat, directly under your shoulders, fingers spread wide for maximum surface area.
    4. Maintain a neutral spine, avoiding excessive arching or rounding of your back.
    5. Engage your core muscles to create a rigid torso, preventing buckling under pressure.
    6. Keep your head up and eyes forward, scanning your opponent's position and movements.
    7. If transitioning from a guard recovery, ensure your hips are actively pushing away from your opponent to create space before establishing your base.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly placing hands too far forward or too close together: This can lead to wrist hyperextension, damaging the radiocarpal joint and associated ligaments.
    • Rounding the lower back while attempting to get up: This puts immense pressure on the lumbar discs, increasing the risk of disc herniation.
    • Trying to 'muscle' up without proper base: This often results in leaning too far forward, making the knees vulnerable to hyperextension injuries if the opponent pushes into them.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo: Practice lowering into and rising from Stand In Base 20 times, focusing on controlled weight transfer and hip alignment. (0% resistance)
    2. Solo: From a prone position, practice establishing Stand In Base and holding it for 10 seconds, focusing on core engagement. Repeat 15 times. (0% resistance)
    3. Partner Drill: Your partner is in a passive guard. You practice establishing Stand In Base and holding it against light pressure for 30 seconds. Switch roles. (25% resistance)
    4. Partner Drill: Your partner attempts a single leg takedown from guard. You practice establishing Stand In Base and resisting the pull. Repeat 5 times each side. (50% resistance)
    5. Partner Drill: Simulate a guard pass attempt (e.g., knee slice). You practice establishing Stand In Base to defend and prevent the pass. Repeat 5 times each side. (75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling: Focus on using Stand In Base during transitions and scrambles, aiming to maintain control. (100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • After successfully sweeping an opponent and needing to establish a stable base before passing.
    • When your guard is being passed and you need to create a solid defensive posture.
    • When transitioning from a submission attempt that fails, to avoid being put in a disadvantageous position.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • The Stack Pass: If the opponent tries to stack you by driving their hips into your shoulders while in Stand In Base, immediately drive your hips down and back, widening your base and using your hands to push their hips away. This re-establishes your center of gravity over your base.
    • The Leg Drag: If the opponent tries to drag your leg across your body, keep your hips active and use your free hand to block their chest or shoulder, preventing them from isolating and controlling your leg. Maintain a tight base.
    • The Knee-Slice Pass: If the opponent attempts to drive their knee across your body, actively use your hands to create a frame against their hip or thigh, pushing them away and maintaining your base width. Keep your hips low and engaged.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to Stand In Base after being swept?

    Wrist pain often occurs because you're relying on your wrists to absorb the impact of your body weight without proper alignment. Ensure your hands are directly under your shoulders, fingers spread, and your weight is distributed across your entire hand and forearm. Actively engage your core and push the mat away with your hands, rather than just letting your weight collapse onto them.

    Q: How do I Stand In Base effectively against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, your base width and hip positioning are paramount. Instead of simply placing your knees down, actively drive your hips back and down, creating a wider, more stable triangular base. Use your hands to push their hips away or create frames against their chest to prevent them from collapsing your base with their weight. Think about creating space and leverage, not just resisting their mass.

    Q: When is the best time to transition out of Stand In Base?

    Stand In Base is a temporary, transitional position. You should aim to transition out as soon as you have established a more dominant position or have a clear path to one. For instance, after a sweep, once you feel your opponent is stabilized, you should be looking to transition to a knee-on-belly or side control. If you remain in Stand In Base too long, you become a static target for guard recovery or counter-passes.

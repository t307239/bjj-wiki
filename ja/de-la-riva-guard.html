De La Riva Guard: A Foundational BJJ Open Guard

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

De La Riva Guard starts from an open guard position, typically when your opponent is standing or in a kneeling pass attempt. Its primary goal is to create off-balancing opportunities and control your opponent's legs for sweeps or submissions.

White belts often struggle because they lack precise body control and frame awareness, leading to uncontrolled movements and increased injury risk.

The key mechanical insight is using your hooking leg and frames to control distance and angles, creating leverage to disrupt your opponent's base.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Opponent is in front of you, either kneeling or standing. Assume a seated open guard.
    2. Grip your opponent's left pants leg with your right hand, just above the ankle. Ensure your palm faces inward, thumb down.
    3. Hook your left foot around the outside of your opponent's right shin, with your ankle flexed and toes pointing towards their knee.
    4. Place your left hand on your opponent's right knee or thigh to create a frame. Your elbow should be slightly bent, and your forearm should press against their leg.
    5. Shift your hips to your left, creating a slight angle away from your opponent. Your right leg remains extended, ready to adjust.
    6. Use your right hand on their pants to pull them slightly forward and to your left, disrupting their balance. Your left arm frame prevents them from collapsing onto you.
    7. As your opponent's weight shifts, use your left foot to gently push their knee away and your right leg to sweep their ankle outward, initiating an off-balancing motion.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending your hooking leg straight: This can hyper-extend your knee and damage the ACL or PCL if your opponent applies pressure or pivots.
    • Correct movement: Maintain a slight bend in your hooking knee and use your foot to control their shin, not to push their entire leg away.
    • Gripping too low on the pants (e.g., the foot): This provides insufficient leverage and can lead to your hand slipping, causing your opponent to pass easily or you to lose control.
    • Correct movement: Grip the pants securely above the ankle, allowing for a strong pulling action.
    • Not creating a strong frame with your non-hooking arm: This allows your opponent to drive forward and collapse your guard, potentially injuring your spine or ribs.
    • Correct movement: Maintain a firm, slightly bent arm frame against their knee or thigh.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Drill: Practice the leg hook and grip placement without a partner. Focus on the hip angle and foot placement. (10 reps each side, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner Drill: With a partner kneeling, practice establishing the De La Riva hook and pant grip. Partner offers no resistance. (10 reps each side, 0% resistance)
    3. Frame and Hook Drill: Partner gently tries to step forward. You practice creating the arm frame and adjusting your hip angle to maintain distance. (10 reps each side, 25% resistance)
    4. Basic Sweep Attempt: Practice the initial sweep motion from De La Riva Guard. Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing the sweep to occur. (10 reps each side, 50% resistance)
    5. Guard Retention Drill: Partner attempts to pass. You focus on maintaining the De La Riva hook and frame, preventing the pass without attempting a sweep. (5 mins, 75% resistance)
    6. Controlled Rolling: Attempt sweeps and transitions from De La Riva Guard against a resisting partner who is actively defending. (3 rounds x 3 mins, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is in your guard and attempts to establish a knee-on-belly or pressure pass.
    • Opponent is standing in front of you, trying to break down your closed guard.
    • Opponent has attempted a single leg takedown and is now in a compromised kneeling position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Trap: Opponent attempts to step over your hooking leg. Immediately bring your free leg up to trap their stepping leg, creating a mutual guard scenario.
    • Base and Pressure: Opponent tries to stack you. Widen your base, push off their hips with your free hand, and drive your hips up to break their base.
    • Switch to Half Guard: If your opponent successfully penetrates your guard and you lose the hook, immediately transition to a half guard by getting your knee inside their thigh.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I do De La Riva Guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs from gripping too tightly with an extended or flexed wrist. Ensure your wrist is neutral and your grip is firm but not strained. Imagine holding a delicate bird – you need a secure grip, but you don't want to crush it. Also, avoid letting your opponent pull directly on your hand; use your body to control the direction of their movement.

    Q: How can a white belt use De La Riva Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on using your De La Riva hook to control their base and off-balance them. Don't try to lift them; instead, use precise hip movements and leg pushes to create angles and disrupt their weight distribution. Your arm frame is crucial for preventing them from driving forward and using their weight against you.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from De La Riva Guard to another guard?

    Switching guards is about adapting to your opponent's reaction. If your opponent effectively clears your hook or establishes a strong pressure pass, don't force the De La Riva. Immediately look to transition to a more defensive or advantageous position like half guard or butterfly guard. The key is to maintain a strong base and frame at all times.

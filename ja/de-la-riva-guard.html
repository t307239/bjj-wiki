De La Riva Guard: The Foundation for White Belts in BJJ

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

De La Riva Guard is an open guard position initiated from your back, aiming to control your opponent's leg and create sweeping or submission opportunities.

White belts often struggle by over-extending their base or losing hip connection, making them vulnerable to passes.

The core mechanic is maintaining a tight frame with your knee and shin while actively controlling the opponent's ankle or shin to disrupt their balance.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From your back, establish a closed guard initially. Opponent is in your guard.
    2. Release one leg, bringing your knee towards your chest. Your opposite leg remains in closed guard, hooking their hip.
    3. With your free foot, step your heel towards your opponent's ankle, aiming to hook just above it. This is the 'De La Riva hook'.
    4. Simultaneously, extend your hooking leg slightly, creating a frame with your shin and knee against their shin/calf. Your foot should be flexed.
    5. Use your other hand to grab their ankle or the cuff of their pants on the same side as your hook. Maintain a strong grip.
    6. Your opposite leg's foot (the one not hooking) should be planted on their hip, acting as a secondary frame and base.
    7. Maintain a slight lean back, keeping your hips engaged and close to your opponent's base to prevent them from posturing up or passing easily.
    8. Your non-hooking arm can reach for their opposite collar or sleeve to control their posture and upper body.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Hyperextension: Reaching too far with the hooking leg, causing the knee to bend backward beyond its natural range of motion, can damage the ACL or PCL.
    • Correct Alternative: Maintain a slight bend in the hooking knee, using your shin and ankle to create the frame, not just the foot.
    • Hip Disconnection: Allowing your hips to drift away from the opponent while hooking, creating a large space, makes you susceptible to a stack pass where they drive forward.
    • Correct Alternative: Actively drive your hips into your opponent, maintaining a tight connection to their base even as you extend your hooking leg.
    • Ankle Sprain/Twist: Over-rotating your ankle or foot when hooking, especially if the opponent resists by stepping heavily, can lead to ligament tears.
    • Correct Alternative: Keep your hooking foot slightly flexed and your ankle stable, focusing the pressure on your shin and knee frame.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hooking Practice (0%): Practice stepping your hooking foot to various points on your own leg (ankle, shin) and holding the flexed foot position for 10 seconds. Repeat 10 times per leg.
    2. Solo Frame and Hook (0%): On your back, practice the De La Riva hook and simultaneously placing your other foot on your hip and holding a collar grip. Hold for 10 seconds. Repeat 10 times.
    3. Partner Static Hook (25%): Partner lies on their back. You enter De La Riva from your back. Partner lightly posts their foot. Focus on securing the hook and frame without any pressure. 5 reps per side.
    4. Partner Dynamic Hook (50%): Partner lightly walks their foot forward and backward. You maintain the De La Riva hook and frame, adjusting your hip angle. 5 reps per side.
    5. Entry and Control (75%): Partner attempts a basic guard pass (e.g., knee-slice). You enter De La Riva and focus on maintaining the hook and frame, preventing the pass. Partner applies light pressure. 3 rounds, 2 minutes each.
    6. Live Rolling with Focus (90%): Roll with a partner, specifically attempting to enter and maintain De La Riva Guard for sweeps or transitions. Partner actively tries to pass. Focus on defensive stability first. 5-minute rounds.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up, creating space to step your hook in.
    • When your opponent is attempting to pass your guard and is reaching for your legs.
    • When you are on your back and have established a stable base, ready to transition from closed or half guard.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee Slice Pass: Opponent drives their knee across your body. Maintain your hook, post your opposite foot on their hip, and shrimp your hips away to create space and re-guard.
    • Leg Drag: Opponent attempts to pull your hooking leg across their body. Grip their ankle tightly, use your non-hooking foot on their hip to push, and pull your hooking leg back while driving your hips towards them.
    • Stack Pass: Opponent attempts to drive their weight forward and stack you. Maintain your hook and hip connection, use your non-hooking foot to push their hip away, and simultaneously drive your hips into them.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to grip the opponent's ankle in De La Riva Guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs from gripping too hard with an extended, unsupported wrist. Instead of a death grip, use a firm but relaxed grip on the opponent's ankle or pant cuff. Ensure your elbow is bent and close to your body, providing support. Consider a grip on the pant leg cuff as it often requires less wrist flexion and is more stable for white belts.

    Q: How can I use De La Riva Guard against a much larger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on using your frames and hip connection to prevent them from generating forward pressure. Your non-hooking foot on their hip is crucial for creating space and controlling their base. Use your De La Riva hook to off-balance them and create angles for sweeps, rather than trying to overpower them. Prioritize maintaining your structure over aggressive submissions initially.

    Q: When should I switch from closed guard to De La Riva Guard?

    The transition to De La Riva Guard from closed guard is most effective when your opponent is posturing up or attempting to break your closed guard. As they create space by extending their legs or leaning back, this is your opportunity to release one leg, step your hook in, and establish the De La Riva position before they can fully stabilize their base and pass.

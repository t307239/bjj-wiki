De La Riva Guard begins from an open guard scenario, aiming to control an opponent's leg and create angles for sweeps or submissions. White belts often struggle by overextending or losing their base, negating the guard's effectiveness.

The core principle is using the hooked leg to disrupt the opponent's balance and create space, allowing for hip movement and leverage.

This guard excels at controlling distance and creating off-balancing opportunities for the person on their back.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Start from open guard, sitting with knees bent and feet flat on the mat. Opponent is standing or in a low stance facing you.
    2. Establish a sleeve grip on the opponent's nearest arm with your non-hooking hand. Your palm faces upwards, fingers pointing towards their elbow.
    3. With your hooking leg (e.g., right leg), step your foot behind the opponent's corresponding leg (e.g., opponent's right leg), placing your shin across the outside of their thigh, just above the knee.
    4. Your foot should be flexed, with your toes pointing upwards, creating a tight hook.
    5. Your other leg (e.g., left leg) remains positioned to defend your base, with your knee pointing towards the ceiling and your foot flat on the mat, providing a stable base.
    6. Grip the opponent's pant leg just above the knee of the leg you are hooking with your non-hooking hand (e.g., left hand gripping opponent's right pant leg).
    7. Maintain a slight lean back, keeping your hips relatively close to the opponent's hips, but angled slightly to your side.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Injury Risk: Extending your hooking leg straight or allowing the opponent to step directly over it can cause your knee to hyperextend. The correct movement is to keep your hooking leg slightly bent, maintaining tension on the hook, and to use your base leg to shrimp your hips away if they try to pass.
    • Ankle/Foot Sprain Risk: Attempting to hook the opponent's leg with a pointed toe or without maintaining a flexed foot can lead to ankle rolls if they shift their weight or step on your foot. Always keep your hooking foot flexed, toes pointing up.
    • Lower Back Strain: Leaning too far back without a strong base from your non-hooking leg, or attempting to 'pull' the opponent down with excessive upper body force, can strain your lower back. Focus on using your hips to create angles and your hooking leg to control their base, rather than raw pulling power.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hook Placement Drill: Practice stepping your hooking leg behind the opponent's leg and achieving the flexed foot position without any opponent. (50 reps per leg)
    2. Grip and Hook Drill: With a partner holding their leg still, practice establishing the sleeve and pant grips while placing your hook. (25 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    3. Angle Creation Drill: Your partner stands, and you practice entering De La Riva, then shrimp your hips to create an angle. Partner resists passively. (25 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Basic Sweep Entry Drill (e.g., Scissor Sweep): From De La Riva, practice initiating a simple sweep like the scissor sweep, with your partner offering minimal resistance to the sweep motion. (25 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Controlled Sweep and Pass Defense: Your partner attempts a very slow, deliberate pass, and you practice maintaining the De La Riva hook and shrimping away. Then, attempt a basic sweep. (10 rounds, 1 minute each, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling (De La Riva Focus): Roll with a partner who understands the goal is to practice De La Riva. Focus on entering the guard, maintaining the hook, and attempting sweeps or transitions. (3 x 5-minute rounds, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is posturing up or has a wide base, creating an opportunity to hook their leg.
    • When the opponent is actively trying to pass your closed guard or half guard, and you can transition.
    • When you have established control of an opponent's sleeve and are looking to create dynamic off-balancing.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Leg Cut Pass: If the opponent's hooking leg is too far forward or not tight, the opponent can step their corresponding leg (e.g., left leg if you hooked their right) across your body and attempt to cut through your guard. To defend, pull your hooking leg back and shrimp your hips away, creating space. If they commit, transition to a different guard or attempt a sweep.
    • Knee Slice Pass: If the opponent's base is too wide and you can't hook effectively, they may try to drive their knee across your body. Maintain your base leg and try to use your hooking leg to push their knee away or transition to a simpler guard like half guard.
    • Standing Base: If the opponent remains standing with excellent balance and pressure, they can often simply step over your hooking leg and pass. If they achieve this, immediately try to retrieve your guard or transition to a defensive position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to hook their leg in De La Riva Guard?

    Your knee might hurt because you are overextending your leg or allowing it to hyperextend. The De La Riva hook should be a controlled embrace, not a forceful push. Ensure your hooking leg remains slightly bent, and your foot is flexed with toes pointing up. If the opponent applies pressure, use your free leg to shrimp your hips away and create space, rather than resisting with a locked-out knee.

    Q: How do I use De La Riva Guard against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a bigger opponent, leverage and angles are even more critical. Focus on keeping your hips close to their base and using your hooking leg to disrupt their balance rather than trying to lift them. Shrimp your hips to create significant angles, making their weight distribution unstable. Use your sleeve grip to pull their arm across your body, further unbalancing them, and look for sweep opportunities that exploit their size disadvantage.

    Q: When should I switch from De La Riva Guard to another guard?

    You should consider switching guards when the opponent effectively neutralizes your hook by either stepping over it or controlling your hooking leg too tightly. If they manage to establish a strong base and begin to pass, don't stubbornly cling to De La Riva. Transition to a more defensive guard like closed guard or half guard, or attempt a sweep before they fully establish their pass. The key is to recognize when the guard is compromised and adapt.

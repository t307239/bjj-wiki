Headquarters Pass: The Essential BJJ Technique for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Headquarters Pass is a powerful guard pass initiated from your opponent's closed guard, aiming to establish side control.

White belts often struggle with this due to a lack of understanding of weight distribution and leverage.

The key is to break posture and use your hips to drive through their legs, not just push their knees.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grips: Secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (same side) with your left hand.
    2. Posture Break: Pull your opponent's head down towards your chest, creating a significant neck flexion.
    3. Hip Angle: Angle your hips sharply to your right (around 45 degrees), away from your opponent's legs.
    4. Weight Transfer: Drive your left knee forward and slightly across your opponent's body, lowering your base.
    5. Leg Frame: Use your right leg to hook behind your opponent's left knee, preventing them from re-guarding.
    6. Pressure Drive: Simultaneously, push your opponent's head down with your collar grip and drive your hips forward and slightly to the right.
    7. Pass Completion: Continue driving your hips through, bringing your left leg out and establishing side control on your opponent's left side.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Wrist Twisting: Grabbing the sleeve with your palm facing upwards and twisting your wrist inwards while pulling can damage the wrist's carpal bones and ligaments.
    • Correct: Maintain a neutral wrist, palm facing the opponent's sleeve, and pull with your forearm.
    • Neck Hyperextension: Pulling the head down too aggressively without maintaining your own posture can lead to hyperextension of your cervical spine.
    • Correct: Break posture by pulling the head towards your chest, keeping your own chin tucked.
    • Knee Impact: Driving your knee directly into your opponent's hip or thigh bone with excessive force can cause patellar or meniscal damage to both individuals.
    • Correct: Lower your base and drive your hips forward, using your knee for positioning, not blunt impact.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Grip and Posture Break: Practice the collar and sleeve grips, and the initial head pull motion, without a partner (10 reps).
    2. Partnered Posture Break and Hip Angle: With a compliant partner, practice breaking posture and establishing the hip angle, holding for 5 seconds (10 reps).
    3. Controlled Drive (0% Resistance): Partner lies on their back, you practice the hip drive and leg hook without resistance (15 reps).
    4. Light Resistance (25%): Partner offers minimal resistance to posture break and hip drive, you complete the pass (20 reps).
    5. Moderate Resistance (50%): Partner actively tries to maintain guard, you focus on posture, hip angle, and driving through (25 reps).
    6. Live Rolling (75-90%): Integrate the Headquarters Pass into rolling situations, focusing on timing and execution (5 attempts per round).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent attempts to establish or maintain a closed guard.
    • When your opponent's posture is slightly compromised, allowing for a head-pull.
    • When you have established strong collar and sleeve grips against their upper body.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Retention (Legs): Opponent keeps knees tight and feet on hips, preventing the hip angle and leg hook. Maintain base width and drive forward.
    • Posture Control: Opponent maintains excellent posture, making the head pull ineffective. Reset and attempt a different pass or sweep.
    • Frame and Shrimp: Opponent creates frames with their arms and shrimps their hips away, creating space to re-guard. Drive through the space before they can recover.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Headquarters Pass?

    Your wrist pain is likely caused by an improper grip and pulling motion. When you grab the sleeve, your palm should face the opponent's elbow, and you should pull using your forearm, not by bending your wrist inwards. This incorrect 'hooking' action puts excessive strain on the carpal bones and ligaments. Ensure your wrist stays straight and neutral throughout the grip.

    Q: How can I do the Headquarters Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and weight distribution are paramount. Focus intensely on breaking their posture first by pulling their head down towards your chest with a strong cross-collar grip. Then, drive your hips in at a sharp angle (around 45 degrees) to their side, using your body weight to create pressure rather than just brute strength. Your goal is to get your hips lower than theirs and drive through their base.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from a Torreando Pass attempt to a Headquarters Pass?

    You should consider switching to a Headquarters Pass if your Torreando attempt stalls because your opponent is effectively turning their hips away or creating a strong frame. If you find yourself unable to circle your hips around their legs, and you can secure a collar and sleeve grip on their upper body, use that opportunity to pull their head down and initiate the posture break required for the Headquarters Pass. It's a fluid transition based on their reaction.

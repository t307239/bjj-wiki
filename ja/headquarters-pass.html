Headquarters Pass: The Ultimate BJJ Guide for White Belts

The Headquarters Pass is initiated from a standing or kneeling position, aiming to bypass the opponent's guard and establish side control or mount.

White belts often struggle with this pass due to insufficient pressure, incorrect weight distribution, and a lack of understanding of hip control.

The core principle is using your head and chest pressure to collapse the opponent's structure while simultaneously controlling their hips and legs to prevent them from re-guarding.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Opponent is in closed guard, kneeling or standing. Grip opponent's collar lapels with both hands, thumbs inside the lapel fold, fingers gripping the fabric.
    2. Place your forehead onto opponent's sternum, driving your weight downwards and slightly forward.
    3. Your knees should be wider than shoulder-width apart, creating a stable base, with your shins not touching the mat.
    4. Drive your hips forward, creating a strong frame with your head and chest, making it difficult for the opponent to create space.
    5. With your right hand, grip opponent's left leg pant cuff, thumb pointing downwards, fingers gripping the fabric tightly.
    6. With your left hand, grip opponent's right leg pant cuff, thumb pointing downwards, fingers gripping the fabric tightly.
    7. Shift your weight onto your right leg, while simultaneously extending your left leg back and to the side, creating an angle.
    8. Drive your hips forward and to the opponent's right, forcing their legs to open or break their posture as you step your right leg across their body.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly driving forward with only upper body strength: This can hyperextend the cervical spine (neck), leading to whiplash or nerve impingement.
    • Correct movement: Maintain a neutral head position, driving with the hips and chest, keeping the neck aligned with the spine.
    • Trying to step over the legs too early without collapsing the guard: This can cause the opponent to sweep you or isolate your leg, leading to knee ligament tears (ACL/MCL).
    • Correct movement: Ensure you have established strong head and chest pressure to break down the guard before attempting to step over.
    • Gripping opponent's pants too low or loosely: This allows the opponent to easily pull their legs out, leading to a loss of control and potential ankle or knee sprains when your base is compromised.
    • Correct movement: Grip the pant cuff firmly, ensuring your hands are positioned to control the opponent's leg movement.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the entire sequence of grips, head pressure, and hip movement without a partner. Focus on smooth transitions. (10 reps)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Partner remains passive. Focus on executing the exact grips and body mechanics. (20 reps)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance - 25%): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the correct pressure and movement. (30 reps)
    4. Partner drilling (moderate resistance - 50%): Partner actively tries to maintain guard, but without explosive movements. Work on breaking their posture and controlling their legs. (40 reps)
    5. Live rolling (75% resistance): Attempt the pass in a rolling scenario where your partner is defending realistically but not trying to win. Focus on timing and adaptation. (5 minutes)
    6. Live rolling (90-100% resistance): Attempt the pass against a fully resisting opponent. Focus on applying all learned principles under pressure. (5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is in closed guard and you have established a strong, stable base.
    • When the opponent attempts to open their guard or create space to attack.
    • When you have secured strong lapel grips that allow you to control their upper body.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stalling/Framing: The opponent can use their forearms to frame against your face and chest, creating space to re-guard or shrimp away.
    • Biomechanics: The opponent pushes with their elbows and forearms, widening the distance and preventing the forward drive.
    • Leg Pummeling/Hooking: The opponent can try to pummel their legs inside yours or hook your legs to prevent you from stepping over.
    • Biomechanics: The opponent actively moves their legs to entangle or block your leg-stepping motion, often seeking to establish their own guard.
    • Hip Escape/Shrimping: If the opponent creates enough space, they can shrimp their hips away and re-establish their guard.
    • Biomechanics: The opponent uses their feet on the mat to push and slide their hips away, creating distance and closing the passing lane.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try the Headquarters Pass?

    Your neck likely hurts because you are leading the pass with your head and not driving with your hips and chest. This creates excessive hyperextension in your cervical spine. Ensure your forehead is pressed firmly into the opponent's sternum, and your weight is transferred through your hips and chest, keeping your head in a neutral, aligned position.

    Q: How can I use the Headquarters Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes even more critical. Focus intensely on breaking their posture with your head and lapel grips, making their weight feel less significant. Use the hip drive to create an angle, forcing them to shift their weight, which creates openings. Do not try to muscle through; use precise biomechanical control.

    Q: When is the best time to switch my hips and step over in the Headquarters Pass?

    The ideal moment to switch your hips and step over is immediately after you have successfully collapsed the opponent's guard and established control of their legs. You'll feel their structure break and their hips become less mobile. This is when you drive your hips forward and to the side, stepping your leg across their body to secure the pass.

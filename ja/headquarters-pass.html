BJJのヘッドクォーターズパスをマスター！初心者ガイド

The Headquarters Pass is initiated from a standing or kneeling position facing a closed guard. Its goal is to break the opponent's guard and establish side control.

White belts often fail by relying on brute strength and poor posture, leading to instability and injury.

The key is to control the opponent's hips and posture using your own body's structural integrity.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (left sleeve) with your left hand. Maintain a strong, upright posture with your hips tucked under.
    2. Step your left foot forward, placing it slightly outside your opponent's left hip. Your knee should be pointed towards the mat.
    3. Shift your weight onto your left foot, driving your right knee down towards the mat directly behind your opponent's left hip.
    4. Simultaneously, pull down on the collar grip and push the sleeve grip away, breaking their posture and opening the guard.
    5. As their guard opens, drive your right knee further down and forward, creating a wedge between their legs and hips.
    6. Step your right foot forward, parallel to your left foot, creating a stable base. Your hips should be low and driving forward.
    7. Circle your hips to your right, clearing their legs. Maintain pressure with your chest on their chest and your left knee on the mat, then transition to side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly driving your knee down: This can hyperextend your knee joint or damage your patellar tendon if your leg buckles under pressure.
    • Correct movement: Drive your knee down and forward, creating a controlled wedge, maintaining a stable base with your standing leg.
    • Grabbing the gi too high on the collar: This can lead to an unstable grip and allow the opponent to escape or counter easily, potentially straining your shoulder or bicep trying to compensate.
    • Correct movement: Grip the gi firmly at the base of the collar, close to the sternum, for maximum control.
    • Not tucking your hips: Standing upright with your hips extended exposes your lower back to strain and makes you susceptible to sweeps, risking lumbar disc compression.
    • Correct movement: Tuck your hips under, creating a stable, neutral spine and a strong base.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the steps without a partner, focusing on hip movement, weight transfer, and grip placement. (10 repetitions per side, 0% resistance).
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Execute the pass against a compliant partner who offers no resistance. Focus on feeling the correct pressure and hip angles. (10 repetitions per side, 25% resistance).
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers light resistance to guard opening and hip movement. Focus on maintaining your base and pressure. (10 repetitions per side, 50% resistance).
    4. Partner drill (moderate resistance): Partner actively tries to maintain guard and offers moderate resistance to your pass. Focus on overcoming their grips and posture. (10 repetitions per side, 75% resistance).
    5. Flow rolling: Integrate the Headquarters Pass into positional sparring, attempting it when the opportunity arises. Focus on timing and recognizing the correct setup. (5 minutes, 90% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the Headquarters Pass in a full sparring scenario. Focus on commitment and continuous pressure. (5 minutes, 100% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • Opponent's guard is closed and they are posturing slightly upright.
    • Opponent attempts to create distance or frames with their arms and legs.
    • You have established a strong collar and sleeve grip while maintaining good posture.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): As the opponent drives their knee down, immediately shrimp your hips away to create space and prevent them from wedging. This breaks their base and allows you to re-establish guard.
    • Frame and Push Away: Use your forearms to frame against the opponent's chest and head. Push away firmly to create space, then use your legs to push their hips back and re-establish guard.
    • Counter Sweep (e.g., Scissor Sweep): If the opponent commits heavily to driving their knee down and their base is compromised, you can attempt a sweep by using your legs to hook their leg and hip, then explosively driving your hips up and to the side.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Headquarters Pass?

    Knee pain often occurs because you are driving your knee directly down onto your own patella, or your leg is buckling under uncontrolled pressure. Ensure your knee is driving down and forward, creating a wedge, and that your weight is supported by your standing leg and hips, not solely by your descending knee.

    Q: How can I perform the Headquarters Pass against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus heavily on controlling their posture and hips with your grips. Use your body weight and leverage by getting your hips low and driving forward. Instead of relying on raw strength, emphasize precise timing and biomechanical advantage, such as breaking their posture before committing to the knee drive.

    Q: When is the best time to switch grips during the Headquarters Pass?

    The most effective grip change happens after you've initially secured your cross-collar and sleeve grips and begun to break their posture. As you step forward and drive your knee down, you might adjust your sleeve grip to a more advantageous position, like a wrist grip, to further control their arm and prevent them from framing effectively.

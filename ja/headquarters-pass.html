Headquarters Pass: The Ultimate BJJ Technique Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Headquarters Pass is a guard pass initiated from your opponent's closed guard, aiming to establish side control or mount.

White belts often struggle by muscling the pass or neglecting proper weight distribution and framing.

The key to success is using your opponent's hip structure against them while maintaining a strong base and controlling their posture.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Secure both of your opponent's lapels with your hands, fingers inside the lapel fabric.
    2. Posture Control: Pull your opponent's lapels towards your chest, forcing their shoulders to round forward.
    3. Hip Angle: Angle your hips 45 degrees towards your opponent's left side (if passing to the right).
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your opponent's right hip, driving it into the mat.
    5. Leg Placement: Place your right knee on the mat, directly outside your opponent's right hip.
    6. Frame Creation: Extend your left arm, placing your forearm on your opponent's right thigh, creating a frame.
    7. Pass Completion: Drive your chest forward and down, stepping your right leg over their body to secure side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Knee Placement: Driving your knee directly into your opponent's hip bone with excessive force can cause a hip flexor strain or a contusion of the anterior superior iliac spine.
    • Correct: Place your knee slightly outside their hip bone, on the mat, to create a stable base and avoid direct impact.
    • Over-reliance on Arm Strength: Attempting to rip the guard open with only arm strength, while your hips are square, can lead to shoulder impingement syndrome or rotator cuff tears.
    • Correct: Use your lapel grips to break posture and then utilize hip pressure and angling to create the opening.
    • Premature Leg Swing: Swinging your leg over their body before establishing a strong base and hip pressure can result in your opponent sweeping you or creating a dangerous situation for your knee ligaments (MCL/ACL strain).
    • Correct: Ensure your base is solid and their hips are controlled before initiating the leg swing to pass.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling (0%): Practice the grip acquisition, posture break, hip angle, knee placement, and leg swing without a partner for 5 repetitions.
    2. Partnered Grip and Posture Break (25%): With a compliant partner, practice securing lapel grips and breaking their posture for 10 repetitions.
    3. Partnered Hip Pressure and Angle (50%): With a compliant partner, practice driving your weight into their hip and angling your body for 10 repetitions.
    4. Controlled Pass (75%): With a cooperative partner in closed guard, practice the entire Headquarters Pass sequence, allowing them to resist lightly for 5 repetitions per side.
    5. Live Pass with Light Resistance (90%): Attempt the Headquarters Pass against a partner who is actively defending their guard but not actively trying to sweep or submit for 3 minutes.
    6. Full Live Rolling (100%): Integrate the Headquarters Pass into live rolling, focusing on correct mechanics and timing for 5 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent has their knees tightly locked, preventing other passes.
    • When your opponent is trying to create distance with their feet on your hips.
    • When your opponent is posturing up, making it difficult to control their upper body.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Guard Retention (Feet on Hips): If the pass initiator drives forward without breaking posture, immediately place your feet on their hips and push them away to re-establish guard.
    • Scissoring the Legs: As they attempt to bring their knee inside, use your legs to scissor their legs apart and create space to shrimp out or switch to an open guard.
    • Hip Bump Sweep: If they are too close and their weight is directly over you, bridge your hips up and to the side to create an angle for a hip bump sweep.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try the Headquarters Pass?

    Shoulder pain often arises from an incorrect posture break. Instead of using your lapel grips to pull their shoulders forward and break their posture, you might be trying to force their head down with your arms. This puts excessive strain on your shoulder joint. Focus on using your biceps and back muscles to create a pulling motion on the lapels, drawing their shoulders into a rounded position, rather than a direct downward push.

    Q: How can I do the Headquarters Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and biomechanics become even more critical than raw strength. Focus on getting under their center of gravity by creating a strong lapel grip to pull their upper body down. Your hip angle and weight transfer are paramount; drive your hips into their hip and use your body weight to off-balance them. Do not try to muscle through; instead, create an angle where their size becomes a disadvantage for them.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from a Headquarters Pass attempt to another pass?

    You should consider switching if your opponent successfully frames and prevents your hip from driving into theirs, or if they manage to maintain a strong upright posture. If they are actively using their legs to push you away or scissor your legs to create space, it's a signal to disengage from the Headquarters Pass. Look for opportunities to transition to a knee-slice or leg-drag if they are defending the Headquarters Pass by keeping their legs extended.

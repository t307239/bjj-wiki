The Headquarters Pass is initiated from a standing or kneeling position when your opponent is in your guard, aiming to establish side control.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of weight distribution and hip pressure, leading to a loss of control.

The core principle is to create a strong base and use your hips to drive through, not just your upper body strength.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Secure a double-grip on the opponent's left and right pant legs, just above the knees. Maintain a hip-width base, knees slightly bent, weight centered over your feet.
    2. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your right foot, lifting your left knee slightly off the mat. Your hips angle slightly to your left.
    3. Drive: Drive your left shoulder into the opponent's left hip. Simultaneously, step your left foot forward, placing it outside the opponent's left knee.
    4. Hip Pressure: Drive your hips forward and down, pressing your chest towards the opponent's chest. Your left knee should be pointing towards the opponent's right hip.
    5. Leg Control: Use your grips to pull the opponent's legs towards your chest, creating a tight connection. This prevents them from reguarding.
    6. Advance: Step your right foot forward to match your left foot. Your knees should now be on either side of the opponent's hips.
    7. Secure Side Control: Lower your body, driving your chest into the opponent's chest and securing an underhook on their far side (their right armpit if you passed their left side). Your left knee should be near their hip and your right leg extended back for base.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Knee Drive: Driving your knee directly into the opponent's hip joint with force can hyperextend their knee, potentially tearing the ACL or PCL. Instead, aim to place your knee outside their hip and drive your hips forward.
    • Lifting with Arms: Grabbing sleeves and trying to pull the opponent's legs up with only arm strength can strain your biceps and shoulder joints (rotator cuff). Focus on hip pressure and driving your shoulders into their hips.
    • Basing Too Narrow: A narrow base makes you unstable. If the opponent shrimps or bridges, you can fall over, risking knee or ankle injuries from an awkward landing. Maintain a solid hip-width base throughout the pass.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Movement (0%): Practice the hip rotation and weight transfer without a partner. Focus on the feeling of driving your hips forward and angling your body. 20 reps.
    2. Grip and Step (0%): From kneeling, practice securing pant grips and stepping your lead leg forward, mimicking step 3 of the biomechanics. 20 reps.
    3. Controlled Pass Against Static Partner (25%): With a compliant partner lying on their back, practice the entire sequence. Focus on correct grips, hip angle, and pressure without resistance. 5 reps per side.
    4. Passive Guard Resistance (50%): Partner maintains a loose closed guard. You execute the Headquarters Pass, focusing on breaking the guard and driving through. Partner offers minimal resistance. 5 reps per side.
    5. Active Guard Resistance (75%): Partner actively tries to maintain guard or shrimp away. You focus on maintaining pressure and adapting your steps. 5 reps per side.
    6. Live Rolling (90-100%): Attempt the Headquarters Pass during live rolling. Focus on applying the learned biomechanics and grips under full pressure. Aim for 5 successful passes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent opens their guard and attempts to create space for a sweep.
    • When the opponent's hips are slightly away from you, creating a small gap to drive through.
    • After successfully breaking a closed guard and before the opponent can re-establish a strong guard.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Shrimping: As the passer drives forward, the defender shrimpes their hips away, creating space to re-establish guard or escape. The passer must maintain close body contact and forward pressure to prevent this.
    • Leg Re-hook: If the passer's hips are not low enough, the defender can try to re-hook a leg to establish half guard or butterfly guard. The passer must keep their knees tight and hips driving low.
    • Bridging: A well-timed bridge can disrupt the passer's base. The passer must keep their weight low and distributed, with a solid base, to absorb and resist the bridge.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Headquarters Pass as a white belt?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are driving your knee directly into the opponent's hip joint with excessive force, or your knee is positioned too far forward and inward. This can cause hyperextension or direct impact injuries to the knee ligaments. To fix this, focus on angling your hips and driving your *chest* into the opponent's chest, keeping your knee outside their hip and pointing slightly backward as you drive through. Your knee should act as a pivot point, not a striking surface.

    Q: How do I perform the Headquarters Pass against a much bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, pure strength is not your advantage. You must focus on precise biomechanics and leverage. Instead of driving straight through, emphasize creating a strong, narrow base and using your hips to 'cut' through their base. Secure your pant grips tightly and use your shoulder to control their hip. Your goal is to off-balance them by driving your weight *through* their center of gravity, not just pushing against them. Focus on maintaining constant pressure and minimizing space.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate the Headquarters Pass, and what if they keep their guard closed tightly?

    The Headquarters Pass is most effective when the opponent's guard is opening or has just been broken. If their guard is closed tightly, attempting this pass directly can be difficult and expose you to sweeps. First, focus on breaking their closed guard by posturing up and attacking their arms or knees, or by using a knee-slide or toreando pass to initiate the opening. Once the guard is compromised, then look for the angle to execute the Headquarters Pass. Patience and timing are key.

Headquarters Pass BJJ: The White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Headquarters Pass is a guard pass initiated from the opponent's guard, aiming to secure side control.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of precise body mechanics, leading to ineffective attempts.

The key is to use your hip structure to displace their legs, not brute force alone.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Secure your opponent's left knee with your right hand (cross-collar grip on their right lapel with your left hand).
    2. Base: Establish a wide, stable base with your feet shoulder-width apart, knees bent.
    3. Hip Angle: Drive your right hip forward and slightly down, creating a 45-degree angle relative to your opponent's hips.
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your right leg, compressing their left leg by driving your hip into it.
    5. Knee Drive: Simultaneously, drive your left knee towards the mat outside their right hip, creating a base.
    6. Body Rotation: Rotate your torso counter-clockwise, bringing your chest towards their hip.
    7. Pass: Maintain pressure and hip control as you step your right leg over their hips to secure side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect knee drive: Driving your left knee directly forward instead of at an angle can hyperextend your knee joint, risking ACL/MCL tears.
    • Forcing the pass with arms: Relying solely on pulling their collar without hip pressure can lead to shoulder impingement or rotator cuff tears.
    • Insufficient hip displacement: Not driving your hip into their knee allows them to easily re-guard, risking your own balance and potential submissions.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling (no resistance): Practice the hip angle and weight transfer motion repeatedly. 20 reps.
    2. Partner drill (static): Opponent holds a closed guard. Practice grip acquisition and the initial hip drive. 10 reps.
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Opponent offers minimal resistance to the hip drive. Focus on maintaining base. 10 reps.
    4. Partner drill (controlled pass): Opponent actively tries to prevent the pass but without aggressive submissions. 50% resistance. 5 reps.
    5. Flow rolling: Focus on executing the Headquarters Pass when the opportunity arises, with light, cooperative resistance. 75% resistance. 3 mins.
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the Headquarters Pass in a competitive environment. 100% resistance. 5 mins.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent attempts a scissor sweep and their hips are elevated.
    • When your opponent's legs are extended, creating an opening.
    • When you have secured a strong collar and knee grip, controlling their base.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Re-guarding: If the hip drive is weak, immediately bring your knees back to your chest and establish a strong frame to prevent the pass.
    • Hip Escape/Shrimping: If they start to pass, use a hip escape to create space and re-establish guard.
    • Counter-submission: If they overcommit to the pass, look for opportunities to attack with a triangle or armbar.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Headquarters Pass?

    This often happens if you drive your knee directly forward instead of at a 45-degree angle outside their hip. This incorrect movement can cause hyperextension, stressing the ACL and MCL. Ensure your weight is primarily on your hip and base leg, and your passing knee moves to create space, not to apply pressure.

    Q: How do I Headquarters Pass against a bigger opponent who has strong legs?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on breaking their posture and controlling their hips first. Use your lapel grip to pull their upper body towards you, making their legs less effective. Then, initiate the hip drive with precise timing, using your body weight as a wedge rather than trying to overpower them directly.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Headquarters Pass? I always seem to get stuck.

    The Headquarters Pass is most effective when your opponent's legs are slightly extended or when they are actively trying to sweep. Look for moments when their base is compromised, such as when they are shifting their weight for a sweep or when their knee is not firmly planted. Avoid attempting it when their guard is completely closed and tight.

Estima Lock: The Biomechanically Sound BJJ Technique for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Estima Lock is a powerful leg lock submission that attacks the opponent's knee. It is typically initiated from a guard position, aiming to isolate and hyperextend the knee joint.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of positional control and understanding of joint mechanics, leading to frustration and potential injury. Focus on precise body alignment over brute force.

The key to the Estima Lock lies in controlling the opponent's hip while creating leverage with your own hips to drive their knee into a vulnerable position.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting from closed guard: Secure both of the opponent's ankles with your feet, maintaining a closed guard position. Your hips are elevated slightly off the mat.
    2. Isolate one leg: Use your arms to pull the opponent's chosen leg towards your chest, keeping it tight. Your dominant side's leg will be the primary attacking leg.
    3. Hip angle adjustment: Rotate your hips to your dominant side, creating a 45-degree angle between your torso and the opponent's legs. Your supporting leg's knee should be pointed towards the opponent's hip.
    4. Secure the shin: With your dominant arm, reach across and secure a grip on the opponent's shin, just below the knee. Your forearm should be pressed firmly against their tibia.
    5. Control the foot: With your other arm, reach down and secure a grip on the opponent's foot, specifically around the ankle or the heel cup. This grip prevents them from easily extracting their foot.
    6. Hip drive and extension: Drive your hips upwards and towards the opponent's knee, simultaneously extending your legs away from your body. This action creates the hyperextension on the knee.
    7. Maintain tight control: Keep your grip on the shin and foot secure throughout the movement, ensuring the opponent cannot escape. Apply pressure by pushing your hips forward and pulling their foot towards your chest.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying the lock by simply "pulling hard" on the opponent's foot without proper hip control: This can lead to a torn ACL or MCL in the opponent's knee, or a hyperextended knee in the attacker if the opponent escapes unexpectedly.
    • Incorrect hip angle, causing your own knee to be vulnerable: If your supporting leg's knee points outwards instead of towards the opponent's hip, it can lead to a knee-on-knee injury or a twisted knee when the opponent attempts to escape.
    • Gripping too high on the opponent's thigh instead of the shin: This reduces leverage and makes the lock ineffective, potentially allowing the opponent to escape and counter, leading to a strained hip for the attacker.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling (0% resistance): Practice the hip angle adjustment and grip securing sequence with no opponent. Focus on smooth transitions between steps. 20 reps.
    2. Partner drill with static opponent (25% resistance): Opponent lies on their back, allowing you to set up the Estima Lock from closed guard. Focus on correct hip movement and grip. 10 reps per side.
    3. Partner drill with light resistance (50% resistance): Opponent offers minimal resistance to leg isolation and hip control. Focus on driving your hips and extending. 10 reps per side.
    4. Controlled execution (75% resistance): Opponent actively tries to defend but allows the submission to be applied slowly. Focus on maintaining control and finishing the lock. 5 reps per side.
    5. Submission focus drill (90% resistance): Opponent defends more actively, requiring more precise body mechanics to secure the lock. Focus on finishing the submission before they can fully escape. 5 reps per side.
    6. Live rolling (100% resistance): Attempt the Estima Lock during rolling, prioritizing positional control and safety. Tap early if caught in a submission. 3-5 minute rounds.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is in your closed guard and their weight distribution is uneven, creating an opening.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard and their legs become accessible for isolation.
    • When you have established a strong control of the opponent's hips and can dictate the distance and angle.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg extraction: The opponent can attempt to pull their leg out by straightening it and bridging their hips away from your pressure. This requires you to maintain tight grips and hip control.
    • Sweeping counter: If you are too slow in applying the lock, the opponent might use your hip angle to sweep you by driving their weight into your anchored leg.
    • Takedown defense: If the opponent attempts to stand up or drive forward aggressively, it can disrupt your setup and potentially lead to a scramble or counter-takedown.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to Estima Lock?

    Your knee might be hurting because you are not correctly aligning your own body. Ensure your supporting leg's knee points towards the opponent's hip, not outwards, to avoid hyperextension. Also, make sure you are driving your hips forward and extending your legs simultaneously, rather than just pulling the opponent's foot.

    Q: How can I Estima Lock a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on leverage and precision over strength. Use your hips to create a sharp 45-degree angle and drive them into their knee. Secure a very tight grip on their shin and ankle to prevent them from using their size to power out. Prioritize isolating their leg before they can establish a solid base.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt an Estima Lock from closed guard?

    The optimal time is when the opponent's weight is not evenly distributed, often when they are leaning forward to pass or trying to create space. Look for moments when their hips are slightly elevated or when you can effectively pull one of their legs towards your chest, disrupting their base and creating the necessary angle for the lock.

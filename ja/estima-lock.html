Estima Lock BJJ Technique Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Estima Lock is a powerful leg lock applied from specific guard positions, aiming to hyperextend the opponent's knee. White belts often struggle due to a lack of control and understanding of leverage points.

The key to the Estima Lock is isolating the opponent's leg and creating a fulcrum with your body to apply rotational pressure on the knee joint.

Understanding the precise angles of hip and shoulder engagement is crucial for both application and safety, preventing injury to both practitioners.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From Closed Guard, secure a double-pant grip on the opponent's right leg, gripping both sides of the pant cuff just above the ankle. Your hips should be angled slightly to your left.
    2. Slice your left elbow between the opponent's right thigh and your chest, creating a strong frame.
    3. Drive your left shoulder into the opponent's right hip, pushing it away and creating space to bring your right leg over their right leg.
    4. Once your right leg is over their right leg, hook your right foot behind their right knee, ensuring your shin is flush against the back of their knee joint.
    5. Bring your hips forward, lifting your butt slightly off the mat, to position your pelvis as the fulcrum point.
    6. Secure your right hand gripping your own left bicep, forming a "figure-four" grip around their right thigh.
    7. With your left elbow still framing their hip, and your right leg hooked behind their knee, drive your hips forward and slightly upwards. Simultaneously, pull your opponent's ankle towards your chest with your pant grips, creating a rotational hyperextension at the knee.
    8. Maintain a tight frame with your left arm to prevent them from posturing up or relieving pressure.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying the Estima Lock by "pulling hard" with your arms without proper hip engagement: This can strain your own biceps and shoulders, and fails to create the necessary leverage on the opponent's knee, potentially leading to them escaping easily.
    • Incorrect leg placement, where your hooking leg is too high or too low on their thigh: This can result in a knee-bar rather than an Estima Lock, putting excessive strain on the ACL and PCL ligaments of the opponent's knee, risking severe injury.
    • Allowing the opponent to stack you or drive forward aggressively without maintaining your frame: This can lead to your own spine being compressed or your knee being injured if they manage to drive their weight directly onto it.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Drill: Practice the hip and leg movements from closed guard without a partner. Focus on the angle of your hips and the placement of your hooking leg. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner Drill (No Resistance): With a compliant partner in closed guard, execute the steps slowly, focusing on grip acquisition and body positioning. Partner remains still. (20 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    3. Light Resistance Drill: Partner offers minimal resistance to your initial setup. Focus on breaking their posture and securing the leg. (15 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled Resistance Drill: Partner attempts to defend by posturing up or framing. You practice maintaining your position and finishing the lock. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Scenario Drill: Start from a neutral closed guard. Partner attempts to pass. You secure the Estima Lock setup. (10 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling (Controlled): Attempt the Estima Lock in rolling, focusing on setup and proper finishing mechanics. Tap early if you feel any discomfort. (5 attempts or 2 minutes, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured up and you have closed guard.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard and their leg is exposed.
    • When you have successfully swept or reversed the opponent and they are trying to regain guard.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Escape by "stacking" your weight: If the Estima Lock is being applied, the defender can attempt to drive their weight forward, collapsing the Estima Lock application and relieving pressure on the knee. This requires precise weight distribution and forward momentum.
    • "Knee-bar" escape: If the defender feels the pressure is more of a knee-bar, they can sometimes escape by extending their leg and rotating their hip away from the direction of the lock.
    • "Turn-in" escape: The defender can attempt to turn their body into the Estima Lock, bringing their hips closer to the attacker's hips. This can disrupt the angle of attack and allow for a potential escape.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do the Estima Lock as a white belt?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are not creating the correct biomechanical fulcrum. Instead of using your hips and pelvis to apply rotational pressure to the opponent's knee, you might be relying too much on arm strength or incorrect leg positioning. Ensure your hooking leg is flush behind their knee and your hips are driving forward to create the proper leverage point.

    Q: How can I use the Estima Lock against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paramount. Focus on securing tight pant grips and a deep frame with your elbow. The key is to use your hips to create the angle and lift, rather than trying to overpower them. Drive your shoulder into their hip to create space, then quickly get your leg over and hook behind their knee to establish your fulcrum.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Estima Lock from closed guard?

    The optimal time to attempt the Estima Lock from closed guard is when the opponent is postured up, allowing you to create significant space with your hips and frame. If they are slumped or too close, it's harder to get the proper leg over and behind their knee. Look for the moment they try to break your guard or drive forward.

The Estima Lock is a submission targeting the opponent's knee, typically initiated from a closed guard or open guard scenario where you have control of one of their legs.

White belts often struggle by applying excessive, uncontrolled force rather than precise biomechanical leverage, leading to frustration and potential injury.

The core principle is to isolate the opponent's knee joint and apply rotational pressure through controlled hip extension and leg positioning.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure your opponent's right leg with your left arm's bicep locked under their thigh and your left hand gripping your own right shin. Your right leg hooks behind their left hamstring.
    2. Shift your hips to your left, creating an angle of approximately 45 degrees relative to your opponent's body.
    3. With your left hand on your right shin, pull your right leg towards your chest, drawing their knee towards your hip.
    4. Simultaneously, extend your hips upwards and slightly to your left, using your glutes and lower back to drive your hips into their knee.
    5. Your right foot, hooked behind their hamstring, acts as a fulcrum. Ensure your right shin is perpendicular to their femur.
    6. Your left arm's bicep should remain tight under their thigh, preventing them from easily escaping the hip.
    7. Apply upward pressure with your hips while maintaining a stable base with your left leg, creating torque on the knee joint.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying the lock by simply "pulling" with your arms: This strains your biceps and shoulders unnecessarily and fails to generate sufficient torque on the knee, potentially leading to rotator cuff tears or bicep tendonitis.
    • Incorrect hip angle, leading to the knee being too far away: This widens the lever arm and reduces the effectiveness of the hip extension, risking lumbar strain as you overcompensate with your back.
    • Allowing the opponent's foot to escape your control: If their foot can rotate freely, the pressure is not isolated to the knee, increasing the risk of ankle ligament sprains if you apply force incorrectly.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip angle shift and leg isolation without a partner. Focus on the exact foot and leg placement. (10 reps each side)
    2. Partner assisted (0% resistance): Your partner lies on their back. You execute the steps, and they offer minimal resistance to your movements, allowing you to feel the positions. (5 reps each side)
    3. Partner assisted (25% resistance): Your partner offers slight pushback as you attempt to secure the leg and hip angle. Focus on maintaining your frames. (5 reps each side)
    4. Controlled isolation (50% resistance): Your partner defends the leg position lightly. You practice the hip extension and knee isolation without applying submission pressure. (5 reps each side)
    5. Technical execution (75% resistance): Your partner defends more actively. You work to secure the position and initiate the hip drive, stopping just before submission pressure. (3 reps each side)
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Attempt the Estima Lock during rolling, focusing on correct mechanics. Tap immediately if you feel joint pain or if your partner taps. (2 attempts per roll)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is attempting to pass your closed guard and their knee is bent and accessible.
    • During transitions from guard, when you can isolate one of their legs as they try to establish a base.
    • When your opponent is posturing up, creating space between their hips and yours, allowing you to secure their leg.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg escape: As the opponent initiates the hip extension, quickly extend your leg and pivot your hips to create space and disengage the knee.
    • Hip switch defense: If the opponent angles their hips, immediately attempt to switch your hips in the opposite direction to break their angle and relieve pressure.
    • Push and clear: Use your hands to push against the opponent's hips or shoulder, creating enough space to free your trapped leg and create distance.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my hip hurt when I try the Estima Lock?

    Hip pain often stems from an incorrect hip angle and insufficient glute engagement. Instead of driving your hips directly upwards, focus on a 45-degree angle relative to your opponent and actively squeeze your glutes to initiate the hip extension. Ensure your supporting leg (the one not trapping the opponent's leg) has a wide base for stability.

    Q: How can I Estima Lock a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, precise leverage is paramount. Focus on securing a deep grip under their thigh with your bicep and isolating their knee as close to your hip as possible. Use your entire body weight by driving your hips up and forward, not just pulling. Consider using your free hand to grip your own shin for increased pulling leverage.

    Q: Is the Estima Lock a knee bar or something else?

    The Estima Lock is a knee lock submission that targets the knee joint, specifically aiming to hyperextend it. While it shares similarities with a knee bar in that it attacks the knee, the mechanics and typical entry points can differ. The Estima Lock often involves a rotational component of the knee joint, whereas a knee bar typically focuses on straight hyperextension.

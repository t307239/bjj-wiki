Estima Lock: A Precise BJJ Technique Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Estima Lock is a powerful leg lock that attacks the opponent's knee from a specific open guard position. It aims to hyperextend the knee joint for a submission.

White belts often struggle due to improper hip positioning and grip fighting, leading to loss of control or injury. They tend to force the lock rather than using leverage.

The key mechanical insight is creating a strong fulcrum with your hips against their thigh, while controlling their foot and ankle to isolate the knee joint's extension.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a de la Riva guard or similar open guard, establish a strong grip on the opponent's ankle with your non-attacking leg's foot. Your foot should be planted firmly on the mat outside their hip.
    2. Secure a deep grip on the opponent's shin or knee with your free hand, ensuring your forearm is across the front of their tibia.
    3. Swing your attacking leg up and over the opponent's attacking leg, aiming to trap their knee joint between your shin and your hamstring.
    4. Drive your hips forward and upward, creating a strong posterior tilt of your pelvis against the opponent's thigh, just above their knee.
    5. With your gripping hand, pull their ankle towards your body, while simultaneously using your leg trapping to create opposing pressure.
    6. Your attacking leg's knee should point towards the ceiling, and your ankle should be flexed, creating a stable platform.
    7. To finish, extend your hips and push your chest forward, while maintaining the ankle pull and leg trap, forcing hyperextension of their knee.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying pressure before establishing the proper hip fulcrum: This can lead to the opponent escaping or you losing control of their leg, potentially causing a knee injury to your opponent or a strain to your own hip flexors.
    • Gripping too low on the ankle or too loosely on the shin: This allows the opponent to easily escape the lock or twist their leg, increasing the risk of ligament tears (ACL, MCL) in their knee.
    • Not creating sufficient hip elevation and posterior pelvic tilt: This results in insufficient leverage, allowing the opponent to stack you or resist effectively, potentially causing a spinal compression injury to you or a knee hyperextension injury to them if they fall awkwardly.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip movement and leg trap motion without a partner. Focus on the hip elevation and pelvic tilt. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Partner stands, you execute the Estima Lock setup and finish. Focus on grip and body positioning. (25 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to the setup, allowing you to practice maintaining control. (50% resistance, 20 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (controlled resistance): Partner actively tries to resist the lock and escape, but without applying dangerous pressure. (75% resistance, 15 reps per side)
    5. Live rolling (controlled): Attempt the Estima Lock during sparring, focusing on smooth transitions and proper mechanics. Tap early if caught. (90% resistance, 5 minutes)
    6. Live rolling (full): Integrate the Estima Lock into your rolling game, understanding its timing and counters. (100% resistance, 5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent is posturing up from your de la Riva guard, creating space to swing your leg over.
    • When an opponent tries to pass your guard by stepping their leg deep into your guard, offering a clear trapping opportunity.
    • When an opponent is heavy on your hips from a closed guard and you can isolate one of their legs.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stacking: The opponent drives their weight forward and down onto your hips, attempting to crush your pelvis and break your structure. To counter, maintain hip elevation and a strong posterior tilt.
    • Reaping the leg: The opponent attempts to turn their knee away from the lock, often by stepping their foot out and away. To counter, maintain a tight grip on the ankle and control the shin.
    • Peeling the grip: The opponent uses their free hand to peel your gripping hand off their shin or ankle. To counter, secure a deep, strong grip and use your body to protect it.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try to do the Estima Lock?

    Your ankle might be hurting because you are not properly securing the opponent's ankle with your foot during the Estima Lock setup. Ensure your foot is firmly planted on the mat outside their hip and that you are not using your ankle to 'hook' their leg in a way that creates torque. Focus on using your shin and calf to trap their leg, and your foot to control their heel or Achilles tendon for leverage.

    Q: How do I Estima Lock against a bigger opponent who is heavy on my hips?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes even more critical. Focus on creating a strong posterior pelvic tilt by driving your hips up and forward, making their thigh your primary fulcrum. Do not try to overpower them; instead, isolate their knee joint by controlling their ankle and shin tightly, then use your body weight to create the hyperextension. Prioritize getting your trapping leg deep to prevent them from posturing up.

    Q: When is the best time to go for the Estima Lock, and how do I avoid giving up my back?

    The Estima Lock is best attempted when your opponent is posturing up or trying to pass your guard by stepping their leg into your de la Riva or similar open guard. To avoid giving up your back, always maintain control of at least one of their legs with your legs and hips, and keep your base wide enough to prevent them from easily circling to your back. If they start to spin to your back, switch to controlling their leg and attempt to return to guard.

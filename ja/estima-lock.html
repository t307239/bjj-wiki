Estima Lock BJJ: A Biomechanically Accurate Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Estima Lock is a powerful leg lock initiated from a closed guard or half guard, aiming to hyperextend the opponent's knee.

White belts often struggle due to insufficient hip control and incorrect knee alignment, leading to ineffective attempts.

The key insight is to use your hips to drive into the opponent's thigh, creating a fulcrum that isolates and pressures the knee joint.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (left hand) on the opponent's right sleeve.
    2. Simultaneously, use your left foot to hook behind the opponent's right knee and your right foot to hook their left hip, creating a strong base.
    3. Break the opponent's posture by pulling down on the collar and sleeve, bringing their upper body towards your chest.
    4. Untuck your right foot from their hip and swing it over their right hip, placing your shin across the front of their thigh.
    5. Drive your hips upwards and forward, ensuring your right shin is perpendicular to the opponent's thigh, creating a straight line of pressure.
    6. Grip your own right shin with your left hand, securing your leg. Your right foot should be flexed, toes pointing upwards.
    7. Maintain a strong hip bridge, continuing to drive your hips into their thigh while keeping your back flat on the mat. This isolates the knee joint.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect hip drive: Applying pressure by simply pulling with your arms without driving your hips forward can strain your own back and fail to isolate the opponent's knee, potentially causing hyperextension of your own knee if they shift weight.
    • Improper knee alignment: Allowing your own knee to bend excessively or not keeping your shin perpendicular to the opponent's thigh creates a weak lever and risks damaging your own ACL or MCL.
    • Gripping too loosely: A loose grip on your own shin allows the opponent to escape the lock easily and can lead to your arm being overextended, causing shoulder joint strain.
    • Lack of hip control: If you fail to control the opponent's hip with your foot, they can easily posture up and relieve the pressure, negating the lock and risking your guard being passed.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling of hip movement and leg placement: Practice swinging your leg over and driving your hips without a partner. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance).
    2. Partner drill with no resistance: Practice the full Estima Lock sequence with a compliant partner who offers no resistance. Focus on grip and hip mechanics. (5 reps per side, 0% resistance).
    3. Partner drill with light resistance: Partner offers minimal resistance by not actively defending, allowing you to feel the mechanics. (5 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Partner drill with controlled defense: Partner attempts to defend passively, allowing you to work through the setup and finish. (5 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Live rolling scenario (Estima Lock attempt only): Call "Estima Lock setup" to initiate the attempt from closed guard. Partner offers active but controlled defense. (5 attempts, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the Estima Lock when opportunities arise naturally in rolling. Focus on safe application and tapping early if caught. (10 minutes rolling, 90-100% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up in your closed guard, creating space to swing your leg over.
    • When your opponent attempts to stack pass from your closed guard, presenting their leg for the lock.
    • When you have secured a strong cross-collar and sleeve grip, allowing for posture control.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape and Base Reset: If you feel pressure on your knee, immediately hip escape to the side and widen your base, relieving pressure and breaking the lock's structure.
    • Straighten Leg and Push: Actively try to straighten your leg and push your opponent's hips away, disrupting their hip drive and creating space to escape.
    • Roll to the Other Side: If the lock is deep, attempt to roll your body to the side of the trapped leg, using your free leg to push and create an escape angle.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Estima Lock, even when my partner doesn't tap?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are not achieving the correct biomechanical alignment. The Estima Lock applies pressure by hyperextending the knee joint. If your shin is not perpendicular to your opponent's thigh, or if your hips aren't driving forward, you are likely twisting or torquing your own knee joint, not effectively isolating theirs. Focus on driving your hips into their thigh with your shin across the front of their leg.

    Q: How can I Estima Lock a much bigger opponent who has strong posture?

    Against a larger opponent, posture control is paramount. Focus on securing very tight collar and sleeve grips to break their posture down as much as possible before initiating the leg swing. Once your leg is over their thigh, use your entire body weight and hip drive to create the pressure, rather than relying solely on arm strength. If they maintain strong posture, the Estima Lock is unlikely to be effective and you should reset.

    Q: Is it okay to crank the Estima Lock to get a faster tap?

    No, it is never okay to 'crank' any submission in BJJ, especially leg locks. The Estima Lock, like all leg locks, should be applied with controlled pressure. 'Cranking' implies jerky, uncontrolled movements that drastically increase the risk of severe injury (ligament tears, fractures) for both you and your training partner. The goal is to apply steady, increasing pressure until the opponent taps. Focus on the biomechanics of the lock, not brute force.

Estima Lock: A White Belt's Biomechanically Accurate Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Estima Lock is a submission targeting the opponent's knee from a specific outside leg entanglement, typically initiated from a half-guard or 50-50 position.

White belts often struggle by over-rotating their hips or applying force without proper control, leading to inefficient mechanics and potential injury.

The core mechanical principle is isolating the opponent's knee joint through precise hip positioning and controlled linear extension, not raw power.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting Position: Opponent's weight is on your chest, you are in half-guard with your right leg inside their right leg.
    2. Grip: Secure your opponent's right ankle with your left hand (palm facing up, fingers pointing towards their toes).
    3. Hip Angle: Drive your right hip forward and slightly to your left, creating a 45-degree angle between your torso and your opponent's leg.
    4. Leg Control: With your right leg, hook your opponent's right thigh from the outside, ensuring your shin is across their hamstring.
    5. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your right hip, lifting your opponent's ankle slightly off the mat.
    6. Arm Extension: Extend your left arm straight, pulling their ankle towards your left hip to control their leg's rotation.
    7. Knee Isolation: Simultaneously, drive your right knee towards the mat while extending your hips forward. This isolates their knee.
    8. Submission: Apply a controlled linear extension of your hips, pushing your right knee away from you, to hyperextend their knee joint.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hip Rotation: Attempting to turn your hips 90 degrees to face your opponent's head while holding the ankle. This can cause your own knee or ankle to twist awkwardly, risking ACL or MCL tears.
    • Correct Movement: Maintain a 45-degree hip angle and drive hips forward, not laterally.
    • Gripping Too Loosely: Holding the ankle with a loose grip or allowing the opponent to easily pry your hand off. This allows their leg to escape and can lead to your own leg getting trapped in a dangerous position, risking knee hyperextension.
    • Correct Movement: Secure a firm grip on the ankle with your palm facing up, fingers pointing towards the toes.
    • Jerking Motion: Applying a sudden, jerky motion to extend the hips to finish the submission. This uncontrolled force can lead to the opponent's knee suffering a sudden, severe hyperextension, resulting in ligament damage.
    • Correct Movement: Apply a smooth, controlled, linear extension of the hips.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Drilling (0%): Practice the hip angles, leg hooks, and arm extension from the starting position on the mat without a partner. (20 reps).
    2. Passive Partner (25%): With a compliant partner, practice the initial setup and hip movement. They offer no resistance to your leg entanglement. (15 reps).
    3. Controlled Entanglement (50%): Partner allows you to establish the leg entanglement and hip angle. You practice isolating the knee with controlled hip extension. Partner offers light pressure to simulate defense. (10 reps).
    4. Setting the Grip (75%): Partner attempts to defend the ankle grip and leg position. You focus on securing the grip and establishing the correct hip angle. (10 reps).
    5. Submission Execution (90%): Partner allows the entanglement and grip, then taps immediately when you apply controlled hip extension. Focus on the feeling of isolating the knee. (5 reps).
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Attempt the Estima Lock during rolling. Prioritize setting up the position correctly and only attacking when the opportunity is clear. Tap early if your opponent escapes or counters. (2 rounds).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent has established a strong half-guard or 50-50 guard against you.
    • You have isolated one of your opponent's legs and can achieve the outside leg entanglement.
    • Opponent attempts to pass your guard by stacking or pressuring heavily into your legs.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Escape to Guard: If your opponent establishes the outside leg entanglement and you feel pressure, immediately drive your hips back and attempt to bring your free leg between your hips and their body to re-establish guard.
    • Turn Inward: If the Estima Lock is being applied, turn your hips inward towards the attacker and attempt to roll them over your body, relieving pressure on your knee.
    • Straighten Leg: Actively try to straighten your leg and drive your heel away from the attacker's hips, creating space and reducing the leverage on your knee joint.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Estima Lock, even if I don't finish it?

    This typically happens if you are not properly isolating the knee joint. Instead of a controlled linear extension, you might be twisting your knee or applying pressure unevenly. Ensure your hip is at a 45-degree angle and your right knee drives forward, not sideways, to isolate the tibia from the femur.

    Q: How can I Estima Lock a much bigger opponent who has heavy pressure?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on precision over power. Use your hips to create the 45-degree angle and ensure your right leg is securely hooked on their hamstring. Your goal is to control their ankle firmly and use your hip extension to create the leverage, rather than trying to overpower their weight.

    Q: When is the best time to try the Estima Lock, and what if they just 'give' it to me?

    The Estima Lock is best attempted when you have secured an outside leg entanglement, often after a failed guard pass attempt by your opponent or when transitioning from half-guard. If they 'give' it to you, it might be a trap. Always ensure your own base and body positioning are secure before committing to the submission to avoid being swept or reversed.

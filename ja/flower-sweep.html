This sweep originates from closed guard, aiming to reverse the position and land in top half-guard or mount. White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of leverage and weight distribution.

The key insight is using your hips to create a platform and your legs to drive, not just relying on arm strength.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grips: Secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip on their left arm with your left hand.
    2. Hip Angle: Simultaneously, shrimp your hips to your left, creating a 45-degree angle between your body and your opponent's.
    3. Weight Transfer: Drive your right knee towards your opponent's left hip, using it as a fulcrum.
    4. Leg Action: Extend your left leg to hook behind their right thigh, keeping it tight.
    5. Head & Shoulder Pressure: Lower your head and left shoulder towards the mat, creating downward pressure.
    6. Sweep Motion: Rotate your hips counter-clockwise, pulling their left arm across your body and driving your right knee forward.
    7. Finishing Position: As they fall, transition to your right side, establishing top half-guard or passing directly to mount.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Error: Grabbing the opponent's sleeve and pulling straight up with only arm strength.

    Injury Risk: Torn rotator cuff or elbow hyperextension due to excessive outward rotation and force.

    Correct: Use your hips to angle and your leg to hook, creating leverage; pull the sleeve across your body.

    • Error: Arching your back excessively to try and lift the opponent.

    Injury Risk: Lumbar spine strain or disc injury from improper spinal loading.

    Correct: Focus on driving your hips forward and rotating, keeping your spine neutral.

    • Error: Extending your knee towards the opponent's face or neck when shrimping.

    Injury Risk: Knee ligament damage (ACL/MCL) from awkward twisting or direct impact.

    Correct: Drive your knee towards their hip bone, creating a stable base and pivot point.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Escape & Hook Drill: Practice shrimping and hooking your leg behind your thigh, 20 reps each side, 0% resistance.
    2. Partnered Grip & Hip Angle Drill: With a compliant partner, practice securing grips and shrimping hips to a 45-degree angle, 10 reps, 25% resistance.
    3. Controlled Sweep Execution: Execute the sweep with a compliant partner, focusing on precise body mechanics, 5 reps each side, 50% resistance.
    4. Light Resistance Sweep Practice: Partner offers mild resistance to the sweep, focusing on maintaining leverage and hip drive, 5 reps each side, 75% resistance.
    5. Flow Rolling - Sweep Focus: Attempt the sweep during flow rolling, prioritizing technique over success, 5 attempts, 90% resistance.
    6. Live Rolling - Sweep Application: Integrate the Flower Sweep into live rolling, accepting successful sweeps and failed attempts as learning opportunities, 3-5 minutes, 100% resistance.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is posturing up in your closed guard, creating space.
    • When the opponent's weight is distributed forward, making them unstable.
    • When you have secured strong cross-collar and sleeve grips.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: If the opponent recognizes the sweep and tries to stack you, maintain your grip and hip angle, then drive your hips upwards to counter their stack.
    • Knee Slide to Side Control: If the sweep is initiated but fails, and the opponent attempts to pass, immediately transition to a knee-on-belly or side control by driving your knee across their body.
    • Guard Retention (Open Guard): If you are in open guard and the opponent tries to pass, use your legs to create frames and re-establish your guard, preventing the sweep initiation.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to Flower Sweep from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs from gripping too tightly with your fingers or from the opponent forcefully breaking your grip. Ensure you are using a strong cross-collar grip with your thumb inside the lapel and a firm sleeve grip. When your opponent resists, focus on pulling their arm across your body using your hips and shoulder, rather than just your wrist, to dissipate the force.

    Q: How can I Flower Sweep a much larger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paramount. Focus on creating a significant hip angle (45 degrees or more) by shrimping. Use your entire body to drive your knee into their hip and rotate. Don't try to lift them; instead, aim to unbalance them by pulling their arm across your body and using your leg hook to control their base as you sweep.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Flower Sweep, and what if they defend by tucking their arm?

    The Flower Sweep is most effective when the opponent is posturing up or leaning forward, making them top-heavy. If they tuck their arm to defend, this is often an opportunity. You can use this to your advantage by maintaining your sleeve grip and using it to pull their arm across your body as you execute the sweep, thereby creating an even stronger unbalanced reaction.

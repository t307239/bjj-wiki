Flower Sweep: The Biomechanically Accurate BJJ Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This sweep originates from the closed guard, aiming to invert your opponent and create a superior position.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute force rather than precise leverage.

The key insight is using your hips to create an angle and lift, not just pulling their arm.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure a cross-collar grip on your opponent's gi with your right hand, and a sleeve grip on their left arm with your left hand.
    2. Shift your hips to your left, angling your pelvis approximately 45 degrees away from your opponent's centerline.
    3. Extend your right leg upwards, placing your right foot on your opponent's left hip bone, maintaining a straight knee.
    4. Simultaneously, pull your opponent's left sleeve towards your right shoulder while pressing your right foot into their hip.
    5. As your opponent reacts to the sleeve pull and hip pressure, drive your hips upwards and to your left, rotating your body.
    6. Your right leg acts as a lever, while your left arm guides their upper body rotation.
    7. Continue the hip drive and rotation until your opponent is swept to their left side, and you can immediately transition to mount or side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending your right leg straight into your opponent's chest without hip angling: This can hyperextend your knee joint, risking ACL or meniscus tears. Instead, ensure your hips are angled and your leg drives into the hip bone.
    • Pulling their sleeve directly towards your face with your left arm: This can strain your shoulder rotator cuff muscles and create poor leverage. Instead, pull the sleeve towards your shoulder to initiate the rotation.
    • Attempting to lift your opponent solely with your arms: This leads to ineffective sweeping and can cause bicep or elbow joint strain. Focus on driving your hips to create the primary lifting force.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip shift, leg extension, and arm pull motion without a partner. Focus on the feeling of the hip rotation. (50 reps)
    2. Light grip drilling: With a partner providing minimal resistance, practice securing grips and initiating the hip movement. (25 reps per side)
    3. Controlled sweep attempts: Your partner stands passively. You execute the sweep, focusing on the mechanics. They offer no significant resistance. (20 reps per side)
    4. Partner assists sweep: Your partner understands the mechanics and offers slight resistance, helping you feel the correct timing and leverage. (30 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Flow rolling: Execute the sweep during light sparring, allowing your partner to defend naturally. Focus on smooth transitions after the sweep. (5 minutes, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the sweep in a full-intensity roll, prioritizing correct mechanics over success. (5 minutes, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up in your closed guard, creating space.
    • When your opponent is attempting to pass your guard and their weight is forward.
    • When your opponent has a loose grip on your collar or sleeve, offering an opportunity for a strong grip.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape and Frame: If the sweep is initiated, immediately hip escape to your right and create a strong frame with your right arm against your opponent's hip to prevent the full rotation. This breaks their base.
    • Stalling the Rotation: If you feel the sweep coming, plant your left foot firmly on the mat and use your right hand to push your opponent's left hip away, preventing the body rotation and maintaining your base.
    • Switching Base: If the sweep is partially successful, quickly shift your weight to your right leg and attempt to turn into your opponent, preventing them from establishing mount.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Flower Sweep from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often stems from gripping too tightly with your fingers and not utilizing your forearm and shoulder for the pull. Ensure your cross-collar grip is firm but not strained. When pulling the sleeve, think of rotating your forearm and using your shoulder to guide the arm, rather than a finger-only pull. This distributes the force across your entire arm and reduces stress on the wrist.

    Q: How can I perform the Flower Sweep against a much larger and heavier opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paramount. Focus on the hip angle and the upward drive of your hips; this is where the power comes from, not brute strength. Ensure your right foot is firmly planted on their hip bone to create a solid fulcrum. The cross-collar grip is crucial for controlling their upper body rotation. If they are too heavy to lift directly, use the rotation to off-balance them and create an opening.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Flower Sweep, and what if my opponent anticipates it?

    The Flower Sweep is most effective when your opponent is posturing up or leaning forward, making them vulnerable to an upward and rotational sweep. If they anticipate it, they will likely stiffen their posture and resist the hip drive. In this case, do not force the sweep. Instead, use their anticipation to transition to other guard attacks, such as a triangle choke or armbar, by maintaining your hip angle and grip control.

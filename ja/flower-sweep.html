Flower Sweep: The Biomechanically Precise BJJ Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Flower Sweep is initiated from closed guard, aiming to invert your opponent's base and sweep them over your hips. White belts often struggle due to a lack of coordinated hip and leg movement.

The key is to use your hips as a fulcrum and your legs to create leverage, not just brute strength.

Understanding the rotational force generated by your hips and the precise timing of leg extension is crucial for success.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Establish a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (left sleeve) with your left hand.
    2. Hip Angle: Shift your hips to your left by elevating them slightly off the mat.
    3. Weight Transfer (Opponent): Use your grips to pull your opponent's upper body towards your left hip.
    4. Leg Action (Initiation): Extend your left leg straight, placing the sole of your foot on your opponent's right hip crease.
    5. Leg Action (Leverage): Simultaneously, bring your right knee towards your chest, hooking your right foot under your opponent's left knee pit.
    6. Hip Rotation: Rotate your hips to the right, driving your left foot into their hip crease and pulling their collar grip to initiate a rotational force.
    7. Sweep Execution: As your opponent's weight shifts and they begin to fall forward due to the rotational pull and hip elevation, extend your left leg to complete the sweep and establish a top position.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Hyperextending Knee: Attempting to 'kick' the opponent's hip with a stiff, straight leg can cause hyperextension of your own knee joint (ACL/MCL strain) if the opponent resists strongly or moves unexpectedly. Instead, maintain a slight bend in your left knee and use controlled hip rotation to drive into the hip crease.
    • Neck Strain: Grabbing the opponent's collar too high or pulling with excessive neck flexion can lead to cervical spine strain. Maintain a neutral head position and focus on pulling the opponent's upper body towards your hip, not directly into your neck.
    • Ankle/Foot Twisting: If your foot gets caught or you twist your ankle while sweeping, especially if you're trying to 'push' rather than 'drive' with your left foot, you risk ligament damage. Ensure your left foot is firmly planted on the hip crease with your toes pointing slightly upward, creating a stable base for your hip rotation.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Mobility Drill: Practice hip elevation and rotation on your back, mimicking the hip movement without any grips or opponent (50 reps).
    2. Grip and Hip Isolation: From closed guard, practice establishing grips and then performing the hip elevation and rotation with light resistance from a partner (10 reps per side).
    3. Leg Placement Drill: From closed guard, practice the precise placement of the left foot on the hip crease and the right hook under the knee pit, with no movement (20 reps per side).
    4. Controlled Sweep (0% Resistance): Partner lies on their back, you execute the Flower Sweep with perfect biomechanics, they offer no resistance (10 reps per side).
    5. Controlled Sweep (25% Resistance): Partner offers light, predictable resistance to the sweep, allowing you to feel the timing and weight transfer (15 reps per side).
    6. Controlled Sweep (50% Resistance): Partner actively tries to defend but allows you to complete the sweep after some engagement (20 reps per side).
    7. Live Rolling (90% Intensity): Attempt the sweep in live rolling against a resisting opponent, focusing on correct mechanics over completion (5 minutes, focus on sweep attempts).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured up, creating a gap between their hips and yours.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard by stacking or pressuring forward.
    • When you have established good control of their sleeves and collar, preventing easy escapes.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stacking Pass: If the opponent senses the sweep and attempts to stack you, maintain your grip and drive your hips forward while simultaneously bringing your knees to your chest to create a strong frame, preventing them from crushing you.
    • Knee Shield/Guard Recovery: If the sweep is failing and the opponent is regaining base, immediately bring your right knee up to create a knee shield against their chest and recover your guard by bringing your left leg back into closed guard.
    • Re-guarding: If you lose grips or hip position during the sweep, immediately shrimp your hips away and re-establish closed guard or transition to a different guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Flower Sweep?

    Wrist pain during the Flower Sweep often stems from gripping the opponent's sleeve too tightly with your fingers extended, putting your wrist in a vulnerable, extended position. Instead, grip the sleeve with your knuckles pointing upwards, creating a more neutral wrist alignment, and focus on using your forearm and elbow to control their arm's angle, not just your fingers.

    Q: How can I Flower Sweep against a much larger and heavier opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise timing become paramount. Focus on elevating your hips significantly to create space and ensure your left foot is firmly planted on their hip crease, acting as a strong fulcrum. Use your grips to pull their weight *across* your body, not just *towards* you, emphasizing rotational force to off-balance their superior mass.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate the Flower Sweep, and what if they are too close?

    The Flower Sweep is most effective when the opponent is postured up, creating a vertical line from their hips to their shoulders, and has some distance between their hips and yours. If they are too close, you cannot effectively elevate your hips to create the necessary angle. In this scenario, focus on creating space by shrimping your hips away or transitioning to an open guard to re-establish the ideal distance before attempting the sweep.

Flower Sweep: A Biomechanically Precise BJJ Guide for White Belts

This sweep initiates from closed guard, aiming to invert your opponent and take top position. White belts often struggle by relying on upper body strength rather than hip mechanics.

The key insight is to use your hips to create leverage, lifting and turning your opponent's base away from you.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure a double sleeve grip: left hand on opponent's right sleeve, right hand on opponent's left sleeve. Keep your knees tight to your opponent's hips.
    2. Shift your weight slightly to your left hip, creating a small gap.
    3. Insert your right foot (sole of the foot) on the outside of your opponent's left hip, pressing your heel towards your own glute.
    4. Simultaneously, thread your left arm (underneath your opponent's right arm) to grip their right pants or belt on the same side.
    5. Elevate your hips, driving your right knee upwards and slightly forward, while simultaneously pulling your opponent's left sleeve towards your right hip.
    6. Use your left arm to pull their pants/belt, creating a turning motion. Your right foot on their hip acts as a fulcrum.
    7. As they begin to turn, extend your legs to push them over your hips. Your hips should angle towards your opponent's head.
    8. Immediately transition to S-mount or knee-on-belly as they fall, maintaining control of their upper body with your arms.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Gripping only the sleeves and pulling with arms: This strains the rotator cuff and elbow ligaments, causing tears. Instead, use your legs to create the sweeping motion and maintain sleeve control.
    • Extending legs straight up to lift opponent: This can hyperextend knees and damage ACLs. Instead, drive your knee upwards and forward, using hip elevation.
    • Turning hips away from opponent during sweep: This exposes your back and can lead to injury if they fall awkwardly. Keep your hips angled towards their head throughout the sweep.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice leg and hip movements without a partner, focusing on the hip lift and leg extension. (10 reps each side, 0% resistance).
    2. Partner drill - static grips: Your partner remains still. Practice securing grips, hip elevation, and leg extension. (5 reps each side, 10% resistance).
    3. Partner drill - controlled sweep: Partner offers light resistance, allowing you to execute the sweep. Focus on timing the hip lift and pull. (5 reps each side, 25% resistance).
    4. Partner drill - passive defense: Partner offers no resistance to the sweep, but defends the post-sweep position. Focus on transitioning smoothly. (5 reps each side, 50% resistance).
    5. Live rolling - limited scope: Practice Flower Sweep only when in closed guard, opponent attempts to pass. Focus on setting up and executing the sweep. (5 attempts, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling - full integration: Attempt Flower Sweep whenever the opportunity arises from closed guard. Focus on reacting to opponent's movements and transitioning after the sweep. (10 attempts, 90% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is posturing down with their weight forward in your closed guard.
    • Opponent has a strong grip on your sleeves, making it difficult to break.
    • Opponent is attempting to stack you, creating an opening for a hip escape.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape and Recover Guard: As the sweep begins, immediately hip escape away and bring your knees back to your chest to re-establish closed guard. This negates the sweeping angle.
    • Knee Shield / Shin Across: If you feel the sweep starting, place your shin across your opponent's hip and use your knee to create distance. This prevents the leverage needed for the sweep.
    • Posture Up and Grip Break: If the sweep is anticipated, quickly posture up by straightening your back and using your arms to break their sleeve grips. This removes their control and leverage.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to Flower Sweep?

    Wrist pain often occurs because you're relying too much on pulling the sleeves with your hands. The Flower Sweep's power comes from hip elevation and leg extension, not brute arm strength. To fix this, focus on driving your knee upwards and forwards while simultaneously pushing off their hip with your foot. Keep your wrists neutral and let your hips do the work.

    Q: How do I Flower Sweep against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is even more critical. Ensure your closed guard is tight, preventing them from easily posturing up. When you initiate the sweep, focus on using your hips to lift and turn their base, rather than trying to lift their entire body. Your foot on their hip acts as a pivot point; use it to drive their weight over their own base.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Flower Sweep in a BJJ match?

    The Flower Sweep is most effective when your opponent is leaning forward into your closed guard, or when they are attempting to stack you. Look for moments where their weight is distributed over their hips and knees. Avoid attempting it when they have strong, upright posture, as you'll lack the necessary leverage.

Flower Sweep: The Essential BJJ Sweep for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Flower Sweep is initiated from closed guard, aiming to reverse position and gain top control. It leverages hip extension and leg manipulation to unbalance the opponent.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of coordinated body movement and an over-reliance on brute strength. This leads to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key mechanical insight is creating a strong, stable base with your hips while simultaneously isolating and controlling the opponent's leg, thereby generating rotational force.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish a high collar grip with your right hand, pulling opponent's head towards your chest. Simultaneously, establish a sleeve grip with your left hand on opponent's left sleeve, close to the elbow.
    2. Place the sole of your right foot on the opponent's left hip crease, angling your hips towards your left.
    3. Slide your left foot up opponent's left thigh until your left shin is perpendicular to their thigh.
    4. Extend your right leg forcefully, pushing off the opponent's hip to create distance and leverage.
    5. As you extend your right leg, simultaneously begin to rotate your hips to your left, drawing their left arm across your body with your collar grip.
    6. Use your left foot on their thigh to 'kick' their leg outwards and away from your body, further disrupting their base.
    7. As their base breaks, drive your hips up and to your left, bringing your right knee towards their armpit while your left leg sweeps through to establish side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending your right leg without proper hip rotation: This can hyperextend your knee, leading to ACL or MCL tears. Always initiate hip rotation before full leg extension.
    • Grabbing the opponent's arm with your left hand instead of the sleeve: This allows the opponent to posture up easily, potentially leading to a shoulder strain or dislocation if they resist forcefully. Maintain a firm sleeve grip.
    • Attempting to lift the opponent with your arms: This is inefficient and strains your shoulders and back. Focus on using your legs and hips to generate the sweep, keeping your arms in controlling positions.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip rotation and leg extension movements without a partner (10 reps each side).
    2. Mirror drilling: With a partner, practice the grips and initial hip movement, with partner providing minimal resistance (20 reps total).
    3. Static drill: Partner maintains a stable posture while you execute the sweep, focusing on grip and hip mechanics. Partner offers no resistance to the sweep itself (15 reps each side).
    4. Light resistance drill: Partner actively tries to prevent the sweep by posturing up or keeping their base tight, but without actively countering (10 reps each side).
    5. Flow drill: Execute the Flower Sweep from closed guard, and if unsuccessful, transition to another guard or sweep. Partner actively defends but allows for sweep attempts (5 minutes).
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the Flower Sweep during sparring, focusing on applying the learned mechanics against dynamic resistance (2 rounds).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is posturing up from closed guard, creating space between your chests.
    • Opponent is attempting to pass your guard by widening their base or leaning forward.
    • Opponent is holding your ankles or knees in a static closed guard position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): As the opponent attempts to extend their right leg, immediately hip escape to your left, creating space and breaking the sweep's leverage. This prevents your right foot from having a solid base on their hip.
    • Knee Shield: If the opponent places their right foot on your hip, immediately bring your left knee up to create a knee shield between your hips and their foot, blocking the sweep attempt.
    • Base Reinforcement: If the opponent attempts the sweep, immediately drive your weight forward and down, widening your base and lowering your hips to the mat, making yourself heavier and more difficult to sweep.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my right knee hurt when I try the Flower Sweep?

    Your right knee is likely hurting because you are hyperextending it when you push off the opponent's hip. The correct biomechanics involve initiating hip rotation *before* fully extending your leg. Focus on turning your hips towards your left as you push with your right foot, rather than just pushing straight. This allows your knee to track safely.

    Q: How can I Flower Sweep a much bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing become even more critical. Ensure your collar grip is tight and you're pulling their head towards you to break their posture. The key is to isolate their leg with your foot on the hip and your other foot on their thigh, then use a sharp, rotational hip extension to unbalance them. Do not try to muscle them; focus on the precise sequence of movements to create the sweep.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Flower Sweep in BJJ?

    The Flower Sweep is most effective when the opponent is trying to posture up or when they are leaning forward to pass your guard. If they are too heavy and flat on their back, it's harder to generate the leverage. Look for moments where their weight is forward, and you can use your hips and legs to create a rotational unbalancing action.

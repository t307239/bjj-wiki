Flower Sweep: The White Belt Biomechanics Guide to BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This sweep originates from closed guard, aiming to invert your opponent and create an advantageous position for you to advance. White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of leverage and body mechanics.

The key is not brute force, but precise hip and leg movement to disrupt their base and create an opening.

Mastering the hip escape and the precise timing of your leg's extension are crucial for a successful Flower Sweep.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure a strong grip on your opponent's left shin with your right hand (collar grip on their right lapel with your left hand is optional but recommended for control).
    2. Place your left foot on their left hip crease, applying slight pressure to create space and angle your hips.
    3. With your right hand, pull their left shin across your body, towards your left hip.
    4. Extend your left leg sharply, using your left foot on their hip to push their base away.
    5. Simultaneously, pivot your hips to the right, driving your left hip towards their left hip.
    6. As their weight shifts, your right leg, which was controlling their shin, now swings through and under their left leg.
    7. Immediately transition to a top position, such as side control or mount, as they fall.
    8. Maintain hip connection and control their posture to prevent them from recovering their guard.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Hyperextending the knee: Attempting to 'kick' their leg away with your sweeping leg can cause hyperextension of your own knee, potentially tearing the ACL or PCL if your leg is caught.
    • Correction: Focus on a controlled extension and a sweeping motion, not a forceful kick. Keep a slight bend in your knee throughout the movement.
    • Spraining the ankle: If your sweeping leg gets trapped or you land awkwardly, you can twist your ankle, leading to ligament sprains.
    • Correction: Ensure your foot is flexed and your ankle is stable as you swing your leg through. Be prepared to absorb impact with your hip and core.
    • Lower back strain: Trying to lift or 'throw' your opponent with your upper body instead of using your hips and legs can strain your lower back.
    • Correction: Emphasize hip rotation and the extension of your legs to generate the sweep. Keep your core engaged.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Movement (0%): Practice the hip escape and leg swing motion without a partner. Focus on the timing and coordination of hip rotation and leg extension. (20 reps each side)
    2. Partnered Static Drill (25%): With a partner lying on their back, practice securing grips and executing the leg swing and hip movement, with them offering minimal resistance. (10 reps each side)
    3. Controlled Sweep (50%): Your partner lies on their back and allows you to attempt the sweep, resisting gently as you make contact. Focus on the mechanics. (15 reps each side)
    4. Assisted Sweep (75%): Your partner actively tries to defend, but allows you to complete the sweep if your mechanics are correct. Focus on the transition to the top position. (10 reps each side)
    5. Light Sparring (90%): Spar with a partner who understands the sweep and doesn't 'fight' it too hard, allowing you opportunities to practice the setup and execution. (5-minute rounds, 3 rounds)
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Attempt the Flower Sweep during regular sparring. Focus on integrating it into your guard game. (10-minute rounds, 2 rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up and leaning forward, creating an opportunity to disrupt their base.
    • When your opponent tries to pass your guard by stacking you or pressuring heavily forward.
    • When you have established a strong closed guard and can control their hips and legs effectively.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stacking Defense: If your opponent attempts the sweep, immediately drive your hips down and forward, creating a strong base and preventing them from getting under your legs. Keep your back flat on the mat.
    • Leg Release and Base Recovery: As they attempt to swing their leg through, quickly release your trapped leg and establish a wide base with your knees and feet on the mat to prevent them from getting leverage.
    • Opposite Side Hip Escape: If they begin to sweep, immediately hip escape to your right (if they are sweeping your left leg). This creates distance and makes it harder for them to generate the necessary leverage.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my right wrist hurt when I try the Flower Sweep?

    Wrist pain often stems from gripping too tightly with a flexed wrist or from the pressure of your opponent's weight on your hand. Ensure your wrist is neutral or slightly extended, not bent. Use a firm but not death grip. Consider a lapel grip with your other hand to help stabilize your posture and reduce direct pressure on your wrist.

    Q: How do I Flower Sweep against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on using your hips to create the initial angle and space. Extend your legs with precision rather than power. Your goal is to unbalance them, not to lift them. A strong grip on their ankle and a sharp extension of your sweeping leg will be more effective than trying to muscle them.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate the Flower Sweep from closed guard?

    The optimal time is when your opponent is leaning forward, trying to posture up or pass your guard by driving their weight downwards. This forward pressure makes their base unstable. If they are flat on their back or too far away, the sweep will be difficult to set up and execute effectively.

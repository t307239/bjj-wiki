Flower Sweep: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide to BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Flower Sweep originates from closed guard and aims to reverse the position, placing you on top.

White belts often fail by relying on strength or jerky movements, leading to loss of balance or injury.

The key insight is using the opponent's weight against them by creating a controlled, lever-like action.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grips: Secure opponent's right sleeve with your left hand (palm up, thumb inside) and their right collar with your right hand (palm down, thumb outside).
    2. Hip Angle: Create a slight angle by shifting your hips to your left, away from the opponent's right side.
    3. Leg Placement: Bring your left knee towards your chest, creating a strong frame with your shin against the opponent's right hip.
    4. Weight Transfer: Simultaneously, begin to lift your hips off the mat, driving your left knee upwards and slightly forwards.
    5. Leverage Point: Use your right hand on the collar to pull the opponent's upper body diagonally towards your left hip.
    6. Sweep Action: As you lift your hips and pull the collar, extend your left leg forcefully, driving your shin into their hip and sweeping their base outwards.
    7. Finishing Posture: As they fall, immediately drive your hips down, maintain your grips, and secure a dominant top position (e.g., side control).

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hip Movement: Arching your back excessively (lumbar hyperextension) instead of lifting your hips can strain or injure the lower back.
    • Correct: Focus on engaging your glutes and core to lift your pelvis off the mat.
    • Sleeve Grip Failure: Gripping the opponent's sleeve too loosely or with a weak wrist (ulnar deviation) can cause your grip to slip and your wrist to bend unnaturally (radial deviation), leading to ligament strain.
    • Correct: Maintain a strong, "cupped" grip with your thumb wrapped around the opponent's wrist, keeping your forearm and wrist aligned.
    • Knee Driving Straight Down: Driving your left knee directly downwards into the opponent's hip without the upward lifting motion reduces leverage and can cause knee joint shear, potentially damaging the meniscus or ACL.
    • Correct: Initiate the sweep by driving your knee upwards and then sweeping outwards, using your hips as a fulcrum.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Lift & Sweep Motion: Practice the hip lift and leg extension motion without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Partnered Grip & Hip Lift: With a compliant partner in closed guard, practice securing grips and lifting hips. (25 reps)
    3. Controlled Sweep Execution (0% Resistance): Partner offers no resistance, allowing you to focus on the full motion and hip angle. (25 reps)
    4. Light Resistance Sweep (25% Resistance): Partner lightly resists by keeping hips tight, practice driving through the sweep. (25 reps)
    5. Moderate Resistance Sweep (50% Resistance): Partner actively tries to prevent the sweep by posturing or shrimping. (25 reps)
    6. Live Rolling Application (75-90% Resistance): Attempt the sweep during live rolling, focusing on timing and commitment. (5-10 attempts)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent has their knees close together in your closed guard.
    • When the opponent is posturing up, creating space to attack their base.
    • When the opponent attempts a guard pass, leaving their weight distribution exposed.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: If the opponent attempts the sweep, they can "stack" their weight by driving their hips forward and downward onto your knees, neutralizing the sweep attempt.
    • Shrimping Away: As the sweep is initiated, the opponent can "shrimp" their hips away, creating distance and breaking your leverage point.
    • Standing Up/Passing: If the sweep is slow or poorly executed, the opponent can stand up and attempt to pass your guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Flower Sweep?

    Wrist pain often stems from a weak sleeve grip or incorrect hand positioning. If your left hand grips the opponent's sleeve too loosely or with your palm facing outwards, their arm can straighten and hyperextend your wrist. Ensure a strong, "cupped" grip with your thumb wrapped around the sleeve and your forearm aligned with your wrist, maintaining this structure as you pull.

    Q: How do I Flower Sweep against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paramount. Focus on creating a strong frame with your left shin against their hip and using your right collar grip to pull their upper body towards your left hip, creating an off-balance angle. Your hip lift needs to be explosive, and the leg sweep needs to be a sharp, driving motion, not a slow pull. Think of it as using their size against them by creating a lever.

    Q: Should I always pull with my collar grip during the Flower Sweep?

    The collar grip is crucial for controlling the opponent's upper body and creating the necessary off-balance. However, it's not just a "pull"; it's a diagonal pull towards your left hip, synchronized with your hip lift and leg sweep. If you only pull straight, you'll likely lose balance. The combined action of lifting your hips, driving your knee, and pulling diagonally is what generates the sweep.

Ankle Lock Technique Guide for BJJ White Belts

BLUE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

The Ankle Lock is a submission targeting the opponent's ankle joint, aiming to hyperextend or invert it. It's typically initiated from controlling the opponent's leg, often from within their guard.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body control and an instinct to 'muscle' the submission. This leads to sloppy mechanics and potential injury for both parties.

The key insight is using your hip to create a fulcrum and your body weight to control the opponent's leg, isolating the ankle rather than relying on brute strength.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a position where you control one of the opponent's legs (e.g., opponent is on their back, you are between their legs or to their side), secure a "Double Grip" on the opponent's ankle. This means your hands cup the heel and the top of the foot, fingers pointing towards the toes.
    2. Position your torso facing perpendicular to the opponent's leg, with your hips positioned to create a fulcrum point under their ankle bone (malleolus).
    3. Drive your chest down onto the opponent's thigh, pinning it to the mat, to prevent them from straightening their leg.
    4. Hook your opposite leg (the one not near the opponent's attacked leg) behind the opponent's knee, creating a "figure-four" grip with your legs around their attacked leg.
    5. Shift your weight forward, driving your hips into the opponent's ankle. Your body should form a tight "C" shape around their leg.
    6. To apply the lock, elevate your hips upwards and slightly backwards. This motion hyperextends the opponent's ankle joint.
    7. Maintain constant pressure with your chest on the thigh and keep your grip firm on the ankle throughout the hip elevation to prevent escape or slippage.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying pressure by "pulling up" on the opponent's foot with your hands while your hips are not properly aligned under their ankle. This can lead to a hyperextension of the attacker's own wrist or elbow joint, as well as ineffective pressure on the opponent's ankle.
    • Allowing the opponent to straighten their leg by not maintaining chest pressure on their thigh. This can cause the attacker's knee to buckle inwards, risking a knee injury (ligament tear) to the attacker.
    • Gripping the opponent's foot too low, near the toes, instead of the heel and ankle bone. This allows the opponent to easily pull their foot out and can put undue stress on the attacker's fingers and wrist, potentially leading to a sprain or tendonitis.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Isolation (0%): Practice the grip and body positioning with no resistance. Hold the grip and simulate the hip drive motion for 30 seconds. Repeat 10 times.
    2. Partnered Isolation (0%): With a compliant partner, practice securing the double grip on their ankle and establishing your body position. Focus on hip placement and chest pressure. Hold for 10 seconds. Repeat 5 times per side.
    3. Controlled Movement (25%): Partner remains still. You execute the full motion of the Ankle Lock, focusing on the hip drive and leg control. Ensure a smooth, controlled movement. Repeat 5 times per side.
    4. Assisted Application (50%): Partner offers slight resistance by trying to pull their leg away. You focus on maintaining control and applying the hip drive. Tap immediately if you feel resistance. Repeat 5 times per side.
    5. Live Drilling - Positional (75%): Start from a position where the Ankle Lock is available (e.g., you have side control and can reach a leg). Partner defends actively but does not attempt other submissions. Focus on applying the technique correctly. Tap when caught. Repeat 3 rounds of 1 minute.
    6. Live Rolling (90-100%): Attempt the Ankle Lock during live rolling when the opportunity arises. Focus on correct mechanics and timing, not on forcing the submission. Be prepared to defend counters. Repeat 5 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When you have secured control of the opponent's leg from the side or from within their guard, and they are attempting to posture up or escape.
    • During transitions where the opponent's leg becomes isolated, such as when they attempt to pass your guard or you are in side control and can reach for a leg.
    • When the opponent is on their back, and you are in a position to isolate and attack one of their legs, like from a half guard or a controlling position on the mat.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Leg Straighten and Hip Escape: The opponent attempts to straighten their leg by driving their hips away from you and simultaneously pulling their foot towards their own body. This elongates the lever and reduces the angle of attack.
    • Stand Up and Unwind: If the opponent is holding your ankle, you can attempt to stand up by driving your weight forward and lifting your leg. As you stand, you can then use your free leg to "kick" their grip off or step over their head to unwind.
    • Roll to the Other Side: If the opponent has secured your ankle, you can attempt to roll your body towards the side of the attacked leg. This motion can help break their grip and relieve pressure on your ankle joint, allowing you to escape.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try to do an Ankle Lock on someone?

    Your ankle likely hurts because you are not applying the Ankle Lock correctly, putting stress on your own joints. The primary cause is often a lack of proper hip placement under the opponent's ankle bone. Instead of using your hips as a fulcrum, you might be trying to bend your own ankle or wrist to create leverage, leading to hyperextension or sprains. Ensure your hips are directly beneath the opponent's ankle bone and that you are driving your hips upwards, not bending your own foot.

    Q: How can I Ankle Lock a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise mechanics are paramount. Focus on securing a very tight "Double Grip" on their heel and ankle bone. Crucially, ensure your hips are positioned perfectly under their ankle and that you drive your chest into their thigh to prevent them from posturing up and creating space. The key is to use your body weight and hip movement to create the hyperextension, rather than trying to match their strength. Control their leg with your entire body, not just your arms.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt an Ankle Lock as a white belt?

    As a white belt, the best time to attempt an Ankle Lock is when you have achieved a dominant position where the opponent's leg is isolated and they are not actively defending with their upper body. This often occurs when you have established side control and can reach for a leg, or when you are in their closed guard and have managed to break their posture and isolate one leg. Prioritize control and correct mechanics over attempting it in chaotic scrambles where the risk of injury is higher.

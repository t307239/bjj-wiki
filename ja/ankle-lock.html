Ankle Lock Technique for BJJ White Belts

BLUE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

The Ankle Lock is a submission targeting the opponent's ankle joint, aiming to hyperextend it for a tap.

White belts often rush, leading to uncontrolled movements and potential injury for themselves and their partner.

The key is isolating the ankle and using hip pressure to create torque, not brute strength.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting Position: From open guard or half guard, secure your opponent's ankle with your arms.
    2. Grip: Clasp your hands around the opponent's shin, just above the ankle bone, with your thumbs on the outside of their leg.
    3. Hip Angle: Drive your hips forward and slightly to the side, creating a 45-degree angle relative to the opponent's leg.
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your hips, using your body weight to control the opponent's leg.
    5. Leg Placement: Place your other leg across the opponent's groin, securing their hips and preventing them from turning out.
    6. Arm Action: Pull your clasped hands towards your chest, keeping your elbows tucked in.
    7. Hip Extension: Extend your hips to create the torque on the ankle joint, aiming for a controlled finish.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hip Drive: Driving hips straight forward without a slight angle risks hyperextending your own knee if the opponent turns.
    • Over-rotation of Ankle: Forcing the ankle to rotate internally or externally can tear the lateral or medial collateral ligaments.
    • Gripping Too High: Gripping the calf muscle instead of the shin allows the opponent to easily pull their foot out, losing the submission.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Drill (0%): Practice the grip and hip movement without a partner, focusing on smooth transitions.
    2. Partner Drill (25%): With a compliant partner, practice securing the grip and driving hips, with minimal pressure.
    3. Controlled Isolation (50%): Partner offers slight resistance, focus on maintaining the correct hip angle and leg isolation.
    4. Controlled Submission (75%): Apply the ankle lock with controlled pressure, stopping immediately upon feeling resistance.
    5. Simulated Live (90%): Practice the entry and finish against a resisting opponent, but with clear communication to stop.
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Attempt the ankle lock during live rolling, prioritizing safety and technique over submission.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is in your closed guard and you can isolate one leg.
    • From half guard when you can secure their ankle as they try to pass.
    • When transitioning between positions and an ankle is exposed.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Escape: If the opponent drives hips too far, quickly turn your hips to face them, creating space to pull your leg free.
    • Stacking: If the opponent's hips are too far away, drive your weight forward and stack them to collapse their base.
    • Grip Break: Use your free hand to peel the opponent's grip off your shin or ankle.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my ankle lock feel weak and not tap my opponent?

    Your hip angle is likely incorrect. Instead of driving straight forward, angle your hips 45 degrees to the side of the opponent's leg. This allows your hips to create the necessary torque around the ankle joint, rather than relying solely on arm strength.

    Q: How can I secure an ankle lock against a much larger opponent?

    Focus on controlling their base and preventing them from posturing up. Use your legs to create a tight frame, specifically placing your free leg across their hips to limit their movement. Precise hip angling and controlled hip extension are crucial for leverage.

    Q: The optimal time is when your opponent attempts to pass your guard and their weight is committed to one leg, leaving the other exposed. Look for moments when they are off-balance or have their legs too close together, making it difficult for them to defend.

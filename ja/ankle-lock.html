The ankle lock is a submission targeting the opponent's ankle joint, aiming to hyperextend or invert it for a tap.

White belts often struggle due to improper hip pressure and grip, leading to ineffective submissions or injury.

The key is isolating the ankle and creating leverage with your hips, not just your arms, to control the limb.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a stable open guard or leg entanglement, secure your opponent's ankle with your forearm pressing into the back of their heel (shin-to-heel grip).
    2. Trap their foot between your armpit and chest, ensuring your bicep is tight against their Achilles tendon.
    3. Place your secondary hand on their shin, fingers pointing towards their knee, creating a "figure-four" grip on the ankle.
    4. Drive your hips forward and slightly to the side, aligning your body to create a straight line of force through the ankle.
    5. Extend your legs, straightening your back, while simultaneously pulling your secondary hand's grip towards your chest.
    6. Your hips should be elevated, creating a fulcrum. Your chest should be tight against their shin.
    7. Apply controlled pressure by pushing your hips forward and squeezing your grip, creating plantar flexion and inversion.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly gripping the foot: Grabbing the toes or the top of the foot allows the opponent to escape and can lead to a sprained toe or foot fracture.
    • Applying pressure with straight legs: This puts excessive strain on your own lower back and knees, and reduces leverage, potentially causing a knee ligament tear for the opponent.
    • Jerking motion: Sudden, uncontrolled movements can damage the opponent's anterior talofibular ligament (common ankle sprain) or even cause a fracture.
    • Not isolating the ankle: If the knee is not controlled, the opponent can twist out, risking their ACL or meniscus.
    • Excessive rotation: Forcing rotation can damage the lateral ankle ligaments, leading to severe sprains or fractures.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the grip and hip movement without a partner, focusing on the body mechanics. 20 reps each side.
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Partner lies flat. You practice the setup, grip, and hip extension. 10 reps each side.
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to the grip and foot isolation. 5 reps each side.
    4. Partner drill (controlled escape): Partner attempts a basic escape from the initial position. You practice maintaining control and applying pressure. 5 reps each side.
    5. Live rolling (limited leg lock attempts): Focus on securing the ankle lock position. 5 minutes, 50% resistance.
    6. Live rolling (full leg lock attempts): Attempt the ankle lock when the opportunity arises. 5 minutes, 75-90% resistance.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is on their hands and knees and you have a leg trapped in your closed guard or half guard.
    • During scrambles where you gain control of a single leg.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard and their weight is on their leg.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Escape the grip by shrimping your hips away and rotating your foot outwards.
    • If caught, immediately try to stand up and pull your leg free, minimizing ankle flexion.
    • If the opponent is too tight, roll in the direction of their hip to relieve pressure and potentially reverse the position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my ankle lock feel weak and my opponent doesn't tap?

    This is likely due to insufficient hip engagement. Instead of just pulling with your arms, focus on driving your hips forward and upward, creating a strong fulcrum. Ensure your bicep is tight against the Achilles tendon and your secondary hand is pulling the shin towards your chest to maximize leverage.

    Q: How can I apply an ankle lock against a much larger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on securing a very tight grip and isolating their ankle completely, preventing them from using their size to bridge or stack. Drive your hips forward with maximum force and ensure your body is aligned to create a straight line of pressure through their ankle joint.

    Q: When is the right time to attempt an ankle lock without getting swept or countered?

    The best times are when your opponent is on all fours and you can isolate a single leg, or when they are actively trying to pass your guard and their weight distribution is unfavorable. Avoid attempting it when you are in a compromised position, as it leaves you vulnerable to sweeps and other submissions.

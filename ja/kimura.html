Kimura Technique Guide for BJJ White Belts

The Kimura is a shoulder lock applied from various positions, most commonly closed guard, aiming to hyperextend the opponent's shoulder joint.

White belts often rush, failing to establish proper body control and creating openings for escape or counter-attacks.

Success hinges on isolating the opponent's arm and creating a strong mechanical lever with your hips and core.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure your opponent's arm by placing your right forearm across their bicep and your left hand gripping your own right wrist (figure-four grip).
    2. Simultaneously, your opponent's left leg must be trapped between your hips, preventing them from posturing up or creating space.
    3. Angle your hips 45 degrees to your left, lifting your hips off the mat slightly to create tension on the trapped arm.
    4. Your right knee should drive into your opponent's left hip, preventing them from turning into you.
    5. Maintain a tight figure-four grip, keeping your elbows tucked close to your body.
    6. Begin to lift your opponent's elbow towards the ceiling by extending your hips and simultaneously rotating your torso to your left.
    7. Apply upward pressure with your hips and a slight inward rotation of your shoulders to create shoulder hyperextension.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying outward force on the opponent's arm with your own arm: This can sprain or tear the rotator cuff or labrum of your own shoulder due to excessive abduction and external rotation.
    • Trying to force the submission by pulling the arm up without hip elevation: This puts immense strain on the opponent's shoulder and elbow, risking ligament tears, and often fails to create sufficient leverage.
    • Not controlling the opponent's hips and base: This allows them to shrimp out, turn into you, or bridge, negating the submission attempt and potentially leading to injury for both parties.
    • Gripping the opponent's wrist instead of your own wrist in the figure-four: This creates a weak grip and allows the opponent to easily pull their arm out, while also risking wrist hyperextension for yourself.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the figure-four grip and hip elevation with no resistance (10 reps).
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Secure the Kimura grip and practice hip elevation and rotation without applying pressure (10 reps per side).
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Establish the grip and execute the movement, allowing your partner to resist slightly by not fully extending their arm (25% resistance, 5 reps per side).
    4. Controlled submission drill: Work to finish the Kimura from closed guard, with your partner tapping early to avoid injury (50% resistance, 5 reps per side).
    5. Positional sparring: Practice the Kimura setup and finish from closed guard during positional sparring rounds (75% resistance, 2 minutes per side).
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the Kimura during live rolling, focusing on correct setup and execution rather than immediate submission (90-100% resistance, 5 minutes).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent postures up in your closed guard, extending their arms.
    • Opponent attempts to pass your guard, leaving an arm within reach.
    • During scrambles or transitions where an opponent's arm becomes isolated.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Escape to closed guard: If the Kimura is being applied from guard, the opponent can attempt to pull their arm back, create space by pushing your hips away, and re-establish a closed guard.
    • Shrimp and turn away: The defender can shrimp their hips away from the attacker and turn their body towards the attacker's legs, relieving pressure on the shoulder.
    • Tuck the elbow and bridge: The defender can try to tuck their elbow in close to their body and bridge their hips to create a moment of slack, allowing them to pull their arm free.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Kimura?

    Your wrist likely hurts because you are gripping the opponent's wrist instead of your own wrist in the figure-four. This incorrect grip does not create a strong mechanical lever and can lead to hyperextension of your own wrist. Always ensure your grip is a secure figure-four by gripping your own wrist with your opposite hand.

    Q: How do I Kimura a bigger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on creating a strong mechanical advantage rather than brute strength. Ensure your hips are elevated and angled correctly (45 degrees) to create leverage. Use your legs to control their hips and prevent them from posturing up. The key is isolating the arm and using your core and hip drive to generate the turning motion.

    Q: When is the best time to go for a Kimura in BJJ?

    The Kimura is most effective when the opponent's arm is extended and isolated, making it difficult for them to defend. This often happens when they posture up in your guard, try to pass your guard by reaching, or during transitions where an arm is momentarily exposed. Avoid attempting it when their elbow is tucked tight to their body or when they have solid base and posture.

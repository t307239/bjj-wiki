The Bridge and Roll is a fundamental escape from bottom positions like side control or mount. It aims to reverse the position, putting you on top.

White belts often struggle because they rely on brute strength instead of leverage. This leads to inefficient movement and potential injury.

The core insight is using your hips to create space and your shoulders to drive forward, generating rotational momentum.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From bottom position (e.g., side control), establish a tight grip on your opponent's nearest arm's sleeve with your far-side hand, and their collar with your near-side hand. Your elbows should be tucked.
    2. Tilt your pelvis upwards, creating a 'bridge' by driving through your shoulders and the balls of your feet. Keep your knees bent and hips elevated.
    3. Simultaneously, drive your hips towards your opponent's head, creating a wedge with your hip bone against their torso.
    4. Shift your weight onto your opponent's chest, extending your bridged hips slightly further.
    5. Rotate your body by driving your shoulder closest to their head into their chest, initiating a rolling motion.
    6. As you roll, keep your grip on the sleeve and collar to maintain control and leverage.
    7. Continue the roll, aiming to end up in a dominant top position, usually guard or side control yourself.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect spinal hyperextension: Bridging too high with the lower back unsupported can cause lumbar spine strain or disc injury. The power should originate from the glutes and hamstrings, not just the lower back.
    • Neck injury from uncontrolled roll: Rolling without controlling the opponent's upper body can lead to your head or neck being trapped or twisted. Always maintain control of their arm and collar.
    • Wrist or elbow hyperextension: Gripping too loosely or allowing the opponent to straighten your arm during the roll can cause joint damage. Maintain tight, controlled grips throughout the movement.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Bridge: Practice bridging your hips off the mat without a partner. Focus on driving through your feet and engaging your glutes. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Bridge and Shrimp: From bottom guard, practice bridging and then shrimping your hips to create space. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Partner Assisted Bridge and Roll: Have a partner lie on top of you in side control. You bridge and they gently resist, allowing you to feel the hip drive and initial roll. (10 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled Bridge and Roll: With a partner in side control, perform the full bridge and roll with light resistance. Focus on smooth execution and maintaining grips. (20 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Bridge and Roll from Mount Escape: Practice the bridge and roll from mount with a partner who is applying moderate pressure. (30 reps, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling Application: Attempt the bridge and roll during live rolling situations when you find yourself in a disadvantageous bottom position. (Until successful or opponent counters, 90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent has established side control with a strong cross-face and you feel your hips are being flattened.
    • When an opponent is attempting to pass your guard and you are caught in a transitional, unstable position.
    • When an opponent has secured mount and is applying significant pressure, making it difficult to breathe or move.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass Defense: If the opponent tries to stack you during the bridge, immediately bring your knees to your chest and try to regain guard or escape to your knees.
    • Hip Escape and Reguard: If the roll is incomplete, use your free hip to escape and immediately try to establish guard again, preventing them from securing a dominant position.
    • Counter-Roll/Re-Attack: A skilled opponent might attempt to counter-roll with you. Be prepared to transition to a new submission or dominant position if they overcommit to the counter.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to Bridge and Roll in BJJ?

    Lower back pain during the bridge and roll often indicates that you are initiating the movement primarily from your lumbar spine rather than engaging your glutes and hamstrings. To fix this, focus on driving through the balls of your feet and squeezing your glutes to lift your hips, creating a stable bridge from your shoulders and feet, not just your lower back.

    Q: How can I do the Bridge and Roll BJJ escape against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes even more critical. Ensure your grips are extremely tight on their sleeve and collar to prevent them from easily breaking them. Focus on driving your hip bone into their sternum with maximum force and using your shoulder to create a strong rotational pivot, rather than relying on lifting their weight directly.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Bridge and Roll BJJ escape, and what if they anticipate it?

    The ideal time is when you feel your opponent settling into a dominant position and their weight distribution is favorable for a roll (e.g., they are leaning forward). If they anticipate it, they might try to 'stack' you. Be ready to immediately bring your knees to your chest and regain guard, or escape to your knees, rather than continuing the roll into a disadvantageous position.

Torreando Pass: Your First BJJ Guard Pass

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

The Torreando Pass starts from a standing or kneeling position against an opponent's closed guard, aiming to bypass their legs and achieve side control.

White belts often struggle because they focus on raw strength, neglecting the subtle weight shifts and hip angles needed for efficient movement.

The core principle is using your hips to drive around their legs, turning your body into a wedge, not pushing against their guard.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Secure your opponent's pant legs at the shins, just above the ankle bone, with your thumbs inside the fabric and fingers wrapped tightly.
    2. Base: Establish a wide, stable base with your knees slightly bent, feet shoulder-width apart, maintaining a low center of gravity.
    3. Hip Angle: Immediately angle your hips sharply to one side, away from the opponent's legs, creating a diagonal line between your hips and their hips.
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your lead leg, lifting your trailing leg slightly off the mat.
    5. Footwork: Step your trailing foot around the opponent's nearest leg, aiming to place it outside of their knee.
    6. Hip Drive: As your trailing foot lands, drive your hips forward and around, using your angled torso to clear their legs.
    7. Grip Release & Transition: As your hips clear, release your grips and immediately transition to a side control position by lowering your chest to their torso and securing a cross-face grip.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Trying to "pull" yourself around with arm strength: This can cause shoulder impingement or rotator cuff tears by creating excessive internal rotation and adduction.
    • Straightening your knees while driving hips: This puts immense stress on your knee joints, risking ligament tears (ACL/MCL) due to hyperextension.
    • Forcing your hips through without angling: This can lead to hip flexor strains or groin pulls from excessive adduction and internal rotation against resistance.
    • Not maintaining a low base: Falling backwards risks lumbar spine compression or tailbone injury if you land awkwardly.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling (0%): Practice the footwork and hip angling with no partner, focusing on smooth transitions between steps. (5 reps)
    2. Solo Gripping and Footwork (0%): With a gi on the mat, practice securing pant grips and performing the footwork and hip angles. (10 reps)
    3. Partnered Static Drilling (25%): Your partner lies on their back with legs extended. Practice securing grips, stepping around, and driving hips with minimal resistance. (5 reps per side)
    4. Partnered Light Resistance Drilling (50%): Partner attempts to keep legs closed. You focus on breaking grips and executing the pass with controlled hip drive. (5 reps per side)
    5. Partnered Guard Retention Drilling (75%): Partner actively tries to maintain guard. Focus on breaking grips, angling hips, and driving through. (3 reps per side)
    6. Live Rolling (90-100%): Attempt the Torreando Pass in sparring, prioritizing technique over winning the exchange. (3-5 attempts per round)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent's knees are not tightly locked, creating a small gap.
    • When the opponent's hips are relatively flat on the mat, limiting their ability to shrimp effectively.
    • When you have established a solid base and can maintain control of their legs.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Retention (Closed Guard): If the pass is initiated, the opponent can attempt to re-close their guard by shrimping their hips and bringing their knees together.
    • Sweep (Scissor Sweep/Hip Bump Sweep): If the passer's weight distribution is poor, the defender can use the momentum to initiate a sweep.
    • Submission (Triangle Choke/Omoplata): If the passer overcommits to the pass and leaves an arm or leg exposed, a submission can be attempted.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt after trying the Torreando Pass against a stronger opponent?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are trying to force your hips through by straightening your legs or pushing directly against their knees. This hyperextends the knee joint. Instead, always maintain a slight bend in your knees and focus on angling your hips to drive *around* their legs, not *through* them.

    Q: How do I Torreando Pass effectively when my opponent is much bigger than me?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are crucial. Focus on a very tight grip on the pant legs, just above the ankle. Angle your hips aggressively to create space, and use your torso to drive around their legs like a wedge. Do not try to lift or push their legs; instead, use your body's momentum to pivot past them.

    Q: Is it better to start the Torreando Pass from a kneeling or standing position?

    For white belts, starting from a low kneeling or "combat base" position is generally safer and more biomechanically sound. This allows you to maintain a lower center of gravity and better control of your base, reducing the risk of falling. Standing can be more efficient but requires more advanced balance and hip control.

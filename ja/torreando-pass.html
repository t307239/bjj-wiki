Mastering the Torreando Pass in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

The Torreando Pass starts from a closed guard or open guard situation, aiming to move past the opponent's legs to achieve side control.

White belts often fail by relying on brute strength or jerky movements, neglecting the necessary hip and footwork.

The key mechanical insight is using your hips to create space and your feet to pivot around the opponent's legs, not pushing through them.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure an over-under grip on the opponent's left leg with your right arm (right hip over their left hip).
    2. With your left hand, grip the opponent's right pant leg at the ankle, creating a strong frame.
    3. Drive your right hip forward, compressing the opponent's left knee towards their chest, breaking their guard's integrity.
    4. Shift your weight onto your left foot, lifting your right foot off the mat and stepping it outside the opponent's right hip.
    5. Simultaneously, drive your right knee towards the mat, creating a pivot point.
    6. Rotate your hips 90 degrees to your right, moving your body perpendicular to the opponent's legs.
    7. Release the pant grip and immediately establish a cross-face grip on the opponent's left shoulder with your left hand.
    8. Drive your right shoulder into the opponent's chest, maintaining hip pressure, and step your left foot forward to secure side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to 'smash' through the guard by pushing directly with your head and shoulders can strain your neck and upper back ligaments.
    • Forcing a knee into the opponent's hip without proper hip compression can lead to knee joint hyperextension or ligament tears.
    • Gripping only the opponent's foot without controlling the pant leg can result in wrist sprains from their attempts to escape or frame.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip compression and footwork sequence without a partner, focusing on hip angle and weight transfer. (50 reps each side)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Partner remains passive, allowing you to execute the grips and movement. Focus on smooth transitions. (25 reps each side)
    3. Light resistance drilling: Partner offers minimal resistance, perhaps by slightly resisting hip compression or leg movement. (25 reps each side)
    4. Controlled sparring (25% resistance): Focus solely on executing the Torreando Pass against a partner who is actively trying to maintain guard. Do not focus on submissions. (5 rounds)
    5. Controlled sparring (50% resistance): Increase partner's resistance, allowing them to actively defend and attempt escapes. Focus on maintaining the pass. (5 rounds)
    6. Live rolling (75% intensity): Apply the Torreando Pass as a primary objective during sparring, allowing for more dynamic defense and offense. (10 rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent's guard is open or semi-open, with hips not fully locked.
    • Opponent attempts a leg sweep or hip escape, creating a momentary opening.
    • Opponent's knees are not tightly pressed together, allowing for hip penetration.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape and Re-guard: If you feel the pass is being initiated, immediately hip escape your hips away and bring your legs back to form a guard.
    • Knee Shield/Inside Control: As the passer steps their foot outside, use your inside knee to create a shield and your opposite hand to control their head/shoulder, preventing them from flattening you.
    • Technical Stand-up: If the passer loses control or creates a large space, initiate a technical stand-up to regain guard or a neutral position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Torreando Pass from closed guard?

    Your wrist likely hurts because you are gripping the opponent's pant leg too tightly with just your fingers, or you are gripping too low on the foot. Instead, grip the pant leg higher up, near the knee, with your entire forearm engaged for support. This distributes the pressure across your forearm and reduces strain on the wrist joint.

    Q: How can I use the Torreando Pass against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on using leverage and timing rather than brute force. Instead of trying to 'smash' their legs, focus on creating a strong hip compression to break their posture and control their knee. Use your footwork to pivot quickly around their legs, keeping your hips low and driving your shoulder into their chest to maintain pressure.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Torreando Pass rather than a knee-slice pass?

    The Torreando Pass is most effective when the opponent has a relatively open guard or is attempting to create space for a sweep. If their legs are tightly closed and they are actively pressuring forward, a knee-slice pass might be more suitable. The Torreando Pass excels when you can quickly get your hips around their legs before they can fully re-establish their guard.

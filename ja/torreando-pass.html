The Torreando Pass is a standing guard pass initiated from closed guard, aiming to bypass the opponent's legs and achieve side control.

White belts often struggle because they lack the foundational hip mobility and weight transfer needed, leading to a predictable, static approach.

The core principle is using your hip rotation and weight shift to create an angle, forcing the opponent's hips to disengage from your center line.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Secure a deep grip on both of the opponent's pant legs, just above the knees, with your thumbs inside the pant cuff. Maintain a slight bend in your elbows.
    2. Posture: Stand with a wide base, knees slightly bent, and your hips low. Your spine should be neutral, not rounded or hyperextended.
    3. Weight Distribution: Shift approximately 70% of your weight onto your lead leg, which is the leg furthest from the opponent's hip you intend to pass.
    4. Hip Angle: Rotate your hips away from the opponent by approximately 45 degrees, creating a diagonal line between your hips and theirs.
    5. Lead Leg Movement: Step your lead leg back and to the side, clearing the opponent's knee line. Your stepping foot should land slightly behind your standing foot.
    6. Trail Leg Movement: As your lead leg moves, simultaneously drive your trail leg forward and across your body, bringing your hips past their hip.
    7. Grip Release & Transition: As your hips clear, release the grip on the pant leg closest to the opponent and immediately drive your shoulder into their hip to secure side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Injury: Attempting to step your lead leg back without rotating your hips first can cause your knee to buckle laterally. The collateral ligaments (MCL/LCL) are at high risk of sprain or tear. Correct: Always initiate hip rotation *before* stepping back.
    • Ankle Sprain: Stepping with your lead foot at an incorrect angle, or not having a wide enough base, can lead to your foot rolling inward or outward under your body weight. The ligaments of the ankle (ATFL, CFL) are vulnerable. Correct: Maintain a wide, stable base and ensure your stepping foot lands flat on the mat.
    • Lower Back Strain: Trying to force the pass by leaning forward with a rounded back instead of driving with your hips and legs can strain the erector spinae muscles and intervertebral discs. Correct: Keep your spine neutral and engage your core, using hip drive for forward momentum.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Rotation Drill: Practice rotating your hips 45 degrees away from an imaginary opponent while maintaining a wide base. (50 reps per side)
    2. Solo Leg Movement Drill: Practice stepping your lead leg back and across while maintaining hip angle, without any grips. (50 reps per side)
    3. Grip and Hip Rotation: Secure pant grips on a dummy or training partner lying down, practice hip rotation and the initial lead leg step. (Resistance: 0%, 25%)
    4. Full Torreando Movement: Perform the entire sequence of grips, hip rotation, leg movement, and simulated pass against a passive partner. (Resistance: 50%)
    5. Controlled Partner Drill: Work with a partner who offers light resistance, focusing on maintaining your structure and hip angle. (Resistance: 75%)
    6. Live Rolling Application: Attempt the Torreando Pass in rolling, focusing on the biomechanical principles and adapting to partner's reactions. (Resistance: 90%-100%)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is attempting to close their guard tightly, creating a stable platform.
    • When the opponent's hips are significantly lower than yours, creating an opportunity to attack their base.
    • When you have established a strong posture and your opponent is struggling to regain guard.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee Shield (Shin across opponent's hip): If the opponent anticipates the pass and extends their lead leg to place a shin across your hip, immediately stop your forward momentum and transition to a knee-slice pass or pressure pass.
    • Guard Recovery (Hip Escape): If you fail to create a sufficient angle and the opponent starts to hip escape towards you, release grips and maintain a strong base to prevent them from re-establishing guard or sweeping.
    • Stacking (if opponent's hips are too close): If the opponent is very close and preventing hip clearance, instead of Torreando, drive forward and stack them, forcing their hips away from you to transition to side control.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Torreando Pass?

    Knee pain during the Torreando Pass is often due to stepping your lead leg back without adequate hip rotation. This forces your knee to bear rotational stress it's not designed for. To fix this, ensure you rotate your hips 45 degrees away from your opponent *before* you step your lead leg back and across.

    Q: How can I use the Torreando Pass against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and angle are even more critical. Focus on securing very deep pant grips to control their base and rotating your hips aggressively to create a significant angle. Your goal is to get your hips past their center of gravity, making their size less of a factor.

    Q: Should I always release my grips at the same time when doing the Torreando Pass?

    No, the timing of grip release is fluid. You should release the grip on the pant leg closest to the opponent as your hips are clearing their hip line. The other grip is used to help steer their leg away and can be released as you secure your side control position.

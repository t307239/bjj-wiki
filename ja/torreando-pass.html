Torreando Pass: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

The Torreando Pass is a fundamental guard passing technique that starts from the opponent's closed guard. Its goal is to bypass the legs and establish side control.

White belts often fail due to a lack of understanding of weight distribution and hip mobility, leading to getting stuck or swept.

The key mechanical insight is using your hips to create a strong base and your shoulder to direct the pass, rather than relying solely on arm strength.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grips: Secure a double-grip on the opponent's pants, just above the knees. Your hands should be palms-down, fingers pointing towards their feet.
    2. Stance: Adopt a staggered stance with your left foot slightly forward. Your hips should be low, almost touching the mat, and your weight centered.
    3. Hip Angle: Rotate your hips 45 degrees to your right, angling your torso away from the opponent's legs.
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your lead (left) foot, lifting your right leg.
    5. Leg Swing: Swing your right leg in a wide arc, clearing the opponent's guard as you step it behind them.
    6. Shoulder Pressure: As you swing your leg, drive your left shoulder into the opponent's hip on the same side, creating downward pressure.
    7. Step Through: Step your right foot down behind the opponent's hips, landing with your feet parallel and shoulder-width apart.
    8. Establish Side Control: Immediately drive your hips down and forward, securing your left arm under their armpit and your right arm across their chest to establish side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Hyperextension: Attempting to "kick" the leg over the opponent's guard can lead to hyperextending your knee joint, potentially tearing the ACL or MCL. The correct movement is a controlled swing, not a forceful kick.
    • Ankle Sprain: Stepping awkwardly to "get around" the guard without proper hip rotation can cause your ankle to roll inward or outward, leading to a ligament sprain. Maintain a strong base and rotate your hips to clear the legs.
    • Lower Back Strain: Trying to muscle through the pass by arching your back excessively puts immense shear force on your lumbar spine. Keep your core engaged and use your hips and legs for propulsion.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Mobility Drill: Practice the hip rotation and leg swing motion without a partner. Focus on smooth, controlled movements. (50 reps per side)
    2. Solo Stance and Grip Drill: Practice assuming the staggered stance and securing the double-pant grip, focusing on a low center of gravity. (50 reps)
    3. Assisted Pass (0% Resistance): Partner lies on their back with legs extended. Practice the entire Torreando Pass motion, focusing on grip, stance, and hip/leg movement. Partner offers no resistance. (10 reps per side)
    4. Light Resistance Drill (25% Resistance): Partner gently tries to keep their guard closed. Focus on breaking the guard with hip pressure and executing the pass. (10 reps per side)
    5. Controlled Sparring (50% Resistance): Sparring with a focus on executing the Torreando Pass when the opportunity arises. Partner can defend but not actively counter aggressively. (3 x 3 minute rounds)
    6. Live Rolling (75-90% Resistance): Integrate the Torreando Pass into live sparring. Focus on timing and commitment to the pass against a resisting opponent. (5 x 5 minute rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent's closed guard with hips too far away to effectively control your base.
    • Opponent's legs are not tightly locked, allowing for a slight gap to exploit.
    • Opponent attempts to sit up or shrimp, creating an opening to circle past.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Retention (Hip Escape): If the passer attempts to circle, the defender can shrimp their hips away to re-establish guard or create space for a sweep.
    • Knee Shield: The defender can bring a knee up between their hips and the passer's chest, creating a frame to prevent the pass and potentially initiate a sweep.
    • Leg Re-Guard: As the passer steps through, the defender can attempt to re-hook a leg to maintain guard or transition to a different guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to Torreando Pass from closed guard?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are trying to "kick" your leg over the opponent's guard rather than performing a controlled swing. This excessive force and lack of hip rotation can cause hyperextension or strain on the knee joint. Focus on rotating your hips and swinging your leg smoothly, maintaining a stable base throughout the movement.

    Q: How do I Torreando Pass against a much bigger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, emphasize your hip mobility and low center of gravity. Instead of trying to force the pass, use your hips to create angles and leverage. Drive your shoulder into their hip to break their posture, and then use your leg swing to circle their base. Focus on precision and timing over brute strength.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Torreando Pass in BJJ?

    The Torreando Pass is most effective when the opponent's closed guard is not perfectly tight, or when they are trying to create space to open their guard. Look for moments when their hips are slightly away from yours, or when they begin to sit up or shrimp, as these create openings to execute the circling motion of the pass.

Torreando Pass: A White Belt's Biomechanically Accurate Guide

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

The Torreando Pass is a guard-passing technique that begins from an open guard or half guard position, aiming to bypass the opponent's legs to achieve side control.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of precise body control and a tendency to rely on brute force, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The core mechanical principle is creating off-balancing pressure with your hips and maintaining a strong base while your legs clear the opponent's guard. This allows you to sever the connection and move past.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Secure a strong grip on both of the opponent's pant legs just above the knee, ensuring your knuckles are facing upwards.
    2. Base: Establish a wide, stable base with your feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, and your weight distributed evenly.
    3. Hip Angle: Angle your hips approximately 45 degrees towards the opponent's right side (if passing to your right).
    4. Weight Transfer (Forward): Drive your weight forward, pressing your chest towards the opponent's hips, while keeping your back straight.
    5. Lead Leg Movement: Step your lead (right) foot forward and slightly to the outside of the opponent's right knee, clearing their guard.
    6. Trail Leg Movement: Simultaneously, bring your trail (left) foot forward and around, maintaining your base and hip angle.
    7. Grip Release & Transition: As your legs clear, release your pant grips and immediately drive your left hip down and forward, establishing side control and pinning their hip.
    8. Head Position: Keep your head up and on the side you are passing towards, creating a strong frame and preventing them from re-guarding.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Hyperextension: Attempting to 'kick' your leg through the guard with a straight knee can cause hyperextension of the knee joint, leading to ACL/MCL tears. The correct movement is a controlled step with a bent knee, driven by hip mobility.
    • Ankle and Foot Sprains: Planting your foot flat and twisting abruptly to turn your hips can lead to ankle ligament tears. The correct movement involves pivoting on the ball of your foot with controlled hip rotation.
    • Lower Back Strain: Rounding your back and 'hunching' over the opponent to create pressure strains the lumbar spine. Maintain a neutral spine and drive pressure through your hips and chest.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip & Leg Movement (0%): Practice the hip angle and leg stepping motion without any partner. Focus on smooth, controlled transitions. (20 reps each side)
    2. Grip & Hip Angle (0%): On a mat, assume the hip angle and practice grabbing phantom pant legs, focusing on the feel of the weight distribution. (20 reps each side)
    3. Partnered Static Pass (25%): With a compliant partner on their back, practice the grips and hip angle, then step your legs through and establish side control without them resisting. (10 reps each side)
    4. Partnered Light Resistance Pass (50%): Partner offers minimal resistance (e.g., lightly holding your arms or shoulders). Focus on driving through their resistance. (10 reps each side)
    5. Partnered Moderate Resistance Pass (75%): Partner actively tries to maintain their guard or hip escape. Work on breaking their grips and maintaining base. (5 reps each side)
    6. Live Rolling Integration (90%): Attempt the Torreando Pass during sparring when the opportunity arises, focusing on applying the learned mechanics. (5 attempts)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent has their knees close together, creating a tight closed guard that is difficult to break otherwise.
    • When the opponent attempts a sweep or submission from open guard, creating a moment of imbalance you can exploit.
    • When the opponent is attempting to establish a spider guard or lasso guard, leaving their hips slightly exposed.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Re-Guard: If the passer's lead leg is not cleared effectively, the defender can hook the passer's leg with their own foot and pull it back to re-establish guard.
    • Hip Escape and Knee Shield: The defender can hip escape away from the passer's pressure and use their knee to create a frame, preventing the passer from closing the distance.
    • Underhook and Bridge: If the passer gets too deep and their base is compromised, the defender can secure an underhook on the passer's arm and bridge their hips to create space or even sweep.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to Torreando Pass from closed guard?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are trying to 'kick' your leg through the opponent's closed guard. This puts excessive extension force on your knee joint. Instead, focus on stepping your lead leg forward and to the side with a bent knee, driving your hips forward to create space. The movement should originate from your hips, not your leg muscles.

    Q: How do I Torreando Pass against a much bigger opponent who has strong grips?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on severing their grip with precise hand fighting before attempting the pass. Use your hips to create angles and off-balance them slightly, making their grips weaker. Once you have a clean grip on their pants, drive your hips forward and use your body weight to push past their legs. Don't try to muscle through; use technique and leverage.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Torreando Pass during a BJJ match?

    The ideal time to attempt the Torreando Pass is when your opponent has committed to a specific guard structure, like a tight closed guard, or when they are actively trying to initiate a sweep or submission. This is when their weight distribution might be less stable, creating an opening. Avoid attempting it when they are actively defending and have their hips mobile.

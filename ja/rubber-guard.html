Rubber Guard: The Biomechanically Sound Approach for White Belts

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

Rubber Guard starts from closed guard, aiming to isolate an opponent's leg and create offensive angles.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of core stability and poor limb control, leading to immediate escapes.

The key is hip rotation and precise knee positioning to generate leverage and control, not brute strength.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish closed guard with both hooks inside the opponent's thighs. Maintain hip contact.
    2. Select one leg to enter Rubber Guard. Grip your own shin with both hands, fingers interlaced, just below the knee.
    3. Rotate your hips 45 degrees to your right (if using your right leg). Your left foot remains on the mat for base.
    4. Pull the gripped shin across your body, aiming to bring your knee towards your opposite shoulder. Your gripping hands drive the shin forward.
    5. Simultaneously, extend your free leg towards the opponent's opposite hip, creating a frame with your foot.
    6. Your free hand (left hand) grips the opponent's collar, specifically the lapel on the side of the leg you've entered.
    7. Maintain a strong core; avoid letting your hips collapse towards the mat. Keep your back flat.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Hyperextension: Actively pulling your own shin too far past your chest without proper hip rotation can hyperextend the knee of the leg in Rubber Guard. The anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) is at risk. Instead, focus on rotating your hips and driving your knee towards your opposite shoulder.
    • Ankle Sprain: Trying to 'hook' the opponent's leg with your foot from the free leg, rather than framing with the foot, can lead to your ankle rolling outwards when the opponent applies pressure. The lateral ligaments of the ankle are vulnerable. Maintain a stable frame with the ball of your foot pressing into their hip.
    • Lower Back Strain: Arching your back excessively to 'pull' your leg closer, instead of using hip rotation and core engagement, can strain your lumbar spine. The erector spinae muscles are overloaded. Keep your core tight and your back flat on the mat.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Movement (0%): Practice the hip rotation and leg entry from a seated position. Focus on the feel of bringing your knee to your opposite shoulder. 5 reps each side.
    2. Partner Assisted Entry (25%): Your partner lies on their back in closed guard. You practice entering Rubber Guard, focusing on the hip angle and knee-to-shoulder drive without resistance. 5 reps each side.
    3. Controlled Entry with Frame (50%): Partner remains in closed guard. You enter Rubber Guard and establish the frame with your free leg. Partner gently tries to post their hand on your hip. Focus on maintaining the frame. 5 reps each side.
    4. Entry with Lapel Grip (75%): Partner in closed guard. You enter Rubber Guard, establish the frame, and take the lapel grip. Partner offers mild resistance to the lapel grip. Focus on securing the grip. 5 reps each side.
    5. Entry with Passive Posture (90%): Partner in closed guard, actively tries to break your posture but offers no escape attempts. You practice entering, framing, and gripping. Focus on maintaining control against passive posture. 3 reps each side.
    6. Light Rolling (100%): Partner attempts basic guard passes (e.g., torreando, knee slice). You focus on maintaining your Rubber Guard entry and frame against minimal pressure. 1 minute rounds, 3 rounds.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When opponent is posturing up from your closed guard, creating space to insert your leg.
    • When opponent is attempting to stack you from closed guard.
    • When opponent is trying to break your closed guard by pushing your knees apart.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Opponent Stacks: Immediately drive your knee into their chest and use your free leg frame to push their hips away, creating space to re-establish guard or transition.
    • Opponent Attempts to Pull Leg Out: Grip your shin tightly with both hands and use your hips to drive your knee forward, preventing them from disengaging. Keep your free leg extended as a barrier.
    • Opponent Steps Over Your Head: Use your free hand to push their stepping leg away at the hip. Simultaneously, retract the leg in Rubber Guard and re-establish closed guard or a different guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do Rubber Guard?

    The most common cause of knee pain in Rubber Guard for white belts is trying to force the leg into position without sufficient hip rotation. This leads to hyperextension or excessive torque on the knee joint. Ensure you are rotating your hips significantly and using your arms to guide your shin towards your opposite shoulder, rather than just pulling straight back.

    Q: How can I use Rubber Guard effectively against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paramount. Focus on maximizing your hip rotation to create angles that disrupt their base. Use your free leg's frame to create distance and prevent them from crushing you. Prioritize your grips: a strong lapel grip on the same side as your Rubber Guard leg helps control their posture and prevents them from easily escaping.

    Q: When should I transition out of Rubber Guard if it's not working?

    If your opponent consistently defends your entry by maintaining tight posture or successfully frames against your free leg, it's time to transition. Be aware of their weight distribution; if they are overly heavy on top and you cannot create an angle, consider retracting your leg and transitioning to a butterfly guard or attempting a sweep from closed guard. Constant pressure without progress often indicates a need to switch.

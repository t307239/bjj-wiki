Rubber Guard BJJ Technique Guide for White Belts

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

Rubber Guard is a control position initiated from Closed Guard, designed to attack or transition to dominant positions.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of fundamental body control and understanding of leverage, leading to an open posture.

The core principle is using your shin across the opponent's shoulder to create a tight, inescapable framework that limits their movement.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Start in Closed Guard: Maintain knees close to your chest, shins crossed behind opponent's back.
    2. Secure Collar Grip: Grip opponent's gi collar with your right hand, palm facing upwards.
    3. Bring Left Leg Up: Swing your left leg over opponent's head, aiming to place your left foot near your right shoulder.
    4. Hook Behind Head: Use your left arm to hook behind opponent's head, preventing them from posturing up.
    5. Position Shin on Shoulder: Ensure your left shin is firmly across opponent's right shoulder, creating a strong frame.
    6. Control Opponent's Arm: With your right hand, grip opponent's right sleeve or wrist, pulling it across their body.
    7. Adjust Hip Angle: Rotate your hips slightly to the left, creating a strong angle and preventing them from stacking you.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending your leg: This can hyperextend your knee joint, damaging the ACL or MCL.
    • Correct: Maintain a bent knee and actively use your hamstring to control the leg.
    • Allowing opponent to break your grip on their arm: This opens your guard and allows them to pass.
    • Correct: Maintain a consistent, strong grip on their sleeve or wrist, pulling it towards your body.
    • Arching your back excessively to create space: This puts immense pressure on your lumbar spine, risking disc injury.
    • Correct: Use hip rotation and leg control to manage distance, not back extension.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo movement (0%): Practice bringing your leg up and over the head, hooking with your arm, and placing your shin on the shoulder 10 times per side.
    2. Solo movement with grip (0%): Practice the same motion while holding your own sleeve or a towel to simulate grip control, 10 reps per side.
    3. Partner drill - static position (25%): Opponent lies on their back. You establish Rubber Guard. They offer minimal resistance to posture. Hold for 15 seconds, 5 reps.
    4. Partner drill - controlled movement (50%): Opponent attempts to break posture slightly. You maintain the Rubber Guard frame. Focus on keeping the shin on the shoulder and collar grip. 1 minute rounds, 3 rounds.
    5. Partner drill - light resistance (75%): Opponent actively tries to create space and posture up, but without full power. You practice maintaining the guard and transitioning to a sweep or submission attempt. 2 minute rounds, 3 rounds.
    6. Live rolling (100%): Implement Rubber Guard during rolling. Focus on establishing the position and attempting to chain attacks or sweeps. Do not force submissions.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When opponent is posturing up from your Closed Guard.
    • When opponent attempts to stack you from your Closed Guard.
    • When you have a strong collar grip and can control one of their arms.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • OPPONENT STACKS: If opponent attempts to stack, immediately drop your hips and pull their head towards you with your hooking arm. Use your free leg to push off their hip to create space and reset.
    • OPPONENT POSTURES UP: If opponent breaks your grip and postures, use your free leg to push their hip away while retracting the leg on their shoulder. Immediately transition to a different guard like Open Guard or Spider Guard.
    • OPPONENT FIGHTS THE SHIN: If they intensely fight your shin across their shoulder, use your free leg to sweep their other arm away and transition to a Triangle Choke or Armbar attempt.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to put my leg over their head for Rubber Guard?

    This often happens when you try to force your leg over with a straight leg, putting excessive strain on the knee joint. The correct biomechanics involve rotating your hip outward and bending your knee, using your hamstring and gluteal muscles to guide your leg in a controlled arc. Think of it like a windshield wiper motion, not a straight lift.

    Q: How can I use Rubber Guard against a much bigger and stronger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on keeping your shin firmly across their shoulder and using your collar grip to control their posture. Do not try to 'muscle' them. Instead, use your hip rotation to create angles and off-balance them, making them easier to sweep or submit. Your hooking arm behind their head is crucial for preventing them from using their weight advantage.

    Q: I keep losing my collar grip when I try to set up Rubber Guard. What am I doing wrong?

    You are likely relying too much on hand strength alone. The grip needs to be supported by your body's structure. Ensure your elbow is tucked, and your forearm is tight against your own chest. As you bring your leg over, use your other arm to pull their sleeve or wrist across their body, which helps to break their posture and secure your collar grip more effectively. It's a coordinated movement, not just a hand fight.

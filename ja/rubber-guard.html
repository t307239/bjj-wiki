Rubber Guard: The Essential BJJ Guide for White Belts

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

Rubber Guard starts from Closed Guard, aiming to control the opponent's posture and create submission opportunities. It's a dynamic position requiring precise body mechanics.

White belts often struggle by overextending or creating weak frames, leading to potential injury or position loss. Understanding the core mechanics is crucial.

The key is the controlled inversion and the creation of a strong, flexible frame using your shin and hip, not just your leg muscle.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From Closed Guard, secure your opponent's right arm with your left hand on their wrist (S-Grip: thumb wrapped around wrist bone).
    2. Simultaneously, bring your right leg up and over your opponent's head, aiming to place your right shin across their neck.
    3. Your right hip should angle slightly towards your opponent's left side, creating a wedge.
    4. Grip your right shin with your right hand (Cupped Grip: fingers curled into the shin) to reinforce the frame.
    5. Keep your left elbow tucked to your ribs, preventing them from posturing up easily.
    6. Your right knee should be bent, creating a 'shelf' that restricts their head movement.
    7. Maintain a slight posterior pelvic tilt, keeping your hips close to their hips to prevent them from creating space.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Hyperextending your knee when bringing your leg over the head: This can cause anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) or meniscus tears due to excessive valgus stress.
    • Forcing the leg over without hip rotation: This strains the hip flexors and can lead to labral tears. Instead, rotate your hip internally and externally to guide the leg.
    • Gripping your shin too loosely: This allows the opponent to break your guard easily and can lead to wrist strain on your gripping hand. Maintain a firm, cupped grip on your shin bone.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the leg-over-head motion with a pillow or mat, focusing on hip rotation and shin placement (5 reps per side).
    2. Solo drill: Practice the grip sequence and hip angling without an opponent (10 reps).
    3. Partner drill (0% resistance): Opponent allows you to establish Closed Guard and then you transition to Rubber Guard, holding for 5 seconds (5 reps).
    4. Partner drill (25% resistance): Opponent gently tries to break your guard while you hold Rubber Guard (5 reps per side).
    5. Partner drill (50% resistance): Opponent actively attempts to posture up and pass your guard (5 reps per side).
    6. Live rolling (75% resistance): Incorporate Rubber Guard attempts against a resisting opponent, focusing on retention and transitions.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up, attempting to pass your closed guard.
    • When you have good control of their posture with your legs.
    • When you feel an opening for a submission or sweep from closed guard.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Guard Pass (e.g., Torreando Pass): If the opponent creates significant distance and begins to circle, immediately transition to a closed guard or butterfly guard to re-establish control.
    • Posture Up: If the opponent forcefully straightens their back, creating a strong frame with their hands on your hips, you must quickly bring your hips in and try to flatten their posture with your grips.
    • Sweeps (e.g., Scissor Sweep): If the opponent is off-balance and you have a strong leg over their head, you can initiate a sweep by driving your hips and using your free leg to propel them over.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to put my leg over my opponent's head in Rubber Guard?

    Knee pain often stems from forcing the leg over without proper hip rotation. The knee joint is not designed for this type of rotational stress. Instead, focus on unlocking your hip and rotating your femur internally and externally to guide your shin across the neck. Think of it as a hip-driven movement, not a knee-driven one.

    Q: How can I use Rubber Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, your hip control and frame are paramount. Use your shin across their neck and your hip as a wedge to prevent them from flattening you. Focus on maintaining a tight connection and using their weight against them to create angles for sweeps or submissions like the triangle choke, rather than trying to overpower them.

    Q: Is Rubber Guard always good for white belts to learn, or should I focus on basic closed guard first?

    White belts should first master the fundamentals of Closed Guard: maintaining posture control, hip escapes, and basic sweeps. Rubber Guard builds upon these. Learning it too early without a solid foundation can lead to injury and ineffective technique. Focus on the foundational elements before adding the complexity of Rubber Guard.

Precise BJJ Knee Bar Technique for White Belts

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

The Knee Bar is a leg lock targeting the knee joint, most commonly attacked from closed guard or half guard when the opponent's leg is isolated.

White belts often fail by attempting to 'muscle' the lock or by not understanding the precise hip extension required, leading to ineffective submissions or injury.

The core mechanic is to hyperextend the knee by using your hips as a lever against the opponent's thigh, while simultaneously controlling their foot and hip.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure your opponent's right leg by placing your left thigh across their hip and your right thigh over their hamstring.
    2. Grip your own right shin with both hands, forming a tight figure-four grip, knuckles facing upwards.
    3. Drive your hips forward and slightly to your left, creating a perpendicular angle between your torso and your opponent's leg.
    4. Place your left foot firmly on your opponent's hip, using it as a brace to prevent them from stacking you.
    5. Extend your hips forcefully upwards and slightly backwards, perpendicular to the floor.
    6. Simultaneously, pull your gripped shin towards your chest, drawing their heel towards your glutes.
    7. Maintain pressure by keeping your elbows tucked and your back straight, focusing on the extension of their knee joint.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hip Angle: Attempting to 'pull' the leg with your arms while your hips are not properly angled (less than 90 degrees to the opponent's leg) puts excessive rotational stress on the opponent's knee, risking ACL/MCL tears.
    • Over-Extension of Own Knee: Bending your own supporting knee (the one on the mat) too deeply while driving your hips forward can lead to hyperextension injuries in your own knee.
    • Gripping the Shin Too Loosely: A loose figure-four grip on your own shin allows the opponent's heel to slip, negating the leverage needed for the lock and potentially causing your hands to slip and injure your wrists.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Drill: Practice the figure-four grip and hip extension movement without a partner. Focus on hip angle and torso position. (50 reps)
    2. Partner Drill (No Resistance): With a compliant partner, execute the steps of the Knee Bar from closed guard, focusing on grip and hip placement. (10 reps per side)
    3. Partner Drill (Light Resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to leg isolation. Focus on maintaining control of the leg and hip angle. (5 reps per side)
    4. Controlled Setup: Partner actively defends the setup, but allows you to establish the Knee Bar position. Focus on hip drive against slight resistance. (5 reps per side)
    5. Technical Sparring (Controlled): Sparring with a focus on attacking and defending leg locks. Opponent actively defends, but taps early to Knee Bar attempts. (3 rounds, 5 min each)
    6. Live Rolling: Incorporate Knee Bar attempts naturally within rolling. Understand the timing and positional cues. (Full rounds, focusing on submission)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent extends their leg from closed guard, creating an opening.
    • When your opponent attempts to stack you from closed guard and their leg becomes isolated.
    • When you successfully sweep or transition to a position where an opponent's leg is trapped and extended.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stacking Defense: Opponent drives their weight forward and down, attempting to compress your body and break your grip, while simultaneously trying to pull their leg out backwards.
    • Hip Escape (Sweeping): Opponent uses their free leg to hip escape away, creating space and breaking the perpendicular angle of the Knee Bar.
    • Tucking the Knee: Opponent tucks their knee joint inwards towards their body, bending the knee and reducing the extension pressure.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my Knee Bar hurt my own knee when I try it?

    This often happens when you're not properly angulating your hips. Instead of driving your hips perpendicular to your opponent's leg, you might be pulling their leg towards you with your arms while your hips are too parallel. This creates a shearing force on your own knee joint. Ensure your hips are driving forward and slightly to the side, creating a lever action on their thigh, not a pulling action on their shin.

    Q: How can I apply a Knee Bar against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paramount. Focus on isolating their leg completely by controlling their hip with your foot (e.g., left foot on their hip). Your hip drive needs to be explosive and timed perfectly, aiming to create that critical 90-degree angle. Do not rely on brute strength; instead, use their weight against them by maintaining a tight grip on your shin and extending your hips with maximum force.

    Q: When is the best time to go for a Knee Bar from closed guard?

    The most opportune moment is when your opponent makes a mistake by extending their leg outwards or when they try to 'stack' you. As they extend their leg, you can quickly bring your shin across their thigh and secure your grip. If they stack, your foot on their hip becomes crucial for maintaining balance while you execute the hip drive, turning their pressure into your submission attempt.

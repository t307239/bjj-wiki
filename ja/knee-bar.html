The Knee Bar is a leg lock targeting the knee joint, typically applied from positions like closed guard or side control. Its goal is to hyperextend the opponent's knee, forcing a submission.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body awareness, leading to uncontrolled movements and potential injury for themselves or their partner.

The key mechanical principle is isolating the opponent's leg and using your hips to create a fulcrum, thereby controlling their knee's range of motion.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From Closed Guard: Secure both of the opponent's legs with your own legs, creating a tight "box" around their hips.
    2. Isolate One Leg: Use your arms to "figure four" grip the opponent's ankle and shin on one leg, keeping their knee straight.
    3. Hip Angle: Rotate your hips 90 degrees to your side, away from the opponent's hips, to create leverage.
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your side, bringing your hips closer to the opponent's knee.
    5. Knee Control: Place your opposite arm (the one not gripping the leg) across the opponent's thigh, just above the knee, to prevent them from turning.
    6. Hip Extension: Drive your hips forward and upward, creating a straightening force on the opponent's knee.
    7. Finish: Maintain pressure through hip extension until the opponent taps.
    8. Release: Immediately loosen pressure and return to a safe position if the opponent taps or you feel resistance.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hip Angle: If you don't rotate your hips 90 degrees, you'll be pulling the leg rather than creating hyperextension, straining your own back and potentially tearing the opponent's ACL from shear force.
    • Loose Leg Grip: Failing to "figure four" grip the opponent's leg tightly allows them to escape the hold or create space, leading to a loss of control and potential knee hyperextension in the wrong direction for you.
    • Premature Hip Drive: Driving your hips forward before securing the leg isolation and hip angle can cause your own knee to hyper-extend as the opponent falls or shifts weight.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Drill: Practice the "figure four" grip on your own leg, focusing on the hip rotation and hip extension movement without a partner. (20 repetitions)
    2. Partner Drill (No Resistance): From closed guard, have a partner lie on their back. Practice securing their leg and performing the hip rotation and extension. Partner remains passive. (10 repetitions per side)
    3. Partner Drill (Light Resistance - 25%): Partner actively resists the leg isolation by posturing slightly. Focus on maintaining control and completing the hip movement. (15 repetitions per side)
    4. Partner Drill (Medium Resistance - 50%): Partner attempts to defend by pulling their leg out or bridging. Focus on maintaining the "figure four" grip and hip control. (15 repetitions per side)
    5. Situational Drilling (75%): Start from closed guard and allow the partner to attempt to pass. Execute the knee bar setup as they expose their leg. (10 repetitions per side)
    6. Live Rolling (90-100%): During rolling, actively look for opportunities to apply the knee bar from closed guard. Focus on control and proper mechanics, not speed. (3-5 attempts per round)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured up in your closed guard and their legs are extended.
    • When you have successfully transitioned to a position where one of their legs is isolated and accessible.
    • After a failed sweep or guard pass attempt where the opponent's leg is left vulnerable.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Escape: If the opponent fails to secure the "figure four" grip, immediately pull your leg back and attempt to stand or transition to a different guard.
    • Hip Escape & Bridge: If the opponent initiates the knee bar but doesn't have a strong grip, escape your hips to the side and bridge to relieve pressure, then try to spin out or pass their guard.
    • Straighten and Turn: If caught in the knee bar, try to straighten your leg as much as possible and turn your hips towards the opponent's head to relieve pressure on the knee joint.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my own knee hurt when I try to do a Knee Bar?

    Your knee is likely hurting because you are not achieving the correct biomechanical position. If your hips are not rotated 90 degrees and you are pulling the leg instead of creating hip extension, you are applying shear force to your own knee joint. Ensure your hips are the fulcrum and drive them forward and upward, not just backward.

    Q: How can I apply a Knee Bar against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a bigger opponent, perfect isolation and control are paramount. Focus on securing a tight "figure four" grip on their leg and using your hips to create a solid base before initiating the hip extension. Do not try to muscle it; leverage your body weight by rotating your hips and driving them forward and upward.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Knee Bar from Closed Guard?

    The optimal time to attempt a knee bar from closed guard is when your opponent postures up, extending their legs and creating space between your hips and theirs. This allows you to isolate one leg effectively and get your hips into the correct position to create the necessary leverage for the submission.

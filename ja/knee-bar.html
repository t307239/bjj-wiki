Knee Bar Technique: A Biomechanically Accurate Guide for BJJ White Belts

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

The Knee Bar is a leg lock applied from various control positions, aiming to hyperextend the opponent's knee joint. It is a powerful submission that targets a single joint.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of leverage and a tendency to use brute strength, leading to ineffective application and potential injury.

The key mechanical insight is controlling the opponent's hip and creating a fulcrum with your own body to isolate and hyperextend the knee.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From Closed Guard, secure an over-hook with your right arm around the opponent's left leg, gripping your own left bicep. Your left leg hooks under their right leg.
    2. Shift your hips to your left, creating an angle. Extend your right leg straight, placing your right foot against their hip for a stable base.
    3. With your left leg, hook behind their right knee. Your left shin should be perpendicular to their thigh.
    4. Grip their heel with your left hand, thumb pointing upwards. Your right hand grips their ankle, fingers pointing downwards.
    5. Arch your back, bringing your chest towards your knees. This action pulls their heel towards your hips.
    6. Simultaneously, extend your hips forward and upward, pushing your own knees towards the mat. This creates the hyperextension force.
    7. Maintain control of the ankle and heel throughout the movement. Your body forms a fulcrum, with your hips being the pivot point.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying excessive upward force on the opponent's ankle without controlling their hip. This can lead to a torn ACL or MCL as their knee buckles sideways.
    • Gripping the opponent's shin instead of their heel and ankle. This allows them to escape the grip and can strain your own wrist and elbow joints.
    • Attempting to lift the opponent's entire body with your arms. This is inefficient and can lead to shoulder or back injuries; focus on hip extension and arching your back.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip shift, leg placement, and grip acquisition without a partner. (5 reps per side)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Walk through the entire sequence with a compliant partner, focusing on correct body positioning and grip. (5 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner attempts to resist slightly by keeping their legs tight. Focus on maintaining control and hip angle. (5 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner drill (moderate resistance): Partner actively tries to defend by pulling their leg out or bridging. Focus on maintaining leverage and completing the submission. (5 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Live rolling (controlled): Apply the Knee Bar during rolling, but tap early if the submission is not secure to avoid injury. (3 attempts per round)
    6. Live rolling (full intensity): Attempt the Knee Bar against a resisting opponent, prioritizing safety and controlled application. (5 attempts per round, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When you have established a strong Closed Guard and can control their hips.
    • When you have successfully transitioned to a position where their leg is isolated, such as after a failed guard pass attempt.
    • When your opponent is posturing up heavily, creating an opportunity to attack their legs.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Leg Escape: If the opponent has control of your heel, immediately create space by pushing their hips away with your free leg and try to untangle your leg.
    • Bridging and Turning: If the Knee Bar is being applied, bridge your hips and turn your body away from the direction of the knee hyperextension. This relieves pressure on the knee.
    • Guard Retention: If the Knee Bar is being attempted from guard, focus on maintaining a strong guard by keeping your hips close to your opponent's and preventing them from creating the necessary angle and leverage.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do a Knee Bar?

    Your knee might hurt because you are not isolating the joint correctly. Instead of focusing on hip movement and arching your back, you might be trying to pull their leg with your arms, which puts torque on your own knee. Ensure your shin is perpendicular to their thigh and you are using your hips to create the hyperextension, not just arm strength.

    Q: How can I apply a Knee Bar against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Do not try to overpower them. Focus on creating a significant hip angle and using your entire body weight to generate the submission. Secure their heel tightly and ensure your hips are driving forward and upward, creating a fulcrum with their knee as the pivot point. A strong grip on the heel is crucial.

    Q: When is the best time to go for a Knee Bar in BJJ?

    The optimal time to attempt a Knee Bar is when your opponent is in a compromised position, such as when they are trying to pass your guard and their leg is extended. Also, if you have established a strong control position like Closed Guard or Half Guard and can isolate one of their legs, it's a good opportunity. Avoid attempting it when you are off-balance or have poor body control.

BJJ Knee Bar: A Biomechanically Accurate Guide for White Belts

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

The Knee Bar is a leg lock that targets the opponent's knee joint, primarily applied from positions like closed guard or side control where you can isolate a leg.

White belts often fail because they focus on brute strength rather than precise leverage, leading to missed submissions and potential injury to themselves or their partner.

The key mechanical insight is using your hips to create extension and torque on the opponent's knee, forcing them to tap before structural damage occurs.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Secure the opponent's leg by placing your arm under their thigh, gripping your own bicep. Your chest should be tight against their hip.
    2. Position your hips perpendicular to the opponent's leg, ensuring your pelvis is angled to maximize leverage.
    3. Bring your other leg over the opponent's hip, placing your foot firmly on the mat for base and stability.
    4. Trap the opponent's foot by placing your shin across their ankle, preventing them from rotating out.
    5. Drive your hips forward, extending your torso and creating a straight line from your hips to their knee.
    6. Squeeze your knees together, maintaining a tight frame around their leg.
    7. To finish, lift your hips upwards while simultaneously pushing your chest forward, creating hyperextension at the knee joint.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying excessive upward force on the knee before achieving hip extension: This can cause hyperextension of the knee, damaging the ACL or MCL.
    • Allowing the opponent to free their foot by not trapping their ankle: This creates a gap, allowing the opponent to escape and potentially injuring your own leg if you overextend.
    • Gripping too low on the opponent's thigh instead of your own bicep: This reduces leverage and can lead to the opponent escaping or you losing control of the leg.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip and body positioning without a partner, focusing on the leg trap and hip extension movement. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Partner lies on their back, you practice the grip and body mechanics of the Knee Bar. Focus on slow, controlled movements. (10 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner actively tries to resist the leg trap but doesn't pull hard. Focus on maintaining control and hip extension. (5 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (controlled resistance): Partner attempts to defend by stacking or pulling their leg. Focus on maintaining hip angle and hip drive. (5 reps per side)
    5. Scenario drill: Start from a dominant position (e.g., closed guard), isolate the leg, and execute the Knee Bar with light resistance. (5 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling (controlled): Attempt the Knee Bar during positional sparring, focusing on correct setup and execution with 75% resistance.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is in your closed guard and you can isolate one leg with your arms.
    • When you achieve a strong position in side control and can isolate an opponent's leg.
    • During scrambles where an opponent's leg becomes exposed and accessible.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack defense: Opponent "stacks" their weight by driving their hips into your chest. To counter, maintain hip angle and drive hips forward, preventing them from collapsing your structure.
    • Leg escape: Opponent tries to pull their leg free. To counter, ensure your shin is securely across their ankle and maintain a tight grip on your bicep.
    • Hip escape to avoid pressure: Opponent tries to turn their hips away. To counter, adjust your hip angle and continue to drive forward to maintain the extension.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do a Knee Bar?

    Your knee might be hurting because you are not achieving proper biomechanical extension. Instead of driving your hips forward to extend the opponent's knee, you might be trying to bend your own knee or pull with your arms. Ensure your hips are perpendicular to their leg and drive them forward to create a straight line of pressure.

    Q: How can I effectively apply a Knee Bar against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on isolating their leg completely and using your hips to create the extension, rather than relying on upper body strength. Ensure your shin is tightly locked across their ankle to prevent them from using their size to escape, and drive your hips forward with controlled, deliberate movement.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Knee Bar from closed guard?

    The optimal time to attempt a Knee Bar from closed guard is when the opponent's weight is distributed forward, creating an opening to isolate a leg. Look for moments when they try to pass your guard or when they are posturing up, allowing you to snake an arm under their thigh and secure the leg before they can react.

Precise Biomechanics of the BJJ Knee Bar for White Belts

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

The Knee Bar is a leg lock applied from various guard positions, aiming to hyperextend the opponent's knee joint. It requires precise body positioning and leverage to be effective and safe.

White belts often fail due to a lack of understanding of joint mechanics, leading to ineffective attempts or accidental injury to themselves or their partner. Focusing on control over brute force is key.

The core mechanical principle is creating a fulcrum with your hips while controlling the opponent's thigh and foot, isolating the knee for a controlled hyperextension.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting from Closed Guard: Secure both of the opponent's legs within your guard, ensuring your hips are slightly elevated off the mat.
    2. Isolate one leg: Use your right arm to grab the opponent's left ankle (inside grip, thumb up) and your left arm to secure the opponent's left thigh just above the knee (palm facing yourself).
    3. Hip Angle: Rotate your hips 45 degrees to your left, away from the opponent's trapped leg.
    4. Foot Placement: Place your right foot flat on the opponent's right hip, creating a strong base and preventing them from stacking you.
    5. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your right foot and left hip, driving your hips forward and slightly upwards.
    6. Leg Control: Extend your left leg straight, keeping your left foot tucked behind the opponent's trapped thigh, preventing them from pulling their leg out.
    7. Finish: Squeeze your knees together, pulling your opponent's heel towards your chest while simultaneously pushing your hips forward. This hyperextends the knee joint.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending your own knee while attempting to finish: This can lead to anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) or meniscus tears in your own knee due to hyperextension or twisting forces.
    • Gripping the opponent's foot too loosely and allowing them to spin out: This can result in a dangerous ankle lock attempt from the opponent or a loss of control, potentially causing knee hyperextension if you fall incorrectly.
    • Applying pressure before the leg is fully isolated and controlled: This can lead to the opponent bracing their leg or using their free leg to create space, risking a twisted knee or ankle injury for you as you lose balance.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Isolation Drill: Practice the initial leg isolation and hip rotation without a partner for 20 reps per side. (0% resistance)
    2. Partnered Isolation Drill: With a compliant partner, practice the isolation and hip movement, focusing on grip security and hip angle. Hold the position for 5 seconds, 10 reps per side. (25% resistance)
    3. Controlled Entry Drill: Practice entering the Knee Bar from Closed Guard. Your partner offers no resistance to the entry. 10 reps per side. (50% resistance)
    4. Half-Finish Drill: Enter the Knee Bar and extend hips until the opponent taps or you feel significant tension. Partner provides light resistance to prevent escape. 5 reps per side. (75% resistance)
    5. Full Submission Drill (Controlled): Execute the full Knee Bar with a partner who understands the submission and taps early. Focus on smooth transitions and control. 5 reps per side. (90% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling (Situational): During rolling, specifically look for opportunities to transition to the Knee Bar from Closed Guard. Focus on correct entry and mechanics, not immediate submission. 2 minutes, multiple times. (100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When opponent is posturing up heavily from your Closed Guard, creating space to isolate a leg.
    • When opponent attempts a guard pass and gets their leg caught in your Closed Guard.
    • When opponent is flat on their back, making it difficult for them to shrimp or create angles to defend.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Spin Out: If the opponent attempts to trap your leg, immediately spin your hips away and try to free your leg, creating distance and resetting your guard.
    • Stack Pass: If the opponent starts to extend their hips to finish, the defender can attempt to stack their weight forward onto the attacker's chest, relieving pressure on the knee.
    • Bridge and Roll: The defender can attempt to bridge their hips upwards and roll their body in the direction of the Knee Bar, attempting to land in a dominant position or escape the lock.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do a Knee Bar from Closed Guard?

    Your knee is likely hurting because you are not isolating the opponent's leg correctly or you are not creating the proper hip angle. This leads to your own knee joint absorbing torque instead of the opponent's. Ensure your hips are rotated 45 degrees away from the trapped leg, and that your finishing leg is straight and controlling the opponent's thigh.

    Q: How can I apply a Knee Bar against a much bigger opponent from Closed Guard?

    Against a bigger opponent, leverage and precision are paramount. Do not rely on brute strength. Focus on a tight isolation of their leg, ensuring no space for them to use their weight against you. Your hip rotation and forward drive are crucial for creating the necessary leverage. Ensure your finishing leg is straight and locked behind their thigh to prevent them from easily pulling out.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to a Knee Bar from Closed Guard in BJJ?

    The opportune moment is when the opponent is posturing up, creating a slight gap between their legs, or when they are attempting a guard pass and their leg becomes exposed. You can also initiate it when you feel them trying to 'stack' you, as this often leaves their leg vulnerable. Look for moments where their weight is not fully distributed to defend.

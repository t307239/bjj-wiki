The Knee Bar is a leg lock applied to the opponent's knee joint, typically initiated from a dominant position like side control or from guard.

It aims to hyperextend the knee, forcing a tap without causing excessive strain on other joints.

White belts often fail by applying pressure incorrectly, leading to injuries or the opponent escaping due to poor body mechanics.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Secure the opponent's leg by placing your forearm across the thigh and your other hand gripping your own bicep, creating a "figure four" grip.
    2. Sit up and swing your legs to straddle the opponent's hips, placing your chest against their torso.
    3. Drive your hips forward and slightly upward, creating a lever against the opponent's knee.
    4. Control the ankle by locking your feet together, ensuring the shin is perpendicular to the opponent's thigh.
    5. Maintain a tight grip, preventing the opponent from straightening their leg or rotating their hip.
    6. Apply pressure by extending your hips forward and lifting your chest, hyperextending the knee joint.
    7. Keep your back straight and engage your core to prevent injury to yourself.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying pressure with your hips by extending them backward instead of forward: This can damage the ACL and MCL of the opponent's knee due to uncontrolled rotational or shear forces.
    • Gripping the opponent's shin instead of the thigh with your forearm: This allows the knee to bend freely and can lead to the opponent easily posturing up and escaping, while also risking damage to your own arm if they resist.
    • Failing to control the opponent's hips by not straddling them: This allows the opponent to stack you, potentially injuring your spine or ribs, and prevents proper leverage for the Knee Bar.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip movement and grip transitions on a dummy or air without resistance (10 reps each side).
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Execute the full Knee Bar sequence from a controlled starting position, focusing on perfect form (5 reps each side).
    3. Light resistance drill: Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the mechanics and apply the lock without forcing (5 reps each side, 25% resistance).
    4. Controlled submission drill: Execute the Knee Bar with a partner who understands to tap early, focusing on correct pressure application (5 reps each side, 50% resistance).
    5. Submission drilling with escape attempts: Your partner actively attempts to escape, allowing you to refine your control and finish (5 reps each side, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling application: Attempt the Knee Bar during rolling when the opportunity arises, prioritizing safety and tapping early if caught (start with 90% intensity, move to 100% as comfort and control increase).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is on their back and you have secured a leg isolation, such as from half guard or a failed guard pass.
    • During transitions from side control or mount, when you can isolate one of the opponent's legs.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard and their leg becomes accessible.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • "The Escape": The opponent immediately straightens their leg and attempts to bridge their hips away, creating space to disengage.
    • "The Spin": The opponent attempts to spin their body to face you, relieving the pressure on the knee joint and allowing them to untangle their leg.
    • "The Stack": The opponent attempts to drive their weight forward and down onto you, attempting to crush your body and break the hold, often by driving their hips into your chest.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my Knee Bar feel weak and why can't I finish it against bigger opponents?

    The primary reason for a weak Knee Bar against larger opponents is insufficient hip drive and leverage. Ensure your hips are actively pushing forward, not just sitting back. Your chest should be pressed firmly against their torso, and your feet locked tightly to prevent any hip rotation. Focus on extending your hips like a piston, rather than just pulling with your arms.

    Q: What is the safest way for a white belt to practice Knee Bars without injuring my training partner?

    Start by practicing the mechanics of the Knee Bar on a compliant partner with zero resistance. Focus on achieving the correct body position and grip. Progress to applying the submission with only 25-50% of your strength, and always communicate clearly with your partner. Ensure they know to tap immediately at the first sign of pressure, and you must be ready to release the hold instantly.

    Q: Can I get caught in a Knee Bar from my own Closed Guard?

    Yes, it's possible, although less common and usually a result of a poorly maintained guard. If your opponent stacks you heavily within your closed guard, or if you improperly attempt a sweep and expose your leg, they can potentially isolate and attack your knee. Maintaining active hip control and preventing the stack are crucial defenses.

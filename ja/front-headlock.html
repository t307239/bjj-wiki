Front Headlock Position in BJJ: A White Belt Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Front Headlock is a dominant control position where you secure an opponent's head and one arm, limiting their movement and creating submission opportunities.

White belts often struggle because they lack the foundational body mechanics to maintain control, leading to them being easily escaped or swept.

The key insight is understanding how to use your weight and frames to create a secure, crushing pressure that immobilizes your opponent.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side control or a similar top position, secure your opponent's head with your armpit and trap their lead arm with your opposite arm.
    2. Establish a "collar grip" on the back of their neck with your choking arm's bicep against their throat.
    3. Place your forearm across their face, pinning their head to your chest.
    4. Drive your chest down, angling your hips to the mat, creating downward pressure.
    5. Ensure your shoulder is packed tightly against their head, preventing them from posturing up.
    6. Your non-choking arm should frame against their shoulder or upper arm to prevent them from creating space.
    7. Maintain a tight base with your knees and hips, preventing them from bridging or shrimping away.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Neck Strain: Applying excessive upward force on the opponent's head while they resist by pushing down can hyperextend the white belt's neck, potentially damaging the cervical spine.
    • Correct: Maintain downward pressure with your chest and shoulder, allowing the opponent's head to come to you.
    • Shoulder Impingement: Forcing the opponent's head too close to their own shoulder can cause the white belt's shoulder to get trapped and impinged.
    • Correct: Keep your shoulder slightly away from their head, allowing your forearm to control their face.
    • Lower Back Compression: Attempting to "lean back" to create pressure can compress the white belt's lumbar spine, especially if the opponent is posturing up.
    • Correct: Drive your weight forward through your chest and hips, keeping your back relatively neutral.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Drill (0%): Practice securing the position on a dummy or air, focusing on grip placement and body alignment. (10 reps)
    2. Assisted Drill (25%): With a partner lying flat, practice establishing the Front Headlock without resistance, focusing on control. (10 reps)
    3. Light Resistance Drill (50%): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to practice securing and holding the position. (5 reps each side)
    4. Positional Sparring - Front Headlock Focus (75%): Start from a neutral position, aiming to achieve the Front Headlock. Partner defends the entry and escape. (3 rounds x 2 minutes)
    5. Positional Sparring - Maintaining Control (90%): Start in Front Headlock, partner attempts to escape. Focus on maintaining the position. (3 rounds x 2 minutes)
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Integrate Front Headlock attempts and defenses into regular sparring. (5 minute rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent turtles and you have control of their head and one arm.
    • When you have successfully transitioned to side control and they are trying to create space by posturing up.
    • As a counter to certain guard passes where you can isolate their head and arm.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Escape to Turtle: The opponent tucks their chin and rolls onto their hands and knees, creating a safe space to reset. This requires them to drive their hips away and tuck their chin tightly.
    • Head and Arm Escape: The opponent drives their hips into the attacker's chest and uses their free arm to push away, creating space to roll out or shrimp away. This involves a strong hip drive and frame.
    • Roll Under: The opponent uses their free arm to block the attacker's chest and rolls in the direction of their trapped arm, using momentum to come up to their feet or guard. This is a dynamic escape requiring good timing and body awareness.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try to get the Front Headlock?

    Your neck likely hurts because you are trying to "pull" the opponent's head down with your arms. Instead, you should be driving your chest and shoulder into their head and neck, using your body weight to create pressure. This biomechanically sound approach distributes the force across your torso, not just your neck.

    Q: How do I get the Front Headlock against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on leverage and precise body positioning rather than brute strength. Trap their lead arm and use your chest and shoulder to pin their head. Angle your hips to the mat and drive your weight forward. The goal is to use their weight against them by controlling their base and preventing them from posturing up.

    Q: Is the Front Headlock a submission or a position in BJJ?

    The Front Headlock is primarily a dominant position that *enables* submissions. While certain chokes like the Darce or Anaconda can be applied from this position, the primary benefit for a white belt is the control it offers. Mastering the position first will naturally lead to opportunities for effective chokes.

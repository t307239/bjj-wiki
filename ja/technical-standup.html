BJJ テクニカルスタンドアップ：初心者向け解説

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック

The Technical Stand-Up is a crucial transition from a grounded position, typically guard, to a standing posture. It allows you to regain your feet safely and control distance.

White belts often struggle by pushing off with their hands or trying to stand too quickly, leading to instability and potential injury. They lack the coordinated weight transfer required.

The key is to use your hips to drive upwards, creating an angle for your leg to extend and support your weight, all while maintaining a strong base.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From Closed Guard: Secure an Over-Under grip on both of your opponent's sleeves, pulling them towards you. Your hips are flat on the mat, knees tucked.
    2. Initiate the Sit-Up: Drive your hips back and up, simultaneously lifting your chest. Your back remains relatively straight.
    3. First Leg Extension: As your hips rise, extend your left leg (if you started with your right leg forward) straight out, placing your foot flat on the mat, toes pointing slightly outward.
    4. Hip Angle: Your pelvis should now be angled forward, creating a stable tripod with your extended left leg and both hands on the mat.
    5. Second Leg Insertion: Bring your right knee (the one that was initially forward) towards your chest, then plant your right foot flat on the mat behind your left foot.
    6. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight forward onto your feet. Push off the mat with your hands as your body becomes supported by your legs.
    7. Base and Posture: Stand up fully, maintaining a wide base with your feet shoulder-width apart. Keep your hands ready to frame or grip, and maintain upright posture.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Jerking Upward with Arms: A white belt may try to yank themselves up using only their arms. This can lead to shoulder joint hyperextension or a rotator cuff tear as the humerus bone is pulled beyond its normal range of motion. The correct movement is to drive with the hips, using arms for balance and initial push.
    • Knee Valgus Collapse: Standing up with knees caving inward towards each other. This places excessive stress on the medial collateral ligament (MCL) and anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) of the knees, risking a tear. Maintain a wider base, ensuring knees track over the toes.
    • Head-First Plummet: Attempting to stand by dropping the head forward and down. This can cause neck hyperextension or flexion injuries, potentially damaging cervical vertebrae or spinal discs. Keep the head up and maintain a neutral spine throughout the movement.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Drive Drill: Lie on your back in guard position. Practice driving your hips up and back, extending one leg out to a tripod position. Repeat 10 times per side. (0% resistance).
    2. Solo Leg Extension & Plant: From the tripod position, practice bringing the second leg in to a standing base. Focus on foot placement. Repeat 10 times per side. (0% resistance).
    3. Partner Assisted Stand-Up: With a partner lying on their back, practice the Technical Stand-Up. They offer minimal resistance, simply allowing you to move. Focus on smooth execution. 5 reps each side. (25% resistance).
    4. Controlled Stand-Up: Partner applies light pressure, preventing you from standing too easily but not actively resisting. Focus on proper weight transfer. 5 reps each side. (50% resistance).
    5. Technical Stand-Up with Guard Retention: Practice the Technical Stand-Up while the partner attempts to maintain guard or initiate sweeps. Focus on maintaining balance and posture. 3 reps each side. (75% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling Transition: Incorporate the Technical Stand-Up into live rolling scenarios, aiming to stand up when appropriate. Focus on successful execution under pressure. 5 successful stand-ups per round. (100% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is in your guard and you want to create space to stand or transition to a different guard.
    • When you have successfully swept your opponent and need to get to your feet to secure a dominant position.
    • When you are in a scramble and need to safely return to a standing or more advantageous grounded position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Pull & Sweep: If the opponent initiates the Technical Stand-Up, immediately pull guard by re-establishing closed guard or a similar control. If they are too far, perform a sweep by driving into their hip with your foot on their hip and extending your leg.
    • Knee Tap/Single Leg Defense: As they begin to stand, if their base is unstable, attack their lead leg with a knee tap or attempt to secure a single leg takedown by dropping your level and driving into their hip.
    • Postural Breakdown: If they are halfway up and not fully postured, drive your forehead into their sternum and push them back down, collapsing their base and potentially creating an opportunity for a sweep or submission.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I do the BJJ Technical Stand-Up?

    Wrist pain during the Technical Stand-Up often stems from excessive weight being placed directly onto the wrist joint in an extended position. This can happen if you push off with your hands too early or too forcefully before your hips and legs are providing sufficient support. Ensure your weight shifts to your hips and legs first, using your hands primarily for balance and a final push as you stand.

    Q: How do I Technical Stand-Up against a bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, the key is to emphasize hip drive and leverage, rather than brute strength. Focus on creating a strong angle with your hips to lift yourself, and ensure your extended leg is firmly planted to create a stable base. Do not try to push them away with your hands; use them for balance as your body rises, and be prepared to quickly establish grips for control once you are up.

    Q: When should I use the Technical Stand-Up in BJJ instead of just standing up normally?

    The Technical Stand-Up is specifically designed for situations where you are starting from a grounded position, most commonly your guard. It allows for a controlled and biomechanically sound ascent, minimizing the risk of being swept or losing your base. A 'normal' stand-up is more appropriate when you are already on one knee or are in a situation where you can simply rise without needing to navigate a guard or compromised position.

The Technical Stand-Up is a fundamental BJJ transition used to safely and efficiently stand up from a grounded position, typically when your opponent is in your guard.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of coordinated body mechanics, leading to them being easily swept or put in submissions.

The key is to create a stable base and controlled momentum by using your hands and legs as levers, not just to push off the ground.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Start from Closed Guard. Secure a Collar Grip with your right hand on your opponent's left lapel, and a Sleeve Grip with your left hand on their right sleeve.
    2. Arch your back and drive your hips slightly forward, creating space to bring your right leg through.
    3. Place your right foot flat on the mat, hip-width distance from your body, with your knee directly over your ankle.
    4. Simultaneously, place your left hand flat on the mat, shoulder-width from your body, creating a tripod with your right foot and left hand.
    5. Shift your weight onto your left hand and right foot, lifting your hips off the ground.
    6. Bring your left leg through, placing your left foot flat on the mat next to your right foot, creating a stable base.
    7. Push off your left hand, extending your elbow fully, and drive your hips upwards and forwards.
    8. Maintain your grip on the collar and sleeve, keeping your posture tall and base wide, ready to defend or attack.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly planting your foot too far forward: This places excessive shear force on the knee joint, risking ACL or meniscus tears.
    • Pushing off with bent elbows: This fails to create proper leverage, leading to uncontrolled falling and potential wrist fractures upon impact.
    • Attempting to stand up directly without creating space: This allows the opponent to maintain control and potentially initiate a sweep or submission, risking shoulder impingement or neck strain.
    • Not maintaining a wide base with feet: This results in a narrow base, making you unstable and prone to being swept, potentially leading to hip or lower back injuries.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Visualization: Mentally rehearse the steps, focusing on body positioning and weight transfer (10 reps, 0% resistance).
    2. Mirror Drills: Perform the movement in front of a mirror, checking for correct angles and hand/foot placement (20 reps, 0% resistance).
    3. Partner Assisted (No Resistance): Have a partner lie on their back. Practice the Technical Stand-Up, with your partner offering minimal assistance to maintain balance (30 reps, 25% resistance).
    4. Controlled Partner Drill: With a partner in guard, practice the Technical Stand-Up. They offer light, passive resistance by holding grips but do not actively try to stop you (40 reps, 50% resistance).
    5. Technical Stand-Up Against Light Pressure: Practice with a partner applying light guard retention. Focus on creating space and maintaining your base (50 reps, 75% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling Application: Attempt the Technical Stand-Up during controlled rolling sessions, focusing on successful execution rather than immediate attack (5 rounds x 2 minutes, 90% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is in your Closed Guard and you want to establish a standing base for a takedown or to create distance.
    • When you've successfully swept your opponent and need to transition to a stable standing position before they recover guard.
    • When you've been put into a bad position on the ground and need to safely return to your feet.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • When the opponent attempts to stand up, you can counter by securing a strong Collar and Sleeve grip yourself, preventing them from creating space and pulling them back into guard.
    • If they begin to stand, immediately transition to a Half Guard or Single Leg X Guard to disrupt their base and prevent them from completing the stand-up.
    • As they push off, if they leave an arm exposed, you can attempt to secure a grip and transition into a submission like a Triangle Choke or Armbar.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I do the Technical Stand-Up in BJJ?

    Wrist pain during the Technical Stand-Up often stems from pushing off the mat with a bent elbow, which sends shockwaves directly to the wrist joint. Ensure you are fully extending your elbow and transferring your weight onto your forearm and shoulder, creating a solid, stable frame. Also, ensure your palm is flat and fingers are spread wide for maximum surface area and stability.

    Q: How do I do the Technical Stand-Up in BJJ against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, creating sufficient space is crucial. Use your legs to push their hips away forcefully before bringing your leg through. Focus on driving your hips UPWARDS and FORWARDS, using your arms as levers, rather than trying to simply 'muscle' your way up. Maintain a wide, stable base with your feet once you're up.

    Q: When is the best time to use the Technical Stand-Up in BJJ if I'm on the bottom?

    The Technical Stand-Up is ideal when your opponent is in your guard and you want to re-establish a dominant standing position, perhaps to initiate a takedown or escape a dangerous situation. It's most effective when you have some control over their posture and can create just enough space to bring your leg through cleanly before they can react.

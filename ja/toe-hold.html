Toe Hold Technique Guide for BJJ White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

The Toe Hold is a submission targeting the ankle joint, typically initiated from a controlled leg entanglement like the 50-50 guard or by isolating a single leg.

White belts often rush this submission, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury due to a lack of understanding of the required leverage.

The key insight is to create a fulcrum with your body, isolating the foot and applying rotational pressure to the ankle, not the knee or shin.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a stable 50-50 guard, secure your opponent's foot with your armpit, ensuring their heel is firmly against your bicep. Your opponent's foot should be perpendicular to your torso.
    2. Place your free hand on the outside of their foot, fingers pointing towards their toes, creating a "cup" around their heel.
    3. Drive your hips forward and slightly to the side of your opponent's hips, creating a stable base and initiating hip extension.
    4. Simultaneously, bring your knees together, creating a "shelf" for their heel to rest against. Your knees should be angled slightly inward.
    5. Use your bicep and forearm to control the instep of their foot, preventing them from pulling it away.
    6. Apply pressure by extending your hips and slightly lifting your chest, creating a rotational force on the ankle joint.
    7. Maintain a neutral spine and avoid arching your back; all pressure comes from controlled hip and torso extension.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying pressure by pulling their foot towards your chest with your arms: This can hyperextend the knee or strain the Achilles tendon as the ankle has insufficient support. The correct movement is to drive your hips forward to create leverage.
    • Gripping the shin or calf instead of the foot: This distributes pressure incorrectly and can lead to knee hyperextension or ligament damage. Always cup the heel and control the foot directly.
    • Trying to "crank" the submission with your arms: This is inefficient and dangerous, risking shoulder or elbow injury for you and knee ligament damage for your opponent. The submission is generated by hip extension, not arm strength.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling of grip acquisition and hip drive: Practice securing the foot and driving your hips forward without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Static positional drilling: Partner lies on their back, you secure the foot and practice the hip drive and knee-to-knee position with 0% resistance. (20 reps per side)
    3. Controlled resistance drilling: Partner offers minimal resistance (25%) as you apply the hip drive and pressure. Focus on maintaining control. (10 reps per side)
    4. Light pressure drilling: Partner offers light resistance (50%) as you execute the technique, focusing on maintaining the correct body mechanics. (10 reps per side)
    5. Live rolling simulation: Practice setting up and attempting the Toe Hold from a controlled position (e.g., 50-50 guard) with 75% resistance, tapping immediately if caught.
    6. Live rolling integration: Attempt the Toe Hold during live rolling (90-100% resistance) only when the opportunity arises naturally, prioritizing safety and controlled execution.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have established a stable 50-50 guard and can isolate your opponent's foot.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard by extending their leg, creating an opening.
    • When you have secured a strong leg entanglement and can control their hips to prevent them from posturing up.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Foot Escape: If your opponent's heel is not firmly cupped, they can attempt to "walk" their heel out by rotating their foot and pulling it towards their body. Maintain a tight grip and hip pressure.
    • Guard Retention: If the submission is not fully locked, the opponent can attempt to regain guard by bridging or shrimping their hips away. Focus on maintaining hip-to-hip connection.
    • Re-angling: The opponent can attempt to turn their foot away from the pressure by rotating their ankle. Use your body to block this rotation and maintain the perpendicular angle of their foot to your torso.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try to do a Toe Hold?

    This usually happens when you are applying pressure incorrectly, often by trying to pull the foot with your arms rather than driving your hips forward. This can hyperextend your own ankle or strain your Achilles tendon. Ensure your hips are driving forward, creating a fulcrum with your bicep and knee, and that you are cupping the heel firmly.

    Q: How can I apply a Toe Hold against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise biomechanics are even more critical than strength. Focus on isolating their foot and creating a solid base with your hips. Do not try to overpower them; instead, use your body weight to create the rotational force on their ankle by driving your hips forward and keeping your knees tight. A stable 50-50 guard is crucial for this.

    Q: When is the best time to go for a Toe Hold in BJJ?

    The Toe Hold is best attempted when you have established a strong leg entanglement, particularly the 50-50 guard, and can control your opponent's hip. It's also a good option when your opponent is trying to pass your guard and extends a leg, or when they are attempting to stack you from a leg entanglement. Avoid attempting it when your own guard is compromised or you are off-balance.

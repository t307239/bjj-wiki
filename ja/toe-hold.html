The Toe Hold is a powerful leg lock targeting the ankle joint, often initiated from various guard positions or when an opponent's leg is exposed.

White belts often rush this technique, leading to injury, or fail to secure the necessary control, making it ineffective.

The key is isolating the foot and creating a perpendicular torque on the ankle by controlling the heel and toes simultaneously.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Secure the opponent's foot with your forearm on the instep and your hand cupping the heel. Your other hand grips your own forearm to create a strong frame.
    2. Hip Angle: Position your hips perpendicular to the opponent's leg, creating a strong base.
    3. Weight Transfer: Drive your hips forward and slightly down, creating a pulling sensation on the opponent's foot.
    4. Knee Position: Keep your knee pointed towards the ceiling, preventing your own leg from being compromised.
    5. Torque Application: Simultaneously pull your gripping hand towards your chest while pushing your hips forward to extend the ankle.
    6. Foot Control: Ensure your forearm remains firmly on the instep, preventing the toes from escaping.
    7. Finishing: Apply steady, controlled pressure until the tap. Never yank or twist violently.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Knee Drive: Driving your knee towards the opponent's hip instead of keeping it vertical can strain your own knee ligament (MCL/LCL) as the opponent's leg forces it outwards.
    • Jerking Motion: A sudden, jerky pull on the foot can cause a severe ankle sprain or even a fracture due to uncontrolled torsional force on the talocrural joint.
    • Loose Foot Control: Allowing the opponent's toes to slip out from under your forearm grip means you are applying pressure to the shin, not the ankle, risking a calf slicer or hyper-extension of the knee.
    • Hip Alignment: Facing the opponent directly instead of angling your hips creates a weak structure and allows them to easily escape or counter.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Foot Isolation Drill: Practice gripping and isolating an imaginary foot (or a training dummy's foot) with the correct forearm and hand placement, holding for 5 seconds. (50 reps)
    2. Partnered Static Grips: With a partner lying on their back, practice securing the Toe Hold grip on their ankle without any pressure. Focus on achieving the correct forearm-on-instep, hand-on-heel grip and hip angle. (25 reps per leg)
    3. Controlled Ankle Extension: From a static grip, with your partner offering minimal resistance (0% resistance), practice the hip drive and controlled extension motion. Focus on the sensation of pressure on the ankle. (20 reps per leg)
    4. Slow Motion Toe Hold: Work with a partner who provides very light, controlled resistance (25% resistance). Execute the full Toe Hold sequence slowly, focusing on maintaining all grips and body positioning. Partner taps early. (15 reps per leg)
    5. Live Toe Hold Attempt (Controlled): In a rolling scenario, actively look for opportunities to set up the Toe Hold. Your partner knows it's coming and offers moderate resistance (50-75% resistance), focusing on defending the setup and escape. Tap immediately if you feel any dangerous pressure.
    6. Live Rolling with Intent: During live rolling, attempt the Toe Hold when the opportunity arises. Your partner provides full resistance (90-100%), but you are instructed to tap early if you feel any joint pain, and they are instructed to release immediately upon your tap. (Focus on 1-2 attempts per round).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent is in your closed guard and extends a leg out, exposing their ankle.
    • When you have successfully swept or transitioned to a position where you can isolate one of their legs, such as from an open guard or half guard.
    • When an opponent attempts a guard pass and their leg becomes accessible for control.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape and Leg Straightening: If the Toe Hold is being applied, immediately hip escape away from the pressure and drive your leg straight to create space and break the grip.
    • Stomp/Foot Release: With the opponent's hand gripping your heel, use your free foot to stomp down on their hand or forearm, attempting to break their grip.
    • Knee Shield/Guard Retention: If the Toe Hold is being set up from guard, use your free leg to create a knee shield, pushing against the opponent's hip or chest to prevent them from achieving the correct angle and hip drive.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try to do a Toe Hold?

    This usually happens when you're not properly isolating the ankle joint. If your forearm is pressing on the shin instead of the instep, or if your hand is not securely cupping the heel, you're applying pressure to the wrong area. Ensure your forearm is across the top of the foot and your hand is firmly around the heel, allowing you to create torque directly on the ankle's rotational axis.

    Q: How can I do a Toe Hold against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paramount. Focus on securing a very tight grip on their foot, ensuring your forearm is firmly across their instep and your hand is locked around their heel. Use your hips to create a strong perpendicular angle, and drive them forward to extend their ankle. Don't try to overpower them; instead, use their size against them by creating a strong rotational force on their ankle.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Toe Hold from closed guard?

    The ideal moment from closed guard is when your opponent attempts to pass by extending one of their legs. As they bring a leg forward, you can use your ankles to hook their thigh and then transition your arm to secure their foot. This typically occurs when they are trying to create space or posture up, giving you the opening to isolate and attack their ankle.

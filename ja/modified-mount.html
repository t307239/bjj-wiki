Modified Mount is a dominant top position achieved after securing a standard mount, offering superior control and attack angles.

White belts often struggle by applying excessive force or losing base, making them vulnerable to escapes.

The key is maintaining a low center of gravity and distributing weight directly onto the opponent's sternum and hips.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From standard mount, maintain grips on opponent's hips or pants to prevent bridging.
    2. Shift your weight forward, lowering your hips until they are near the opponent's sternum.
    3. Bring your knees wider than shoulder-width apart, creating a stable base.
    4. Your feet should be either tucked under the opponent's thighs or flat on the mat, depending on their mobility.
    5. Keep your chest pressed into their chest, creating a "crab-like" posture.
    6. Angle your hips slightly towards one of the opponent's shoulders, creating pressure.
    7. Ensure your elbows are tucked in, preventing arm-based attacks and maintaining a tight frame.
    8. Maintain consistent downward pressure through your hips and chest.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Knee Position: Placing knees too close together or directly on the opponent's hips allows them to bridge and escape. This can strain the opponent's knees and your own hip flexors if forced into an awkward position.
    • Lifting Hips Too High: Raising your hips off the opponent's body creates a gap, allowing them to shrimp or bridge effectively. This can lead to a loss of position and potential knee or lower back strain from sudden shifts in weight.
    • Allowing Opponent to Control Arms: Leaving your arms extended or open for grabs by the opponent invites submissions like armbars or kimuras. This can lead to wrist, elbow, or shoulder ligament damage from uncontrolled joint manipulation.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Mobility Drill: Practice lowering and widening hips, focusing on controlled movement. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner Static Hold: Assume Modified Mount on a compliant partner. Hold the position for 15 seconds, focusing on weight distribution. (5 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Controlled Transition Drill: Partner starts in guard. You pass and establish standard mount, then transition to Modified Mount. Partner offers minimal resistance to escapes. (5 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Escape Readiness Drill: Partner attempts basic escapes (bridge, shrimp) from Modified Mount. You react by adjusting weight and base to maintain position. (5 reps, 50% resistance)
    5. Submission Setup Drill: From Modified Mount, practice initiating a common submission setup (e.g., armbar prep). Partner defends passively. (5 reps, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling - Position Focus: Attempt to maintain Modified Mount for 1 minute against a resisting opponent. Focus on position, not submissions. (3 rounds, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • After successfully establishing standard mount and the opponent attempts to bridge or shrimp.
    • When the opponent's hips are elevated, creating an opportunity to lower your base.
    • As a transition from a failed submission attempt from mount.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Bridging: The opponent attempts to drive their hips upwards. You counter by lowering your hips further and driving weight into their sternum, preventing them from gaining upward momentum.
    • Shrimping: The opponent attempts to create space by pushing your knee with their foot and shrimping their hips away. You counter by widening your base, keeping your chest low, and driving your knee forward to block their escape.
    • Guard Recovery: The opponent tries to bring their knees between your legs to re-establish guard. You counter by maintaining tight hip pressure and keeping your knees wide, preventing them from inserting their legs.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to get into Modified Mount?

    Lower back pain often stems from hyperextending your spine or lifting your hips too high. Instead of arching your back, focus on lowering your sternum towards your opponent's sternum and keeping your core engaged. Think about tucking your tailbone slightly. This biomechanical shift distributes weight through your hips and legs, not your lower back.

    Q: How can a smaller white belt maintain Modified Mount against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise weight distribution are crucial. Instead of trying to overpower them, focus on keeping your hips heavy and low, pressing your sternum into their chest. Angle your body slightly towards one of their shoulders to create a more acute pressure point. This makes it harder for their larger frame to generate escape movements.

    Q: The optimal time to transition is when you feel the opponent beginning to initiate an escape, such as a bridge or a shrimp. By proactively lowering your base and widening your knees, you can often shut down their escape attempt before it gains momentum. It's a defensive adjustment that also reinforces your positional dominance.

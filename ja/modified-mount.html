Modified Mount: A White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Modified Mount is achieved from the standard mount position, aiming to transition to a more dominant, controlled posture. White belts often struggle due to a lack of hip control and understanding of weight distribution.

The key is to maintain constant hip pressure, preventing your opponent from bridging or shrimping away.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From standard mount, ensure both knees are inside your opponent's armpits, with hips low and chest on their chest.
    2. Place your right hand on your opponent's left hip bone, fingers pointing towards their feet.
    3. Place your left hand on their right hip bone, fingers pointing towards their feet.
    4. Shift your weight forward, driving your sternum into their sternum, creating a "shoulder lock" with your chest.
    5. Simultaneously, drive your hips down and slightly forward, creating a fulcrum on their hips.
    6. Your knees should be angling slightly inwards, creating a "frame" that prevents them from creating space.
    7. Maintain a wide base with your feet slightly wider than shoulder-width apart, providing stability.
    8. Your elbows should be tucked in, preventing them from being easily attacked.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Releasing hip pressure to "reach" for grips: This allows the opponent's hips to move, potentially leading to a hip escape or bridge. The lumbar spine can be strained if the opponent bridges with uncontrolled momentum.
    • Angling knees outwards instead of inwards: This creates openings for the opponent to shrimp their hips and escape the mount. The knee joint can be hyperextended or twisted if the opponent forces a movement into the open space.
    • Standing up too quickly from mount: This often leads to losing balance and control, potentially falling into the opponent's guard. The ankles and knees are at risk of twisting if you land awkwardly or off-balance.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo: Practice transitioning from a prone position to mount, then settling into Modified Mount, focusing on hip pressure and knee angle. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner Drill: With a compliant partner, practice establishing mount and then shifting into Modified Mount, holding for 5 seconds. (20 reps, 10% resistance)
    3. Partner Drill: Practice the weight transfer and hip drive from step 4-6 of the biomechanics section, holding the position for 10 seconds. (30 reps, 20% resistance)
    4. Partner Drill: Work on maintaining Modified Mount against light bridging attempts from the opponent. Focus on maintaining hip pressure. (15 reps, 30% resistance)
    5. Partner Drill: Transition from Modified Mount to a basic submission like an armbar, focusing on maintaining the position until the submission is locked. (10 reps per side, 40% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling: Attempt to establish and maintain Modified Mount for at least 15 seconds during rolling. (5 rounds, 75% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have successfully secured the standard mount position and your opponent is actively trying to bridge or shrimp.
    • Immediately after passing the guard and achieving mount, before your opponent can establish defensive frames.
    • When your opponent is turtled or attempting to turtle from mount, creating an opportunity to secure a tighter, more controlling mount.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Bridge and Roll: The opponent bridges by driving their hips upwards and into the attacker's base, attempting to roll them over. The attacker must maintain strong hip pressure and a wide base to resist.
    • Shrimp (Hip Escape): The opponent creates space by pushing off the mat with their feet and shrimping their hips away, aiming to create distance and escape the mount. The attacker must keep their knees tight and maintain forward chest pressure.
    • Elbow Escape: The opponent attempts to create space by bringing an elbow to the mat and shrimping their hips. The attacker must prevent the elbow from reaching the mat by driving their knees and hips forward.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to hold Modified Mount?

    Your lower back pain is likely due to hyperextension caused by a lack of engagement of your glutes and core. Instead of arching your back, focus on driving your hips forward and down, keeping your spine neutral. Engage your abdominal muscles and squeeze your glutes to create a stable base and transfer weight through your hips, not just your lower back.

    Q: How can I maintain Modified Mount against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, your weight distribution becomes paramount. Instead of relying solely on downward pressure, focus on creating a "frame" with your knees and hips that prevents their body from moving. Drive your sternum into their sternum and maintain a tight, chest-to-chest connection. Think of "pinning" their hips with your own, rather than just sitting on them.

    Q: Should I always use my hands on their hips in Modified Mount?

    Using your hands on their hips provides crucial control and prevents them from shrimping away, but it's not the only option. Some practitioners prefer to place their hands on the mat for a wider base, or even hook their opponent's armpits. The core principle remains: maintain constant pressure and prevent your opponent from creating space. The hand-on-hip grip is a common and effective starting point for white belts due to its direct control of hip movement.

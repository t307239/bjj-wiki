Modified Mount: The Foundation of Control in BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Modified Mount is a dominant top position achieved after establishing standard mount. It secures superior control and sets up submissions.

White belts often struggle by being too high or too low, losing base and allowing escapes.

The key is maintaining a tight connection with your opponent's hips and keeping your weight centered over theirs.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From standard mount, ensure both knees are inside the opponent's armpits.
    2. Maintain a wide base, with feet flat on the mat.
    3. Shift your weight slightly forward, driving your hips down towards their pelvis.
    4. Secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand, reaching across their chest to their left collar.
    5. Secure a secondary grip on their left sleeve with your left hand, pinching it between your elbow and torso.
    6. Tighten your core and press your chest into their chest, creating a "hug" with your upper body.
    7. Gently adjust your knee position to be just outside their hips, maintaining pressure and preventing them from bridging.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly placing knees too high on the opponent's chest can cause your own hips to lift, losing base and allowing them to shrimp out. This can strain your lower back ligaments. The correct position is with knees framing their hips.
    • Gripping too loosely on the sleeve allows the opponent to create space and potentially escape. This can lead to wrist sprains if they forcefully pull away. Always pinch the sleeve firmly.
    • Trying to "smash" down with excessive forward pressure without proper hip alignment can hyperextend the opponent's neck or shoulders, risking injury. Maintain centered weight and control.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice transitioning from standard mount to Modified Mount without a partner, focusing on hip pressure and grip placement. (5 reps each side, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner drilling (passive): With a compliant partner, practice establishing Modified Mount from standard mount. Focus on controlling their hips. (10 reps, 25% resistance)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Practice transitioning to Modified Mount while your partner attempts to shrimp or bridge. Focus on maintaining your base. (15 reps, 50% resistance)
    4. Submission attempts: From Modified Mount, practice a basic submission like a cross-collar choke, focusing on maintaining the position while attacking. (20 reps, 50% resistance)
    5. Live rolling (controlled): Incorporate Modified Mount into controlled sparring rounds. Focus on achieving the position and maintaining it. (3 rounds, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling (full): Apply Modified Mount in regular sparring, focusing on transitions and submission threats. (5 rounds, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • After successfully establishing standard mount and the opponent is trying to bridge or shrimp away.
    • When you have secured a strong cross-collar grip and can feel their shoulders pressing into the mat.
    • As a transition from a failed submission attempt from standard mount.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Shrimp Escape: If the opponent attempts to shrimp, maintain your cross-collar grip and drive your chest into their side, preventing them from creating space. If they succeed in creating space, immediately transition to side control.
    • Bridge and Roll: If the opponent bridges aggressively, anticipate the movement and post your foot on their hip. Shift your weight to the opposite side and use your collar grip to help pull them off balance.
    • Elbow Frame: If the opponent tries to frame with their elbow inside your knee, adjust your knee position to the outside of their hip and use your grip to pull them in closer.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to get Modified Mount?

    This often occurs if you're reaching too far across with your collar grip, causing your shoulder to internally rotate excessively. Ensure your bicep is against their neck and your elbow is tucked in. Also, avoid driving your weight directly down on their shoulder; focus on driving your hips forward and keeping your chest connected to theirs.

    Q: How do I maintain Modified Mount on a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and connection are more important than brute force. Focus on keeping your hips heavy and your chest glued to their chest. Use your legs to "hug" their hips, preventing them from bridging. Your collar grip should be tight, and you might need to adjust your secondary sleeve grip to control their arm more effectively, preventing them from creating frames.

    Q: Is Modified Mount the same as S-Mount?

    No, Modified Mount and S-Mount are distinct positions. In Modified Mount, both of your knees frame the opponent's hips, and your weight is distributed directly over their torso. In S-Mount, one knee is often on the mat and the other leg is "hooked" around the opponent's arm or head, creating a more dynamic attacking platform. Modified Mount is generally a more stable control position.

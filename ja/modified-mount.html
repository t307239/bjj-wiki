Modified Mount: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Modified Mount is a dominant top position achieved from standard mount, offering superior control and submission opportunities.

White belts often struggle by being too high or too low, losing connection and allowing the opponent to escape.

The key is maintaining hip pressure and creating an unassailable base by controlling the opponent's hips with your knees.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From standard mount, secure your opponent's hips with your knees. Your shins should be perpendicular to their torso.
    2. Shift your weight forward, driving your hips down towards their hips. Aim to make your pelvis heavier than theirs.
    3. Place your left knee on the mat, slightly behind and to the left of your opponent's hip bone. Your left foot should be tucked under your right thigh.
    4. Extend your right leg, placing your right foot flat on the mat, roughly shoulder-width from your left knee. This creates a stable base.
    5. Grip your opponent's left hip with your right hand, palm facing inward, fingers pointing up. This prevents them from bridging.
    6. Grip your opponent's right hip with your left hand, palm facing inward, fingers pointing up. This adds to hip control.
    7. Maintain a slight forward lean, keeping your chest close to their chest. Your base should feel like a strong tripod.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Placing knees too wide apart: This overloads the knee joints and can cause medial collateral ligament (MCL) tears. The correct position is knees roughly hip-width apart, forming a stable base.
    • Arching your back excessively to stay high: This puts immense stress on the lumbar spine, leading to potential disc herniation. Maintain a neutral spine and use hip pressure instead.
    • Letting your hips fall too low and forward: This can cause your shins to press into the opponent's thighs, creating an opening for them to shrimp or bridge. Keep your hips driven down and slightly back, controlling their hips.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice transitioning from standard mount to Modified Mount on an empty mat. Focus on hip pressure and base. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): From standard mount, your partner remains still. Execute the transition to Modified Mount and hold for 10 seconds. (20 reps)
    3. Partner drill (light pressure): Your partner offers minimal resistance by trying to bridge lightly. Maintain Modified Mount. (25% resistance, 30 reps)
    4. Partner drill (controlled movement): Your partner attempts to shrimp out. You maintain Modified Mount and prevent the escape. (50% resistance, 20 reps)
    5. Partner drill (simulated escape): Your partner actively tries to escape by bridging and shrimping. Focus on maintaining control. (75% resistance, 15 reps)
    6. Live rolling: Attempt Modified Mount during sparring. Focus on applying the technique correctly. (90-100% resistance, 5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • After successfully passing the guard and achieving standard mount.
    • When your opponent tries to bridge or shrimp from standard mount, and you need to secure a more stable position.
    • When you are looking to transition to armbar or cross-collar chokes from mount.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Shrimp (Hip Escape): If the opponent's hips are too far forward, the defender can shrimp their hips away to create space and potentially escape the mount.
    • Bridge: A well-timed bridge can unbalance the opponent and create an opportunity to escape the mount, especially if their base is compromised.
    • Elbow Escape: If the opponent is trapped, they can try to bring their elbow inside and use it as a frame to create space and initiate a shrimp.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to stay in Modified Mount?

    Your lower back pain likely stems from arching your spine to compensate for insufficient hip pressure. Instead of arching, drive your hips down towards your opponent's hips. This biomechanically aligns your pelvis and spine, transferring weight directly to their base, not your own back.

    Q: How can I achieve Modified Mount against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is key. Focus on driving your hips down with extreme downward pressure, almost as if you're trying to 'sit' on their hips. Use your grip on their hips to prevent them from using their mass to push you up. Your base needs to be wide and stable.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from standard mount to Modified Mount?

    The optimal time to transition is immediately after securing standard mount, before your opponent has a chance to establish their defensive frames or initiate a bridge. If they are already trying to bridge, the Modified Mount's hip control is essential to nullify their attempt and maintain dominance.

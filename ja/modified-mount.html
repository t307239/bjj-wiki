Modified Mount is a superior variation of the standard mount position, offering greater control and submission opportunities. It is achieved by shifting your hips slightly back and anchoring your knees wider than shoulder-width.

White belts often struggle because they lack the body awareness to maintain proper hip pressure and base, leading to the opponent escaping easily. They might also overextend their limbs, creating openings.

The key to Modified Mount lies in establishing a stable, low base with your hips, creating a "seatbelt" effect with your legs that prevents the opponent from bridging or shrimping out.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Start from a standard mount position with your knees inside the opponent's armpits.
    2. Shift your hips back by approximately 5-10 cm, lowering your center of gravity.
    3. Widen your knees so they are slightly wider than the opponent's shoulders, creating a stable base.
    4. Drive your shin bones down into the mat, engaging your quadriceps to create upward pressure.
    5. Maintain a strong "seatbelt" grip: one arm over the opponent's far shoulder, the other under their near armpit, with your hands clasped behind their back or on their opposite shoulder blade.
    6. Keep your chest pressed into the opponent's chest, creating a tight connection.
    7. Tuck your chin to your chest to protect your head and neck, and maintain a neutral spine.
    8. Distribute your weight primarily onto your hips and knees, not your feet, to avoid being easily swept.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Pain: Pushing knees directly into the opponent's armpits with no hip shift can hyperextend the knee joint, causing ACL or MCL tears. Maintain a wider base and lower hips.
    • Lower Back Strain: Arching your back to maintain chest pressure without engaging your core leads to Lumbar spine flexion and strain. Keep your spine neutral and engage your abdominal muscles.
    • Shoulder Impingement: Reaching too far for grips or forcing your arms into tight spaces can pinch the rotator cuff. Maintain a controlled range of motion and prioritize hip pressure over extreme arm extension.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Shift Drill: From a seated position on the mat, practice shifting your hips back and widening your knees, focusing on hip mobility and stability. (10 reps x 0% resistance)
    2. Mount to Modified Mount Transition: Starting in mount, practice the hip shift and knee widening motion without a partner. Focus on the feeling of a stable base. (20 reps x 0% resistance)
    3. Partnered Static Mount: Your partner lies on their back. You establish mount, then practice the modified mount transition and hold for 10 seconds. Partner remains completely still. (15 reps x 0% resistance)
    4. Partnered Light Resistance Mount: Establish modified mount. Your partner gently tries to bridge or shrimp. Focus on maintaining your base and grip. (10 reps x 25% resistance)
    5. Controlled Mount Escapes: Your partner attempts to escape from modified mount. You practice maintaining the position with controlled hip pressure and subtle adjustments. (10 rounds, 30 seconds each x 50% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling: Apply Modified Mount in sparring, focusing on maintaining the position and transitioning to submissions. (5 rounds, 3 minutes each x 75-90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • After successfully passing the guard and establishing mount.
    • When your opponent attempts to bridge or shrimp aggressively from standard mount, creating space.
    • As a transition from knee-on-belly when the opponent turns away.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): If the attacker's hips are too high and their base is narrow, the defender can shrimp their hips out to the side, creating space to recover guard. The attacker must maintain low hips and wide knees to prevent this.
    • Bridge and Roll: A well-timed bridge, combined with a hip escape, can disrupt the attacker's base, forcing them to abandon the mount. The attacker needs to maintain a strong, low base to absorb this.
    • Elbow Escape: If the attacker's knees are too far forward, the defender can use their elbows to create space and "walk" their hips out, similar to a guard recovery. The attacker must keep their knees tucked and hips low.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why do my knees hurt when I try to hold Modified Mount?

    Knee pain in Modified Mount often stems from excessive direct pressure on the patellofemoral joint or the collateral ligaments. This happens when your knees are too close together, or you are not properly lowering your hips to create a stable base. Ensure your knees are wider than shoulder-width and your hips are low, driving down into the mat. This distributes the pressure more evenly and reduces strain on the knee joints.

    Q: How can I use Modified Mount against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, Modified Mount is about leverage and control, not brute strength. Focus on keeping your hips extremely low, almost pinning your glutes to their back. Use your forearms and shins to actively "frame" against their ribs and hips, preventing them from posturing up. Your seatbelt grip should be tight, controlling their upper body to limit their ability to generate power.

    Q: When is the best moment to switch from standard mount to Modified Mount?

    The optimal time to transition to Modified Mount is when you feel your opponent beginning to create space to escape, typically by bridging or shrimping. As they initiate that movement, you can use their momentum to shift your hips back and widen your base. This "catches" their escape attempt and solidifies your superior position before they can fully recover their guard.

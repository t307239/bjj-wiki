Modified Mount: The Foundation of Control in BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Modified Mount is a dominant position achieved after securing a standard mount, allowing for superior control and attacking angles.

White belts often struggle by being too high or too loose, losing the connection needed to maintain pressure.

The key is a stable, low base with hip-to-hip connection, preventing the opponent from bridging effectively.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From standard mount, retract your knees towards your hips, lowering your base. Your shins should be parallel to the mat.
    2. Shift your weight forward, driving your hips down and slightly forward, creating a 'hip-to-hip' connection with your opponent's pelvis.
    3. Ensure your knees are wider than your opponent's hips, preventing them from easily bringing their knees to their chest.
    4. Secure your grips: either cross-collar grips with thumbs inside the lapel, or gable grip with your hands clasped behind their upper back.
    5. Maintain pressure by actively driving your sternum into their chest, creating a strong anterior lean.
    6. Keep your feet flexed, toes pointing down, to provide a stable base and prevent your ankles from being easily attacked.
    7. Your hips should remain heavy, sinking towards the mat, not hovering above your opponent.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee-to-ankle hyper-extension: An opponent bridging hard while you are too high on their hips can cause your knees to hyperextend backward. This damages the ACL and PCL. Correct: Maintain a low base with knees angled towards the mat, ensuring your hips are below their pelvis for leverage.
    • Ankle lock vulnerability: When your feet are flat and loose, an opponent can hook your heel and apply a toe-hold or ankle lock, potentially tearing ligaments. Correct: Keep your feet flexed, toes pointed down, and actively use your shins to maintain a tight connection to their hips.
    • Loss of base leading to sweep: If your weight is not distributed forward and your hips are too far back, a simple hip escape or bridge from the opponent can easily unbalance you, leading to a sweep. Correct: Drive your hips down and forward, creating a solid base with your sternum pressed into their chest.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice sinking your hips and establishing the low base from a seated position. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Your partner lies on their back. You mount and practice sinking your hips into the modified mount position, establishing grips. (20 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Your partner offers minimal resistance to your hip movement. Focus on maintaining the low base and hip-to-hip connection. (20 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Transition drilling: Start from a standard mount and practice the transition to modified mount. Partner offers slight bridge attempts. (15 reps, 50% resistance)
    5. Controlled rolling: Execute modified mount and maintain the position for 30 seconds, focusing on pressure and control. Partner attempts to escape. (10 rounds, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Apply modified mount during regular sparring, focusing on maintaining the position and transitioning to submissions. (5 rounds, 90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Immediately after securing a standard mount and feeling the opponent's hips begin to tighten or create space.
    • When your opponent attempts to shrimp or bridge, and you can use their momentum to sink your hips lower.
    • After successfully defending an attempted escape from standard mount, using the opportunity to establish a more secure position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape to Guard Recovery: If the opponent sinks their hips and prevents you from lowering your base, perform a hip escape to their side and immediately try to bring your knee between your hips to re-establish guard.
    • Bridge and Roll: If the opponent is too high and their base is weak, a well-timed bridge into their chest can disrupt their balance and potentially flip them over.
    • Knee-to-Chest Frame: If the opponent tries to flatten you out from modified mount, create a frame with your forearm and knee, pushing their chest away to create space to bring your guard back.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to get into Modified Mount?

    Lower back pain often stems from an anterior pelvic tilt and insufficient hip flexion. You might be arching your back excessively instead of driving your hips down and forward. Ensure you are actively flexing your glutes and engaging your core, allowing your pelvis to hinge naturally. Focus on sinking your hips directly onto your opponent's, not just pressing your chest into them.

    Q: How do I maintain Modified Mount against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and connection are paramount. You must get your hips *underneath* their center of gravity. Focus on a 'hip-to-hip' connection, where your pelvis is directly in contact with their pelvis. Use your shins to hug their hips tightly and drive your sternum into their chest to prevent them from creating space. Don't try to muscle them; use your weight distribution.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from standard mount to Modified Mount?

    The optimal time is when you feel the opponent attempting to create space or bridge. As they push into you to shrimp or bridge, use that reaction to sink your hips lower and tighter. If you are too high and they are actively resisting, it's harder to transition. Look for that moment of instability in their defense to secure your more dominant, lower base.

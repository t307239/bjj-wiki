Modified Mount is a dominant position achieved from a standard mount where you adjust your weight distribution for better control and submission opportunities.

White belts often struggle by staying too high or too low, allowing their opponent to escape or create space.

The key insight is maintaining constant pressure through your hips and knees while keeping your base wide for stability.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From standard mount, secure your opponent's shoulders with your hands.
    2. Shift your weight forward, driving your hips down towards your opponent's pelvis.
    3. Bring your knees wider than shoulder-width apart, anchoring them to the mat.
    4. Adjust your chest to be perpendicular to your opponent's chest, minimizing the gap.
    5. Control your opponent's hips by digging your shin bones into their inner thighs.
    6. Ensure your weight is distributed primarily on your opponent's chest and hips, not on your knees.
    7. Maintain a strong base by keeping your feet flexed and your toes lightly on the mat.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Sitting too high on the opponent's chest: This can hyperextend the opponent's spine, risking lumbar injury. The correct position is with hips driving down.
    • Resting weight on knees: This puts excessive pressure on the knee joint, potentially damaging the ACL or meniscus. Always keep weight on hips and chest.
    • Allowing opponent to bridge effectively: A wide, stable base with hips low prevents the opponent from generating upward force, thus avoiding shoulder or neck strain.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice transitioning from regular mount to modified mount without a partner. Focus on hip and knee placement. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner drilling: With a compliant partner, practice the transition and holding the position. (20 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    3. Controlled positional sparring: Focus solely on achieving and maintaining modified mount against light resistance. (5 rounds, 50% resistance)
    4. Positional sparring with submission focus: From modified mount, attempt to secure submissions. Partner defends. (5 rounds, 75% resistance)
    5. Limited submission sparring: Engage in rolling with the goal of reaching modified mount and attacking submissions. Opponent can escape. (5 rounds, 90% resistance)
    6. Full rolling: Apply modified mount in a live rolling scenario. (10 minutes, 100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When opponent is flat on their back from a sweep or guard pass.
    • When you have secured a strong standard mount and opponent is trying to buck you off.
    • When you want to transition to an armbar or Ezekiel choke from a dominant mount.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): If the opponent's hips are too far forward, a quick hip escape creates space to recover guard.
    • Bridge and Roll: If the opponent fails to maintain a low base, a well-timed bridge can unbalance them and lead to an escape.
    • Armbar Defense: If the opponent overcommits to the transition, you can attempt to trap an arm and transition to an armbar defense.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my opponent always escape Modified Mount when I try it?

    Your opponent is likely escaping because you are not maintaining sufficient hip pressure. Ensure your hips are driving *down* towards their pelvis, not just sitting on top. Additionally, check that your knees are wide and digging into their inner thighs to prevent them from creating space to shrimp out.

    Q: How do I maintain Modified Mount against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on using your entire body weight effectively and maintaining a strong base. Instead of trying to overpower them, emphasize hip pressure and keeping your chest perpendicular to their torso. Use your shin bones to control their hips and prevent them from generating upward force.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from regular mount to Modified Mount?

    The optimal time is when your opponent is trying to buck you off or escape your standard mount. This is when they are most vulnerable to a weight shift. Also, if you are setting up a submission like an armbar, transitioning to Modified Mount can provide a more secure platform.

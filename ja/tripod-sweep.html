Tripod Sweep: A Biomechanically Precise Guide for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Tripod Sweep originates from the closed guard, aiming to unbalance your opponent and reverse the position. It's a foundational sweep for controlling the guard.

White belts often fail by relying on brute strength or incorrect body angles, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The core principle is leveraging your legs as a stable tripod while actively breaking your opponent's posture and creating a clear angle for the sweep.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Establish a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (left sleeve) with your left hand. Your opponent's hips should be close to yours.
    2. Leg Placement: Insert your right foot (sole of foot) onto your opponent's hip, creating a frame. Your left knee should be tucked towards your chest, creating a tight guard.
    3. Postural Break: Pull down strongly on the collar grip and slightly forward on the sleeve grip, forcing your opponent's upper body towards you. Simultaneously, push your right foot into their hip.
    4. Hip Angle: Rotate your hips sharply to your left, away from your opponent. Your right hip should lift off the mat slightly.
    5. Leg Pivot: As you rotate your hips, pivot your left leg. Your left knee should move towards your opponent's left armpit, and your left foot should hook around their left leg, near the ankle or calf.
    6. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your right leg (planted on the hip) and your left leg (hooking their leg). Your upper body continues to pull them down and forward.
    7. Sweep Execution: Extend your right leg forcefully from the hip while maintaining the pull on the collar and sleeve. This action, combined with your hip rotation and leg hook, will cause your opponent to fall to their right.
    8. Transition: Immediately after the sweep, drive your hips forward and secure a dominant top position, typically side control or mount, by continuing to drive your weight into them.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hip Extension: Attempting to lift your opponent solely by extending your legs without proper hip rotation. This can strain your lower back and knees, as the force is not biomechanically distributed.
    • Knee Valgus/Varus Stress: Allowing your knee to buckle inward (valgus) or outward (varus) when attempting the leg pivot or sweep. This can lead to ligament tears (ACL/MCL) in the knee due to unnatural torque.
    • Neck Strain from Pulling: Yanking the collar grip with excessive cervical spine extension or flexion. This can cause neck sprains or nerve impingement, especially if the opponent resists with their head position.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip & Leg Movement: Practice the hip rotation and leg insertion/pivot motion without a partner. Focus on the feeling of the tripod base. (50 reps each side, 0% resistance).
    2. Partnered Frame & Pull: With a compliant partner in your guard, practice establishing grips, framing with your foot on the hip, and pulling posture down. Focus on the initial break. (20 reps, 25% resistance).
    3. Partnered Hip Angle & Pivot: Add the hip rotation and leg hook movement. Your partner remains still, allowing you to feel the mechanics. (20 reps, 25% resistance).
    4. Full Sweep Execution (Static Partner): Execute the entire Tripod Sweep against a partner who is actively trying to resist but not actively countering. Focus on perfect body mechanics. (15 reps, 50% resistance).
    5. Controlled Sweep Attempts: Work with a partner who understands the sweep. You attempt the sweep, they offer mild resistance, allowing you to feel the timing and pressure. (10 reps, 75% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling Application: Attempt the Tripod Sweep during live rolling, focusing on recognizing the opportunity and executing the mechanics under dynamic pressure. (Ongoing, 100% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up and leaning forward, creating a clear opportunity to break their posture.
    • When your opponent is attempting to pass your guard and their weight is forward, making them susceptible to a sweep.
    • When you have a strong cross-collar grip and can effectively control their upper body.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass Defense: If the opponent attempts to stack pass by driving their weight down, immediately widen your base and drive your hips up, creating space. Release the collar grip and bring your knees together to defend.
    • Knee-to-Elbow Defense: If the opponent tries to bring their knee to their elbow to break your guard, maintain your grip on the collar and sleeve. Use your free hand to push their attacking knee away, preventing them from establishing a strong base.
    • Postural Reset: If the sweep is failing, immediately bring your knees back to your chest and re-establish a tight guard. Do not expose your legs or neck to further submission attempts.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Tripod Sweep?

    Wrist pain often occurs from gripping too tightly with your fingers and not utilizing your forearm and shoulder to generate pulling power. When gripping the collar, ensure your thumb wraps around and your forearm is engaged. Instead of just pulling with your hand, use your entire upper body's weight and rotational force to break posture, reducing strain on your wrist.

    Q: How do I Tripod Sweep against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes even more critical. Focus intensely on breaking their posture *down* towards you before initiating the sweep. Use your foot on their hip as a solid fulcrum. When you rotate your hips, drive *away* from their center of gravity and extend your legs with maximum explosive power to unbalance their superior mass.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Tripod Sweep in BJJ, and what if they have no-gi grips?

    The Tripod Sweep is best attempted when your opponent is postured high and leaning forward, making their base unstable. In no-gi, if they have no specific grips, you'll rely on controlling their shoulders or upper arms with your hands. Focus on creating a strong frame with your leg on the hip and a secure hook on their leg, driving your hips to create the necessary angle for the sweep.

The Tripod Sweep originates from closed guard and aims to elevate your hips to unbalance and sweep your opponent. It's a fundamental guard retention and sweep tool.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength or poor body positioning, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury. Focus on the mechanical leverage, not just muscle.

The core principle is creating a stable tripod with your legs and one arm, then using your hips to generate the upward and backward force needed for the sweep.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish closed guard with both hooks inside the opponent's thighs, hips connected. Your knees should be close to your chest.
    2. Secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand, palm facing inward, gripping the fabric near the opponent's sternum. Keep your left elbow tight to your body.
    3. With your left hand, secure a sleeve grip on the opponent's right sleeve, gripping firmly at the wrist or forearm. Maintain a slight pull to break their posture.
    4. Shift your hips slightly to your left, bringing your right knee forward and across the opponent's chest, creating a strong frame.
    5. Lift your left leg, bending your knee, and place the sole of your left foot onto the opponent's right hip bone. This forms the 'tripod' with your right knee and left foot.
    6. Simultaneously, begin to drive your right knee upwards and slightly backward, while pulling down on the cross-collar grip. Your hips should rise off the mat.
    7. Use your left foot on their hip to push and guide their body away from you as you continue to drive your hips up and back, initiating the sweep motion.
    8. As the opponent begins to fall, transition your hips to the side and begin to drive your knees forward to establish side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending your knee or ankle when placing your foot on the opponent's hip can strain or tear the ACL or MCL. Always maintain a slight bend in your knee and control the placement of your foot.
    • Gripping too tightly with your left hand on the sleeve and pulling straight down can hyperextend your elbow, risking a sprain or dislocation. Maintain a controlled pull, focusing on breaking their posture, not just yanking the arm.
    • Attempting to sweep by simply arching your back and pushing with your legs without hip elevation can lead to lower back strain. Focus on driving your hips upwards and then backward, engaging your core.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip movement and leg placement from closed guard on the mat, with no resistance (0%). 20 repetitions.
    2. Partner static drill: With a compliant partner in closed guard, practice the grip acquisition and hip elevation without attempting the sweep (25%). 10 repetitions per side.
    3. Partner controlled sweep: With a compliant partner, execute the full tripod sweep motion, allowing them to resist slightly but not actively defend (50%). 5 repetitions per side.
    4. Light resistance drill: Your partner offers mild resistance to your sweep attempt, requiring you to generate more force with your hips and grips (75%). 5 repetitions per side.
    5. Flow rolling: Incorporate the tripod sweep into a flow rolling session, where you and your partner focus on technique over winning (90%). 3 minutes.
    6. Live rolling: Apply the tripod sweep during live rolling, accepting that it may not always work, but focusing on the mechanics (100%). 5 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up and their weight is forward, making them unstable.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard by stacking or pressuring their chest into yours.
    • When you have established strong grips and can control their posture and base.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape & Re-Guard: If the opponent starts to fall sideways, immediately hip escape to your side and re-establish closed guard or an open guard position.
    • Knee-to-Chest Defense: If the opponent attempts to drive their knee into your chest during the sweep, use your arms to frame and push their head away, maintaining your guard connection.
    • Base Recovery: If the opponent manages to maintain their base, immediately switch to a different sweep or submission threat. Do not force a failed sweep; reset to closed guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my right wrist hurt when I try the Tripod Sweep?

    Wrist pain often stems from an insecure collar grip or excessive tension. Ensure your cross-collar grip is deep and your palm is facing inward, gripping the fabric rather than just the lapel edge. Also, avoid locking your wrist; maintain a slight bend and use your forearm and biceps to pull, not just your wrist strength.

    Q: How can I perform the Tripod Sweep effectively against a much larger and heavier opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on the mechanics of creating the 'tripod' with precise foot and knee placement on their hip and chest. Drive your hips UP and then BACK with maximum explosiveness, using their weight against them rather than trying to muscle through. Secure a strong cross-collar grip to break their posture significantly.

    Q: I keep losing my guard after attempting the Tripod Sweep. What am I doing wrong?

    Losing guard typically happens if you don't commit to the sweep or if your base is compromised. After placing your foot on their hip and initiating the hip drive, you must fully commit to the upward and backward motion. If the sweep fails, immediately bring your knee back to your chest and re-establish your closed guard hooks, rather than letting your legs fall open.

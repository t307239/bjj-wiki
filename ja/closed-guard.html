Closed Guard: The Foundation of BJJ for White Belts

WHITE★☆☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Closed Guard starts from the bottom position, wrapping your legs around your opponent's torso to control their posture and create submission opportunities.

White belts often fail by being too rigid, losing the space needed for hip movement, or relying on static grips instead of dynamic pressure.

The core principle is maintaining hip connection while creating angles with your legs and hips to off-balance your opponent, enabling attacks.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Secure both of your opponent's sleeves at the wrist with your hands (e.g., "Double Sleeve Grip").
    2. Leg Placement: Bring your feet to your opponent's hips, with the soles of your feet pressing against their hip bones.
    3. Locking the Guard: Cross your ankles behind your opponent's back, ensuring your heels are close to your own glutes. This creates a tight "lock."
    4. Hip Angle: Tilt your hips upwards towards the ceiling, creating a slight gap between your lower back and the mat.
    5. Posture Control: Use the sleeve grips to pull your opponent's posture down, bringing their head towards your chest.
    6. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight slightly to one side, creating an angle with your hips, preparing for a sweep or submission.
    7. Knee Pressure: Keep your knees tight around your opponent's torso, preventing them from posturing up or passing your guard.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Injury (ACL/MCL): Attempting to "kick" the legs over the opponent's shoulders without proper hip angling can cause excessive internal rotation and valgus stress at the knee.
    • Correct: Use hip elevation and a controlled leg wrap, ensuring knees are angled inward and feet are crossed securely.
    • Lower Back Strain: Trying to lift the opponent by arching the back instead of using hip elevation and leg tension can lead to acute lumbar strain.
    • Correct: Drive your hips up towards the ceiling and use your leg strength to control the opponent's base.
    • Wrist Sprains: Gripping sleeves too tightly with an open hand and allowing the opponent to twist their arms out can hyperextend the wrist.
    • Correct: Use a "figure-four" grip on the sleeve and maintain a slightly bent elbow, ready to adjust.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Elevation: Practice lifting your hips off the mat and holding the position for 10 seconds, 10 reps.
    2. Solo Guard Closure: Practice wrapping your legs around a dummy or pillow, locking your ankles, 10 reps.
    3. Partner Guard Closure (0% Resistance): Partner lies on their back, you practice closing guard, locking, and maintaining posture control. 10 reps each side.
    4. Partner Guard Closure with Light Grip (25% Resistance): Partner provides minimal resistance to your sleeve grips. Practice pulling posture down and holding the lock. 5 reps, 2 minutes.
    5. Partner Sweep Setup (50% Resistance): Practice isolating a sleeve and hip bumping to create an angle for a sweep. Partner offers slight resistance to your movements. 5 reps, 3 minutes.
    6. Live Rolling (75%-90% Resistance): Focus solely on maintaining Closed Guard, preventing posture, and attempting one sweep or submission attempt per minute. 5 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When an opponent attempts to pass your guard by standing up.
    • When an opponent is close to you and has not established a strong base.
    • As a defensive reaction when you are taken down and the opponent tries to establish side control.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS (from opponent's perspective):

    • Posture Control: Opponent "posts" their hands on your hips, extending their arms to create frames and prevent you from pulling them down.
    • Knee Slice Pass: Opponent drives one knee across your body, attempting to displace your leg and break the guard.
    • Standing Posture: Opponent uses their weight and grip fighting to break your leg lock and stand up.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to control my opponent's sleeves in Closed Guard?

    This often happens when you grip with an open palm and allow your opponent to twist their arm. To prevent this, use a "figure-four" grip on the sleeve, where your thumb wraps around the opponent's wrist and your other fingers grip the fabric. Maintain a slightly bent elbow; this creates a stronger, more stable grip and protects your wrist from hyperextension.

    Q: How can I use Closed Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, your focus must be on using angles and hip mobility to negate their weight. Instead of trying to pull them straight down, use your sleeve grips to create off-balancing angles by pulling one shoulder down and to the side. Elevate your hips to create space, allowing you to adjust your leg position and look for sweeps that utilize leverage rather than brute strength.

    Q: You should consider transitioning if your opponent consistently maintains excellent posture, making it impossible to create angles or secure submissions. Also, if they are effectively "framing" on your hips with their hands, preventing you from closing the distance, it's time to consider opening your guard to a different position like Open Guard or Half Guard to create new opportunities.

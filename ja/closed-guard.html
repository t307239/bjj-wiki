クローズドガード：白帯のためのBJJの基礎

Closed Guard begins from being on your back with your opponent between your legs, controlling their posture. It aims to create control, set up submissions, and initiate sweeps.

White belts often fail by tensing up, allowing their opponent to establish strong base and posture. This leads to predictable, low-percentage attacks.

The key insight is using your legs as flexible, powerful levers to control distance and break your opponent's balance, not just as a static barrier.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From supine position, bring knees towards chest. Hook feet behind opponent's thighs, crossing ankles over shins to form the "lock".
    2. Ensure knees are tight to opponent's hips, creating a "frame" that limits their forward pressure.
    3. Grip opponent's collar(s) with both hands, palms facing each other (cross-collar grip) or one palm up, one palm down (standard grip).
    4. Alternatively, grip opponent's sleeves near the elbow with your hands, fingers pointing towards their elbows.
    5. Maintain a slight pelvic tilt upwards, engaging your glutes to "hug" the opponent and prevent them from standing.
    6. Keep your elbows tucked to your sides, creating a "short" guard and preventing arm-based attacks like armbars.
    7. Distribute your weight evenly on your back, maintaining a stable base while keeping your hips mobile.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly "locking" the guard by crossing shins too high can hyperextend the knees, potentially tearing the ACL or MCL. Always cross ankles over shins, keeping knees below hip height.
    • Tensing your legs rigidly to "hold" the guard allows your opponent to stack you, potentially compressing your spine or causing disc injury. Maintain a "springy" feel, using glute and hamstring engagement for control.
    • Gripping the opponent's pants too low (ankles) allows them to easily "kite" step out, breaking your guard. Instead, grip the pants high on the thigh or the collar/sleeves for better posture control.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the leg lock (crossing ankles over shins) and hip tilt motion for 30 repetitions, focusing on smooth transitions.
    2. Solo drill: Practice establishing collar and sleeve grips from the locked guard position, 30 repetitions.
    3. Partner drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice locking the guard and holding the grips for 1 minute, then releasing and re-locking, 5 repetitions.
    4. Partner drill (25% resistance): Practice maintaining closed guard while your partner attempts to "post" their hands on your hips to create space, 3 rounds of 1 minute.
    5. Partner drill (50% resistance): Practice transitioning from closed guard to a basic sweep (e.g., scissor sweep) while your partner offers minimal resistance, 3 rounds of 1 minute.
    6. Live rolling (75-90% resistance): Apply closed guard techniques in sparring, focusing on maintaining the guard and setting up one submission or sweep attempt per round.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard from a standing or kneeling position.
    • When your opponent is in your guard and has not established a strong, stable base.
    • When you have successfully "pulled guard" or the opponent has "passed" to another position where you can regain closed guard.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Posture Control: Use collar and sleeve grips to "break" your opponent's posture by pulling their head down and their arms forward, creating opportunities for sweeps and submissions.
    • Hip Escape & Angle Creation: Use subtle hip movements to create angles, making it harder for your opponent to maintain their base and easier for you to attack.
    • Leg Control & Leverage: Use your legs to "hug" the opponent's hips, controlling their distance and preventing them from posturing up or driving forward.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to hold Closed Guard?

    Lower back pain often stems from excessive arching of your spine to compensate for a lack of hip engagement. Instead of arching, focus on tilting your pelvis upwards and squeezing your glutes. This engages your core and hamstrings, creating a stable base without straining your lower back.

    Q: How do I maintain Closed Guard against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on "pinching" your knees together tightly to control their hips and prevent them from driving forward. Use your arms to break their posture by pulling their head down and their arms forward, creating leverage. Don't try to "hold" them; instead, use your legs to create constant tension and off-balance them.

    Q: When is the best time to attack from Closed Guard, and when should I focus on sweeping?

    Attack from Closed Guard when you've successfully broken your opponent's posture and they are leaning forward, exposing their neck or arms. Sweep when your opponent is unbalanced and their weight is not firmly planted on their feet. Look for opportunities where their hips are not aligned with their shoulders.

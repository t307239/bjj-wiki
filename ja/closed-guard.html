Closed Guard: The Foundation of BJJ for White Belts

WHITE★☆☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Closed Guard starts with your opponent inside your legs, creating a secure, closed position. It aims to control your opponent's posture and create submission or sweep opportunities.

White belts often fail by allowing their hips to become flat or by overextending their limbs, losing the control needed for leverage.

The key mechanical insight is maintaining a strong 'hip connection' and using your legs as a vise to break posture and limit their movement.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Sit up with knees bent at 90 degrees, feet flat on the mat. This establishes your base.
    2. Bring your opponent between your knees, ensuring your shins cross tightly across their hips.
    3. Grip the back of your opponent's collar with both hands, palms facing each other. This is the 'Collar Grip'.
    4. Simultaneously, grip one of your opponent's sleeves with your opposite hand, thumb inside the cuff. This is the 'Sleeve Grip'.
    5. Tuck your chin to your chest and pull your knees towards your chest, creating a 'hug' with your legs. This breaks their posture.
    6. Arch your lower back slightly and drive your hips forward, creating a tight 'frame' with your legs against their hips.
    7. Keep your elbows tucked to your sides, preventing them from posturing up or creating space. Maintain pressure on their hips.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Hyperextension: Extending legs too straight and too far apart to 'trap' the opponent. This can lead to hyperextension of the knee joint, potentially tearing the ACL or MCL.
    • Correct: Keep knees bent and actively 'hug' the opponent's hips, maintaining a constant tension.
    • Elbow Valgus Stress: Allowing the opponent to push your arms outwards while your elbows are bent. This puts excessive valgus stress on the elbow joint, risking UCL tears.
    • Correct: Keep elbows tucked in close to your body, using your forearms to frame against their biceps.
    • Lower Back Strain: Arching the lower back excessively without engaging the core and hip flexors. This can lead to lumbar disc compression and muscle strains.
    • Correct: Engage your core by drawing your belly button towards your spine and use your hip flexors to drive your hips forward.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Hug: Lie on your back, practice bringing knees to chest and crossing shins, holding for 30 seconds. (10 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Solo Guard Formation: Practice sitting up, bringing knees to chest, and crossing shins. Then, lie down and practice assuming the closed guard from a seated position. (10 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Partnered Guard Formation (No Resistance): Your partner lies down. You practice sitting up, establishing collar and sleeve grips, and closing your guard around them. (10 reps, 0% resistance)
    4. Partnered Posture Break Drill: With partner lying down, you establish closed guard. Practice pulling them in and breaking their posture using your leg hug and hip drive. Partner offers no resistance to posture break. (10 reps, 10% resistance)
    5. Controlled Guard Retention: Your partner stands up slightly. You maintain closed guard and practice keeping your legs tight, preventing them from opening your guard. Partner gently tries to create space. (5 minutes, 25% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling (Guard Focus): Attempt to maintain closed guard and work towards sweeps or submissions. Partner actively tries to pass your guard. (5 minutes, 75% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is within arm's reach and you can establish grips.
    • When your opponent attempts to enter your guard or is in your guard without good posture.
    • When you want to control the distance and prevent your opponent from passing.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Posture Control: Continuously pull your opponent towards you with your leg hug and collar grips, breaking their posture to limit their options.
    • Hip Connection: Maintain a tight connection between your hips and your opponent's hips. Drive your hips forward to create a strong frame.
    • Limb Control: Secure your collar and sleeve grips to prevent your opponent from posturing up or establishing dominant grips on you.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I use Closed Guard?

    Wrist pain in Closed Guard often stems from gripping too tightly with a straight wrist or allowing your opponent to leverage your wrist in an awkward position. Ensure your wrists are neutral and slightly flexed, not hyperextended. When gripping collars, maintain a firm but not death-like grip, and focus on using your forearms and biceps to control your opponent's arms and posture.

    Q: How do I use Closed Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on **hip connection** and **posture control**. Your goal is to prevent them from using their weight advantage by keeping their hips close to yours and their posture broken. Use your legs to create a tight vise, constantly pulling them in. Prioritize breaking their posture with your leg hug and collar grips, making it difficult for them to generate pressure.

    Q: When should I switch from Closed Guard to another position?

    You should consider switching from Closed Guard if your opponent has excellent posture control, is effectively stacking you, or is creating significant space to pass. If they are consistently defending your sweeps and submissions, it might be time to transition. Look for opportunities to open your guard and transition to an Open Guard or attempt a sweep when they overcommit to a pass.

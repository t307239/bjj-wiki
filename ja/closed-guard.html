Closed Guard: Your Foundation in BJJ

WHITE★☆☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Closed Guard is a fundamental BJJ position where you control your opponent from your back, legs wrapped around their waist.

White belts often struggle because they lack control of the opponent's base and posture, leading to them being postured up too easily.

The key is maintaining a tight connection and controlling the opponent's hips and posture with your legs and core engagement.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Lie on your back with knees bent, feet flat on the mat. Bring your knees towards your chest.
    2. Swing your legs around the opponent's hips so your ankles cross behind their lower back, creating a secure "lock". Ensure your shins are pressed together.
    3. Grip the opponent's collar with both hands, palms facing you (cross-collar grip). Alternatively, grip their sleeves near the elbow.
    4. Pull the opponent down by drawing their hips towards you using your legs and arms. Keep your hips slightly elevated off the mat.
    5. Maintain a tight frame by keeping your elbows tucked in and your knees pressing towards your chest, preventing the opponent from posturing up.
    6. Shift your hips to the side to create angles for sweeps and submissions, always keeping your legs locked.
    7. Use your foot on the hip (near the opponent's armpit) to create space and control their posture further, or to initiate sweeps.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly crossing ankles too high on the back: This can strain the lumbar spine and place excessive pressure on the opponent's kidneys, leading to potential back injury for both parties. Always cross ankles at the lower back.
    • Letting knees splay open: This breaks the seal of the guard, allowing the opponent to easily pass by stepping their legs through. This can lead to knee hyperextension injuries. Keep shins pressed together.
    • Overextending your own back: Trying to pull the opponent down with just your arms while your back is arched puts immense strain on your lumbar spine, risking disc injury. Use your legs and core to pull them down, keeping your back relatively flat.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling (0%): Practice the leg lock and hip movement from lying down. Focus on the shin-to-shin pressure and ankle crossing. 50 reps.
    2. Partner drill - static grip (0%): Your partner lies on their back. You practice entering and securing the closed guard with the correct grips. Partner remains still. 20 reps.
    3. Partner drill - light resistance (25%): Partner tries to lightly resist your guard entry and maintain a slightly upright posture. You focus on locking and controlling their hips. 10 reps.
    4. Partner drill - posture control (50%): Partner actively tries to posture up. You practice using your legs and grips to break their posture and pull them down. 10 reps.
    5. Sweep attempts from closed guard (75%): With the guard locked and posture broken, attempt a basic sweep like the scissor sweep. Partner offers light defense. 5 reps per sweep.
    6. Live rolling - guard retention (90%): Start from closed guard. Focus on maintaining the guard against a partner attempting to pass. Avoid getting stacked. 2-minute rounds.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is in your guard and you are on your back.
    • When you have successfully transitioned from a failed sweep or submission attempt to a defensive position.
    • When you need to control your opponent's distance and prevent them from advancing their position.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stacking Pass: Opponent drives forward, placing weight on your hips and shoulders, attempting to break your guard. Defend by using your arms to frame against their shoulders and hips, and by driving your hips away to create space.
    • Hip Control: Opponent uses their hands to grip your knees or shins and push them apart. Defend by keeping your shins pressed together and using your arms to control their wrists or elbows.
    • Elbow Escape: Opponent uses their arms to create a frame and step their legs out. Defend by maintaining a tight connection and using your legs to prevent them from creating space.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my back hurt so much when I try to pull my opponent down in Closed Guard?

    Your back pain is likely from overusing your lumbar extensors instead of your legs and core. When pulling, focus on flexing your hips and drawing your knees towards your chest, engaging your hamstrings and glutes. Keep your lower back relatively flat against the mat, avoiding excessive arching.

    Q: How can I effectively use Closed Guard against a much larger and stronger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on controlling their posture and hips with extreme tightness. Use your legs to create a "vise" and prevent them from posturing up. Your grips should target their collar and sleeves to break their posture further. Prioritize sweeps that utilize leverage rather than brute strength, like the scissor sweep or hip bump sweep.

    Q: When should I switch from Closed Guard to another guard, like Open Guard?

    You should consider switching from Closed Guard when your opponent consistently postures up and you cannot break their base, or if they are effectively "stacking" you. If they are too heavy to control, or if you are looking for more dynamic sweep opportunities, transitioning to an Open Guard like Spider Guard or De La Riva Guard can be advantageous.

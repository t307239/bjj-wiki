Closed Guard starts from a seated or supine position with your opponent between your legs, knees bent, and ankles crossed behind their back. It aims to control your opponent's posture and create submission or sweep opportunities.

White belts often struggle by not actively controlling the opponent's hips or posture, leading to them being easily postured up or having their legs easily peeled open.

The single most critical insight is to use your legs as a vise, actively squeezing your knees together and keeping your ankles locked, creating a stable, unbreakable platform.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Initiate from a supine position, opponent on top.
    2. Bring your knees towards your chest, lifting your hips slightly off the mat.
    3. Cross your ankles behind the opponent's lower back, ensuring a secure lock.
    4. Grip the opponent's upper back or collar with both hands, palms facing you.
    5. Pull the opponent's upper body down towards your chest, breaking their posture.
    6. Squeeze your knees together, creating inward pressure on the opponent's hips.
    7. Maintain a slight upward lift of your hips to keep the opponent's weight on you.
    8. Keep your elbows tucked in to maintain control and prevent arm escapes.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect ankle crossing: If ankles are crossed too high on the back, it can place excessive hyperextension stress on the opponent's lumbar spine, potentially causing disc injury.
    • Correct: Cross ankles at the base of the opponent's spine, just above the gluteal fold.
    • Excessive knee outward pressure: Trying to 'push' the opponent away with your knees can lead to your knee joints being torqued outwards, risking ACL/MCL tears.
    • Correct: Focus on squeezing knees inward towards each other, not pushing outwards.
    • Guard opening by 'kicking' legs straight: Attempting to straighten legs to create space or escape can lead to your own hamstrings being overstretched or your hips being forced open, risking groin pulls or hip flexor tears.
    • Correct: Maintain the 'vise' grip, using hip movement and core engagement to manage space.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip and Knee Squeeze: Practice bringing knees to chest and crossing ankles behind your back, then squeezing knees together. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Solo Posture Pull and Squeeze: Combine pulling the opponent's upper back down with your hands while squeezing your knees. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Partner Drill - Static Hold: Partner lies on their back. You enter Closed Guard and hold the position, focusing on grip and squeeze. Partner offers minimal resistance. (10 rounds x 30 seconds, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner Drill - Light Posture Control: Partner tries to posture up slightly. You practice pulling them down and maintaining the squeeze. (10 rounds x 30 seconds, 50% resistance)
    5. Partner Drill - Introduction to Sweep Attempt: Partner offers light defense. You attempt a basic sweep (e.g., scissor sweep) without full commitment. (10 rounds x 45 seconds, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling - Focus on Guard Retention: Roll with a partner, focusing solely on maintaining Closed Guard and preventing them from passing. (5 minutes, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is attempting to pass your guard from a standing or kneeling position.
    • When you are on your back and have an opportunity to establish control.
    • When you have secured a dominant position on the ground and want to initiate offense.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Opponent's Posture Control: If the opponent postures up, use your hands to grab their collar or lapels and pull them down towards your chest, breaking their base.
    • Guard Pass Attempts (e.g., Knee Slice): If the opponent tries to drive a knee through your guard, use your foot on their hip to create angle and frame, while simultaneously squeezing your knees to maintain the lock.
    • Framing and Hip Escapes: If the opponent is pressuring heavily, use your hands to frame against their biceps or shoulders and bridge your hips to create space and re-establish the guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why do my wrists hurt when I try to pull my opponent down in Closed Guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs from gripping too tightly with a bent wrist or by pulling with your arms alone. Instead, use your entire body: engage your core, keep your elbows tucked, and pull your opponent's upper body towards your chest by bringing your knees closer to your body and initiating a hip bridge. This transfers the power from your arms to your entire kinetic chain.

    Q: How can I maintain Closed Guard against a much larger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, the key is to focus on *control* rather than brute strength. Prioritize breaking their posture by pulling them down and keeping your knees tightly squeezed to control their hips. Use your feet on their hips to create angles and prevent them from establishing heavy pressure. Think of your legs as a vise, not a barrier to push against.

    Q: When should I transition from Closed Guard to another position?

    You should consider transitioning if your opponent consistently defends your attacks effectively, or if they are extremely good at maintaining a tight base and preventing you from breaking their posture. If they are successfully 'stacking' you (driving your hips down), it's often a sign to look for sweeps or to transition to a more dynamic guard like Open Guard to create new angles of attack.

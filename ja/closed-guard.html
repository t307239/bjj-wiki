Closed Guard is a fundamental BJJ position where you control your opponent from your back, legs locked around their waist. It's a defensive stronghold offering control and submission opportunities.

White belts often fail by keeping their guard too loose or trying to muscle through, leading to lost control and potential injury. Focus on creating a tight, active structure, not just a static lock.

The key mechanical insight is using your hips to create frames and your legs to dynamically control distance and angles, preventing the opponent from establishing a stable base.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From your back, bring your knees towards your chest, feet flexed. Your hips should be slightly elevated off the mat, creating a "seated" posture.
    2. Place the top of one foot on the opposite knee crease, then wrap your other leg around the outside of their back.
    3. Lock your ankles together behind their back, ensuring your shins are flush against their sides. This is your "lock" or "seal".
    4. Grip their upper back or shoulders with both hands, thumbs pointing upwards, palms facing each other. Keep your elbows in.
    5. "Squeeze" your knees together and slightly lift your hips, pulling them forward and down into your "lock". This constricts their posture.
    6. Maintain pressure by actively flexing your feet and driving your heels down towards their hips. This stretches them out.
    7. If they posture up, use your grips to pull them forward, creating an angle with your hips to off-balance them and initiate a sweep or submission.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Gripping too wide: Placing hands on the opponent's upper back with elbows flared wide creates a large gap. This can lead to shoulder impingement and rotator cuff tears when the opponent drives forward.
    • Leg lock too loose: Not fully closing the "seal" of the guard, leaving a gap between your ankles. This allows the opponent to "stack" you, potentially causing lumbar spine compression injuries.
    • Hips too flat: Lying completely flat on your back with no hip elevation. This makes it easy for the opponent to "pass" your guard by controlling your legs and puts excessive strain on your lower back.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice locking and unlocking your guard, focusing on the ankle seal and hip elevation. (10 reps per side)
    2. Solo drill: Practice gripping the opponent's upper back and pulling them forward with active hip movement. (10 reps)
    3. Partner drill (0% resistance): Practice the guard lock and grip. Partner stands passively. (5 reps)
    4. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner attempts to "stomp" your feet out. Focus on maintaining the lock and hip control. (10 reps)
    5. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner attempts to "stomp" and "stack" you. Focus on hip movement and keeping the guard tight. (10 reps)
    6. Live rolling (75% resistance): Apply Closed Guard. Focus on maintaining control and attempting sweeps. (2 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is on top of you and you have the opportunity to secure your legs around their waist.
    • When you are defending a takedown and land on your back, immediately looking to establish the lock.
    • When you have just been swept and your opponent is attempting to pass your guard, use it as a defensive transition.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass Defense: If the opponent attempts to stack, actively drive your hips up and away, maintaining the ankle lock. Keep your core tight and try to create an angle to escape.
    • "Stomping" the Feet: If the opponent tries to stomp your feet out of the guard, use your grips to pull them forward and create space. Flex your feet and squeeze your knees to maintain the seal.
    • Hip Bump Escape: If the opponent is pressing down heavily, shift your hips to one side, creating a "bridge" motion. Use your grips to pull them to that side, unbalancing them and potentially creating an escape or sweep opportunity.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt so much when I try to do Closed Guard?

    Your lower back pain is likely due to lying too flat on your back with no hip elevation. This puts direct pressure on your lumbar spine. To fix this, actively lift your hips off the mat, creating a more "seated" posture, and ensure your ankles are locked securely behind your opponent's back.

    Q: How can I maintain a tight Closed Guard against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on using your legs as frames and your hips to control their weight distribution, rather than pure strength. Actively squeeze your knees together and flex your feet to create a "tight seal." Use your grips to pull them forward and off-balance, making their size less of an advantage.

    Q: When should I try to attack from Closed Guard versus just holding it?

    You should look to attack as soon as you have established a stable and tight Closed Guard. The moment your opponent becomes unstable, or you create an opening by pulling them forward or off-balance, is your cue to initiate a sweep or submission. Don't just hold; actively look for these opportunities.

Essential Sprawl Technique for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The sprawl is a defensive maneuver initiated when an opponent attempts a takedown, typically a double or single leg. It aims to prevent them from securing the legs and completing the takedown by creating a strong base and weight distribution.

White belts often fail because they react with their upper body, throwing their arms out without proper hip and leg engagement, or they panic and concede the position.

The key is to use your hips and legs to create a heavy, unmovable base, driving your weight down and back to break the opponent's connection and balance.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Initiation: As the opponent shoots for your legs, immediately widen your stance to slightly more than shoulder-width.
    2. Hip Hinge: Hinge at your hips, dropping your pelvis towards the mat, not straight down. Imagine pushing your hips back as far as possible.
    3. Leg Extension: Simultaneously extend your legs straight back, placing the balls of your feet on the mat. Your knees should be slightly bent, not locked.
    4. Weight Distribution: Drive your weight down through your hips and into the mat. Your upper body should be upright, but the primary force is downward and backward from your hips.
    5. Arm Placement: Your arms should frame the opponent's head and shoulders, pushing down to prevent them from lifting or driving through. Do not grab their head.
    6. Core Engagement: Tense your abdominal muscles to create a rigid torso, preventing your spine from rounding.
    7. Head Position: Keep your head up and look forward, maintaining awareness of the opponent's body and intentions.
    8. Maintaining Base: Ensure your base is wide enough that the opponent cannot easily circle around you or lift your hips.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Hyperextending Knees: Attempting to "kick" legs back forcefully can cause hyperextension of the knee joint, leading to ACL or MCL tears.
    • Correct: Focus on hinging at the hips and driving backward, maintaining a slight bend in the knees.
    • Rounding the Back: Arching the lower back excessively while trying to sprawl can strain lumbar muscles and discs.
    • Correct: Maintain a neutral spine and engage the core to support the torso.
    • Placing Hands on Opponent's Head: Grabbing the opponent's head and pulling down can lead to neck injuries for both parties, and allows the opponent to frame and escape.
    • Correct: Use arms to push down on the shoulders or upper back, maintaining head control with your gaze.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Hinge & Leg Extension Drill: Practice the hip hinge and leg extension motion without a partner. Focus on feeling the weight shift. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Shadow Sprawling: Stand with a partner demonstrating a shot. Practice the full sprawl motion, focusing on biomechanics. (30 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Partner Assisted Sprawl (No Resistance): Partner performs a slow, predictable shot. You sprawl and hold. Partner resets. (20 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Light Resistance Sprawl: Partner attempts a controlled shot with minimal forward pressure. Focus on maintaining your base and driving weight. (20 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Controlled Sprawl Exchange: Partner attempts a shot with moderate resistance. You sprawl, and they attempt to recover. Focus on maintaining control. (15 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    6. Sprawl in Rolling: Integrate the sprawl into live rolling scenarios when takedown attempts occur. Focus on correct technique under pressure. (5-minute rounds, 90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent initiates a leg-based takedown (e.g., double leg, single leg).
    • When an opponent attempts to drive forward to break your guard and establish a dominant top position.
    • When an opponent attempts to sweep you from a guard position by attacking your legs.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Head & Arm Control: As you sprawl, immediately use your arms to push down on the opponent's shoulders or upper back, preventing them from driving through or lifting your hips. Maintain your base.
    • Hip Pressure & Driving: Drive your hips back and down, creating a heavy base. Use your glutes and hamstrings to generate force, sinking your weight into the opponent and making it difficult for them to move.
    • Leg Pummeling (Advanced): Once a solid sprawl is established, if the opponent still has hooks, you can begin to "pummel" your legs, either bringing one leg back to a more defensive position or using your feet to push away their hips.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to sprawl?

    Lower back pain during the sprawl often stems from an excessive lumbar curve and lack of hip hinge. Instead of dropping your hips back, you might be bending too much from your waist and rounding your upper back. Focus on hinging at the hips like you're trying to sit back, keeping your spine neutral and engaging your core to support the movement.

    Q: How do I sprawl effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, the key is to use their momentum against them and focus on creating a wide, stable base. As they shoot, widen your stance even further than usual and drive your hips back with maximum force. Your goal is to make their forward momentum carry them past your hips, rather than trying to simply hold them up. Use your arms to push down on their upper back and shoulders to disrupt their balance.

    Q: Should I always sprawl by kicking my legs back as far as possible?

    Kicking your legs back without a proper hip hinge can lead to knee hyperextension and a loss of base. The sprawl is primarily a hip and core-driven movement. Think of it as sitting your hips back and down powerfully, with your legs extending to create a wide, stable platform. Your knees should remain slightly bent, not locked out, to absorb impact and maintain mobility.

The sprawl is your primary defense against takedowns, initiated when an opponent attempts to grab your legs. It prevents them from securing a dominant position by creating a wide, heavy base.

White belts often fail because they treat it as a static block rather than a dynamic movement, leading to poor weight distribution and vulnerability.

The core mechanical principle is extending your hips back and down while simultaneously spreading your legs wide, creating an unassailable platform.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Initiation: As the opponent's hands reach for your legs (e.g., collar-and-elbow grip on your arms), immediately initiate the sprawl.
    2. Hip Extension: Hinge forcefully at your hips, pushing your pelvis backward and downwards.
    3. Leg Spread: Simultaneously, explosively spread your legs wide, aiming for a stance wider than your shoulders.
    4. Foot Placement: Land on the balls of your feet, with your toes pointed slightly outwards for stability.
    5. Weight Distribution: Drive your weight directly down through your hips and torso onto the opponent's upper back or shoulders.
    6. Arm Extension: Extend your arms downwards, framing against the opponent's shoulders or head to prevent them from driving forward.
    7. Core Engagement: Maintain a tight core to transfer power and prevent your hips from collapsing.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Hyperextension: Attempting to block a takedown by rigidly extending your knees backward can lead to hyperextension, damaging the ACL and PCL ligaments. Always maintain a slight bend in the knees.
    • Ankle Sprain: Landing with feet too close together or with ankles rolled inward during a wide leg spread can cause ankle inversion or eversion sprains. Ensure a wide, stable base with toes slightly out.
    • Lower Back Strain: Trying to sprawl by simply bending at the waist without hip extension can put excessive shear force on the lumbar spine, leading to muscle strains. Focus on hinging at the hips.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Hinge & Leg Spread: Practice the hip hinge and explosive leg spread motion without resistance. 10 reps.
    2. Shadow Sprawl: Mimic the sprawl motion against an imaginary opponent. Focus on hip extension and leg width. 10 reps.
    3. Wall Sprawl: Stand facing a wall, practice sprawling against it, using the wall for balance. 10 reps.
    4. Partner Drill (0% Resistance): Partner lies on their back, you practice sprawling over them without them resisting. Focus on hip control. 5 reps.
    5. Partner Drill (25% Resistance): Partner gently pushes your legs as you sprawl. Focus on maintaining base and hip pressure. 5 reps.
    6. Partner Drill (50% Resistance): Partner attempts a very slow, controlled double-leg takedown. You sprawl to defend. 5 reps.
    7. Live Rolling (75% Resistance): Spar with a partner, focusing on using the sprawl against their takedown attempts. 2-minute rounds.
    8. Live Rolling (90-100% Resistance): Full sparring, integrating the sprawl into your defensive strategy. 3-minute rounds.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent attempts a double-leg takedown.
    • When an opponent attempts a single-leg takedown.
    • When an opponent tries to shoot for your legs from a distance.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Pressure & Weight Transfer: After sprawling, immediately drive your hips down and forward, putting your weight on the opponent's upper back. This makes it difficult for them to drive or continue the takedown.
    • Framing with Arms: Extend your arms downwards, placing your forearms or hands on the opponent's shoulders or upper back. This creates space and prevents them from closing the distance or driving their head into your chest.
    • Leg Control & Sweeping: Once a stable sprawl is established, you can use your legs to control the opponent's hips or legs, potentially initiating a sweep or transitioning to a better defensive position like half-guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why do my knees and lower back hurt after I try to sprawl in BJJ?

    This often happens from improper hip hinging. Instead of bending at your waist, focus on pushing your hips *back* and *down* forcefully. Ensure your knees stay slightly bent, not locked out, to absorb the impact and protect your joints. Imagine your pelvis is a hinge.

    Q: How can I effectively sprawl against a much bigger BJJ opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, your sprawl must be explosive and precise. Focus on spreading your legs wider than your shoulders and driving your hips down with maximum force. Use your arms to frame their shoulders, creating separation and preventing them from using their weight effectively against you. Think 'wide base, heavy hips'.

    Q: When is the right time to sprawl in BJJ, and what if I'm too slow?

    The sprawl is a reaction to a leg attack attempt. You need to anticipate and move as soon as you see the opponent's hips lower and their hands reach for your legs. If you're too slow, focus on drills that improve hip explosiveness and reaction time. Practice the hip hinge and leg spread motion repeatedly.

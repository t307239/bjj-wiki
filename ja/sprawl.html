The sprawl is your primary defense against takedowns, initiated when an opponent attempts to grab your legs. It stops their forward momentum and establishes a dominant control position.

White belts often fail due to a lack of kinetic chain integration, treating it as a simple leg movement rather than a full-body expulsion.

The key is to drive your hips back and down, simultaneously extending your legs and creating a wide, stable base to absorb and redirect their weight.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Initiation: Opponent initiates a double-leg-takedown or single-leg-takedown by reaching for your legs.
    2. Hip Hinge: Immediately hinge at your hips, keeping your back straight, as if sitting back into a chair.
    3. Leg Extension: Simultaneously extend your legs backward and slightly outward, landing on the balls of your feet.
    4. Hip Drive: Drive your hips down towards the mat, creating a heavy pressure onto the opponent's upper back.
    5. Weight Distribution: Your weight should be distributed over a wide base, with your knees bent and feet flat or on the balls of your feet, preventing them from lifting your hips.
    6. Arm Placement: Extend your arms downwards, framing against the opponent's upper back or shoulders to prevent them from driving forward.
    7. Core Engagement: Actively engage your core muscles to maintain a rigid torso and transfer power from your hips through your entire body.
    8. Head Position: Keep your head up and look forward, not down at your feet, to maintain spatial awareness and balance.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Hyperextended Knees: Attempting to 'kick' legs back without a proper hip hinge can lead to hyperextension of the knee joint, causing ACL or meniscus tears.
    • Lower Back Strain: Arching your back excessively while trying to push hips down strains the lumbar spine, risking disc compression or muscle pulls.
    • Ankle/Foot Injury: Landing flat-footed or with heels down can cause ankle rolls or fractures if the opponent shifts their weight unexpectedly.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Hinge & Leg Extension: Practice the hip hinge and leg extension motion repeatedly without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Solo Sprawl with Bodyweight: Perform the full sprawl motion, driving hips down, with only your own bodyweight. Focus on stability. (50 reps)
    3. Partner Assisted Sprawl (0% Resistance): Partner stands and simulates a takedown attempt (no grip). You sprawl. Focus on mechanics. (10 reps per side)
    4. Partner Assisted Sprawl (25% Resistance): Partner attempts a light, slow takedown. You sprawl and hold. Focus on weight distribution. (10 reps per side)
    5. Partner Assisted Sprawl (50% Resistance): Partner attempts a more committed takedown. You sprawl and aim to maintain top position. (10 reps per side)
    6. Live Rolling (Takedown Defense Focus): Focus on defending takedowns during rolling, applying the sprawl with intent. (3 x 3-minute rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When an opponent initiates a double-leg-takedown or single-leg-takedown.
    • When an opponent attempts to change levels and drive forward into your legs.
    • As a reactive defense against any forward-moving, leg-seeking attack.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Heavy Hip Drive: Upon successful sprawl, drive your hips down with maximum force, pinning the opponent's upper body and preventing them from recovering.
    • Weight Shift to Side Control: After the sprawl, shift your weight to one side, establishing side-control by trapping their arm and hip.
    • Head and Arm Control: If the opponent attempts to 'sit out' or recover guard, immediately secure a head and arm control to prevent their movement and set up control.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt after doing sprawls?

    Lower back pain often stems from an improper hip hinge. Instead of sitting your hips back, you might be arching your lower back. Focus on initiating the movement by pushing your hips backward as if you're trying to touch a wall behind you with your glutes, keeping your spine neutral.

    Q: How do I sprawl effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are crucial. Instead of just pushing back, use their forward momentum against them. As they commit to the takedown, explosively drive your hips down and back, aiming to land your weight on their upper back and shoulders. Create a wide base to prevent them from easily driving through you.

    Q: Should I try to kick my legs out when doing a sprawl?

    No, 'kicking' your legs out is biomechanically inefficient and risky. The sprawl is a hip-driven movement. Focus on hinging at the hips and extending your legs backward while simultaneously driving your hips down. This creates a powerful, stable base and protects your knees from hyperextension.

BJJスプロールをマスターする: 白帯の必須ディフェンス

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The sprawl is a defensive maneuver used when an opponent attempts a takedown or to pass your guard. It involves extending your legs backward to create distance and prevent them from securing a dominant position.

White belts often fail by simply falling backward or not committing their weight effectively, leaving them vulnerable to follow-up attacks.

The key mechanical insight is to drive your hips down and back, creating a strong base with your legs spread wide, like a starfish, to smother their attack.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. When an opponent shoots for a double-leg takedown, immediately widen your base and extend your feet backward.
    2. Simultaneously, drop your hips towards the mat, aiming to place your pelvis lower than their shoulders.
    3. Drive your chest down onto their upper back, creating upward pressure on their head and shoulders.
    4. Spread your legs laterally as wide as possible, with your feet flexed and heels driving into the mat.
    5. Maintain a strong spinal extension, keeping your back straight and head up.
    6. Distribute your weight evenly across your feet and shins, creating a heavy, immovable base.
    7. If they are still driving forward, use your arms to frame against their head or shoulders, pushing them away while maintaining hip pressure.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly falling backward: This can cause hyperextension of the lumbar spine, leading to lower back pain and potential disc injury. The correct movement is to drive hips DOWN and BACK, not just fall.
    • Narrow leg stance: A narrow sprawl with feet close together offers no base and can lead to knee valgus collapse, damaging the ACL and MCL. Spread legs WIDE like a starfish.
    • Letting opponent grip legs: If the opponent secures grips on your ankles or shins, and you don't have a strong base, they can use that leverage to sweep or complete the takedown, potentially causing ankle sprains or knee ligament damage. Maintain active foot pressure and hip drive.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Sprawl Practice (0%): Stand with feet shoulder-width apart. Practice the hip drop and leg extension motion 20 times.
    2. Sprawl Against Air (0%): Imagine an opponent shooting. Practice executing the sprawl with full hip drive and leg extension, holding for 5 seconds, 15 reps.
    3. Coach-Assisted Sprawl (25%): Have a coach lightly push your shoulders forward and practice your sprawl, focusing on hip drive and leg spread. 10 reps per side.
    4. Partner Sprawl (50%): With a partner standing, have them lightly shoot a takedown (no real force). Execute your sprawl, focusing on maintaining base and hip pressure. 10 reps per side.
    5. Sprawl Against Light Shot (75%): Partner shoots with moderate force. Focus on reacting quickly, driving hips down, and maintaining a wide base. 10 reps per side.
    6. Sprawl Against Full Shot (90%): Partner attempts a committed takedown. Execute the sprawl with full commitment, focusing on control and preventing penetration. 5 reps per side.
    7. Live Sparring (100%): Incorporate sprawl defense during rolling, focusing on reacting to takedown attempts and guard pass defenses. Focus on accuracy and retention, not submission. 5 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent attempts a double-leg or single-leg takedown.
    • When an opponent is attempting to pass your guard from a standing or kneeling position.
    • As a reaction to prevent an opponent from establishing deep control on your legs.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Drop and Leg Extension: The core sprawl. Drive hips down and back, extending legs wide to create a heavy barrier.
    • Overhook and Push: If the opponent penetrates your legs, secure an overhook on their arm and push their head down and away while maintaining hip pressure.
    • Stance and Base Adjustment: If the initial sprawl doesn't fully stop the penetration, immediately adjust your base and foot placement to maintain balance and prevent them from elevating your hips.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt after sprawling?

    Lower back pain after sprawling often stems from extending your spine too much or not driving your hips down and back effectively. Instead of just falling backward, focus on actively pushing your hips towards the mat and slightly forward to meet your opponent's head. This creates a stable base and protects your lumbar spine.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise mechanics are key. Focus on a wider and more explosive leg spread, driving your hips lower and further back to create a substantial barrier. Use your chest to push down on their upper back and head, making it difficult for them to drive through your base. Don't try to out-muscle them; focus on creating an unmovable platform.

    Q: You should anticipate the shot and begin to sprawl as soon as you recognize the opponent's intent to shoot for a takedown or penetrate your guard. Early recognition allows for a more effective sprawl before they establish deep penetration. Look for their level change and forward momentum, which are precursors to a shot.

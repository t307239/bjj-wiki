Sprawl: The Essential BJJ Defense for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The sprawl is a defensive maneuver initiated when an opponent attempts a takedown, typically a double-leg or single-leg. Its goal is to prevent them from securing a grip and taking you down, establishing a dominant position for yourself.

Many white belts fail by simply falling backward or flailing their legs, creating openings. They lack the coordinated hip and leg drive necessary for effective resistance against a determined attacker.

The key insight is to create a wide, stable base by extending your legs and lowering your hips, effectively 'sprawling' your body weight over the opponent's hips and shoulders.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Opponent initiates a penetration step for a takedown (e.g., reaching for your legs).
    2. Immediately, extend your hips backward forcefully, driving your pelvis towards the mat.
    3. Simultaneously, spread your legs wide apart, aiming for a base wider than shoulder-width.
    4. Drive your knees outward and slightly forward, creating a strong wedge with your shins and thighs against the opponent's upper thighs/hips.
    5. Lower your chest towards the mat, aiming to place your torso over the opponent's upper back or shoulders.
    6. Maintain contact with your feet flat on the mat, toes pointed slightly inward for grip on the mat.
    7. Distribute your weight evenly over your hips and the opponent's body, creating pressure and preventing them from lifting or driving forward.
    8. If the opponent has collar or sleeve grips, immediately break them with your hands as you sprawl.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Falling backward without hip extension: This often leads to the opponent completing the takedown and can cause hyperextension or sprains in the lower back or knee if the legs are not properly positioned.
    • Kicking legs out wildly: This destabilizes your base, making you easy to sweep or trip, and can lead to ankle or knee ligament tears from awkward twisting forces.
    • Stiff-legged sprawl without hip drive: This puts immense pressure on the knees and can cause patellar tendonitis or meniscus tears, as the force is not absorbed by the hips.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo sprawl against air: Practice the hip extension and leg spread motion without resistance. 50 reps.
    2. Sprawl against a stationary partner: Partner stands with hands at your hips. You sprawl as they lightly push forward. 50 reps.
    3. Sprawl against slow penetration step: Partner performs a slow double-leg penetration step. You sprawl and hold. 25 reps per side.
    4. Sprawl against controlled penetration step: Partner performs a controlled penetration step. You sprawl and aim to maintain position. 50 reps.
    5. Sprawl against light resistance penetration step: Partner attempts a controlled takedown. You sprawl and focus on control. 75% resistance, 25 reps.
    6. Live rolling: Focus on sprawling during takedown attempts. 100% resistance, 5 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent shoots for a double-leg takedown.
    • When an opponent attempts a single-leg takedown.
    • When an opponent tries to duck under your arm for a takedown from the clinch.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Drop: As you sprawl, drop your hips sharply to the side of the opponent's head, making it difficult for them to drive forward or secure a grip.
    • Leg Overhook: As you sprawl, use one leg to hook over the opponent's hip or thigh, creating a strong barrier and preventing them from hip escaping.
    • Head Pressure: As you sprawl, drive your forehead or the side of your head into the opponent's shoulder or head, forcing them to break their posture and making it harder to drive.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why do my knees hurt when I try to sprawl?

    Knee pain during a sprawl often stems from not driving your hips back and down effectively. Instead of a controlled descent, white belts tend to land with straight legs, forcing the knee joint to absorb the impact. Ensure your hips are the primary driver, extending backward and lowering your body, allowing your knees to bend naturally under control.

    Q: How do I sprawl effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, your sprawl needs to be explosively wide and low. Focus on driving your hips back with maximum force, creating a wide base that spans their hips. Simultaneously, drive your knees outward and slightly forward to create a strong wedge, preventing them from driving through your legs.

    Q: Should I sprawl by kicking my legs back or dropping my hips?

    You should prioritize dropping your hips. Kicking legs back often leads to a straight-leg landing, which is biomechanically inefficient and can injure your knees. The correct sprawl involves driving your pelvis backward and downward, then spreading your legs wide and bending your knees to create a stable, low base that covers the opponent.

Knee Slice Pass: A White Belt's Biomechanically Accurate Guide

The Knee Slice Pass is a fundamental guard pass initiated from a standing or kneeling position against an opponent's closed guard, aiming to break their posture and establish side control.

Many white belts struggle because they rely on brute force rather than precise body mechanics, leading to ineffective passes and potential injury.

The key to a successful Knee Slice Pass is generating forward pressure with your hips and driving your knee across the opponent's body to create a wedge, forcing them to open their guard.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Secure a collar grip with your right hand, pulling the opponent's lapel towards your sternum. Your left hand establishes a sleeve grip on their left arm, keeping it extended.
    2. Position your hips forward, slightly to your right, creating a 45-degree angle relative to the opponent's hips. Your base should be shoulder-width apart for stability.
    3. Drive your right knee forward, aiming to place it just outside and slightly in front of the opponent's left hip. Your left leg remains planted, foot flat on the mat.
    4. With your right knee on the mat, begin to drive your hips forward and down, perpendicular to the opponent's body. Maintain a strong chest-to-chest connection.
    5. Simultaneously, pull the opponent's left arm across their body with your sleeve grip, creating a slight rotation. This disrupts their base and makes it harder to recover guard.
    6. Continue to drive your hips forward, using your knee as a pivot point. Your right shin should be perpendicular to the mat, and your left leg acts as a stable base.
    7. As your right knee slices across, your goal is to bring your left leg to a standing or kneeling position on the other side of their body, establishing side control. Your hips should now be directly over their hip.
    8. Apply downward pressure with your chest and hips, ensuring your weight is distributed over their sternum. Maintain the collar grip to control their head and posture.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Hyperextension: Attempting to drive your knee forward too aggressively without proper hip alignment can cause your knee to buckle outwards, leading to collateral ligament (MCL/LCL) tears.
    • Ankle Sprain: Planting your left foot too far back or turning it inwards while driving forward can result in an ankle inversion sprain when your weight shifts unevenly.
    • Lower Back Strain: Rounding your back and attempting to lift the opponent with your arms instead of driving with your hips can strain the lumbar spine, especially if the opponent is much larger.
    • Wrist Injury: Gripping too tightly with the sleeve and collar without proper forearm engagement can lead to wrist hyperextension or tendonitis.
    • Hip Flexor Strain: Overextending your hips in an attempt to force the pass can strain the hip flexor muscles.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Drive Drill: From kneeling, practice driving your hips forward and generating pressure with your knee in the air, focusing on hip articulation. 10 reps per side.
    2. Solo Knee Slice Motion: From kneeling, practice the full Knee Slice movement without a partner, focusing on hip angle and knee placement. 10 reps per side.
    3. Passive Partner Knee Slice: With a partner lying on their back, practice the Knee Slice Pass, focusing on grip placement and hip drive. Partner offers minimal resistance. 5 reps per side, 50% resistance.
    4. Controlled Resistance Drill: Progress to a partner who actively tries to recover guard but doesn't resist the pass aggressively. Focus on maintaining pressure and hip control. 5 reps per side, 75% resistance.
    5. Light Sparring (Guard Passing Focus): Engage in light sparring with a focus on executing the Knee Slice Pass. Your partner can attempt to defend but not actively attack. 3 rounds, 90% resistance.
    6. Live Rolling: Apply the Knee Slice Pass in live rolling situations, aiming for successful passes and transitions to side control. 5 minutes per round, 100% resistance.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is in closed guard and has their knees tightly locked, preventing other passes.
    • When the opponent's hips are slightly elevated, creating an opening for your knee to slice through.
    • When the opponent attempts to shrimp away, creating space for you to drive your knee across their body.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Recovery (Shrimping): If the opponent drives their knee across, the defender can shrimp their hips away to re-establish guard, requiring the passer to maintain tight hip connection.
    • Leg Entanglement (e.g., Butterfly Guard): The defender can use their free leg to hook under the passer's thigh, creating a butterfly guard position to sweep or regain guard.
    • Hip Escape and Frame: The defender can attempt to create space by pushing on the passer's hips with their hands and escaping their hips to the side, forcing the passer to readjust their base.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I do the Knee Slice Pass?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are driving your knee directly into the mat or the opponent's hip without proper biomechanical alignment. Ensure your hips are angled at 45 degrees and you are driving *across* their body, using your shin as a pivot. Your knee should land slightly beside their hip, not directly on it, and you should maintain a strong base with your planted leg to absorb pressure.

    Q: How can I Knee Slice Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus more on hip pressure and leverage rather than brute strength. Secure a strong collar grip to control their posture and prevent them from posturing up. Drive your hips forward with precision, using your knee as a wedge to off-balance them. Aim to get your chest tight to their body to minimize their ability to use their weight against you.

    Q: When is the best time to switch my Knee Slice Pass leg?

    You should only switch your Knee Slice Pass leg if the initial attempt is completely stalled and the opponent has effectively shut down that side. A premature switch can expose your back or lead to a loss of position. Prioritize completing the pass on one side by driving your hips and maintaining pressure; only consider switching if the opponent's defense on that side is exceptionally strong and you are in a stable position.

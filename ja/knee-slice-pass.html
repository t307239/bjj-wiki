Knee Slice Pass: A Biomechanically Precise Guide for BJJ White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

The Knee Slice Pass is a fundamental guard pass initiated from standing or top half-guard, aiming to break your opponent's leg control and achieve side control.

White belts often struggle by trying to force the pass with raw strength instead of controlled pressure and leverage.

The core principle is using your knee as a wedge to displace their hips, allowing your body to slice through their guard.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Secure a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (same-side as your cross-collar grip) with your left hand.
    2. Position your left knee directly in front of your opponent's hip joint, creating a perpendicular angle between your thigh and their torso.
    3. Drive your weight forward, pressing your left hip down and slightly forward, while maintaining a wide base with your right foot planted firmly.
    4. Slice your left knee across their body, aiming to place it on the mat or slightly past their centerline, while simultaneously pulling their collar towards your chest.
    5. As your knee passes, pivot your right foot to face your opponent's hips, creating a 45-degree angle between your bodies.
    6. Extend your right leg back, creating a strong base and preventing them from reguarding.
    7. Drive your chest into their hip, maintaining downward pressure, and secure your side control by controlling their far arm and hip.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Rushing the pass by lifting your hips too high: This can lead to hyperextension of your knee or ankle ligaments as you fall with uncontrolled force.
    • Stiff-legging your driving leg: Applying pressure with a straight leg can injure your knee joint (ACL/MCL) by forcing it into a valgus position.
    • Allowing opponent to hook your leg: If your driving leg is not actively slicing and your hips aren't low, they can easily sweep or take your back, risking knee and hip injuries from awkward falls.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip angle, knee placement, and weight transfer without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Focus on the exact grip and body positioning, with your partner passively accepting the pass. (20 reps per side)
    3. Light resistance drill: Your partner offers minimal resistance, focusing on maintaining their guard. (30 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    4. Controlled resistance drill: Your partner actively tries to maintain guard but doesn't actively counter. (20 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    5. Flow rolling: Apply the Knee Slice Pass in a controlled rolling scenario, aiming for successful passes. (5 minutes, 90% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Execute the Knee Slice Pass during full sparring. (Focus on 3 attempts per round, 100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is framing with their hands on your hips in closed guard.
    • When they attempt to open their guard and create space for a sweep.
    • When you have established top half-guard and are looking to advance.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Re-guarding: As the knee slices, the opponent can use their free leg to re-establish guard by placing their foot on your hip.
    • Hip Escape and Frame: The opponent can shrimp their hips away and create a frame with their hands on your shoulder to prevent your chest from driving in.
    • Leg Hook and Sweep: If the passer's hips are too high, the opponent can hook the passing leg and use their hips to sweep them.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Knee Slice Pass?

    Your knee likely hurts due to improper knee positioning and excessive force. Ensure your driving knee is placed *in front* of your opponent's hip, not directly on it, and that your hip angle is perpendicular, not parallel, to their body. Avoid locking your knee straight; maintain a slight bend and drive forward with your hip and core.

    Q: How can I Knee Slice Pass a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise weight distribution are paramount. Focus on breaking their posture by pulling on the collar and keeping their upper body close. Your goal is to cut your slicing knee *under* their hip bone, displacing their base. Drive your own hips down and forward, using your body weight to create the pressure, rather than trying to muscle their legs apart.

    Q: When is the best time to use the Knee Slice Pass instead of other passes?

    The Knee Slice Pass is most effective when your opponent's legs are relatively extended or when they are attempting to create space to open their guard. It excels against opponents who rely on a tight closed guard or struggle to maintain strong frames. If they have a very flexible or strong open guard, other passes like the Torreando might be more suitable initially.

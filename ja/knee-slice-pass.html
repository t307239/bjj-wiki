Knee Slice Pass: A White Belt's Biomechanically Accurate Guide

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

The Knee Slice Pass is a fundamental guard passing technique that begins when your opponent is in your guard, typically closed or half guard. Its goal is to break their base and establish side control.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute force or incorrect body positioning, leading to stalled passes or injury. They fail to understand the importance of hip pressure and leverage.

The single most critical mechanical insight is using your hip to drive your knee across their body, creating a powerful wedge that forces them to adjust or concede the pass.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Secure a collar grip with your right hand, pulling their head down and slightly to your left. Simultaneously, secure a far-side hip grip with your left hand, placing your palm on their hip bone.
    2. Base: Establish a wide, stable base with your feet shoulder-width apart, slightly behind your hips.
    3. Hip Angle: Lower your hips to the mat, creating a 45-degree angle between your torso and your opponent's legs.
    4. Knee Drive: Drive your right knee forward and across your opponent's body, aiming to place it on the mat beside their hip.
    5. Weight Transfer: As your knee drives across, transfer your weight forward onto your chest and shoulders, pressing down on their hips.
    6. Leg Action: Your left leg should remain straight and anchored, preventing them from re-guarding or shrimping away.
    7. Head Position: Keep your head up and your chest pressed into their hip, maintaining forward pressure.
    8. Completion: Once your knee is across and your chest is on their hip, you can begin to free your right leg and establish side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Knee Placement: Driving your knee directly into your opponent's shin or knee joint can cause hyperextension or ligament tears (ACL, MCL). The correct movement is to slice your knee across their hip bone, not into their leg structure.
    • Premature Leg Lift: Lifting your right leg too early before establishing hip pressure allows your opponent to trap your leg and potentially sweep or attack. This can lead to ankle or knee sprains from awkward positioning.
    • Lack of Hip Pressure: Simply walking your leg across without driving your hips forward creates a gap, allowing your opponent to re-guard or create space. This ineffective pressure can strain your own lower back as you overextend.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Mobility Drill: Practice driving your hips forward and rotating them while on your hands and knees. 20 repetitions per side.
    2. Knee Slice Motion (No Partner): From a kneeling position, practice the knee slice motion, focusing on hip rotation and driving your knee across an imaginary line. 15 repetitions per side.
    3. Knee Slice with Light Resistance (0%): Start on your knees. Your partner lies on their back. Practice the knee slice motion, focusing on the mechanics without any actual resistance. 10 repetitions per side.
    4. Knee Slice with Grip Focus (25%): Your partner offers minimal resistance. Focus on securing the collar and hip grips and executing the knee slice, allowing them to follow your movement. 15 repetitions per side.
    5. Knee Slice with Controlled Resistance (50%): Your partner actively tries to prevent the pass but doesn't use powerful frames. Focus on maintaining hip pressure and driving your knee through. 20 repetitions per side.
    6. Knee Slice with Realistic Resistance (75%): Your partner actively defends with frames and attempts to create space. Focus on adapting your pressure and transitions. 30 repetitions per side.
    7. Live Rolling (90-100%): Attempt the Knee Slice Pass during live rolling, focusing on applying the learned mechanics against a fully resisting opponent.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is attempting to close their guard on you.
    • When your opponent has established a loose half guard, leaving their hips accessible.
    • When your opponent attempts to shrimp away, creating a slight gap at their hip.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Shrimping Away: If the passer's knee is not yet fully across and their hip pressure is weak, shrimp your hips away to create distance and re-establish guard. The key is to move your hips perpendicular to their forward drive.
    • Re-guarding: If the passer's knee is across but they are not maintaining sufficient chest pressure, use your free leg to hook their hip and swing it back to bring your knees between you, re-establishing guard.
    • Framing and Creating Space: The defender can use their forearms to frame against the passer's shoulder or chest, creating space to either shrimp or re-guard. This requires active pushing and maintaining a strong posture.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Knee Slice Pass?

    Knee pain often occurs when you drive your knee directly into your opponent's tibia or patella, or if you land awkwardly. Ensure your knee slices across their hip bone, not their leg. Maintain a controlled descent and use your hips to absorb impact, not your kneecap.

    Q: How do I Knee Slice Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on leverage and precision over brute force. Secure strong collar and hip grips to control their posture. Drive your hips forward with maximum intention and use your body weight to create a wedge; don't just walk your leg. Prioritize breaking their base before attempting to drive through.

    Q: When is the best moment to initiate the Knee Slice Pass, and what if they react quickly?

    The ideal moment is when you have a stable base and have secured strong grips, just as they begin to settle their hips or attempt to adjust their guard. If they react quickly by bridging or shrimping, maintain your hip pressure and be ready to adjust; don't commit fully if space disappears, instead, reset your grips and base.

Knee Slice Pass: The Essential BJJ Technique for White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

The Knee Slice Pass is initiated from your opponent's guard, aiming to clear their legs and establish side control.

Many white belts struggle by being too upright or using their arms excessively, leading to ineffective attempts.

The core principle is using your knee as a fulcrum to displace their hips and sever the connection.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting from your opponent's guard, establish a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand, thumb inside, and a deep sleeve grip on their left arm with your left hand.
    2. Place your left knee on the mat approximately one foot away from your opponent's hip, angled slightly inward towards their body.
    3. Drive your hips forward and down, creating a tight base with your feet hip-width apart, ensuring your weight is distributed over your hips and not your knees.
    4. Begin to slide your right knee forward, aiming to place it along the outside of their left thigh, perpendicular to their body.
    5. Simultaneously, pull their left arm across their body with your left hand, creating a frame and preventing them from re-guarding.
    6. Rotate your torso, driving your right hip forward and down, using your right knee to apply pressure to their thigh and hip.
    7. As you continue to drive forward, your right knee will slice across their body, forcing their hips to turn away from you.
    8. Once their hips are turned, secure side control by placing your right shin across their torso and your left knee on the mat, creating a strong base.
    9. Maintain pressure by keeping your chest close to their body and your hips low, preventing them from creating space.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect knee placement: Placing your knee directly on your opponent's shin bone can cause hyperextension of your own knee or fracture their tibia. Always aim for the fleshy part of the thigh/hip.
    • Excessive forward leaning without hip drive: This leads to your weight being on your hands and knees, leaving your base unstable and vulnerable to sweeps. Instead, drive your hips forward and down, keeping your chest close.
    • Allowing opponent to hip escape: If you don't maintain constant pressure and control of their hips, they will easily re-establish guard. Your hips must remain heavy and connected to their hips.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip and knee movement without a partner, focusing on the precise path of the knee and hip drive. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Your partner lies on their back, and you perform the Knee Slice Pass, focusing on grip and weight transfer. (25 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the pressure and movement. (20 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner drill (moderate resistance): Your partner actively tries to prevent the pass, but allows you to work through the mechanics. (15 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Partner drill (heavy resistance): Your partner defends actively, simulating a live roll scenario. (10 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Implement the Knee Slice Pass during sparring, focusing on timing and pressure. (5-minute rounds, 90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent attempts to close the guard after you've established a dominant top position.
    • When your opponent is in half-guard and you have secured a strong cross-face and underhook.
    • When your opponent attempts to shrimp away from your pressure, creating an opening for your knee to slice.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape and Re-guard: As the knee slices, the defender hips away and brings their guard back into play. The passer must anticipate this by keeping their hips heavy and continuing to drive forward.
    • Technical Stand-up: If the passer is too slow or loses connection, the defender can technical stand-up, creating an opportunity to escape. The passer must maintain chest-to-chest contact.
    • Knee Shield/Push: The defender uses their free leg to create a barrier and push the passer's knee away. The passer must use their grip and body weight to overcome this pressure.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt after attempting the Knee Slice Pass?

    Knee pain often stems from placing your knee directly on your opponent's tibia or fibula, or from leaning too far forward without proper hip drive. Ensure your knee lands on the fleshy part of their thigh and always drive your hips forward to maintain a stable base, protecting your knee joint.

    Q: How can I effectively Knee Slice Pass against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise weight transfer are crucial. Focus on establishing a strong cross-collar grip to control their head and prevent them from posturing up. Use your hips to drive *under* their center of gravity, rather than trying to push their weight directly. Break down their posture before initiating the pass.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from a failed Knee Slice Pass attempt?

    If your Knee Slice Pass is being successfully defended by a strong knee shield or hip escape, don't force it. Instead, use the momentum to transition to a leg drag by securing their ankle and pulling it across their body, or transition to a stack pass by driving your weight over their hips and shoulders.

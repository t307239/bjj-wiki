Knee Slice Pass: The Essential BJJ Technique for White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

This pass initiates from a standing or kneeling position against an opponent's guard, aiming to break their leg control and establish side control.

White belts often struggle due to insufficient hip pressure and incorrect weight placement, allowing the opponent to re-guard.

The key insight is to use your knee as a lever to slice across their hips, creating a powerful pressure that forces them to adjust.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand (if passing on your right) and a sleeve grip on their left arm with your left hand.

    Hip Angle: Immediately angle your hips 45 degrees away from their guard.

    Weight Transfer: Begin transferring weight onto your left leg, pushing your hips forward.

    1. Knee Placement: Drive your right knee towards their hip crease, aiming to place it slightly below their hip bone.

    Base Width: Maintain a base width slightly wider than your shoulders for stability.

    1. Weight Distribution: Shift your weight onto your right knee and shin, driving it into their hip.

    Torso Angle: Keep your torso angled forward, chest close to their body.

    1. Leg Drive: Extend your left leg backward, creating a stable base and driving your hips forward.

    Pelvis Angle: Your pelvis should be directly over your left heel.

    1. Slicing Motion: Slice your right knee across their hips, moving it towards the opposite side of their body.

    Pressure Point: Apply pressure through your right knee and shin into their hip socket.

    1. Clearing the Leg: As your knee slices through, use your left hand to peel their left leg off your hip.

    Maintain Posture: Keep your head up and back straight throughout the movement.

    1. Establishing Side Control: Once their legs are cleared, bring your left knee to the mat beside their head.

    Weight Distribution: Drive your hips down onto their torso, pinning them with your weight.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect knee-to-hip alignment: Driving your knee directly into the opponent's kneecap or shin can hyperextend their knee joint, leading to ligament tears (ACL/MCL).

    Correct: Aim your knee at their hip crease, applying pressure to their pelvis.

    • Leaning back instead of driving forward: If you lean back, you lose forward pressure, and your opponent can easily shrimp or re-guard, potentially trapping your leg and causing a knee-bar attempt on your trapped leg.

    Correct: Maintain forward pressure with your chest and hips towards their body.

    • Allowing opponent's leg to stay on your hip: If you don't actively clear their leg, they can use it to bridge or sweep, and you risk twisting your ankle or knee if your foot gets caught.

    Correct: Actively peel their leg off your hip with your free hand as you slice.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling of the hip angling and knee slicing motion without a partner (20 reps per side).
    2. Practicing the grips and hip angle on a stationary partner who offers no resistance (0% resistance, 10 reps per side).
    3. Drilling the full knee slice pass against a passive partner who allows the pass (25% resistance, 5 reps per side).
    4. Adding light resistance from the partner: They may attempt to keep their legs closed or slightly resist the slice (50% resistance, 5 reps per side).
    5. Increasing partner's resistance: They actively try to maintain guard and counter the pass (75% resistance, 3 reps per side).
    6. Live rolling scenario: Attempting the knee slice pass during sparring, focusing on correct mechanics and pressure (90-100% resistance, focus on execution).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent's legs are extended or they have a loose closed guard.
    • When they attempt to create space by pushing on your hips.
    • When you have established a strong cross-collar and sleeve grip.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Backstep Counter: If the opponent tries to hip escape or shrimp away, backstep your leg to the outside and drive your hips down for side control.
    • Re-guarding with the other leg: If they manage to bring their other leg over your hips, transition to a leg drag pass or attempt a stand-up pass.
    • Framing and Re-guarding: The opponent can frame with their hands on your hips and knees to create space and bring their legs back into guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Knee Slice Pass?

    Your knee likely hurts because you're driving it directly into the opponent's tibia or fibula, or you're angling your knee incorrectly. Ensure your knee aims for their hip crease, and your shin is perpendicular to the mat, creating a stable base. Avoid letting your knee buckle inwards; maintain a strong, straight line from your hip to your foot.

    Q: How do I Knee Slice Pass against a bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on generating more forward pressure and using your hips as a wedge. Instead of just slicing your knee, actively drive your chest into their sternum as you pass. Use your grips to control their posture and prevent them from creating frames, ensuring your hip angle is sharp to off-balance them.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Knee Slice Pass from half guard?

    The Knee Slice Pass is less effective directly from half guard as their legs are already controlling one of yours. It's more of a transition *out* of half guard. If you've secured an underhook and are driving your knee across their body to flatten them out, that motion can evolve into a knee slice if you manage to clear their remaining leg and establish side control.

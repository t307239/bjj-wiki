BJJ白帯向けアームトライアングルチョーク

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック

This choke is primarily initiated from side-control or mount, aiming to compress the carotid artery against the opponent's shoulder. It's a powerful submission that can end a match quickly.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of precise body alignment and pressure application. They might focus on squeezing with their arms, neglecting the crucial role of hip and torso rotation.

The single most important mechanical principle is using your own body to create the strangulation, not just brute arm strength. Rotating your hips and driving your chest into their neck is key.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side-control, establish a secure over-under arm control with your right arm under their left armpit and your left arm over their head. Your left hip should be tight to their left hip.
    2. Drive your chest down, flattening your torso onto theirs, and retract your hips slightly, creating a base. Your head should be off to the side of their neck.
    3. Bring your left knee across their body, aiming to place your left foot flat on the mat or slightly angled, creating a powerful base. Your left thigh should be perpendicular to their torso.
    4. With your left hand, reach under their head and secure a collar grip on your own right triceps (or the back of your right elbow). This is the gable grip or figure-four grip.
    5. Rotate your hips away from your opponent (counter-clockwise if you are on their left side) while simultaneously driving your chest forward and into their neck. This hip rotation generates the primary pressure.
    6. Keep your head upright and your back straight, avoiding rounding your spine. Your weight should be distributed over their chest and neck.
    7. Squeeze your elbows together and drive your chest forward. The combination of hip rotation and chest pressure compresses the carotid artery.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly driving your head forward and into their shoulder can hyperextend your neck, risking cervical strain or disc injury. The correct movement is to keep your head upright and use your torso rotation.
    • Over-squeezing with just arm strength without hip rotation can lead to bicep or shoulder strain, as the power is not biomechanically leveraged. Always prioritize hip drive and torso engagement.
    • Allowing your hips to sink too low or not driving them forward can reduce choke effectiveness and put your own lower back at risk of strain from poor posture. Maintain an active, driven hip position.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the body positioning and hip rotation without a partner. Focus on the sequence of bringing the knee across and setting the grip. (10 repetitions per side)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner, walk through the steps of the choke, focusing on achieving the correct body alignment and grip. (5 repetitions per side)
    3. Light resistance drill (25%): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to practice the setup and hip drive. Focus on maintaining control. (5 repetitions per side)
    4. Controlled pressure drill (50%): Your partner tries to defend minimally, allowing you to feel the pressure build as you execute the choke. (5 repetitions per side)
    5. Simulated submission drill (75%): Your partner actively defends, but you are allowed to finish the choke if they don't escape within a set time. Focus on transitions and adjustments. (3 repetitions per side)
    6. Live rolling (90-100%): Attempt the Arm Triangle Choke in a live rolling scenario, prioritizing technique over outcome. Understand when the opportunity arises. (As opportunities present themselves)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have dominant side-control and your opponent is turtled or trying to bridge away.
    • When you have mount and your opponent is defending by turning into you, creating an opening.
    • When you are in north-south position and can transition to a side-control setup.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip escape and shrimp: The opponent escapes their hips away from the choke by bridging and shrimping, creating space to relieve pressure.
    • Flattening out: The opponent attempts to flatten their body, distributing the pressure and making it harder to isolate the neck.
    • Turning into the choke: The opponent turns their body into the direction of the choke, often to relieve pressure on the carotid artery and potentially create an escape or sweep opportunity.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my arm hurt when I try the Arm Triangle Choke from side control?

    Your arm likely hurts because you are relying too much on squeezing with your arms alone, rather than using your hips and torso for leverage. This can strain your biceps and shoulders. Focus on driving your chest into their neck and rotating your hips to generate the choking pressure, keeping your arms in a supportive rather than primary power-generating role.

    Q: How can I finish the Arm Triangle Choke against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, precise hip and torso mechanics are even more critical. Ensure your chest is driving directly into their neck and their shoulder is acting as the fulcrum. Avoid trying to muscle them; instead, focus on achieving a tight seal with your body, driving your hips forward, and rotating your torso to constrict the airway and carotid artery.

    Q: When is the best time to set up an Arm Triangle Choke in BJJ?

    The ideal time is when your opponent is in a compromised position, such as when they are turning into you from side-control, or when they attempt to turtle. These moments create a natural opening for you to secure the over-under arm control and begin driving your body into their neck. Avoid attempting it when you are in a defensive or unstable position.

X-Guard is an open guard position where one leg hooks inside the opponent's thigh and the other controls their ankle, enabling sweeps and submissions. It's a dynamic position requiring precise body control.

White belts often struggle with X-Guard due to a lack of understanding of weight distribution and frame creation, leading to unstable positions. They tend to rely on brute strength rather than leverage.

The core principle of X-Guard is using your hips to elevate and off-balance the opponent, creating angles for attack. Your body acts as a fulcrum.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a seated open guard, establish a Lasso Guard grip on the opponent's right arm with your left hand (around the bicep). Simultaneously, place your right foot on the opponent's left hip, toes pointing upwards.
    2. With your left hand, secure a sleeve grip on the opponent's right sleeve. Your right foot on their hip should be pressing them away slightly.
    3. Transition your right leg to hook *inside* the opponent's left thigh, just above the knee. Your foot should be flexed, driving into their thigh.
    4. Bring your left leg up to control the opponent's right ankle with your left foot, heel digging into their heel bone. This is your "hook leg".
    5. Simultaneously, bring your hips forward and slightly to your left, creating a strong base. Your right knee should be pointing towards the ceiling, and your left knee towards the floor.
    6. Use your Lasso grip to pull the opponent's right arm across their body, creating a diagonal pull. Your right leg (hooking the thigh) drives the opponent's hip away.
    7. Execute the sweep by extending your right leg, lifting their hip off the mat, while simultaneously pulling their arm across. Your hips remain elevated and driving forward.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending your legs outwards when attempting to hook the thigh can strain your groin and inner thigh adductors. The correct movement is to drive your foot *into* the thigh with a flexed ankle, then hook.
    • Over-extending your knee in the ankle control can lead to ACL or MCL tears. Maintain a slight bend in your knee and focus on controlling the ankle bone, not the shin.
    • Trying to "pull" the opponent over with arm strength alone, instead of hip elevation, can cause shoulder impingement or rotator cuff tears. The primary power comes from hip drive and triangulation.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg transitions and hip elevation without a partner. Focus on the feeling of creating the X-shape with your legs. (50 reps per side)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice entering X-Guard and performing the sweep motion. Focus on grip accuracy and hip movement. (20 reps per side)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance. Focus on maintaining your frames and completing the sweep. (15 reps per side)
    4. Half-speed rolling: Roll with a partner at 50% speed, allowing time to set up X-Guard and execute techniques. Focus on timing and body positioning. (3 rounds)
    5. Controlled rolling: Increase resistance to 75%. The goal is to successfully enter X-Guard and attempt sweeps or submissions. (4 rounds)
    6. Live rolling: Attempt X-Guard during live rolling, focusing on applying the learned biomechanics under full resistance. (5 rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When an opponent is posturing up from your closed guard, creating space to transition.
    • When an opponent is attempting a guard pass and their weight distribution is predictable.
    • When you have established a strong open guard and the opponent is reaching in to break your grips.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass Defense: If the opponent stacks you, drive your hips into them and use your Lasso arm to pull their head down, creating space to shrimp out or transition to a sweep.
    • Knee Slice Pass Defense: If the opponent attempts a knee slice, use your hook leg to push their knee away and your ankle control to pull their leg to the opposite side, creating an angle to sweep.
    • Leg Drag Defense: If the opponent attempts a leg drag, immediately bring your hips back and use your hook leg to block their hips from advancing. You can then attempt to retrieve your leg or transition to a sweep.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my hip hurt when I try to get into X-Guard?

    Your hip likely hurts because you are trying to force the position with hip flexion alone, rather than using your legs to create leverage. Ensure your hook leg is firmly planted inside the opponent's thigh and your ankle control leg is actively pulling. Focus on driving your hips *forward* and *upward* with your leg framework as the primary engine, not just isolated hip movement.

    Q: How can I use X-Guard effectively against a much larger and heavier opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on precise grip fighting to control their posture. Use your hook leg to actively off-balance their weight *before* attempting the sweep. Think of using their own weight against them by creating sharp angles with your hips and legs, rather than trying to lift them directly. Your Lasso arm is crucial for breaking their base.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from Closed Guard to X-Guard?

    The optimal time to transition from Closed Guard to X-Guard is when the opponent is posturing up and creating a slight gap between their hips and yours. As they extend their legs to create base, you can break your closed guard grips and immediately begin the leg entanglement process, using their upward movement to your advantage to initiate the X-Guard entry.

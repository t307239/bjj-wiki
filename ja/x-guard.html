X-Guard is an open guard position where you control one of your opponent's legs with both of yours, creating a strong base to attack or sweep. White belts often struggle by overextending or failing to establish a solid frame, leading to immediate guard passes.

The key to X-Guard is creating an off-balancing angle and using your hips and legs to generate leverage, not brute strength. Think of it as a sophisticated trap for their base.

Understanding the triangular structure formed by your legs around their leg is crucial. This triangle, combined with precise hip movement, allows you to control their posture and initiate powerful sweeps.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a seated open guard, establish a 'Spider Guard' style grip on the opponent's right sleeve with your left hand (palm facing up, thumb inside). Simultaneously, with your right hand, secure a 'Pants Grip' on the opponent's right pants, just above the knee.
    2. Extend your left leg, placing your left foot on the opponent's right hip bone, creating a strong frame. Your right leg should remain tucked, with your right shin crossing in front of the opponent's right thigh.
    3. Initiate the X-Guard by lifting your hips off the mat and driving them towards the opponent's right leg. Your left foot pushes off their hip, while your right shin "hooks" their thigh from the inside.
    4. Simultaneously, bring your right leg up and around the opponent's right thigh, so your right foot hooks behind their right knee. Your left leg remains extended, hooking their right hip.
    5. Your opponent's right leg is now trapped between your right shin and your left thigh. Your left foot remains on their hip, maintaining the frame and distance.
    6. Your hips should be angled approximately 45 degrees away from your opponent. Your weight is distributed to create a stable base, preventing them from easily stacking you.
    7. To initiate a sweep, drive your hips forward and slightly upwards, while simultaneously pulling their sleeve and pushing their hip away with your left foot. This breaks their base and creates an opportunity for a sweep.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending your knee: Overextending your right knee while trying to hook your opponent's leg can hyperextend your knee joint, potentially tearing the ACL or PCL.
    • Correct: Maintain a slight bend in your right knee, allowing for controlled movement and power generation.
    • Gripping too low on the pants: Gripping the opponent's pants below the knee provides insufficient leverage to control their leg. This leads to them easily stepping out or passing.
    • Correct: Grip the pants just above the knee to maintain control of the thigh and hip.
    • Failing to create a hip frame: Not using your left foot to push off the opponent's hip creates a weak base, allowing them to collapse your guard and pass easily.
    • Correct: Actively push off the opponent's hip with your left foot to maintain distance and control.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo movement: Practice transitioning from a seated guard to the X-Guard position without a partner, focusing on hip height and leg placement. 50 reps each side.
    2. Static X-Guard hold: Have a partner lie on their back. You establish X-Guard and hold the position for 30 seconds, focusing on maintaining frame and hook. 5 sets each side.
    3. Controlled sweep initiation: From X-Guard, have a partner lightly resist. Practice the hip drive and pull/push motion to initiate a sweep, stopping before full rotation. 20 reps each side, 25% resistance.
    4. Half-speed sweep execution: With a partner, execute a full sweep from X-Guard at half speed, focusing on correct timing of hip drive and grips. 10 reps each side, 50% resistance.
    5. Sweep completion with recovery: Perform X-Guard sweeps with a partner, completing the sweep and immediately recovering to a stable guard position. 15 reps each side, 75% resistance.
    6. Live rolling with X-Guard focus: During live rolling, actively attempt to establish and utilize X-Guard for sweeps for 2-minute rounds. 5 rounds, 90-100% resistance.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent is posturing up and leaning forward, creating an opening for leg entanglement.
    • When an opponent attempts a guard pass and their weight is distributed forward, allowing you to hook their leg.
    • When you have successfully transitioned from a Spider Guard or De La Riva Guard and can isolate one of their legs.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: If the opponent establishes X-Guard, they will drive their hips forward. The defender should immediately drive their hips back and down, attempting to "stack" the X-Guard player, collapsing the structure.
    • Leg Release (Knee-to-Chest): The opponent in X-Guard will have one leg hooked. If the defender can bring their free leg (the one not being attacked) in front of their own chest and push their knee into their own opponent's face, they can often create enough space to pull their trapped leg free.
    • Hip Escape and Posture Up: The defender can perform a strong hip escape to the side of the trapped leg, simultaneously driving their hips down and away. This breaks the X-Guard angle and allows them to stand up or transition to a more dominant position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to set up X-Guard?

    Your knee pain likely stems from hyperextension or excessive inward rotation. When you attempt to hook your opponent's leg, ensure your knee remains slightly bent and that you are driving your hips forward, not forcing your leg into an unnatural position. The power should come from hip drive and core engagement, not just leg extension.

    Q: How can I use X-Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paramount. Focus on maintaining a strong frame with your non-hooking leg on their hip to control distance and posture. Use your grips to break their balance *before* you commit to the sweep. Their size means they have more mass to move, so off-balancing them effectively is key.

    Q: When should I transition from my regular guard to X-Guard?

    You should consider transitioning to X-Guard when your opponent's weight is forward and they are leaning into your guard. This is common during guard pass attempts or when they are posturing up. Look for moments where you can isolate one of their legs and quickly establish the hook and frame before they can react and stabilize their base.

X-Guard: A Biomechanically Precise BJJ Guide for White Belts

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

X-Guard is an open guard position where you place one foot on the opponent's hip and the other foot on their inner thigh/knee, creating a unique lever. It allows for sweeps and transitions by controlling distance and attacking the opponent's base.

White belts often struggle with X-Guard because they lack the nuanced body control and understand the importance of precise angles and weight distribution. They tend to rely on brute strength rather than leverage.

The core principle of X-Guard is using your legs as levers to unbalance the opponent. Your hips must be mobile and actively used to create off-balancing angles, not passively waiting for an opportunity.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a seated open guard, establish a standard collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (opponent's right sleeve) with your left hand.
    2. Swing your right leg between the opponent's legs, placing your right foot's sole against their right hip bone. Keep your right knee slightly bent and pointing upwards.
    3. Simultaneously, bring your left leg up and hook your left foot's sole onto the opponent's inner thigh, just above their knee. Your left knee should point towards the ceiling.
    4. Shift your hips to your left, creating a 45-degree angle relative to the opponent. Your weight should be distributed onto your left hip.
    5. Extend your right leg slightly, pushing against the opponent's hip to create space and leverage. This is not a forceful push but a controlled extension.
    6. Pull down on the opponent's collar with your right hand and pull their sleeve towards you with your left hand, simultaneously driving your hips forward and slightly up.
    7. As the opponent leans forward to stabilize, drive your hips further left and begin to lift your opponent's right leg with your left leg, initiating a sweep.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Hyperextension: The opponent applying a heavy forward pressure pass (e.g., stack pass) while your right leg is extended against their hip can force hyperextension of your right knee if you don't maintain a slight bend and proper hip angle. The correct movement is to immediately retract your right leg and shift your hips to defend.
    • Ankle Sprain: Attempting to quickly adjust your left foot's position on the opponent's thigh without proper hip mobility or balance can lead to your left ankle rolling inwards if the opponent shifts their weight unexpectedly. The correct action is to maintain a stable base with your left foot and adjust your hip angle to create space rather than relying on foot placement alone.
    • Lower Back Strain: Trying to 'pull' the opponent down with excessive upper body force while your hips are not properly angled or engaged can strain your lower back. The correct technique emphasizes hip drive and leverage, not upper body pulling strength.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Mobility Drill: Perform 20 hip circles in each direction, focusing on creating a wide range of motion. Repeat 3 times.
    2. Leg Placement Drill (No Resistance): Practice entering X-Guard from a seated position, focusing on precise foot placement on the hip and thigh. Perform 10 successful entries on each side.
    3. Frame Creation Drill (Light Resistance - 25%): With a partner kneeling, practice establishing the X-Guard grips and creating the leg frames without any weight transfer. Focus on the 45-degree hip angle. 15 reps per side.
    4. Hip Drive and Unbalance Drill (Moderate Resistance - 50%): From X-Guard, have your partner offer slight resistance. Practice driving your hips and executing the initial leg extension to unbalance them. 10 successful unbalancing attempts per side.
    5. Sweep Entry Drill (Significant Resistance - 75%): Practice entering X-Guard and initiating the sweep motion against a partner who is actively trying to prevent it but not passing. Focus on timing the hip drive with their weight shift. 5 successful sweeps per side.
    6. Live Rolling (90-100%): Integrate X-Guard attempts into live rolling, focusing on maintaining the position and executing sweeps when opportunities arise. Do not force the technique; look for natural openings.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent attempts to pass your guard from a standing or kneeling position and you have a moment to establish grips.
    • When an opponent is postured up and you have the opportunity to invert or adjust your hips to enter the position.
    • When you are in open guard and have successfully isolated one of the opponent's legs with your leg.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee-Slice Pass: If the opponent attempts to knee-slice, immediately retract your X-Guard legs and transition to closed guard or a deep half-guard by securing a grip on their far leg and hip.
    • Stack Pass: If the opponent attempts to stack, drive your hips forward and to the side to create space, then use your legs to push their hips away and either re-establish guard or transition to a different open guard.
    • Forward Pressure Pass: If the opponent applies heavy forward pressure, use your hips to create a slight angle and your legs to push their hips back. If they persist, switch to a butterfly guard or single leg takedown defense.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do X-Guard?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are overextending it or allowing it to be twisted. When placing your foot on the opponent's hip, maintain a slight bend in your knee, and ensure your knee is tracking over your toes, not collapsing inwards or outwards. The power comes from your hip drive, not from locking out your knee.

    Q: How do I use X-Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on using their weight against them by creating a strong angle with your hips. Instead of trying to lift them, aim to use your X-Guard to off-balance them and create opportunities for sweeps that utilize leverage, such as a tripod sweep or a scissor sweep initiated from the X-Guard position. Precise grip fighting to control their posture is crucial.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from X-Guard to a different guard?

    You should consider switching guards if your opponent successfully neutralizes your X-Guard by establishing a strong base and preventing you from creating angles or off-balancing them. If they begin to flatten you out or apply excessive pressure without giving you an opening, it's often safer and more effective to transition to a guard like closed guard or butterfly guard by retracting your legs and re-establishing control.

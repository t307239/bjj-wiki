X-Guard: A White Belt's Biomechanical Foundation in BJJ

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

X-Guard is an open guard position where one leg is hooked inside the opponent's leg and the other is placed on their hip, creating a strong base for sweeps and transitions.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of weight distribution and frame creation, leading to easily being passed.

The core principle is using your hip as a lever and your leg on the hip as a fulcrum to disrupt their base and generate sweep opportunities.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting from a closed guard, break posture by grabbing opponent's collar lapels with both hands.
    2. Untuck your right leg, placing your right foot on the opponent's left hip joint, toes pointed forward.
    3. Simultaneously, tuck your left leg inside the opponent's right thigh, hooking their leg with your left shin and ankle.
    4. Your left knee should be pointing towards the ceiling, creating a strong frame against their hip.
    5. Sit up slightly, bringing your hips forward and creating a 90-degree angle between your torso and the floor.
    6. Maintain a tight connection with your left hook, ensuring your shin is perpendicular to their thigh.
    7. Your right leg on the hip acts as a lever; push off their hip to create space and initiate sweeps.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly placing your foot on the opponent's knee instead of their hip: This can cause hyperextension and tearing of the opponent's knee ligaments (ACL, MCL). Always aim for the hip bone.
    • Overextending your back when trying to create space: This puts immense strain on your lumbar spine, risking disc herniation. Keep your core engaged and use your legs to create separation.
    • Allowing your opponent to stack you by not maintaining a strong frame with your left leg: This can lead to a crushing pressure on your spine and ribs, causing potential fractures. Ensure your left shin is firmly planted against their inner thigh.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice entering X-Guard from a seated position, focusing on leg placement and hip drive. 20 repetitions.
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Practice the transition from closed guard to X-Guard with a compliant partner. 10 repetitions per side.
    3. Partner drilling (light pressure): Partner attempts to maintain posture. Focus on maintaining X-Guard position. 50% resistance, 5 minutes.
    4. Sweep attempts (controlled): Partner provides minimal defense. Focus on executing sweeps from X-Guard. 75% resistance, 5 repetitions.
    5. Guard retention drills: Partner attempts to pass from X-Guard. Focus on recovering the guard. 75% resistance, 5 minutes.
    6. Live rolling: Implement X-Guard in sparring. Focus on positional control and sweep opportunities. 90% resistance, 3 rounds.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When opponent is posturing up in your closed guard.
    • When opponent attempts to pass your open guard by stepping around.
    • When you have a strong underhook or collar grip that allows you to break their posture.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: Opponent drives their weight forward, attempting to flatten you. Defend by widening your base and pushing their hips away with your right leg.
    • Toreando Pass: Opponent attempts to spin around your legs. Maintain your left hook and use your right leg to push their hip away, preventing them from circling.
    • Leg Drag: Opponent attempts to pull your leg across their body. Keep your left knee tight to their hip and use your right foot to push their hip away, creating space to re-hook.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my hip hurt when I try to get into X-Guard?

    Hip pain often stems from improper entry mechanics. If you're forcing your leg into position without creating adequate space, you're likely jamming your hip socket. Ensure you're using your hands to break posture and your free leg to clear space before tucking your hooking leg.

    Q: How can I use X-Guard effectively against a much bigger and heavier opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on using your right leg on their hip as a fulcrum to elevate and off-balance them, rather than trying to muscle them. Maintain a tight left hook to control their base and prevent them from simply crushing you.

    Q: When is the best moment to switch from closed guard to X-Guard?

    The optimal moment is when your opponent attempts to create distance or posture up. As they push away or stand up, this is your opportunity to break their posture with your grips and immediately transition your legs to the X-Guard configuration before they can solidify their base.

The Leg Drag Pass is a fundamental guard pass that starts from open guard or half guard, aiming to isolate one of the opponent's legs and advance past it to side control.

White belts often struggle because they lack the body awareness to control hip pressure and leg angles, leading to them being swept or controlled.

The key insight is using your opponent's own leg as a lever to break their base and create an angle for your hips to pass.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From standing or kneeling, establish a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a same-side sleeve grip (left sleeve) with your left hand.
    2. Step your left foot outside your opponent's right knee, creating a 45-degree angle with your hips.
    3. Drive your left knee towards the mat, aiming to place it just outside your opponent's right hip, not directly on their thigh.
    4. Use your right hand on the collar to pull their upper body towards your left side, breaking their posture.
    5. With your left hand, grip their right pant leg just above the ankle, creating a secure anchor.
    6. Begin to drag their right leg across your body, moving it towards your left hip, while simultaneously driving your hips forward and to your right.
    7. As their leg is dragged, your hips will naturally move past their right hip, allowing you to transition to side control by securing a cross-face grip and controlling their far arm.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect knee placement: Driving your left knee directly onto your opponent's thigh can hyperextend their knee joint, risking ACL or MCL tears.

    The correct movement is to place your knee outside their hip, guiding their leg across.

    • Over-extending your grip on the pant leg: Gripping too high on the thigh can allow your opponent to shrimp and escape.

    The correct grip is just above the ankle for maximum control of their leg's movement.

    • Relying on upper body strength: Trying to pull the leg across solely with your arms will lead to fatigue and a weak pass.

    The correct biomechanics involve using your hips and body weight to drive the pass.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling of hip angle and leg placement without a partner (50 reps each side).
    2. Partner drilling: Practicing grips and hip angle with a compliant partner, focusing on the initial leg drag motion (20 reps each side, 0% resistance).
    3. Partner drilling: Adding the pull to break posture and starting the leg drag, with partner resisting slightly by keeping legs bent (20 reps each side, 25% resistance).
    4. Partner drilling: Executing the full leg drag pass to side control, with partner offering mild resistance to the pass (30 reps each side, 50% resistance).
    5. Live rolling: Attempting the Leg Drag Pass against a resisting opponent who is actively defending their guard (5 minutes, 75% intensity).
    6. Live rolling: Incorporating the Leg Drag Pass into your game against a fully resisting opponent, focusing on maintaining control and finishing the pass (5 minutes, 90% intensity).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is in a closed guard and you are working to break it open.
    • When your opponent has an open guard and their legs are extended, creating an entry point.
    • When your opponent is in half guard and you are looking to pass to side control.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Retention (Shrimping): If the leg drag is initiated but not fully controlled, the opponent can shrimp their hips away, creating space to re-establish guard.
    • Technical Stand-up: If the passer's base is unstable and they are slow to advance, the opponent can attempt a technical stand-up to create a scramble.
    • Back Take: If the passer is too slow or their hips are not aligned correctly, the opponent can attempt to spin behind them as they pass.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Leg Drag Pass from guard?

    Your knee likely hurts due to improper knee placement during the pass. If you are driving your knee directly onto your opponent's thigh or shin, you are putting excessive pressure on your own knee joint, potentially hyperextending it. Ensure your knee is placed outside your opponent's hip, guiding their leg across your body, not directly on their leg.

    Q: How can I Leg Drag Pass against a much bigger opponent who has strong hips?

    Against a larger opponent, you must emphasize breaking their posture and hip control first. Use a strong cross-collar grip and pull their upper body significantly towards your left side as you step your left foot outside their hip. This off-balancing is crucial before you attempt to drag their leg. Focus on the angle and leverage, not brute strength.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate a Leg Drag Pass, and what if they keep their guard closed?

    The Leg Drag Pass is most effective when your opponent's guard is open or partially open, and their legs are extended. If they maintain a tight closed guard, focus on breaking their guard first using standard methods like the hip bump sweep or knee-through pass. Once the guard is open, you can then look for opportunities to set up the Leg Drag.

Leg Drag Pass: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide to BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Leg Drag Pass is a fundamental guard pass initiated from a standing or kneeling position, aiming to secure control of one of your opponent's legs to break their guard and achieve side control.

White belts often struggle with this pass due to a lack of precise body mechanics, leading to inefficient movement and potential injury, often trying to force the pass rather than guiding it.

The key insight is to use your hips and core to 'drag' the opponent's leg across their body, creating a linear path for your body to follow and secure the dominant position.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Establish a cross-collar grip with your right hand on the opponent's left collar and a cross-sleeve grip with your left hand on their left sleeve.
    2. Hip Angle: Lower your hips by bending your knees, bringing your pelvis closer to the mat than your opponent's.
    3. Weight Transfer: Drive your weight forward and slightly to your left, creating a diagonal line from your head to your right hip.
    4. Leg Placement: Step your right foot forward, placing it outside your opponent's left knee, ensuring your knee is pointing towards their hip.
    5. The Drag: With your left hand, pull their left sleeve firmly across their body towards your right hip, simultaneously rotating your hips to mirror this motion.
    6. Body Alignment: As their leg drags across, your left shoulder should move towards their hip pocket, and your right shoulder should move towards their head.
    7. Securing Side Control: Drive your right knee to the mat inside their leg, and swing your left leg over their hips to establish side control, maintaining chest-to-chest pressure.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Knee Drive: Driving your knee directly into the opponent's hip socket with excessive force can cause hyperextension or tearing of the ACL or MCL in their knee.
    • Correct Action: Step your foot outside their knee and drive your knee towards their hip *pocket*, not directly into the joint.
    • Twisting at the Waist: Forcefully twisting your torso to pull the leg without proper hip alignment can lead to lumbar spine strain or disc herniation.
    • Correct Action: Initiate the drag with your hips and use your arm to guide the leg, maintaining a stable core.
    • Overextension of the Arm: Yanking the sleeve with a fully extended arm can put undue stress on the elbow joint, potentially causing a hyperextension injury.
    • Correct Action: Keep a slight bend in your elbow and use your body's momentum to pull the sleeve across.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Drag: Practice the hip drag motion on your own, simulating the weight transfer and leg pull without a partner (50 reps).
    2. Static Partner Leg Drag: With a compliant partner on their back, practice the initial grips and hip drag motion, focusing on the feeling of the leg moving across the body (25% resistance, 10 reps per side).
    3. Controlled Guard Pass Entry: Partner maintains a loose closed guard. Practice entering the Leg Drag, establishing grips, and driving your hip, stopping before the pass is complete (50% resistance, 5 reps per side).
    4. Full Leg Drag Pass (No Resistance): Execute the complete Leg Drag Pass against a partner who offers no resistance after the initial guard is broken (75% resistance, 5 reps per side).
    5. Light Resistance Leg Drag Pass: Partner attempts light, passive defense (e.g., framing with arms, not actively resisting the pass). Focus on maintaining control and finishing the pass (90% resistance, 5 reps per side).
    6. Live Rolling Leg Drag Pass Attempts: Integrate the Leg Drag Pass into live rolling, focusing on timing and recognizing opportunities without forcing it (100% resistance, 5 attempts per round).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is attempting to close their guard from a distance.
    • When your opponent has their knees inside your hips but their feet are not yet locked.
    • When your opponent is attempting to shrimp away from your pressure, creating space.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The "Switch Hips" Defense: If the opponent feels the drag, they can quickly switch their hips to face you, making the leg drag impossible and creating an opportunity for them to establish a guard.
    • The "Overhook and Frame" Defense: The opponent can secure an overhook on your arm and frame with their free hand on your shoulder, preventing your body from collapsing onto them and buying time to escape.
    • The "Backstep and Recover" Defense: If the opponent feels the pressure and the leg is being dragged, they can attempt to backstep their hips out and re-establish guard or even attempt a sweep.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to Leg Drag Pass?

    Your shoulder likely hurts because you are overextending your arm to pull the opponent's leg, or you are not properly rotating your hips. Ensure you maintain a slight bend in your elbow and use your core and hip rotation to drag the leg, not just your arm strength.

    Q: How can I Leg Drag Pass against a much bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on precise weight distribution and hip angle. Instead of trying to muscle through, use your low base and the diagonal pull of the leg to disrupt their balance. Secure your grips firmly and drive your hips forward and to the side to create the drag.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Leg Drag Pass from half guard?

    The Leg Drag Pass is less common from a traditional half guard where your knee is trapped. It's more effectively initiated when you've just passed the half guard and are in the process of breaking their legs apart, or when they attempt to recover their half guard from side control.

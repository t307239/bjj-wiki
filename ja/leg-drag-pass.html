The Leg Drag Pass is initiated from a standing or kneeling position against an opponent's closed guard or half guard. Its goal is to establish side control by controlling one of the opponent's legs and displacing their hips.

White belts often fail by trying to force the pass with raw strength rather than understanding the leverage points. This leads to poor body positioning and increased risk of injury.

The key mechanical insight is to use your hips to "drag" the opponent's leg across their body, creating a fulcrum that breaks their base and allows you to secure a dominant control position.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Secure a deep cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (e.g., left sleeve) with your left hand.
    2. Hip Angle: Lower your hips to the mat, angling your pelvis towards your opponent's left hip (if passing to your right).
    3. Weight Transfer: Drive your right knee forward, aiming to place it on the mat slightly past your opponent's left hip.
    4. Leg Control: As you drive your knee, use your left arm to hook your opponent's left ankle or shin, pulling it towards your chest.
    5. Hip Displacement: Simultaneously, use your right hip to push into your opponent's left hip, forcing their body to rotate away from you.
    6. Head Position: Keep your head down, chest to chest, maintaining pressure and preventing them from bridging.
    7. Pummeling: As their leg is dragged across, pummel your right leg underneath their hips to secure side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Valgus/Varus Collapse: Trying to drive your knee directly forward without proper hip angling can cause your knee to collapse inward (valgus) or outward (varus), potentially tearing the ACL or MCL.
    • Lower Back Hyperextension: Forcing the pass by arching your lower back excessively can lead to muscle strains or disc compression.
    • Wrist Sprains: Gripping too tightly with the sleeve grip and then having the opponent resist with a strong pull can cause hyperextension or twisting of the wrist joints.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Isolation (0%): Practice the hip angle, knee drive, and arm hook motion without a partner. Focus on smooth transitions. (20 reps)
    2. Passive Partner - Grip & Drag (25%): Partner lies on their back, you secure grips and practice the leg drag motion, pulling their leg across. Partner offers no resistance. (15 reps each side)
    3. Passive Partner - Full Pass Setup (50%): Partner provides minimal resistance as you execute the entire pass from guard. Focus on hip displacement and knee positioning. (10 reps each side)
    4. Light Resistance - Controlled Pass (75%): Partner actively tries to maintain guard but doesn't actively defend the pass. Focus on maintaining pressure and correct body mechanics. (5 reps each side)
    5. Controlled Sparring - Focus on Pass (90%): Spar with a partner, with the explicit goal of attempting the Leg Drag Pass. Partner defends normally. (3 rounds, 3 minutes each)
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Attempt the Leg Drag Pass during regular sparring. Focus on applying the learned mechanics and adapting to your opponent's reactions.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When opponent is in closed guard and you have established a strong grip on their collar and sleeve.
    • When opponent is in half guard and their leg is extended, creating an opening for a leg drag.
    • When opponent attempts to shrimp away, creating space to attack their base and leg.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape and Re-guard: When the leg is being dragged, the defender can immediately shrimp their hips away and re-establish guard, preventing the pass.
    • Knee Shield / Frame: The defender can use their free leg to create a "knee shield" or frame against the passer's chest, preventing the forward pressure and hip displacement.
    • Stand Up / Base Out: If the passer's knee drive is too far forward, the defender can attempt to "base out" by standing up or pushing their hips back, resetting to a neutral position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Leg Drag Pass from closed guard?

    Your wrist likely hurts because you are gripping the opponent's sleeve too tightly with a straight wrist, and they are resisting by pulling or twisting. To fix this, keep a slight bend in your wrist, forming a "hook" with your fingers, and grip the fabric higher up on their forearm. Focus on using your forearm and bicep to control their sleeve, not just your hand.

    Q: How can I Leg Drag Pass against a much bigger opponent who has strong hips?

    Against a bigger opponent, raw strength won't work. Focus intensely on the biomechanics of the hip angle and weight transfer. Instead of trying to pull their leg, use your hips to "drag" it across their center of gravity. Drive your chest into their hips as you execute the pass, using their size against them by making them uncomfortable and off-balance.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from a failed Leg Drag Pass to another technique?

    If your opponent successfully defends the Leg Drag Pass by re-guarding or creating a strong frame, don't force it. As you feel their hips reset, you can transition by either releasing your grip and attempting a knee-slice pass if they are flat on their back, or by switching to a "smash pass" if they are on their side, using your weight to flatten them out further.

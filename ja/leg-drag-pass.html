Leg Drag Pass BJJ: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Leg Drag Pass is a guard passing technique initiated from a standing or kneeling position against an opponent's closed guard or open guard. Its goal is to isolate one of the opponent's legs and advance to side control.

White belts often struggle by trying to muscle through the pass or by not understanding the rotational mechanics involved. This leads to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The core principle is using your hips to create a strong lever, rotating your body to break the opponent's posture and control their base, thereby freeing your leg to advance.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Start kneeling facing your opponent, with their legs inside your knees (closed guard assumed). Secure an "over-under" grip: right hand on opponent's left hip, left hand on opponent's right collar.
    2. Shift your weight slightly onto your right hip, angling your torso 45 degrees to your left. Your left knee should be planted firmly on the mat.
    3. Use your left hand to break the opponent's posture by pulling their collar down and slightly to your left. Simultaneously, drive your right hip forward, creating a wedge against their left hip.
    4. Step your right foot outside the opponent's left hip, planting it firmly on the mat. Your right shin should be perpendicular to the mat, creating a barrier.
    5. Release the collar grip. Your right forearm now snakes under the opponent's left thigh, securing a "forearm under thigh" grip with your palm facing upwards.
    6. Drive your left shoulder into the opponent's chest, creating forward pressure. Your hips should be driving forward and slightly to your right.
    7. Rotate your body 90 degrees counter-clockwise. Your chest should now be facing the mat, with your left knee driving towards the opponent's right hip and your right leg extended behind you.
    8. As you rotate, your right leg (the one you've "dragged") slides across the opponent's body. Your goal is to have your right hip flush against their left hip, controlling their base.
    9. Immediately secure a "cross-face" grip with your right hand on the opponent's left cheek/jaw and your left hand on their right arm/sleeve to prevent them from turning back in. Your left knee can now come up to their hip or chest to establish side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect hip drive: Attempting to lift the opponent's leg instead of driving your hip forward. This can strain the lumbar spine due to hyperextension and place excessive pressure on the knees if the opponent resists by pushing back.
    • Knee positioning: Planting your left knee too far forward or in line with your opponent's hip, leading to a "twisted knee" scenario if they shrimp or attempt a sweep. The knee should be angled towards their hip for stability.
    • Over-rotation without control: Spinning your body too fast without establishing the leg drag grip. This leaves your own leg exposed to sweeps and can cause ankle or knee ligament sprains as you lose balance.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling (0%): Practice the hip drive, torso rotation, and leg slide motion without a partner. Focus on the body mechanics. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drilling, no resistance (25%): With a compliant partner, practice the entire sequence of grips and movements. Focus on establishing the correct positions and grips. (20 reps per side)
    3. Partner drilling, light resistance (50%): Partner offers mild resistance to posture break and hip drive. Focus on maintaining pressure and control. (15 reps per side)
    4. Partner drilling, dynamic resistance (75%): Partner actively tries to prevent the pass by framing and hip escaping. Focus on adapting your pressure and timing. (10 reps per side)
    5. Flow rolling, focus on Leg Drag (90%): During flow rolling, actively look for opportunities to initiate the Leg Drag Pass. The opponent will defend more dynamically. (5 minutes, focus on Leg Drag attempts)
    6. Live rolling (100%): Apply the Leg Drag Pass in a competitive scenario, understanding that the opponent will actively resist and counter. (3 rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent attempts a closed guard pass by posturing up, creating an opening for your hips to drive in.
    • Opponent's legs are extended or they are attempting a guard recovery, leaving one leg momentarily accessible.
    • You have successfully broken the opponent's posture and have an over-under grip, allowing you to initiate the hip drive.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): If the attacker drives their hip into yours, you can shrimp your hips away and bring your leg back to regain guard or create space.
    • Re-guarding with the free leg: As the attacker attempts to drag your leg, use your free leg to hook their opposite hip or armpit and pull yourself back into guard.
    • Elevating the Leg: If the attacker gets their forearm under your thigh, you can try to lift your leg by extending your knee and straightening your leg, making it difficult for them to maintain the drag.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Leg Drag Pass?

    Knee pain often arises from improper knee positioning during the pass. If your left knee is planted too far forward or directly in line with your opponent's hip, it becomes vulnerable to twisting forces when they shrimp. Ensure your left knee is angled towards their hip and your body rotates around it, rather than your knee being the primary pivot point.

    Q: How do I Leg Drag Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing become paramount over strength. Focus on the biomechanical principle of using your hips to create a wedge and drive *under* their weight. Secure a deep collar grip to break their posture first, then drive your hip into their hip to disrupt their base before attempting the leg drag. Small, precise movements are more effective than brute force.

    Q: The ideal moment is when the opponent attempts to pass your guard by posturing up or driving their weight forward. This creates a momentary opening where their hips are closer to you and their base is less stable. Look for the opportunity when they commit their weight forward, allowing you to effectively drive your hip into theirs and initiate the rotation.

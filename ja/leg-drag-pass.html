The Leg Drag Pass is a guard passing technique that starts from your opponent's closed guard or open guard and aims to secure side control.

White belts often struggle because they rely on brute force instead of precise body mechanics and leverage.

The key insight is to use your hips to peel their leg away and then drive your chest into their hip, creating a powerful pinning action.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Secure a collar grip with your right hand (palm facing up) and a sleeve grip on their left sleeve with your left hand. Your hips are slightly angled towards your right.
    2. Break Guard: With your right hand pulling their collar towards your chest, use your left hand on their sleeve to pull their left arm across your body. Simultaneously, drive your left knee towards the mat inside their legs, creating space.
    3. Leg Peel: As you drive your left knee down, use your right hand (still on their collar) to push their left hip away from you. Your goal is to create a gap between their legs.
    4. Hip Connection: Immediately step your right foot forward, placing it outside their left hip. Your right knee should be pointing towards the mat.
    5. Weight Transfer: Drive your chest into their hip and shoulder, keeping your hips low and heavy. Your left leg remains bent and tucked, preventing them from reguarding.
    6. Leg Drag: Slide your left leg across their hips, trapping their left leg between your thigh and torso. Your left shin should be perpendicular to their body.
    7. Secure Side Control: Maintain chest-to-hip pressure. Extend your right leg back to establish a stable base. Your left arm can secure their far hip or waist to prevent them from turning.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Injury: Attempting to forcefully rip the leg over their body without creating hip space can cause your knee to twist unnaturally. This can lead to ACL or meniscus tears. The correct way is to use hip pressure to peel their leg first.
    • Lower Back Strain: Driving forward with your upper body while your hips are too high puts immense strain on your lower back. This can result in muscle pulls or disc issues. Always keep your hips low and connected to their hip.
    • Ankle Sprain: Trying to force your leg into position when their guard is too tight can lead to your ankle being twisted outwards. Focus on creating the space with hip and collar control before initiating the leg drag.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Peel & Knee Drive (0%): From your knees, practice driving your left knee down and using your right hand to push an imaginary hip away, creating space. Repeat 20 times.
    2. Solo Leg Peel & Hip Connection (0%): From your knees, practice the hip peel and then stepping your right foot forward outside the hip. Repeat 20 times.
    3. Partner Assisted Leg Peel & Hip Connection (25%): With a partner on their back, you are on your knees. Practice the hip peel and stepping your foot outside their hip. Partner offers minimal resistance. Repeat 10 times per side.
    4. Partner Assisted Leg Drag Setup (50%): From guard, partner on their back. Practice the initial grips, breaking guard, and peeling the leg. Partner offers light resistance to their leg. Repeat 10 times per side.
    5. Controlled Leg Drag Pass (75%): Start from guard. Execute the Leg Drag Pass with controlled resistance from your partner. Focus on maintaining chest-to-hip pressure and securing side control. Repeat 5 times per side.
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Attempt the Leg Drag Pass during live rolling. Focus on correct biomechanics and timing. Do not force it if the opportunity isn't there. Aim for 3 successful passes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is in closed guard and their hips are slightly away from you, creating a small gap.
    • When your opponent opens their guard and their legs are extended, offering an opportunity to isolate one.
    • When your opponent attempts a sweep from guard, creating a moment of imbalance you can exploit.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Reguard Attempt: If they try to bring their leg back in, immediately drive your chest into their hip and use your trailing leg to push their knee away, maintaining your position.
    • Shrimping Away: If they shrimp to create space, maintain your chest-to-hip connection and use your free leg to block their hip from moving further away.
    • Knee Shield: If they establish a knee shield, you must first break the shield by driving your shoulder into their knee and then re-establishing your chest-to-hip connection.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try the Leg Drag Pass?

    Your shoulder likely hurts because you are trying to force the pass by pulling their leg with your arm while your body is not properly aligned. This puts excessive torque on your shoulder joint. Ensure you are using your hips to peel their leg and driving your chest into their hip for control, rather than relying solely on arm strength.

    Q: How do I Leg Drag Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise timing are even more critical. Focus on creating a significant space with your hip pressure before committing to the step. Once you've initiated the drag, drive your chest deep into their hip and use their own weight against them by staying low and connected. Do not try to lift or manhandle them; instead, aim to off-balance and control their base.

    Q: When is the best moment to transition from the Leg Drag Pass to another pass?

    The best moment to transition is if your opponent successfully defends the initial leg drag by bringing their leg back or establishing a strong base. Instead of forcing the drag, you can use the established chest-to-hip pressure to transition to a knee-slice pass, or if they turn away, to secure a back take. The key is to recognize when the primary pass is no longer viable and adapt quickly.

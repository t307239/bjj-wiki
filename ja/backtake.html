BJJ Back Take: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This technique starts from a dominant position, often after a sweep or guard pass, and aims to establish control of the opponent's back.

White belts often struggle due to uncontrolled movements and poor weight distribution, leading to loss of position or injury.

The key insight is to use your hips and legs to create a stable, controlling base while your upper body secures the choke or controls the opponent's posture.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side control, secure a "seatbelt grip" on the opponent's far arm and the collar of their gi with your opposite hand.
    2. Shift your weight forward, driving your chest into the opponent's shoulder, flattening them to the mat.
    3. "Hitchhike" your outside leg (the one closer to the opponent's head) by placing your foot on the mat behind their hip, creating a frame.
    4. "Walk" your hips towards the opponent's head, rotating your torso to face their head.
    5. Unwind your "seatbelt" arm and immediately secure a "body triangle" by wrapping your legs around their waist, with your foot tucked under your opposite knee.
    6. Simultaneously, bring your "free" arm across their neck to secure a "collar grip" (or palm-to-palm grip if no-gi).
    7. Drive your shoulder into their carotid artery while pulling your opponent's head towards your chest to tighten the choke.
    8. Maintain a "hip-to-hip" connection, ensuring your hips are tight against their waist to prevent them from turning in.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly "pulling" the opponent's head down with your arm can damage your shoulder or their neck; instead, use your core to drive their head into your chest.
    • Over-extending your elbow when securing the body triangle can lead to elbow hyperextension or ligament tears; maintain a slight bend in your elbow and focus on squeezing your thighs together.
    • Attempting to "jump" to the back without establishing a solid base can result in falling off and landing awkwardly, risking knee or ankle sprains; always maintain hip-to-hip contact and secure your hooks before fully transitioning.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip and leg movement of the "hitchhike" and "hip walk" without a partner (10 reps each side).
    2. Solo drill: Practice securing the body triangle and the collar grip from a prone position (10 reps each side).
    3. Partner drill (0% resistance): From side control, have your partner lie flat. Execute the entire back take sequence, focusing on grip accuracy and hip movement (5 reps).
    4. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner provides minimal resistance, allowing you to focus on the transition mechanics (10 reps).
    5. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to defend, requiring you to adjust your weight and grips (15 reps).
    6. Live rolling (75% intensity): Integrate the back take into sparring, focusing on timing and recognizing opportunities (5 attempts or 2 minutes).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent turtles their back to you from side control.
    • When you have successfully swept an opponent and they attempt to give you their back.
    • When you are in a dominant position like mount and the opponent tries to escape by turning away.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • "Elbow-to-knee" defense: If the opponent attempts the back take from side control, bring your elbow to your knee on the side they are trying to advance, creating a frame to prevent hip penetration.
    • "Hip Escape and Turn-In": If they secure the body triangle, aggressively "hip escape" away from their head and simultaneously "turn in" towards them, facing their chest to break the triangle and regain guard.
    • "Hand Fight and Posture Up": If they secure the collar grip, actively "hand fight" to break their grip and "post up" with your base, preventing them from establishing control and creating space to escape.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to do the Back Take from side control?

    This often happens if you are "pulling" the opponent's head down with your arm during the transition. Instead, focus on using your chest and core to drive your weight forward into their shoulder, flattening them. Your "seatbelt" arm should be used to control their posture, not to yank their head.

    Q: How can I Back Take against a much bigger opponent who is very heavy?

    Against a heavier opponent, leverage is key. Focus on using your hips to "hitchhike" and "walk" them towards their head, creating an angle. Don't try to lift them; instead, use their weight against them by shifting your weight and creating a stable base with your legs to control their movement as you transition.

    Q: When is the best time to try for the Back Take in BJJ? I feel like I miss opportunities.

    The best opportunities arise when the opponent is "turned away" from you or "turtling." This typically happens during transitions like guard passes, sweeps, or when they are trying to escape a dominant position. Look for moments when their back is exposed and their posture is compromised.

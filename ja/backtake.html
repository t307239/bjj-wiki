This technique transitions from a dominant position, typically guard or side control, to securing the opponent's back.

White belts often fail by rushing or lacking control, leading to lost positions or injury.

The key is to maintain a strong connection while creating space and shifting your base to secure the hooks.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From Closed Guard, establish Collar and Sleeve grips on the same side (e.g., opponent's left collar and left sleeve).
    2. Secure the opponent's arm with your opposite arm (e.g., right arm grips opponent's left sleeve).
    3. Shift your hips to the side of the controlled arm, creating a 45-degree angle relative to the opponent's torso.
    4. Undercut the opponent's hip with your thigh, lifting slightly to break their base.
    5. Simultaneously, drive your shoulder into their hip on the side of the controlled arm, creating a fulcrum.
    6. Swing your free leg (the one not controlling the hip) over their far shoulder, aiming for their back.
    7. As your leg clears, immediately establish a body triangle or seatbelt grip, ensuring your hips are tight to their back.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Trying to 'pull' the opponent over without hip rotation: This can hyperextend the opponent's shoulder or elbow, causing ligamentous tears (e.g., rotator cuff, UCL).
    • Forcing the leg over without clearing the hip: This can lead to knee hyperextension or twisting injuries in your own knee joint.
    • Losing connection during the transition: This can result in your opponent escaping or counter-attacking, potentially leading to wrist sprains from uncontrolled limb movements.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip shift and leg swing motion without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): Focus on establishing grips and executing the hip undercut and leg swing. Partner remains passive. (10 reps each side)
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner lightly resists hip movement. Focus on maintaining connection and hip angle. (10 reps each side)
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner tries to maintain base. Focus on creating space and driving the shoulder into the hip. (10 reps each side)
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner actively tries to prevent the transition. Focus on maintaining hooks once achieved. (5 reps each side)
    6. Live rolling (90% effort): Apply the technique in a sparring scenario, focusing on correct mechanics over winning. (5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is posturing up from Closed Guard, creating an opening.
    • When transitioning from Side Control and the opponent is trying to escape.
    • When the opponent attempts to stand up from your guard.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Opponent flattens their base: If the opponent flattens their base and prevents the hip undercut, immediately revert to a strong guard retention position (e.g., Closed Guard).
    • Opponent spins away: If the opponent spins away as you swing your leg, do not chase. Instead, maintain your grip and transition to a single-leg X-guard or attempt a sweep.
    • Opponent frames with their arm: If the opponent frames with their arm to prevent your leg from coming over, use that frame to create space and drive your shoulder into their hip to off-balance them further.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to take the back from closed guard?

    The pain likely stems from over-extending your shoulder when trying to 'pull' the opponent over without using your hips and base correctly. Instead of pulling, focus on a controlled hip shift to the side and driving your shoulder into their hip to create leverage. This biomechanical shift protects your shoulder joint and uses your larger muscle groups for power.

    Q: How do I take the back against a bigger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on precise grip fighting to break their posture and control their sleeves. The key is the hip-to-hip connection; as you shift your hips to the side, use your leg to underhook their hip and drive your shoulder into their opposing hip. This creates a fulcrum that allows you to off-balance them and take their back, rather than relying on brute strength.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a back take from closed guard?

    The optimal time is when the opponent's posture is broken and they are leaning forward or attempting to create space. Look for moments when their weight is forward, making it easier to off-balance them. Avoid attempting when they have a strong, upright base, as this makes the transition significantly harder and increases the risk of injury.

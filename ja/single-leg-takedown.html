This takedown is initiated from a standing position when an opponent is within arm's reach. Its goal is to destabilize and bring the opponent to the mat by controlling one of their legs.

White belts often rush this technique, focusing on speed over control, leading to missed entries and potential injuries.

The core principle is using your body's mass and leverage to drive through the opponent, not just pull them down.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Initiate from a staggered stance, left foot forward, right foot back. Opponent is facing you.
    2. Secure a 'collar-and-sleeve' grip on the opponent's left side: right hand grips their left collar, left hand grips their left sleeve near the wrist.
    3. Step your right foot forward, angling your hips to be perpendicular to the opponent's centerline. Your right knee should track slightly outside their right knee.
    4. As you step, drop your level by bending both knees, lowering your hips below the opponent's hips.
    5. Secure a 'body-lock' grip by reaching your left arm around the opponent's waist, securing your right bicep with your left hand.
    6. Simultaneously, drive your right shoulder into the opponent's chest/sternum, initiating forward momentum.
    7. Keeping your head up and driving forward, lift the opponent's left leg by straightening your right leg and pulling upwards with your body lock. Your opponent's weight will shift to their right leg.
    8. Continue driving forward and slightly to the opponent's left as you complete the lift, guiding them to the mat to your right.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to 'pull' the opponent down with arm strength alone: This can strain the shoulder joint (rotator cuff tendons) and elbow joint (ligament damage) due to hyperextension or twisting under load. Instead, use your body's driving force and leg lift.
    • Lunging with the head down and forward: This puts the neck and cervical spine at extreme risk of hyperextension or compression injuries. Always keep your head up and drive through your chest.
    • Not bending knees sufficiently to get hips below opponent's: This results in a 'standing' tackle. The knee of your lead leg can hyperextend and damage the ACL or MCL. Always drop your hips low before initiating the drive.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo stance and hip angle drill: Practice the staggered stance and rotating hips to a perpendicular angle 20 times.
    2. Shadow drill with grip and step: Practice securing collar-sleeve grips, stepping, and dropping levels without a partner, 20 reps.
    3. Partner drill - No resistance: Partner stands passively. Practice the full takedown sequence with exact grips and body mechanics, 10 reps per side.
    4. Partner drill - Light resistance (25%): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the weight transfer and lift, 10 reps per side.
    5. Partner drill - Moderate resistance (50%): Partner actively tries to resist, but allows the takedown to be completed, 10 reps per side.
    6. Live rolling (75-90%): Attempt the single leg takedown during rolling with a willing partner, focusing on correct execution and not just success, 5 attempts total.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent takes a step forward with the same leg you are targeting (e.g., opponent steps with their left leg, you attack their left leg).
    • When you have a strong collar-sleeve grip and have successfully pulled the opponent slightly off-balance forward.
    • When the opponent is posturing up from a guard attempt or is about to initiate a takedown themselves.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • "Sprawl": When the attacker dives for your leg, immediately drop your hips straight down and extend your legs backward, placing your weight over the attacker's back. This prevents them from securing the leg.
    • "Whizzer": If the attacker secures a body lock, use your arm (on the same side as the attacked leg) to hook over the attacker's arm and apply downward pressure. This breaks their posture and control.
    • "Switch Hips/Finish": If the attacker has your leg but you feel them losing control, quickly switch your hips to face the attacker and drive your weight into them, attempting to turn into them or finish the takedown from a different angle.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try a single leg takedown in BJJ?

    Knee pain often occurs when you don't bend your knees enough to get your hips below the opponent's. This forces you to lunge forward with a straight or slightly bent lead leg, leading to hyperextension and potential ligament damage (ACL/MCL). Always drop your hips low and ensure your lead knee tracks outside the opponent's knee.

    Q: How do I perform a single leg takedown against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are crucial. Focus on sinking your hips extremely low, ensuring they are below the opponent's center of gravity. Use a strong collar-sleeve grip to pull them forward and off-balance before initiating the shot. Drive your entire body mass forward and upward, using your legs and core, rather than relying on pulling strength.

    Q: Should I shoot for a single leg takedown with my head up or down?

    You should always shoot for a single leg takedown with your head UP and to the SIDE, driving through your chest. Dropping your head down and forward puts your neck at extreme risk of severe injury from hyperextension or compression. Keeping your head up allows you to see your target and maintain structural integrity.

This technique begins from a standing position, aiming to off-balance your opponent and secure a leg to drive them to the mat.

White belts often rush, neglecting fundamental weight distribution and grip integrity, leading to missed opportunities or injury.

The core insight is to use your head and shoulder pressure to collapse your opponent's base while securing the leg with your arms.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Initiate by attacking the opponent's lead leg. Secure a "collar grip" (overhook on the opponent's neck/shoulder) and a "pant grip" (grip on the outside of the opponent's lead pant leg, just above the knee).
    2. Step your non-attacking leg (your right leg if attacking their left) diagonally forward and slightly to the outside of their lead leg.
    3. Lower your level by bending your knees to approximately 90 degrees, ensuring your hips are lower than your opponent's hips.
    4. Drive your head and shoulder into the opponent's chest, aligning your head to the side of their torso, not directly into their face.
    5. With your collar grip, pull their upper body towards you, while simultaneously "hiking" the pant grip upwards and backward.
    6. Transfer your weight forward onto your lead leg, driving your hips into their hip on the side of the leg you are attacking.
    7. Continue driving forward, maintaining head and shoulder pressure, to collapse their base and bring them to the ground.
    8. Once the opponent is down, secure the leg with both arms and drive through to finish the takedown.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attacking with straight legs: This can hyperextend your knee joint if the opponent counters by sprawling, leading to ACL or MCL tears.

    Correct movement: Always maintain a deep bend in your lead knee, keeping it behind your toes.

    • Gripping too high on the pants: This provides less control and leverage, making it easier for the opponent to escape and potentially causing wrist strain.

    Correct movement: Grip the outside of the pant leg just above the knee, allowing for maximum leverage.

    • Driving with the upper body only: This neglects the crucial hip drive and weight transfer, making the takedown ineffective and risking shoulder or neck injury from the impact.

    Correct way: Combine head/shoulder pressure with a powerful hip drive and forward weight transfer.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling: Practice the footwork, level change, grip acquisition, and head position without a partner. (50 reps per leg)
    2. Partnered Static Drilling (0% resistance): Partner stands passively. Focus on executing all steps of the takedown with proper grips and weight transfer. (20 reps per leg)
    3. Partnered Light Resistance Drilling (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the movement and grip control. (15 reps per leg)
    4. Partnered Controlled Resistance Drilling (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to defend but without full power or commitment. Focus on overcoming their initial defense. (10 reps per leg)
    5. Live Situational Sparring (75% resistance): Start from a standing clinch and attempt the takedown. Partner defends realistically. (5 attempts per leg)
    6. Live Rolling (90-100% resistance): Integrate the takedown attempt into regular sparring. Focus on timing and execution under pressure. (As opportunities arise)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is standing and off-balanced, with their weight on one leg.
    • When you have achieved a dominant collar grip and can control their posture.
    • When the opponent exposes their lead leg during movement or during a failed attack.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Sprawl: As the attacker lowers their level, the defender quickly extends their legs back, placing their hips on the ground and pushing their weight onto the attacker's shoulders. This neutralizes the leg attack and creates space.
    • Whizzer (Overhook): The defender secures a strong overhook on the attacker's waist or arm, preventing the attacker from pulling them forward and allowing them to "circle out" or use the leverage to initiate their own sweep or takedown.
    • Foot Sweep/Trip: While the attacker is committing to the single leg, the defender can use their free leg to sweep the attacker's supporting leg or trip them as they drive forward, capitalizing on the attacker's compromised base.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try a Single Leg Takedown in BJJ?

    Knee pain during a single leg takedown in BJJ often stems from attacking with a straight lead leg. When your opponent sprawls, your knee joint can hyperextend, stressing the ACL and MCL. Always ensure your lead knee is deeply bent, with your shin perpendicular to the mat, and your hips are lower than your opponent's.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paramount. Focus on getting *under* their center of gravity by achieving a deep level change and driving your head into their chest. Secure a strong collar grip to break their posture and pull them down, then use your hips to drive into their hip on the attacked leg's side. Small, explosive movements are more effective than trying to muscle them.

    Q: The optimal time to attempt a single leg takedown is when your opponent is slightly off-balanced, perhaps after they've attempted their own move, or when they are not fully squared up. Look for moments where their weight is predominantly on one leg, or when you can effectively break their posture with a collar grip, making it difficult for them to defend.

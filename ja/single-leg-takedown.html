Master the Single Leg Takedown in BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This technique initiates from a standing or clinch position, aiming to control and bring your opponent to the mat by securing one of their legs.

Many white belts struggle by lunging forward without establishing a stable base or proper body alignment.

The key is to lower your level, drive through your hips, and maintain head control to prevent your opponent from posturing up.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a standing stance, establish a collar grip with your left hand on the opponent's right collar and a sleeve grip with your right hand on their left sleeve. Maintain a distance of approximately one arm's length.
    2. Lower your hips by bending your knees, ensuring your weight is centered over your feet. Your stance should be shoulder-width apart for stability.
    3. Step your left foot forward, slightly past your opponent's right foot, and simultaneously drive your right shoulder into their chest. Your head should be positioned on the outside of their right shoulder line.
    4. Secure a "whizzer" grip with your right arm around their waist, palm facing upwards, to control their upper body rotation. Your left hand maintains the collar grip.
    5. Drive your hips forward and slightly down, creating a fulcrum with your lead leg. Your chest should be pressed against their torso.
    6. As you drive, lift their right leg by cupping it with your left arm and pulling it upwards and slightly backward. This destabilizes their base.
    7. Continue driving forward and slightly to your right, using your whizzer to prevent them from turning into you, until their weight is fully committed to your grip and they fall to the mat.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Lunging forward with straight legs: This can cause hyperextension of the knee joint, potentially tearing the ACL or PCL due to excessive anterior or posterior shear forces.

    The correct action is to bend your knees and maintain a stable base throughout the movement.

    • Grabbing the opponent's leg too low without body control: This leaves your head exposed and your hips too high, making you susceptible to being swept or taken down. It also puts undue stress on your own lower back from trying to lift without proper leverage.

    The correct action is to establish upper body and head control with the whizzer and head position before initiating the leg lift.

    • Dropping your head to the mat while attempting the takedown: This can lead to neck injuries, including whiplash, as your head becomes an uncontrolled point of impact or leverage for the opponent.

    The correct action is to keep your head up, looking towards your opponent's chest or shoulder, maintaining a strong neck posture.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the footwork, level change, and hip drive motion without a partner. Focus on smooth transitions and maintaining a stable base. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the entire takedown motion, focusing on grip placement, body angles, and controlled execution. (25 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the weight transfer and grip effectiveness. Focus on maintaining control. (20 reps per side)
    4. "Shadow" rolling: Simulate the takedown attempt during live rolling, but stop just before full contact or completion if the opportunity arises. (5 minutes)
    5. Live rolling (controlled): Focus on initiating the single leg takedown against a resisting opponent, prioritizing safe execution and completion. Accept successful and unsuccessful attempts. (10 minutes)
    6. Live rolling (full intensity): Integrate the single leg takedown as a natural attack during sparring, aiming for successful execution against a fully resisting opponent. (15 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up and leaning forward, creating an opening for a level change and penetration step.
    • When your opponent is attempting a collar or sleeve tie, allowing you to counter by dropping your level and attacking their legs.
    • During transitions where your opponent is reaching for your legs or attempting to establish a dominant grip, creating an opportunity for a reactive takedown.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • "Sprawl": When the opponent penetrates for the single leg, immediately lower your hips and extend your legs backward, placing your weight over their upper back and shoulders. This prevents them from driving forward and lifting your leg.
    • "Cross-face and Hip Escape": If the opponent secures the single leg and begins to drive, use your free arm to "cross-face" their head, pushing it away. Simultaneously, hip escape to the side to create space and break their grip or posture.
    • "Level Change and Grip Break": If the opponent is driving forward but hasn't fully secured the leg, execute a rapid level change yourself, creating a brief moment of instability for them, and simultaneously attempt to break their grips on your leg or waist.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Single Leg Takedown?

    Knee pain often stems from attempting the takedown with straight legs or by lunging forward without a proper level change. This places excessive stress on the knee joint ligaments. To fix this, always bend your knees deeply before penetrating, ensuring your hips are lower than your opponent's. Drive through your hips and maintain a stable base, avoiding a straight-leg lunge.

    Q: How can I perform a Single Leg Takedown against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are crucial. Focus on a deep penetration step and driving your shoulder into their chest to disrupt their base. Use your whizzer effectively to control their upper body and prevent them from using their weight to push you off. Prioritize securing a strong grip on the leg and driving forward with your hips, rather than trying to lift their entire weight.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Single Leg Takedown in a BJJ match?

    The optimal time is when your opponent exhibits forward momentum or is postured high, creating an opening for you to lower your level and penetrate. Avoid attempting it when they are already low and stable, or when they are actively defending their legs. Look for moments of transition or when they reach for your collar or sleeves.

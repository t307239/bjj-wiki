Precise Single Leg Takedown Technique for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Single Leg Takedown initiates from a standing position, aiming to secure one of your opponent's legs and bring them to the mat.

Many white belts rush this, forgetting the fundamental base and pressure needed for success.

The core principle is to lower your center of gravity below your opponent's and drive forward, not pull.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Start in a staggered stance, feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent. Maintain a squared hip posture.
    2. Secure a collar grip with your lead hand, pulling down slightly to disrupt their posture. Your rear hand grips their lead pant leg at the ankle or calf.
    3. Step your rear foot forward, placing it outside and slightly behind your opponent's lead foot. Your hips should be angled at approximately 45 degrees to their forward-facing body.
    4. Lower your level by bending your knees deeply, sinking your hips below their waistline. Keep your back straight and chest up.
    5. Drive forward with your lead leg, pushing off the mat. Your head should be driven into their chest or shoulder, creating a powerful forward momentum.
    6. As you drive, sweep their secured leg upwards and backwards with your gripping arm. Simultaneously, maintain forward pressure with your upper body.
    7. Continue driving forward, collapsing your opponent to the mat on their hip. Your lead knee should land on the mat in front of you, creating a stable base for follow-up.
    8. Transition immediately to a dominant position like side control or mount, maintaining control of their leg.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly lunging forward with straight legs: This can lead to hyperextension of the knee joint, potentially tearing the ACL or MCL.
    • Gripping too high on the leg (e.g., thigh): This offers less control and can result in your head being exposed to strikes or allowing them to easily step out. The correct grip is at the ankle or calf.
    • Rounding your back and dropping your head: This compromises your base and exposes your neck to submissions. Maintain a straight back and drive your head into their chest or shoulder.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling: Practice the steps without a partner, focusing on hip angles and weight transfer. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner Static Stance Drill: With a willing partner standing passively, practice securing grips and stepping into the takedown position without resisting. (25 reps per side, 10% resistance)
    3. Partner Controlled Entry Drill: Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to practice sinking your level and driving forward. (25 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner Light Resistance Drill: Your partner actively tries to sprawl or resist slightly, allowing you to feel the pressure needed. (20 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Live Rolling - Controlled Takedown Attempts: During rolling, focus on executing the single leg takedown. If unsuccessful, reset or transition to guard. (5 minutes, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling - Full Takedown Integration: Attempt the single leg takedown as a primary offensive option during live rolling. (10 minutes, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent extends their lead leg slightly while in a staggered stance.
    • Opponent overcommits to a strike or posture change, creating an opening.
    • During transitions where your opponent is momentarily unbalanced or their weight is shifted.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Sprawl: Opponent immediately drops their hips and extends their legs back, creating a wide base to prevent you from sinking your hips below theirs. Your counter is to maintain forward pressure and attempt to peel their hips away.
    • Whizzer (Overhook): Opponent secures an overhook on your lead arm and uses it to drive their weight into you, preventing you from driving forward. Your counter is to circle out or attempt to switch to a double leg.
    • Foot Swap/Stiff Arm: Opponent quickly swaps their leg position to face you directly and uses a stiff arm to create space. Your counter is to immediately transition to a double leg or disengage.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I attempt a single leg takedown in BJJ?

    Knee pain during single leg takedowns often stems from hyperextension or twisting. This occurs when you lunge forward with straight legs or fail to maintain proper hip alignment as you drive. Ensure you bend your knees deeply, sink your hips below your opponent's, and drive forward with your legs and core, rather than just pulling.

    Q: How can a white belt effectively perform a single leg takedown against a much larger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are critical. Focus on sinking your level significantly lower than theirs, making their mass less of a factor. Secure a deep pant grip at the ankle for maximum control and drive forward with your entire body weight, aiming to off-balance them rather than simply overpower them. A quick, decisive entry is key.

    Q: For white belts, the ideal time to attempt a single leg takedown is when your opponent is slightly off-balance or has just finished an offensive movement, creating a momentary opening. Look for moments when they extend their lead leg or shift their weight forward, giving you a clear target and opportunity to enter low and with speed.

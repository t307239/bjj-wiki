Mastering the Single Leg Takedown in BJJ for White Belts

This technique initiates from an upright stance, typically when your opponent is also standing or in a low guard. Its goal is to destabilize and bring your opponent to the mat by controlling one of their legs.

Many white belts fail by relying on brute force, neglecting proper hip positioning and weight transfer, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key insight is to use your hips to drive forward and upward, creating an angle that unbalances the opponent and allows you to secure the takedown.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Stance: Maintain a balanced, staggered stance with your lead leg slightly forward, knees softly bent. Your opponent should be within striking distance.
    2. Grip: Reach for your opponent's lead leg with your lead arm, securing a "collar grip" on the back of their thigh or hamstring. Your trail arm "collar grips" their hip or waist.
    3. Entry: Lower your level by bending your knees and hips, ensuring your chest is close to their hip. Your lead knee should be just above the mat, pointing forward, not inward.
    4. Weight Transfer: Drive your hips forward and slightly upward, creating a strong base. Your lead leg acts as a pivot point.
    5. Drive: Use your legs and core to drive your opponent backward and to the side. Your head should be driven into their hip or torso.
    6. Finishing: As they begin to fall, maintain control of the leg and hip. Step your trail leg to secure a stable position, often transitioning to side control or mount.
    7. Head Position: Keep your head up and driven into your opponent's hip to prevent them from posturing up and to maintain balance.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Valgus Collapse: Attempting to drive forward with your lead knee collapsing inward (valgus) can tear the ACL and MCL. The correct movement is to keep your lead knee tracking over your second toe, pointing forward.
    • Lower Back Hyperextension: Rounding your back excessively and hyperextending your lower back while trying to lift the opponent can lead to disc injuries. Maintain a neutral spine and use your leg drive.
    • Neck Strain from Improper Head Position: Driving your head into the opponent's side with your neck twisted can cause cervical strain. Keep your head neutral and use it as a wedge, not for forceful impact.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling (0% resistance): Practice the entry, hip drive, and leg hook motion without a partner. Focus on the correct footwork and hip angles. (20 reps)
    2. Partnered Entry and Grip Control (25% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the initial entry and securing the leg and hip grips. Focus on maintaining close contact. (15 reps)
    3. Controlled Takedown Execution (50% resistance): Execute the full takedown motion with a partner who offers light resistance, focusing on driving and bringing them down safely. (10 reps)
    4. Takedown to Positional Transition (75% resistance): Perform the takedown and immediately transition to a dominant top position (e.g., side control). Partner offers more dynamic defense. (8 reps)
    5. Live Takedown Attempts (90% resistance): Within a sparring scenario, focus on initiating and completing the single leg takedown against a resisting opponent. Don't worry about scoring, focus on execution. (5 reps)
    6. Full Sparring Integration (100% resistance): Integrate the single leg takedown into live rolling, using it when the opportunity arises. Focus on timing and reaction to opponent's movements. (3 rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is reaching for your legs or attempting a takedown themselves.
    • When your opponent is in a low guard and you have achieved a stable, close distance.
    • When your opponent overcommits to a forward movement, creating an opening.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Sprawling: When the opponent initiates the single leg, immediately extend your hips and legs backward, driving your weight onto their upper body. Keep your hips low and your base wide.
    • Whizzer: As the opponent secures the leg, use your lead arm to "whizzer" over their shoulder, driving your elbow down and across their back. This creates leverage to break their grip and control their posture.
    • Switch Hips/Posting: If the opponent secures the leg but you are not fully destabilized, quickly "switch hips" by turning your hips towards their entry. Post your free hand on the mat to regain balance and potentially step over their attacking leg.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do a Single Leg Takedown in BJJ?

    Knee pain often stems from improper knee alignment during the entry. If your lead knee collapses inward (valgus) as you drive forward, it puts excessive stress on the ACL and MCL. Ensure your lead knee tracks directly over your second toe, maintaining a straight line with your foot, and that your drive comes from your hips and core, not just your leg.

    Q: How can I effectively perform a Single Leg Takedown against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are crucial. Focus on getting your head and shoulder into their hip crease to break their base. Instead of trying to lift, drive your hips forward and slightly upward, using your momentum to pull them off balance. Securing a deep "collar grip" on their thigh is essential for control.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Single Leg Takedown if my opponent is standing in BJJ?

    The ideal time to attempt a single leg takedown is when your opponent is leaning forward or has overextended themselves. Look for opportunities when they are reaching for your legs, attempting their own takedown, or when you've successfully closed the distance and their base is compromised. Avoid attempting when they are stable and upright, as this makes it much harder to break their posture.

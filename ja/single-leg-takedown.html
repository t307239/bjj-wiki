Single Leg Takedown Technique for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This technique initiates from an open stance facing your opponent, aiming to secure and elevate one of their legs to disrupt their base and bring them to the mat.

Many white belts struggle by lunging forward with their head up, exposing their neck and failing to drive through their hips.

The core principle is to use your head as a wedge, drop your hips below your opponent's center of gravity, and drive forward with your entire body mass.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From an upright stance, establish a collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip on your opponent's left arm with your left hand.
    2. Step your left foot directly forward, placing it slightly wider than shoulder-width apart and slightly ahead of your opponent's right foot.
    3. Simultaneously, drop your right knee to the mat, aiming to place it directly behind your left heel, creating a stable tripod base.
    4. Drive your head into your opponent's chest, specifically against their sternum, using it as a wedge to initiate forward pressure.
    5. Secure a "whizzer" grip by reaching your right arm over your opponent's back and gripping your own left bicep or tricep, creating a strong connection.
    6. Keeping your head driven forward and your hips low, drive your entire body weight forward and slightly to your left, elevating your opponent's left leg.
    7. Continue the forward drive, shifting your weight onto your left foot while maintaining the elevation of their leg, guiding them to the mat.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Lunging forward with your head upright and disconnected from your opponent's body: This exposes your neck to chokes and can cause hyperextension of your cervical spine if your opponent postures up.
    • Dropping your knee directly onto a hard surface without proper padding or hip positioning: This can lead to direct knee cap impact injuries or ligament strains from awkward angles.
    • Attempting to "pull" the leg up with your arms instead of driving with your hips: This overloads your shoulder and elbow joints, risking rotator cuff tears or elbow hyperextension.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling: Practice the footwork, hip drop, and head drive motion without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Partner Stance and Grip Practice: Stand with a compliant partner, practice establishing grips and taking your stance. No movement. (30 reps)
    3. Controlled Entry and Hip Drive: Partner stands still. You practice the entry, hip drop, and head drive, stopping before leg elevation. (40 reps)
    4. Single Leg Entry with Light Lift: Partner offers minimal resistance. Practice the full entry and a light lift, focusing on hip drive. (25% resistance, 30 reps)
    5. Controlled Takedown Execution: Partner offers moderate resistance. Execute the takedown, focusing on the finish. (50% resistance, 20 reps)
    6. Live Rolling Application: Integrate the single leg takedown into live rolling, attempting it when the opportunity arises. (75-100% resistance, 5 attempts per round)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is posturing up from your guard, creating a gap.
    • When your opponent is reaching for your legs to pass your guard, offering an opening.
    • When your opponent takes a wide stance to pressure into you, creating an imbalance.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • "Whizzer" Defense: If your opponent attempts a single leg, immediately "whizzer" your right arm over their back, grip your own bicep, and drive your hips into them to block their forward momentum.
    • "Sprawl" Counter: If you feel the single leg attempt, explosively push your hips back and widen your base, placing your weight on your feet and lifting your hips high to break the opponent's grip and balance.
    • "Re-Defend and Circle Out": If the opponent secures the leg, immediately plant your free foot firmly, use your free arm to push against their head or shoulder, and circle your body away from the direction of the takedown.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try a single leg takedown in BJJ?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are dropping your knee directly onto the mat without proper hip alignment or sufficient forward drive. This can cause direct impact on the patella or strain the ligaments. Ensure your supporting leg's knee is positioned behind your heel, and your hips are driven forward and low, absorbing the impact through your entire body's momentum.

    Q: How can I do a single leg takedown against someone much bigger than me in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and proper biomechanics are crucial. Focus on getting your hips significantly lower than theirs before initiating the drive. Use your head as a firm wedge against their chest to create an immovable point of contact. The key is to drive forward with your entire body's mass, not to try and lift them, but to unbalance them by driving through their center of gravity.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a single leg takedown if I'm a white belt in BJJ?

    As a white belt, the best opportunities arise when your opponent creates an obvious opening, such as reaching for your legs to pass your guard or posturing up from your guard. Avoid attempting it when they have a solid base and are actively pressuring into you. Look for moments of slight imbalance or when they extend a leg towards you.

Master the Single Leg Takedown in BJJ: A White Belt Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Single Leg Takedown is initiated from a standing position, aiming to off-balance and bring your opponent to the mat by controlling one of their legs.

Many white belts struggle due to fear of falling or an incomplete understanding of weight transfer, leading to lunging and poor structure.

The key is to penetrate deeply with your lead leg and drive forward, using your opponent's own weight against them.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Stance: Assume a balanced fighting stance, feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, and hips low.
    2. Penetration Step: Step forward with your lead leg, placing it outside your opponent's lead leg, aiming for your knee to be roughly aligned with their ankle.
    3. Head Position: Drive your head to the outside of your opponent's torso, creating a strong shoulder-to-hip connection.
    4. Grip: Secure a "collar-and-bicep" grip on the opponent's lapel and the bicep of their lead arm, or a "double-collar" grip if lapels are accessible.
    5. Underhook (Optional but Recommended): If possible, secure an underhook on your opponent's lead arm to further control their posture and prevent them from easily turning in.
    6. Hip Angle and Drive: Angle your hips inward, tucking your rear leg behind your opponent's lead leg. Drive forward from your hips and the balls of your feet, not by lifting.
    7. Weight Transfer: Transfer your weight forward and slightly to your opponent's side, using your head and shoulder pressure to destabilize them.
    8. Finish: As your opponent begins to fall, maintain control of the leg and drive them to the mat, ensuring your head stays up and your body remains connected.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Lunging with a straight knee: This can cause a direct hyperextension injury to the knee joint, specifically damaging the ACL or meniscus.
    • Correct: Penetrate with a bent knee, driving forward from the hips.
    • Grabbing the ankle without penetration: This leaves your base unstable and allows the opponent to easily spin out or sweep you, potentially straining your lower back as you pull.
    • Correct: Achieve deep penetration and maintain head-and-shoulder pressure.
    • Dropping your head to the mat: This exposes your neck and head to strikes or chokes, and compromises your ability to drive forward effectively, leading to a loss of balance.
    • Correct: Keep your head up and drive through your shoulders and hips.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling (0% resistance): Practice the penetration step, head position, and hip drive motion without a partner. 10 reps each side.
    2. Partnered Static Drilling (0% resistance): Partner stands still. Practice the penetration, grip acquisition, and hip drive. 5 reps each side.
    3. Partnered Light Resistance (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to practice the drive and sweep. 5 reps each side.
    4. Controlled Entry Drilling (50% resistance): Partner initiates a light tie-up. You practice recognizing the opportunity and executing the takedown. 5 reps each side.
    5. Live Takedown Exchange (75% resistance): Focus solely on takedown attempts within a designated area. Partner defends realistically but doesn't actively counter sweep. 3 rounds, 2 minutes each.
    6. Live Rolling (90% resistance): Integrate the single leg takedown into live sparring, aiming to use it when the opportunity arises. 5 minute rounds, focus on realistic application.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up from your guard, creating an opportunity to attack their base.
    • During a collar-and-sleeve tie-up where you can break their posture and step in.
    • When your opponent attempts a takedown and you can sprawl effectively, then transition to a single leg.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Sprawl and Turn: When the opponent penetrates, react by sprawling your hips back and turning your hips away, creating a "frame" with your lead leg to prevent them from securing the leg.
    • Whizzer (Overhook): Secure an overhook on the opponent's lead arm and drive your shoulder into their head and upper back, using your body weight to disrupt their drive.
    • Spin Behind: As the opponent drives forward, use your hips and a quick step to move behind them, often leading to a rear-naked choke or back control.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt after attempting a single leg takedown?

    Knee pain after a single leg often stems from "lunging" with a straight lead leg. This forces the knee joint to absorb impact and can lead to hyperextension, damaging the ACL or meniscus. Always ensure your lead knee is bent upon penetration, and drive forward from your hips, not by extending your leg.

    Q: How do I perform a single leg takedown against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on deep penetration and head pressure. Your goal is to get under their center of gravity. Drive your head into their hip/rib cage and use your shoulder to create a "lever" effect. Ensure your lead leg is deep, almost parallel to their supporting leg, and drive forward explosively from your hips.

    Q: Should I grab the ankle or the thigh for a single leg takedown?

    For white belts, it's biomechanically safer and more effective to aim for a "high" single leg, gripping the opponent's thigh just above the knee. This allows you to maintain better posture and control their hips. Gripping the ankle often requires you to bend too far forward, compromising your base and leading to potential back or knee injuries.

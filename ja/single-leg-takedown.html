This technique is initiated from a standing position when an opponent's legs are within reach, aiming to bring them to the mat.

White belts often struggle by lunging forward without establishing proper control or weight distribution.

The core principle is using your hips to drive through their base, not just pulling their leg.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Initiate with a collar grip (right hand on opponent's left collar) and a sleeve grip (left hand on opponent's right sleeve).
    2. Step your right foot forward, angling your hips to be perpendicular to your opponent's stance, creating a "T" shape.
    3. Lower your level by bending your knees, keeping your back straight and chest up.
    4. Drive your right shoulder into the opponent's chest, maintaining a strong base with your feet shoulder-width apart.
    5. Secure a "whizzer" grip by placing your left arm over their back, hooking your hand under their armpit.
    6. Simultaneously, reach down with your right hand and grip their right ankle or pant leg just above the ankle.
    7. Drive forward with your hips and legs, pulling the opponent's ankle towards your groin while pushing their upper body away with your shoulder and whizzer.
    8. Maintain forward pressure and lower your right knee to the mat as you complete the takedown, guiding them down.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Lunging forward with a straight back and head up: This can lead to hyperextension of the knee or ankle on the leg being attacked, as your weight is not properly supported.
    • Grabbing the opponent's foot with an extended leg: This puts your own knee at risk of twisting or buckling if the opponent resists or moves suddenly.
    • Attempting to lift the opponent's leg high: This often results in losing balance and falling backward, potentially injuring your own back or neck from the impact.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo movement: Practice the hip angle and footwork without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Shadow drilling: Execute the entire takedown motion against an imaginary opponent, focusing on hip drive and grip transitions. (50 reps)
    3. Controlled grip fighting: With a partner standing still, practice securing grips and moving into the "T" position. (25% resistance, 20 reps)
    4. Static takedown execution: Partner stands still, you execute the takedown with controlled pressure. (50% resistance, 20 reps)
    5. Resisted takedown attempts: Partner offers light resistance, allowing you to practice driving through. (75% resistance, 15 reps)
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the single leg takedown during rolling, focusing on timing and commitment. (100% resistance, 5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is in a wide, staggered stance with one leg forward.
    • During a break in combat where opponents are facing each other at close range.
    • When the opponent overextends a strike or forward movement.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • "Sprawl": Immediately drop your hips and chest to the mat, extending your legs backward to flatten your opponent and break their grip.
    • "Whizzer Counter": If the opponent has a whizzer, quickly circle your hips away and attempt to secure a "cross-face" grip to break their posture.
    • "Switch Step": If the opponent is driving forward aggressively, perform a quick "switch step" with your feet, bringing your attacked leg back and stepping your other leg forward to transition to a "guillotine choke" or "double leg takedown" defense.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do a single leg takedown in BJJ?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are lunging forward with a straight leg and are not properly driving with your hips. This puts excessive rotational or axial load on the knee joint. To fix this, focus on bending both knees deeply, keeping your chest up, and driving your hips forward into the opponent's torso as you secure the ankle grip, ensuring your supporting leg is bent and stable.

    Q: How can I successfully perform a single leg takedown against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, your goal is to use angles and speed to disrupt their base. Focus on a low penetration step, ensuring your lead knee is outside their hip line and your hips are lower than theirs. Secure a tight ankle grip and drive your shoulder into their chest to off-balance them, using your body weight and momentum to complete the takedown rather than relying solely on brute strength.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a single leg takedown in a BJJ match?

    The optimal time to attempt a single leg takedown is when your opponent is in a slightly off-balanced or staggered stance, often after they have attempted a forward movement or strike. Look for moments when their weight is not evenly distributed, or when they have a leg extended forward that you can isolate. Avoid attempting it when they are perfectly balanced and braced.

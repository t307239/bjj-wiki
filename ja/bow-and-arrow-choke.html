This choke is typically applied from the back mount position when the opponent is turtled or defending their neck. It aims to restrict blood flow to the brain by compressing the carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of precise body control and grip fighting, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury. They focus on raw strength rather than leverage.

The key insight is creating a fulcrum with your shin against their hip while simultaneously isolating their collar and arm for maximum leverage and choking pressure.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish back mount with hooks in. Secure the opponent's collar with your right hand (if applying from their left side), gripping deep at the fabric's base.
    2. With your left hand, secure the opponent's sleeve, specifically the wrist area, to control their posture and prevent them from posturing up.
    3. Your right shin should be placed across the opponent's hip, perpendicular to their spine, acting as a pivot point.
    4. Rotate your hips to the left, driving your right shin into their hip with upward pressure, creating a strong base and leverage.
    5. Simultaneously, pull the collar grip towards your chest and slightly upwards, while the sleeve grip pulls their arm across their body, tightening the choke.
    6. Arch your back, bringing your chest closer to the opponent's back, further increasing the tension on the choke.
    7. Maintain a strong hip angle, ensuring your pelvis remains elevated and driving into their hip to prevent them from escaping your base.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying excessive upward force with the shin directly into the opponent's lower back can strain their lumbar spine and potentially cause disc compression.
    • Grabbing the opponent's gi lapel too loosely and attempting to yank the head downwards can lead to neck hyperextension, risking cervical ligament damage.
    • Rushing the setup without establishing a solid base and proper grip isolation allows the opponent to turn into you, potentially leading to knee or ankle injuries for both parties.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the body positioning and hip rotation without a partner. Focus on placing the shin on the hip and rotating. (50 reps)
    2. Mirror drilling: With a partner providing passive resistance, practice establishing the collar and sleeve grips and performing the hip rotation. (25 reps per side)
    3. Grip isolation drill: Focus solely on securing the collar and sleeve grips with a compliant partner, ensuring the correct grip depth and tightness. (30 reps per side)
    4. Shin-to-hip pressure drill: With a partner in a turtle position, practice driving your shin into their hip and rotating your hips, feeling the leverage. (20 reps per side)
    5. Controlled submission drill: Apply the Bow and Arrow Choke on a fully compliant partner, focusing on smooth execution and feeling the choke. (50% resistance, 20 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling simulation: Apply the Bow and Arrow Choke in a controlled sparring session, focusing on timing and precision rather than immediate submission. (75% resistance, 5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent turtled while you have back control.
    • Opponent is attempting to peel your hooks off while you are on their back.
    • Opponent is trying to turn into you from side control and you can transition to their back.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Escape the choke by turning your head away from the choking arm and using your free hand to create space between your neck and their arm.
    • If they are setting up the choke, immediately widen your base and drive your hips forward to break their shin-to-hip connection and relieve pressure.
    • If they have the grips, try to peel their collar grip off by creating a strong frame with your forearm against their bicep and shrugging your shoulder.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Bow and Arrow Choke?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you grip the opponent's sleeve too loosely or far up their arm. This forces your wrist to bend at an unnatural angle to compensate for the lack of control. Instead, grip the opponent's wrist firmly, keeping your forearm parallel to the mat, to maintain a strong lever. This prevents excessive wrist flexion and distributes the pressure across your forearm.

    Q: How do I Bow and Arrow Choke a bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise body mechanics are paramount. Focus on sinking your collar grip deep and securing their wrist tightly to prevent them from creating frames. Ensure your shin is firmly planted on their hip and use your entire body weight to drive into that fulcrum. Elevate your hips significantly and arch your back to maximize the choking pressure, rather than relying on brute strength.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate the Bow and Arrow Choke?

    The Bow and Arrow Choke is most effective when the opponent is turtled or actively trying to escape your back control. This is when their posture is compromised, and their neck is exposed. It's also an excellent option when they attempt to turn into you from a bad position, as you can use their momentum to transition to their back and secure the choke. Avoid attempting it if they have a strong, upright posture.

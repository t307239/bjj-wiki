Ezekiel Choke: The White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Ezekiel Choke is a powerful choking technique typically initiated from closed guard or side control, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body awareness and a tendency to rely on brute strength rather than leverage and precise mechanics.

Success hinges on creating a tight, internal frame with your forearm and the opponent's gi sleeve, effectively compressing their carotid arteries.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish an "S" grip with your right hand: thumb inside the gi sleeve, fingers wrapped around the forearm itself, creating a "fist" inside the sleeve.
    2. Your left hand then grips your own right wrist, with your left thumb pointing upwards, creating a strong "bicycle grip" or "power grip" on your right forearm.
    3. Angle your hips to your right, creating space and lifting your hips slightly off the mat, bringing your body closer to your opponent's left side.
    4. Drive your right forearm (the one inside the sleeve) across the opponent's throat, ensuring it makes firm contact with their carotid artery.
    5. Simultaneously, drive your left forearm (the one gripping your wrist) underneath their chin, pressing it against the front of your own right forearm.
    6. Maintain a tight base by keeping your knees pulled towards your chest and your feet locked around your opponent's hips.
    7. Initiate the choke by squeezing your elbows together and driving your right forearm deeper into their neck, while your left forearm acts as a fulcrum, creating immense pressure.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying excessive upward force with your hips without proper neck compression: This can strain your lower back ligaments (lumbar spine) and cause acute pain.

    Correct: Focus on driving your forearm across the neck and squeezing elbows, not just lifting your hips.

    • Gripping your own wrist too loosely or with fingers pointing down: This compromises the "bicycle grip" and can lead to wrist hyperextension, potentially damaging the radiocarpal joint.

    Correct: Ensure your left thumb points up and you create a solid, compact grip on your right forearm.

    • Overextending your neck or arching your back excessively: This puts undue stress on the cervical spine and can lead to muscle strains or nerve impingement.

    Correct: Keep your head neutral and engage your core to maintain a stable posture.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the "S" grip and wrist grip on your own arm, focusing on the feel of the "bicycle grip" (50 reps).
    2. Solo drill: On the floor, mimic the hip angle and forearm placement without a partner, focusing on the correct hand and forearm positioning (50 reps).
    3. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner in your closed guard, practice establishing the grips and arm placement. Partner remains still. (10 reps per side).
    4. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to grip establishment. Focus on maintaining tightness. (10 reps per side).
    5. Partner drill (simulated choke): From closed guard, establish grips. Partner gently resists the choke. You apply pressure until they tap or you feel the submission is imminent. (20 reps total).
    6. Live rolling (controlled): Attempt the Ezekiel Choke from closed guard with a partner who understands the technique. Focus on correct application, not necessarily finishing. (3 rounds, 90% effort).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is posturing up from your closed guard, creating space between your bodies.
    • When your opponent tries to stack pass your guard, bringing their weight directly down on top of you.
    • When your opponent is flat on their back, allowing you to establish a strong hip angle and forearm penetration.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Grip Break (Opponent's Action): If the opponent feels the choke, they can try to break your "S" grip by prying your thumb and fingers apart with their free hand. You must maintain pressure and transition if the grip fails.
    • Shoulder Escape (Opponent's Action): The opponent can try to create space by shrugging their shoulders upwards or turning their head away from the choking arm. You counter by driving your choking arm deeper and adjusting your hip angle.
    • Hip Escape to Guard Pass (Opponent's Action): If the opponent successfully creates significant space and breaks your guard, they will attempt to hip escape and pass. You must immediately transition to a defensive guard or attempt a sweep.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Ezekiel Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often stems from an incorrect "bicycle grip" where your supporting hand is not firmly locked onto your choking forearm. If your supporting hand is too loose or angled incorrectly, your wrist can hyperextend under the pressure, straining the radiocarpal joint. Ensure your supporting thumb points upwards and you create a solid, compact unit with both forearms.

    Q: How can I effectively apply the Ezekiel Choke against a much larger and stronger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paramount. Do not rely on strength. Focus on creating a tight "S" grip and a secure "bicycle grip" to maximize your forearm's cutting action. Angle your hips to your side to create a fulcrum effect, and use your legs to control their posture and prevent them from posturing up, which often negates the choke's effectiveness.

    Q: The Ezekiel Choke is most effective when your opponent's head is slightly extended or neutral, allowing your forearm to press directly into the carotid artery. If they tuck their chin tightly, the choke becomes difficult. In this scenario, you can either transition to a triangle choke by bringing your leg over their shoulder, or attempt to "pop" their chin up by using your free hand to lift their head slightly before re-establishing the choke.

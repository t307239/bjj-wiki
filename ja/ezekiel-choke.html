Ezekiel Choke: A Biomechanically Precise BJJ Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Ezekiel Choke is a powerful submission typically applied from closed guard, aiming to constrict the carotid arteries. White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of precise grip placement and body mechanics.

The key insight is using your own arm as a fulcrum and your opponent's lapel as the cutting edge, rather than brute force.

Understanding this leverage is crucial for effective and safe application, even against larger opponents.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish closed guard, ensuring your hips are close to your opponent's hips. Your knees should be tight around their torso, creating a strong base.
    2. With your right hand, reach across your opponent's neck and grip their left lapel with your thumb inside the fold, creating a "thumb-in" grip.
    3. With your left hand, reach under your opponent's chin, palm facing up, and grip your own right wrist. This forms a "grip-on-grip" or "figure-four" grip.
    4. Slightly elevate your hips by pushing down with your feet on the mat, creating space to bring your opponent's head closer to your chest.
    5. Contract your chest muscles and pull your elbows towards your body, tightening the grip on your own wrist and pulling your opponent's lapel into their neck.
    6. At the same time, drive your hips forward into your opponent, compressing their upper body and neck against your chest.
    7. Maintain this pressure, ensuring your head is positioned to prevent your opponent from posturing up, and hold until submission or release.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying the choke by simply "pulling hard" with your arms: This can strain your shoulder joint (rotator cuff ligaments) or elbow joint (medial collateral ligament) due to excessive external rotation or valgus stress.

    The correct action is to use your chest and hip drive to create the choking pressure, with arm movement being secondary.

    • Gripping the lapel too loosely or with fingers exposed: This allows the lapel to slip, forcing you to compensate with excessive grip strength at the wrist, potentially leading to wrist sprains (scapholunate ligament).

    Ensure a deep, secure grip with your thumb inside the lapel, creating a solid anchor point.

    • Allowing your opponent to posture up by not controlling their head: This can lead to your own neck being hyperextended if you try to force the choke, causing cervical spine strain.

    Keep your head pressed against your opponent's shoulder or chest to maintain control and prevent posture.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the grip sequence and hip elevation with no resistance. Focus on the feeling of the lapel in your neck and the wrist-on-wrist connection. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Execute the entire sequence with a compliant partner, focusing on precise hip movement and chest compression. (20 reps)
    3. Light resistance drilling (25%): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the choke engage. Focus on maintaining your closed guard.
    4. Controlled resistance drilling (50%): Your partner attempts to defend lightly. Work on maintaining your grips and hip position as they try to create space.
    5. Moderate resistance drilling (75%): Your partner actively defends. Focus on adjusting your hips and maintaining pressure as they try to break your posture or grips.
    6. Live rolling (90-100%): Attempt the Ezekiel Choke when the opportunity arises in sparring. Do not force it; focus on positional control and timing.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is stuck in your closed guard and trying to posture up.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard and you can secure the lapel grip before they establish control.
    • When your opponent is on their back and you have established a strong closed guard with good hip control.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Posture Up and Grip Break: The opponent immediately breaks posture by driving their hips back and away, simultaneously using their free hand to break the grip on the lapel or wrist. This creates space and relieves pressure.
    • Ear Block: The opponent uses their free hand to block the pressure on their neck by placing their hand or forearm on their own carotid artery, preventing the choke from fully engaging. They then use this to create space to posture up.
    • Hip Escape and Frame Creation: The opponent immediately hips out to the side, creating an angle and using their arms to frame against your chest or hips, disrupting your hip-to-hip connection and allowing them to relieve pressure and escape.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Ezekiel Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain during the Ezekiel Choke often stems from gripping your own wrist too tightly with excessive ulnar deviation (bending the wrist inwards). This puts undue stress on the carpal ligaments. Instead, maintain a neutral or slightly extended wrist position and focus on using your forearm and bicep to create the choking pressure against your opponent's lapel, rather than solely relying on wrist strength.

    Q: How can a white belt like me effectively use the Ezekiel Choke against a bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, the Ezekiel Choke relies entirely on precise biomechanics, not strength. Focus on getting your hips very close to their hips to limit their posture. When you secure the lapel and wrist grip, use your chest to pull their head down and your hips to drive forward and slightly up. This hip drive compresses their upper body and makes the choke effective regardless of size difference.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt an Ezekiel Choke in BJJ, and what if my opponent defends by reaching for my arms?

    The Ezekiel Choke is best attempted when your opponent is somewhat stuck in your guard, especially if they are trying to create space or posture up. If they defend by reaching for your arms, this is often a sign they are uncomfortable with the lapel grip. Use this opportunity to maintain your grip, bring their head closer, and continue with the hip drive, as their defensive posture can sometimes make the choke even tighter.

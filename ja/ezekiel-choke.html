Ezekiel Choke: The White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Ezekiel Choke is a powerful strangulation technique typically applied from closed guard, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain via the carotid arteries.

Many white belts struggle because they rely on raw strength rather than precise body mechanics, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key insight is using your own arm as a fulcrum against your opponent's neck, amplified by your hip and shoulder pressure.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Start from closed guard, with your hips close to your opponent's hips.
    2. Grip: Insert your right forearm (for a right-handed choke) into your opponent's collar, palm facing upwards, fingers inside.
    3. Grip: With your left hand, grab the outside of your right forearm, creating a fist with your thumb pointing upwards.
    4. Posture: Your opponent will likely try to posture up; use this to your advantage by maintaining tight hip control.
    5. Rotation: Rotate your right wrist inwards (towards your opponent's sternum) while simultaneously pulling your left fist downwards.
    6. Pressure: Drive your hips forward and slightly upward, creating a wedge with your right forearm against their trachea/carotid arteries.
    7. Completion: Continue the inward wrist rotation and downward fist pull until your opponent taps.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect elbow flare: Extending your elbow excessively outwards when gripping can hyperextend the shoulder joint, risking rotator cuff tears.
    • Correct: Keep your elbow tucked close to your body, forming a tight angle.
    • Over-reliance on grip strength: Trying to squeeze the choke solely with hand strength can lead to forearm muscle fatigue and wrist sprains.
    • Correct: Use your entire body's leverage, especially hip and shoulder drive, to create the choke.
    • Neck cranking motion: Twisting your opponent's head excessively with your forearm can strain the cervical spine, leading to neck injuries.
    • Correct: Focus pressure directly on the carotid arteries and trachea, not on twisting the head.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo grip practice: Practice the grip formation on your own arm, focusing on hand placement and wrist angle. (10 reps each side).
    2. Partner grip and pressure drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner in closed guard, practice establishing the grips and feeling the forearm placement against the neck. (10 reps each side).
    3. Partner hip and arm movement drill (25% resistance): Practice the hip drive and arm rotation while your partner offers minimal resistance to posture. (10 reps each side).
    4. Controlled choke application drill (50% resistance): Apply the choke with controlled pressure, allowing your partner to tap early. Focus on the feeling of the choke. (10 reps each side).
    5. Simulated choke drill (75% resistance): Practice the full motion, but stop short of a submission, focusing on smooth transitions and body mechanics. (5 reps each side).
    6. Live rolling application (90% resistance): Attempt the Ezekiel choke during live rolling, focusing on timing and recognizing opportunities without forcing it.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent postures up slightly from closed guard, creating space for your arm insertion.
    • When your opponent's head is tilted slightly to the side, exposing the carotid artery.
    • When you have established strong hip control and can prevent them from easily posturing up.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Posture Up and Frame: The opponent can break the grip by creating a strong frame with their arms and posturing up, creating distance. This requires immediate disconnection of the grip.
    • Grip Break and Knee Shield: If the choke is being applied, the opponent can use their free hand to pry open the choking arm's grip and simultaneously use their knee as a shield to create space and defend.
    • Sweep or Transition: A proactive defense involves the opponent using the moment of the choke attempt to initiate a sweep or transition to a better position, disrupting the choke's setup.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Ezekiel Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often stems from excessive pronation (twisting inwards) of the forearm without proper support from the shoulder and hip. This places undue stress on the radioulnar joints. Ensure your thumb is pointing upwards on the attacking forearm and that you are driving with your hips and shoulders, using your entire body as a lever, not just your wrist.

    Q: How can a white belt effectively use the Ezekiel Choke against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on getting your hips as close to theirs as possible to minimize their size advantage. Instead of pushing, use their weight against them by driving your shoulder into their chest and rotating your hips to create the choke's pressure, rather than trying to 'pull' them into it.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Ezekiel Choke, and why do white belts get it wrong?

    The optimal time is when your opponent attempts to posture up, creating a slight gap. White belts often fail by trying to force the choke when the opponent is already flat, or by not having their hips close enough, making the arm insertion difficult and the choke ineffective. Focus on the setup and the precise moment of opportunity.

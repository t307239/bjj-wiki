Ezekiel Choke: The Ultimate BJJ White Belt Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Ezekiel Choke is a powerful lapel choke applied from various positions, most commonly closed guard. It aims to cut off blood flow to the brain by compressing the carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle because they rely on brute strength instead of leverage and precise hand placement. This leads to ineffective chokes and potential injury.

The core mechanical principle is creating a tight fulcrum with your forearm against the opponent's carotid artery, using your other arm's grip to drive that forearm inward.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, ensure your hips are tight to your opponent's hips, creating a stable base.
    2. Grip your own gi sleeve: place your non-choking hand's thumb inside your choking hand's sleeve, forming a "fist" shape.
    3. Insert your choking hand's knuckles (between thumb and index finger) into the opponent's gi collar, aiming for the trapezius muscle.
    4. Your choking forearm should lie across the opponent's neck, with your wrist bent towards your chest.
    5. Your non-choking hand's thumb should remain inside your sleeve, creating tension.
    6. To initiate the choke, drive your hips forward slightly while simultaneously pulling your choking forearm inward and down, compressing the carotid artery.
    7. Maintain a stable base by keeping your knees tucked and your weight balanced over your hips.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying excessive upward wrist pressure with your non-choking hand: This can cause hyperextension of your own wrist, leading to ligament damage (e.g., TFCC tear) or carpal tunnel syndrome. Instead, focus on driving your choking forearm inward.
    • Gripping the gi too shallowly or too deep in the collar: A shallow grip offers little pressure, while a deep grip can slip and allow the opponent to escape. The correct grip is on the trapezius area, just below the ear.
    • Attempting to "muscle" the choke by arching your back forcefully: This strains your spine and is biomechanically inefficient. The power comes from hip drive and forearm compression, not spinal hyperextension.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hand and grip placement on your own gi for 10 repetitions.
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): With a compliant partner in closed guard, practice the full motion of the choke, focusing on hip drive and forearm pressure for 5 repetitions per side.
    3. Light resistance drilling: Partner offers minimal resistance (e.g., hands lightly on your shoulders). Execute the choke for 5 repetitions per side.
    4. Moderate resistance drilling: Partner actively tries to defend by framing or creating space. Execute the choke for 3 repetitions per side.
    5. Controlled rolling (Ezekiel Choke only): Initiate rolling with a partner, with the sole objective of attempting the Ezekiel Choke from closed guard, for 3 minutes.
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the Ezekiel Choke as a natural progression of your game, with full resistance, for 5 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is turtled and you have control of their back.
    • When you have secured closed guard and your opponent is posturing up.
    • When your opponent is attempting to pass your guard and you can isolate an arm.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Elbow escape: If the choke is applied too loosely, the opponent can drive their chin down, tucking it inside the choking forearm, and then create space by bridging their hips away.
    • Posture break and grip release: The opponent can break the grip by reaching their own hand inside the choking forearm and pulling their sleeve away from your choking hand.
    • Head and arm escape: The opponent can turn their head away from the choke, creating a "hole" to relieve pressure, and then use their free arm to push away and escape.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do an Ezekiel Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often stems from excessive, uncontrolled flexion or extension of your own wrist. When you grip your sleeve, if you then pull your gripping hand upwards or twist your wrist unnaturally, you can strain the carpal ligaments. Focus on keeping your wrist in a neutral position and driving your choking forearm inward, using your hips for power, not your wrist.

    Q: How can I apply the Ezekiel Choke effectively against a much larger and stronger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise mechanics are paramount. Ensure your closed guard is tight, preventing them from posturing up. When applying the choke, focus on a deep collar grip and a firm sleeve grip. Instead of trying to "pull" them into the choke, drive your hips forward and use your body weight to create the crushing pressure with your forearm against their carotid. The smaller you can make their neck space, the more effective it will be.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to an Ezekiel Choke from side control in BJJ?

    The Ezekiel Choke is not a primary submission from side control. However, if your opponent bridges and you end up in a "grapevine" position or they attempt to shrimp away, you might be able to transition. As they try to create space, their head might become accessible for a collar grip. A more common transition is from north-south, where you can secure a collar grip and then drive your forearm across their neck. Always prioritize control before attempting the choke.

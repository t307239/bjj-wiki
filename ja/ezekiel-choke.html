Ezekiel Choke: A Biomechanically Accurate BJJ Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Ezekiel Choke is a powerful lapel choke typically initiated from closed guard or side control. It aims to constrict blood flow to the brain by compressing the carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle by overemphasizing strength or incorrect grip placement, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key is to create a fulcrum with your forearm and use your opponent's neck as the resistance point, rather than just pulling.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish a standard cross-collar grip with your right hand, thumb inside the lapel, fingers gripping the fabric near the opponent's sternum.
    2. Insert your left forearm underneath your right forearm, palm facing upwards, and grip your own right bicep with your left hand.
    3. Ensure your left forearm rests against the side of your opponent's neck, creating a fulcrum point.
    4. Lower your hips slightly, bringing your pelvis closer to your opponent's hips, reducing the distance.
    5. Rotate your torso counter-clockwise, simultaneously driving your right forearm inwards towards your opponent's trachea.
    6. Arch your back and extend your chest slightly, creating opposing forces with your grip and forearm pressure.
    7. Maintain a stable base by keeping your knees tight and your feet interlocked behind your opponent's back.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Gripping the opponent's lapel too loosely or too high on the neck: This can lead to the lapel slipping, causing no choke and potential strain on your own wrist ligaments as you try to compensate.
    • Forcing the choke by pulling with both arms outwards: This inefficient movement can hyperextend your elbow joints, risking ligament tears (e.g., UCL).
    • Not creating a proper fulcrum with the forearm: Trying to 'squeeze' with just your hands will result in ineffective pressure and can lead to wrist pain and carpal tunnel irritation.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice establishing the grips and forearm placement on a dummy or pillow, focusing on the correct bicep grip and forearm position. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a willing partner, practice the full sequence of grips and body movement from closed guard, focusing on smooth transitions. (20 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the pressure build and make adjustments to your body mechanics. (25% resistance, 15 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (controlled resistance): Your partner actively tries to defend but without excessive force, allowing you to practice finishing the choke. (50% resistance, 10 reps per side)
    5. Simulated rolling: Engage in rolling with a partner, attempting the Ezekiel Choke from closed guard when the opportunity arises, with the understanding to stop if the choke is locked in. (75% resistance, 5 attempts per round)
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the Ezekiel Choke attempt into your regular rolling sessions, understanding that successful submissions will require timing and adaptation. (90-100% resistance, as opportunities arise)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have a tight closed guard and the opponent is postured up, creating space to insert your forearm.
    • When the opponent is defending your other submissions (e.g., armbar, triangle) and leaves their neck exposed.
    • During a guard pass attempt where the opponent is pressing forward and their neck is accessible.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Grip Release and Posture Up: Immediately break the collar grip with your free hand and push your opponent's head away, creating space and regaining your posture.
    • Elbow Escape: If the forearm is trapped, drive your elbow forward and into your opponent's chest, creating an escape angle and disrupting the choke.
    • Hip Escape and Guard Recovery: If the choke is being applied, hip escape to create space, then use your knees to frame and recover your guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Ezekiel Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you rely solely on gripping the lapel with your fingers and pulling outwards. The Ezekiel Choke relies on your forearm acting as a fulcrum against the opponent's neck. Ensure your left forearm is firmly against their neck and you are gripping your own bicep. Then, drive your right forearm inwards, creating pressure through your bicep and forearm, not just your wrist.

    Q: How can a white belt effectively use the Ezekiel Choke against a bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on biomechanical leverage rather than brute strength. Ensure your closed guard is tight, preventing them from posturing up. When inserting your forearm, get it as deep as possible against their neck. Then, use your entire body by lowering your hips and arching your back to create a powerful fulcrum, rather than just pulling with your arms.

    Q: When is the best time to set up an Ezekiel Choke in BJJ, and what if my opponent defends?

    The Ezekiel Choke is most effective when your opponent is postured up and their head is relatively close to your chest, allowing for a deep forearm insertion. If they defend by tucking their chin or posturing up further, do not force the choke. Instead, use their defensive movement to transition to another submission like a triangle choke or armbar, or reset your guard.

Ezekiel Choke: The White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Ezekiel Choke is a powerful submission typically initiated from closed guard or mount, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain via the carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of control and understanding of leverage, leading to frustration and potential injury.

Mastering the Ezekiel Choke hinges on creating a tight "figure four" grip with your arms and using your body weight to apply consistent pressure.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish a "figure four" grip: insert your right forearm (palm up) into your opponent's left collar, gripping your own right bicep with your left hand (palm down).
    2. Your left forearm should be positioned across your opponent's throat, creating the choking mechanism.
    3. Keep your hips elevated slightly, ensuring your pelvis is angled towards your opponent's head, not their feet.
    4. Maintain a narrow base with your feet, crossed tightly behind your opponent's back to prevent them from posturing up.
    5. Apply pressure by driving your right forearm (the one in the collar) forward and downwards, simultaneously pulling your left arm (the one gripping your bicep) back.
    6. Use your chest and upper body to lean into your opponent, transferring weight downwards and slightly forward.
    7. As you apply the choke, your shoulders should be slightly rounded forward, creating a concave shape that enhances the pressure.
    8. The submission is achieved when the opponent taps due to the pressure on their carotid arteries.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly framing with your arms: Extending your elbows fully while trying to "pull hard" can hyperextend your elbow joint, potentially damaging the ulnar collateral ligament (UCL).

    Correct: Keep a slight bend in your elbows and focus on driving your forearm into the collar and your other forearm across the neck, using your torso for leverage.

    • Forcing the choke with neck cranking: Trying to "crank" the submission by rotating your head or neck can strain cervical vertebrae and ligaments.

    Correct: The choke is generated by the forearm pressure and body weight, not by twisting your neck or their head.

    • Over-extending your back: Arching your back excessively to create leverage can put undue stress on the lumbar spine, leading to muscle strains or disc issues.

    Correct: Maintain a neutral spine and use hip elevation and chest pressure to drive the submission.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the grip sequence (figure four) and body positioning without a partner. Focus on the feeling of the forearm placement. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the full mechanics of the choke from closed guard, focusing on smooth transitions and weight transfer. (20 reps)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the choke apply and practice maintaining control. (30 reps per side)
    4. Controlled drilling (50% resistance): Your partner actively tries to defend but allows the choke to be applied to a point where you can feel the pressure. Focus on adjusting your body mechanics to overcome light defenses. (40 reps)
    5. Live rolling (75% resistance): Engage in sparring where your partner defends realistically but you have opportunities to initiate the Ezekiel Choke. Focus on timing and recognizing submission opportunities. (5 minutes)
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Full sparring, aiming to implement the Ezekiel Choke when the opportunity arises. Focus on finishing the submission against active defense. (10 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up in your closed guard, creating a space to insert your arm.
    • When you have established a strong cross-collar grip and can isolate one of their arms.
    • Immediately after a failed sweep or guard pass attempt, when your opponent is momentarily off-balance.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • "Head and Arm Escape" (when applied from mount): If the opponent is mounted and you feel the Ezekiel, turn your head away from the choking arm and bridge your hips to create space, then attempt to drive your opponent over your head.
    • "Grip Break" (when applied from guard): If the opponent is in your guard, try to break their "figure four" grip by prying their gripping hand open with your free hand or by shucking their arm off your bicep. Simultaneously, try to create space by pushing their hips away.
    • "Stacking Defense": If the opponent attempts to stack you, immediately tuck your chin and try to turn into them, using your hips to drive them over your head and relieve pressure from the choke.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Ezekiel Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs when your wrist is bent backwards (hyperextended) during the grip. This happens if you are relying too much on wrist strength to apply the choke. Instead, focus on keeping your wrist straight and using your forearm to drive into their collar and your bicep to pull back, generating power from your shoulder and torso.

    Q: How can a white belt effectively use the Ezekiel Choke against a much larger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are key. Focus on keeping your hips tight to their body in closed guard to prevent them from posturing up. When you establish the Ezekiel grip, ensure your forearm is deep in their collar and your other arm is securely gripping your bicep. Use your body weight by driving your chest into them and tilting your hips to create the choking pressure, rather than trying to overpower them.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Ezekiel Choke, and when should I avoid it?

    The Ezekiel Choke is best attempted when your opponent is postured up in your closed guard, creating a gap for your arm. It's also effective when they are turtling or if you've isolated an arm. Avoid it when your opponent has a very strong base, is actively pressuring your head down, or if you are in a position where you cannot establish a secure grip and body control, as this can lead to them escaping or countering.

The Ezekiel Choke is a powerful submission typically initiated from closed guard or side control, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain by compressing the carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle due to improper grip and lack of understanding of the required body mechanics, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key to a successful Ezekiel Choke lies in creating a tight frame with your forearm and fist, leveraging your body weight to drive into the opponent's neck.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a Closed Guard: Secure your opponent's hips with your legs, creating a closed guard position. Maintain a hip angle of approximately 90 degrees relative to your opponent.
    2. Grip the Sleeve: With your right hand, reach inside your opponent's left sleeve and grasp the fabric firmly, ensuring your fist is inside the sleeve, near the wrist.
    3. Insert the Fist: With your left hand, reach across your opponent's chest and insert your fist into your own right sleeve, creating a "figure-four" grip. Your left palm should face your opponent.
    4. Create the Frame: Position your left forearm across your opponent's upper chest, close to their collarbone. This forms the primary structural support for the choke.
    5. Hip Elevation: Slightly elevate your hips off the mat, shifting your weight forward and creating tension through your core.
    6. Drive Forward: Drive your hips forward into your opponent, simultaneously pulling your right elbow down and back, and pushing your left forearm into their neck.
    7. Tighten the Grip: As you drive forward, continue to tighten the figure-four grip, ensuring your left fist is pressing into your right bicep and your right fist is deep within the sleeve.
    8. Complete the Choke: Maintain forward pressure and the tight grip until your opponent taps.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect grip insertion: Grasping the opponent's sleeve with your knuckles pointing outwards. This can cause hyperextension of your own wrist joint, leading to ligament damage (sprains).
    • Over-reliance on arm strength: Trying to crank the choke with only your arms. This places excessive strain on your shoulder joint and rotator cuff, potentially causing tears or impingement.
    • Insufficient hip drive: Failing to use your body weight and hips to generate pressure. This leads to a weak choke and can result in your opponent easily escaping or counter-attacking.
    • Forcing the choke with neck rotation: Trying to twist your opponent's head. This can injure their cervical spine and your own neck due to uncontrolled leverage.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo grip practice: Practice forming the figure-four grip on your own arm, focusing on the tightness and hand placement. (50 reps).
    2. Static partner drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner in closed guard, practice establishing the grips and creating the frame without any pressure. (10 sets of 3 reps).
    3. Controlled pressure drill (25% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the hip drive and forward movement, allowing them to slightly resist. Focus on maintaining the grip and frame. (10 sets of 3 reps).
    4. Gradual resistance drill (50% resistance): Increase the partner's resistance slightly, allowing them to defend a little. Focus on maintaining your structure and applying the choke with controlled hip pressure. (10 sets of 3 reps).
    5. Advanced resistance drill (75% resistance): Increase the partner's defensive effort. Focus on smooth transitions and maintaining the choke's integrity against active defense. (5 sets of 3 reps).
    6. Live rolling (90-100% resistance): Attempt the Ezekiel Choke during live rolling, focusing on proper timing and execution. Do not force the submission if it's not there. (Incorporate into 5-10 minutes of rolling).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • From Closed Guard: When the opponent's posture is slightly broken and they are close.
    • From Side Control (as a transition): When the opponent tries to shrimp or create space, and you can quickly secure the sleeve grip.
    • When opponent attempts to pass your guard: If they reach inside your guard, their sleeve is often accessible for the grip.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Posture Up and Escape Sleeve Grip: The opponent can immediately stand up, breaking the guard and pulling their arm free. This is the most fundamental defense.
    • Elbow Escape: The opponent can drive their elbow down and inwards, creating space to escape the choke's pressure and re-establish guard or posture.
    • Hip Escape and Frame: The opponent can shrimp their hips away and use their free arm to create a frame against your shoulder or chest, preventing you from driving forward.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Ezekiel Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs when your fist is inserted incorrectly into your own sleeve, causing your wrist to bend at an unnatural angle. Ensure your left fist is inserted palm-up into your right sleeve, and your left forearm is creating the frame across the opponent's neck, not your wrist. This distributes the pressure correctly and protects your wrist.

    Q: How can I execute the Ezekiel Choke against a much bigger and stronger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are crucial. Focus on breaking their posture and securing the sleeve grip first. Instead of relying on raw strength, use your hips to drive forward and create the choking pressure. Ensure your left forearm is firmly established across their neck to create a solid frame that prevents them from posturing up.

    Q: When is the best time to try the Ezekiel Choke in BJJ, and what if my opponent blocks my arm?

    The Ezekiel Choke is most effective when the opponent's posture is slightly compromised or when they are reaching into your guard. If they block your arm, don't force it. Instead, use the opportunity to transition to another submission like a triangle choke or an armbar if their arm is extended. Maintaining multiple submission threats keeps your opponent guessing.

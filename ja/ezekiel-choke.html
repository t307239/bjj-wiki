エゼキエルチョーク BJJ: 白帯完全ガイド

The Ezekiel Choke is a powerful Gi choke applied from various positions, most commonly closed guard, to restrict blood flow to the brain. White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of grip pressure and body alignment.

The key insight is using your own forearm as a blade and your fist as a fulcrum, creating leverage without relying on brute strength.

This choke targets the carotid arteries, leading to a swift tap-out when applied correctly with proper body mechanics.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: From closed guard, insert your right thumb into the left collar of your opponent's Gi, gripping deeply. Your left hand then slides its thumb inside your right fist, gripping the fabric of your right sleeve.
    2. Arm Placement: Bring your right elbow to your chest, creating a tight seal. Your left forearm should cradle your opponent's neck, with your left fist positioned under their chin.
    3. Body Alignment: Lie back slightly, keeping your hips elevated. Your body should be perpendicular to your opponent's torso, creating a strong base.
    4. Initiate Pressure: Begin to straighten your right arm, driving your right forearm (the blade) across their carotid artery.
    5. Leverage Point: Simultaneously, pull your opponent's head towards your left fist using your left forearm and wrist.
    6. Choke Application: As your right arm straightens and your left arm pulls, your right fist (acting as a fulcrum) will intensify the pressure on the carotid artery.
    7. Completion: Maintain a stable base and consistent pressure until your opponent taps. Do not arch your back excessively; focus on controlled extension.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Grip Application: Gripping too loosely on the sleeve or collar allows the opponent to escape easily and can lead to wrist strain. The damage occurs to the wrist's carpal bones and ligaments due to uncontrolled twisting forces.
    • Excessive Back Arching: Arching your back to generate power puts immense stress on the lumbar spine, risking disc herniation or muscle pulls. The correct action is controlled forearm extension and torso engagement.
    • Head Snapping: Jerking the opponent's head forcefully can cause neck whiplash or strain on the cervical spine. Maintain a controlled pulling motion with the forearm against the neck.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Grip Practice: Practice forming the Ezekiel Choke grip without a partner. Focus on thumb placement and sleeve grip. (50 reps)
    2. Static Partner Grip Application: With a compliant partner in your closed guard, practice forming the grips and achieving the correct arm and body alignment. (20 reps per side)
    3. Controlled Choke Execution (0% Resistance): From closed guard, apply the Ezekiel Choke with minimal resistance from your partner. Focus on the feeling of the forearm on the carotid. (15 reps per side)
    4. Light Resistance (25% Resistance): Your partner offers slight resistance to the choke, allowing you to feel how to overcome it with proper mechanics. (15 reps per side)
    5. Moderate Resistance (50% Resistance): Your partner actively tries to defend by creating space or posturing up slightly. (10 reps per side)
    6. Live Rolling (75-90% Resistance): Apply the Ezekiel Choke during live rolling, focusing on timing and recognizing opportunities. (Practice for 5 minutes in rolling rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent is posturing down inside your closed guard, creating a tight space.
    • When an opponent attempts to stack you from closed guard, bringing their head close.
    • When an opponent opens your guard and you can quickly secure the collar and sleeve grips before they establish a strong base.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Defending the Grip: As the opponent attempts to secure the collar grip, use your free hand to peel their hand off the collar or to block their arm from reaching deep. Maintain a tight grip on their sleeve with your other hand to prevent them from isolating it.
    • Creating Space/Posture Up: If the choke is being applied, immediately try to create space by pushing their shoulder away or by posting your foot on their hip. Then, posture up to relieve the pressure and break the grip.
    • Turning into the Choke: Some practitioners will turn their head into the choking arm. This can sometimes relieve pressure by shifting the carotid artery slightly. However, a well-applied Ezekiel Choke will still be effective.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Ezekiel Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain typically occurs when you try to force the choke by twisting your wrist excessively or by gripping the sleeve too loosely, allowing your wrist to bend unnaturally. Ensure your right arm is straight and the pressure comes from your forearm against their neck, with your left hand providing a stable fulcrum. Focus on straightening the right arm and driving the forearm across, rather than a wrist-based twist.

    Q: How can a smaller white belt effectively use the Ezekiel Choke against a much larger opponent in BJJ?

    For a smaller practitioner, the Ezekiel Choke relies entirely on precise biomechanics and grip control, not strength. Focus on getting a deep collar grip with your thumb and a tight sleeve grip with your other hand. Lie back and elevate your hips to create a strong base, then use your body weight and the extension of your right arm, combined with pulling your opponent's head with your left forearm, to generate the choke pressure. Leverage is key.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Ezekiel Choke, and what are common mistakes white belts make regarding timing?

    The Ezekiel Choke is best attempted when the opponent is in a lower posture, especially when they are close and have their head within reach, such as when they are trying to pass your guard or when you have them in your closed guard. A common timing mistake is attempting it when the opponent is postured high, making it difficult to secure the necessary grips and leverage. Another mistake is trying to force the choke before the grips are fully secured and aligned.

Cross Collar Choke: The Essential BJJ Technique for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This technique is primarily initiated from the Closed Guard position, aiming to submit the opponent by restricting blood flow to the brain.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body awareness and a tendency to rely on raw strength rather than leverage.

The core principle is to create a fulcrum with your hips and use your arms to create opposing forces on the carotid arteries.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a Closed Guard: Sit with your back on the mat, knees bent, and feet crossed behind the opponent's hips.
    2. Right Collar Grip: Place your right hand deep into the opponent's left lapel, thumb inside, approximately 1-2 inches from the center of the neck.
    3. Left Collar Grip: Place your left hand deep into the opponent's right lapel, thumb inside, at a similar depth as the right grip.
    4. Hip Elevation: Drive your hips upwards, creating a slight arch in your lower back, lifting the opponent's base off the mat.
    5. Arm Extension and Pull: Extend your arms, pulling both collars towards your chest while simultaneously driving your hips forward.
    6. Leg Squeeze: Squeeze your knees together, tightening your guard around the opponent's torso.
    7. Completion: Maintain pressure, ensuring your elbows are tucked, and the opponent's chin is pressed into your chest.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Gripping too shallowly: This can lead to the opponent easily escaping the grip, causing your fingers to hyperextend and potentially injuring the metacarpals or phalanges.

    Correct: Ensure your thumb is inside the lapel and you are gripping deep, close to the neck.

    • Yanking with the arms without hip drive: This strains the shoulder joint and rotator cuff tendons, as it relies on isolated arm strength rather than full-body leverage.

    Correct: Drive your hips up and forward, using your entire body to generate the choking pressure.

    • Allowing the opponent to posture up excessively: This can result in your arms being pulled upwards, straining the elbow joint and biceps tendon.

    Correct: Maintain a tight closed guard and use your legs to control the opponent's posture.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Grip Practice: Practice establishing the deep collar grips on a dummy or a willing partner without any movement. (50 reps)
    2. Guard Closure and Grip Placement: From a seated position, practice closing your guard and establishing the grips. (30 reps)
    3. Hip Drive and Arm Extension Drill: With the guard closed and grips set, practice the hip drive and simultaneous arm extension motion. (40 reps)
    4. Static Position Hold (25% resistance): Partner holds a neutral posture while you practice the full sequence from guard closure to completion. (10 reps per side)
    5. Controlled Movement Drill (50% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to practice the hip lift and pull. (15 reps per side)
    6. Live Rolling Application (75% resistance): Attempt the Cross Collar Choke in a rolling scenario with a cooperative partner. (5 rounds x 2 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is posturing down towards you in Closed Guard.
    • When the opponent is attempting to break your guard by posturing up.
    • Immediately after successfully sweeping or transitioning to Closed Guard.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Break (Elbow-Knee Escape): If the opponent feels the choke, they can attempt to break your guard by bringing their knees to their elbows and "exploding" their hips out.
    • Grip Release and Posture Up: The opponent can try to peel your grips off by using their hands to push your arms away and immediately regain posture.
    • Flattening Out: The opponent can try to flatten their back against the mat, reducing the space for your hips to drive and the choke to be effective.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Cross Collar Choke from Closed Guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you are gripping the lapel too flatly or not using your thumb correctly. Ensure your thumb is inserted deep within the lapel, creating a firm grip that uses the strength of your forearm and hand, not just the wrist joint. Avoid bending your wrist backward; keep it neutral or slightly flexed, and focus on driving your elbows towards your body to create a stronger pulling force.

    Q: How can I effectively apply the Cross Collar Choke against a much bigger opponent from Closed Guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paramount. Focus on timing your hip drive and using your legs to control their posture; don't let them establish a strong base. When gripping, aim for the deepest part of the collar, and use your arms to pull their head down towards your chest. Squeeze your knees tightly to prevent them from creating space, and ensure your elbows are tucked in to maximize pressure.

    Q: I can't seem to get the Cross Collar Choke to work; is it because I'm not pulling hard enough?

    Pulling 'hard' is rarely the solution in BJJ; it's about precise biomechanics. The Cross Collar Choke works by creating a precise pressure on the carotid arteries, not by brute force. Focus on the hip elevation to break their posture, then use your arms to create a pulling motion that drives their collar into your chest and neck. The squeeze of your legs also plays a crucial role in maintaining control and maximizing the choke's effectiveness.

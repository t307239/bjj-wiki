Cross Collar Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide to BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This choke is initiated from the closed guard, aiming to submit your opponent by compressing their carotid arteries.

White belts often fail by relying on brute strength instead of precise leverage and body mechanics.

Success hinges on isolating the opponent's neck with your gi lapels and using your hips to create the choking pressure.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish a strong "seatbelt" grip on the opponent's upper back with your left arm.
    2. With your right hand, reach across the opponent's chest to grip their left lapel, thumb inside the collar.
    3. Place your left hand on the opponent's right lapel, thumb inside the collar, creating an 'X' grip across their neck.
    4. Drive your hips up and slightly to your left, creating a 45-degree angle between your torso and the opponent's.
    5. Pull your elbows down towards your hips, simultaneously tightening your closed guard around their hips to prevent them from posturing up.
    6. Squeeze your knees together, engaging your adductor muscles to maintain the closed guard's integrity.
    7. Shift your weight slightly forward, using your upper body to drive your forearms into the opponent's neck and collarbones, compressing the carotids.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Grabbing the opponent's gi collar with knuckles facing outwards: This can lead to wrist hyperextension and damage to the radiocarpal joint or collateral ligaments.
    • Trying to 'muscle' the choke by pulling with only the arms: This ineffective movement strains the shoulder joint (glenohumeral joint) and biceps, without generating choking pressure.
    • Letting your hips drop to the mat while attempting the choke: This removes the crucial hip leverage, leading to a loss of position and potential strain on the lower back (lumbar spine) from an awkward posture.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the grip-taking and hip-hip motion without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Practice the full sequence of grips, hip movement, and squeeze. (25 reps)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance - 25%): Opponent offers minimal resistance, focusing on feeling the choke engage.
    4. Partner drill (medium resistance - 50%): Opponent actively tries to defend, requiring more precise body mechanics.
    5. Partner drill (high resistance - 75%): Opponent defends actively, simulating a sparring scenario.
    6. Live rolling (100%): Attempt the choke during rolling, focusing on application and recognizing opportunities.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is postured up in your closed guard, creating space for your hands to enter.
    • When the opponent attempts to stack you, opening their posture slightly.
    • When you have achieved a dominant closed guard position with good hip control.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Elbow-to-Bicep Defense: If the opponent attempts to establish the choke, immediately bring your elbow on the choked side tight to your bicep, creating space and breaking the grip.
    • Hip Escape and Posture Up: As the opponent attempts to create hip angle, escape your hips to the side and drive your posture up, breaking their closed guard and relieving pressure.
    • Grip Break: If the opponent has a strong lapel grip, use a strong grip break by prying their fingers off your gi with both hands, focusing on their thumb-side grip.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Cross Collar Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs when your thumbs are not inserted correctly into the collar. Ensure your thumbs are inside the lapel, creating a neutral or slightly flexed wrist position. Avoid extending your wrist backward as you grip, which puts excessive strain on the carpal bones and ligaments.

    Q: How can I apply the Cross Collar Choke effectively against a much larger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on creating a strong X-grip with your hands and using your hips to generate the choking pressure, rather than trying to overpower them with arm strength. Drive your hips up and into their neck, and use your legs to maintain a tight closed guard, preventing them from posturing up and negating your leverage.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to a Cross Collar Choke from closed guard?

    The optimal time is when the opponent is postured up, creating a slight gap between their chin and chest. This allows your hands to enter the collar cleanly. If they are too flat or have their chin tucked tightly, attempting the choke can be ineffective and leave you vulnerable to counters. Look for moments of slight unposturing or when they attempt to break your guard.

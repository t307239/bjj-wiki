Cross Collar Choke Technique for White Belt BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This technique is initiated from closed guard, aiming to submit the opponent by restricting blood flow to the brain.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of leverage and pressure, leading to ineffective attempts.

Success hinges on creating a tight "frame" with your arms and using your hips to generate the necessary constricting force.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a closed guard position, ensuring both your legs are locked around the opponent's torso.
    2. Reach your right hand to the opponent's left lapel, gripping it securely near the collarbone, thumb-in.
    3. Reach your left hand to the opponent's right lapel, gripping it similarly near the collarbone, thumb-in.
    4. Arch your back slightly, lifting your hips off the mat by engaging your glutes and lower back.
    5. Pull both lapels downwards and slightly inwards, creating tension and bringing the opponent's head closer to your chest.
    6. Drive your elbows down and slightly outwards, simultaneously squeezing your knees together to compress the opponent's body.
    7. Maintain a strong hip base and forward pressure with your hips to maximize the choke's effectiveness.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect grip placement: Grabbing too low on the lapel can allow the opponent to escape by posturing up, potentially straining your shoulder joint (rotator cuff) if you attempt to "pull hard" without proper leverage. Always grip high on the collarbone.
    • Over-extending arms: Trying to "pull" the lapels with straight arms creates a lever that the opponent can easily break by posturing, risking biceps tendon strain or shoulder impingement. Keep a slight bend in your elbows and use your body weight.
    • Neglecting hip pressure: Relying solely on arm strength to squeeze the lapels will be inefficient and can lead to wrist or elbow joint strain. The primary force should come from your hips driving forward and upward.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling (no opponent): Practice the grip placement and hip movement with no resistance. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the full motion of the choke without any resistance. (25 reps)
    3. Light resistance drill (25%): Partner offers minimal resistance to your grips and hip movement. Focus on maintaining control. (10 reps per side)
    4. Moderate resistance drill (50%): Partner actively tries to defend by posturing slightly. Focus on maintaining choke tightness. (10 reps per side)
    5. High resistance drill (75%): Partner actively tries to break grips and posture. Focus on maintaining the choke and hip pressure. (5 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling (90-100%): Attempt the choke in sparring, focusing on correct application and timing. (3-5 attempts per round)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is postured down, with their head close to your chest.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard by stacking you.
    • When you have established a tight closed guard and can control their posture.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Posture Up Defense: The opponent straightens their back and lifts their head, creating space. You must immediately "drive" your hips into them and re-establish a tight grip.
    • Grip Break: The opponent uses their hands to peel your grips off their lapels. You must "grip fight" by re-gripping or transitioning to another submission.
    • Arm Escape: The opponent pulls one arm free from the choke. You must "trap" that arm and transition to an armbar or switch to a different choke.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Cross Collar Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you are gripping the lapel with your thumb facing upwards or if you are trying to "pull" the lapel with a straight wrist. To avoid this, ensure your thumbs are tucked into the lapel, creating a "fist" grip. Also, focus on driving your elbows down and using your body weight to create the choke, rather than just pulling with your wrists.

    Q: How can I apply the Cross Collar Choke effectively against a much larger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise hip movement are paramount. Instead of trying to overpower them, focus on creating a tight "frame" with your legs and arching your back to lift your hips. Drive your hips forward and upward into their chest while simultaneously pulling the lapels downwards. This uses your body weight and their own mass against them, rather than relying on brute strength.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Cross Collar Choke in closed guard, and what if the opponent is too far away?

    The ideal time is when the opponent's head is close to your chest and they are slightly "squared up" or "turtled." If they are too far away, you need to "pull" them in by "climbing" your legs higher up their back and squeezing them closer. Once they are within range, then execute the lapel grips and hip drive. Trying to choke from a distance is ineffective and leaves you vulnerable.

This choke is initiated from the closed guard, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain via the carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle by relying on raw strength instead of leverage, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key insight is using the opponent's posture against them, creating a tight, efficient choke with minimal effort.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish Closed Guard: Secure your ankles behind the opponent's back, hips firmly on the mat.
    2. Right Collar Grip: Place your right hand deep inside the opponent's right lapel, thumb down, fingers pointing towards their left shoulder.
    3. Left Collar Grip: Place your left hand deep inside the opponent's left lapel, thumb down, fingers pointing towards their right shoulder.
    4. Pelvis Angle: Angle your pelvis slightly to your left, creating space to drive your shoulders into their neck.
    5. Elbow Position: Keep your elbows tucked in, creating a strong frame against their chest and shoulders.
    6. Hip Drive: Drive your hips upwards and slightly forward, compressing their upper body into your chest.
    7. Finish: Simultaneously pull your elbows down and slightly out, while continuing the hip drive, to apply pressure to both sides of the neck.
    8. Release: If the submission is not achieved, immediately release the grips and re-establish guard to avoid exposing yourself.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying excessive downward force with your hands on the lapels: This can strain the rotator cuff tendons in your shoulder (supraspinatus, infraspinatus) due to excessive internal rotation and adduction under load. The correct action is to use your elbows and hips to drive your shoulders into the opponent's neck, rather than just pulling down with your arms.
    • Arching your back excessively to create space: This can lead to lumbar spine strain or disc compression. The correct movement involves tilting your pelvis and driving your hips upwards, not hyperextending your spine.
    • Gripping too high on the lapel near the clavicle: This can cause pressure on the trachea, leading to an ineffective choke and potential discomfort or injury to the throat. The grips should be deep, allowing pressure on the carotid arteries.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling of collar grips and body positioning (0% resistance): 10 repetitions per side.
    2. Partner drilling with static posture (25% resistance): Focus on establishing grips and hip drive. 5 repetitions.
    3. Partner drilling with slight posture control (50% resistance): Opponent offers minimal resistance to your setup. 5 repetitions.
    4. Drilling with active posture control (75% resistance): Opponent attempts to posture up or defend. Focus on maintaining grips and hip drive. 5 repetitions.
    5. Controlled rolling with focus on choke setup (90% resistance): Aim to initiate the choke from closed guard, but stop before full pressure. 3 rounds.
    6. Live rolling with submission attempts (100% resistance): Apply the Cross Collar Choke when the opportunity arises. 5 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is postured down and their head is within reach of your closed guard.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard and gets their upper body close to yours.
    • When you have successfully controlled the opponent's posture and they are unable to create significant distance.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Posture Up and Grip Break: The opponent can attempt to stand up straight, breaking your closed guard and creating distance. They can also attempt to peel your hands off their lapels, often by pushing your wrists outwards.
    • Shoulder Pressure and Hip Escape: If the choke is being applied, the opponent can drive their shoulder into your chest and hip escape to the side, relieving pressure and potentially escaping the guard.
    • Head and Arm Control: The opponent can attempt to control your head and arm, creating space and preventing you from getting the proper angle for the choke.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try the Cross Collar Choke from closed guard?

    Neck pain often arises from incorrect grip placement or excessive force applied to the trachea instead of the carotid arteries. Ensure your thumbs are down and your grips are deep within the lapels, applying pressure to the sides of the neck. Focus on using your hips and shoulders to drive into the opponent, rather than just pulling down with your arms.

    Q: How can I use the Cross Collar Choke effectively against a much larger and stronger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paramount. Instead of trying to force the choke, focus on breaking their posture down by pulling their head towards your chest and driving your hips up. Once their posture is broken, their size becomes less of an advantage, and you can use your body weight and precise angles to apply the choke.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to a Cross Collar Choke from closed guard?

    The ideal time is when the opponent's posture is compromised, meaning their head is lower than their hips and they are leaning forward. This often happens when they are attempting a guard pass, or when you have successfully pulled them down. Avoid attempting the choke when they are standing tall and have good posture, as it will be ineffective and can leave you vulnerable.

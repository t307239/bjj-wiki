Granby Roll: The White Belt's Essential Transition

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Granby Roll is a dynamic transition, typically initiated from a disadvantageous position like being flattened out in guard or during a failed sweep attempt.

It allows you to quickly re-establish a better position or escape a bad one by generating forward momentum.

Many white belts overthink it, focusing on speed instead of the precise body mechanics needed for safe and effective execution.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a flattened guard or similar position, establish a tight frame with both forearms against your opponent's hips.
    2. Grip your own left shin with your right hand, and your right shin with your left hand, forming a "figure-four" lock on your legs.
    3. Initiate forward momentum by driving your hips up and towards your head, creating a slight arch in your back.
    4. Simultaneously, extend your legs powerfully, pushing off the mat to generate forward rotation.
    5. Tuck your chin to your chest, protecting your head and neck, and allow your body to roll over your shoulder.
    6. As you complete the roll, aim to land with your hips low and your opponent between your legs, transitioning to a technical stand-up or guard recovery.
    7. Maintain a strong core throughout the movement to prevent spinal hyperextension.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly driving your head into the mat can cause cervical spine compression or concussion. Always tuck your chin and roll over your shoulder, not your head.
    • Failing to create hip elevation before the roll can lead to a "belly flop" landing, straining your lower back and shoulders. Ensure your hips rise significantly before initiating the roll.
    • Not maintaining a tight grip on your shins can cause your legs to splay, leading to uncontrolled rotation and potential knee or ankle injuries. Keep a firm figure-four grip throughout the entire maneuver.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip elevation and leg extension motion without rolling, focusing on the upward drive. (50 reps)
    2. Solo drill: Practice the rolling motion on a soft surface (mats), focusing on tucking the chin and completing the rotation. (50 reps)
    3. Partner drill: With a compliant partner, practice the Granby Roll from a flattened guard position, with the partner offering minimal resistance. (10 reps each side, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner drill: Increase partner resistance, focusing on maintaining frames and driving through the roll. (10 reps each side, 50% resistance)
    5. Partner drill: Simulate a live situation where the Granby Roll is necessary, with the partner attempting to maintain their position. (5 reps each side, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the Granby Roll into sparring, using it only when a clear opportunity arises. (Focus on execution, not winning, 100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent has flattened your guard and you are unable to recover closed guard.
    • After a failed sweep attempt where you are left in a compromised, flattened position.
    • When attempting to escape a bad side control or mount position by generating forward momentum.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Front Headlock: If the opponent attempts a Granby Roll, immediately step your leg over their head and establish a front headlock grip, preventing them from completing the roll.
    • Hip Pressure and Frame: Maintain strong hip pressure and forearm frames against the opponent's hips to prevent them from generating upward momentum and rotation.
    • Trapping the Leg: If the opponent initiates the roll, quickly trap their rolling leg with your own leg and hip, halting their momentum and potentially leading to a sweep.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck feel strained after attempting a Granby Roll?

    Neck strain often occurs when the chin is not tucked adequately, leading to the head striking the mat first or excessive hyperextension. Ensure you actively tuck your chin to your chest *before* initiating the roll, and aim to roll over your shoulder, not directly on your head.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, the key is leverage and timing. Focus on creating a significant upward hip drive to break their base *before* initiating the roll. Use your legs to push off the mat explosively, and don't rely on brute strength. Imagine generating a spring-like action from your hips.

    Q: When is the best time to use a Granby Roll in a BJJ match?

    The Granby Roll is a transitional technique, not a primary attack. It's most effective when you find yourself in a compromised position, such as being flattened out in guard or after a failed sweep, where you need to quickly regain a better structure or escape danger. Don't force it; wait for the opportune moment.

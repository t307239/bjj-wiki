グランビロールの基本テクニック

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate

The Granby Roll is a transition technique, often initiated from the bottom of guard or side control, aiming to rapidly change your body's orientation to achieve a superior position like mount or back control.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of foundational body awareness, leading to uncontrolled flailing and increased injury risk instead of a smooth, intentional movement.

The core mechanical insight is utilizing a powerful hip drive combined with a specific shoulder and head rotation to create momentum and clear your opponent's base.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From bottom of closed guard, secure opponent's collar with your right hand (cross-collar grip) and their right sleeve with your left hand (sleeve grip).
    2. Contract your core and drive your hips upwards and slightly to your left, creating an angle of approximately 45 degrees between your torso and your opponent's hips.
    3. Simultaneously, tuck your chin to your chest and rotate your head and left shoulder towards the mat in a tight, controlled roll.
    4. Extend your right leg forcefully, pushing off the mat to generate upward and forward momentum.
    5. Maintain the collar and sleeve grips throughout the roll, preventing your opponent from posturing up or impeding your rotation.
    6. As your body completes the roll, transition your weight onto your right hip and shoulder, driving your left knee towards the mat.
    7. Immediately seek to establish side control or transition to mount by driving your hips forward and securing a stable base with your knees and hands.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending neck: Tucking chin too loosely or not at all can lead to cervical spine hyperextension or compression, risking a neck injury (whiplash or disc damage).
    • Flailing limbs: Uncontrolled arm and leg movements can cause your opponent to fall awkwardly on you, leading to knee or ankle sprains from twisted joints.
    • Insufficient hip drive: Trying to roll without a powerful hip thrust results in a slow, heavy movement where your opponent can easily counter or trap your limbs, risking shoulder impingement or elbow hyperextension.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo movement: Practice the hip drive and shoulder tuck motion on the mat without a partner, focusing on the feeling of initiating rotation. (50 reps, 0% resistance).
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the full Granby Roll motion, focusing on grip integrity and hip drive. (10 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    3. Controlled transition drill: Execute the Granby Roll and immediately work to secure side control or mount against a partner who offers minimal resistance. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    4. Guard pass entry: From closed guard, initiate the Granby Roll as if to escape, and have your partner follow passively, allowing you to practice maintaining control and transitioning to a dominant position. (10 reps per side, 75% resistance).
    5. Live rolling (controlled): Incorporate the Granby Roll as a transition option during positional sparring, focusing on timing and execution against a resisting opponent. (5 rolls, 90% resistance).
    6. Full sparring: Apply the Granby Roll in live rolling scenarios, aiming for successful transitions to dominant positions. (10 rolls, 100% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When stuck in your opponent's closed guard and they are posturing up heavily.
    • When your opponent attempts a guard pass and leaves an opening for a rapid positional change.
    • When you have achieved a dominant position like side control and want to quickly transition to mount or back control.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip escape and frame: As the opponent initiates the roll, immediately shrimp your hips away and create a frame with your forearm between their hips and yours to stop their rotation.
    • Knee shield: If they are rolling towards your guard, place your knee between your bodies and push to prevent them from completing the rotation and gaining position.
    • Grip fighting: If the Granby Roller maintains grips, focus on breaking those grips immediately by prying with your thumbs or using your free hand to peel their fingers off your collar or sleeve.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to Granby Roll from closed guard?

    Shoulder pain often occurs because you're not generating enough initial hip drive and rotation. Instead of a dynamic roll, your shoulder is bearing the brunt of your body weight as you awkwardly fall. Ensure you tuck your chin tightly and initiate the roll with a powerful upward and forward hip thrust, allowing your body to rotate smoothly rather than forcing it with your shoulder.

    Q: How can I Granby Roll effectively against a much larger and stronger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, the Granby Roll relies even more on precise timing and technique over brute force. Focus on creating a significant hip angle (at least 45 degrees) to break their base before initiating the roll. Use your grips to pull them slightly off-balance and leverage your momentum from the hip drive and leg extension to clear their weight. Do not try to match their strength; use their weight against them by initiating the roll when they are slightly off-balance.

    Q: When is the best time to use the Granby Roll in BJJ, and should I always try to end up in mount?

    The Granby Roll is most effective as a rapid transition when you're stuck or see an opening, particularly when escaping guard or improving from side control. While mount is a common goal, it's not the only outcome. Depending on your opponent's reaction and your body's position, you might transition to back control, side control, or even a sweep. The primary objective is a dynamic positional change that disrupts their control and creates an advantage.

The Granby Roll is a dynamic transition, often initiated from a guard retention scenario or to escape a bad position. It allows you to rapidly change your body's orientation and create space or advance your position.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body awareness and a tendency to 'muscle' through the movement. This leads to inefficient mechanics and potential injury.

The core principle is generating momentum from hip extension and shoulder rotation, using your arms as controlled levers, not rigid supports.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish a strong cross-collar grip (right hand on opponent's left collar) and a sleeve grip (left hand on opponent's right sleeve).
    2. Shift your hips slightly to your left, creating a small angle away from your opponent's centerline.
    3. Drive your left knee towards your opponent's chest, initiating a slight forward push with your hips.
    4. Simultaneously, extend your right leg backward, planting your foot firmly on the mat behind you for a stable base.
    5. Begin to roll onto your right shoulder, tucking your chin to your chest to protect your neck and head.
    6. As your body rotates, use your left sleeve grip to pull your opponent's arm across their body, guiding their weight to follow your roll.
    7. Your right hand, initially providing a light frame, transitions to a push off the mat to complete the rotation and land in a base.
    8. The key is continuous, fluid motion; avoid pausing or hesitating at any point during the roll.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly tucking the head: Leads to neck hyperextension or whiplash, as the cervical spine absorbs the impact of the roll.
    • Correct: Maintain a chin-tucked position throughout the rotation, allowing the upper back to initiate the roll.
    • Over-extending the arms for support: Puts excessive strain on the elbows and wrists, risking ligament tears or sprains.
    • Correct: Use the arms as controlled levers for guidance, not as rigid supports. The roll is generated by the hips and shoulders.
    • Rolling directly backward without hip angle: Places immense pressure on the lower back and can cause spinal compression injuries.
    • Correct: Create a slight hip angle to the side, allowing the rotation to occur along the sagittal plane of the body.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo: Practice the hip extension and shoulder tuck motion on a soft mat, focusing on body awareness. (10 reps)
    2. Solo: Add the arm pull and leg extension without any resistance. Focus on fluidity. (10 reps)
    3. Partner drill (0% resistance): Opponent stays relatively still. Execute the Granby Roll with light grip pressure, focusing on body mechanics. (5 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (25% resistance): Opponent offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the movement against their body. (5 reps per side)
    5. Partner drill (50% resistance): Opponent begins to react slightly, forcing you to adjust your timing and grip. (5 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling (75% resistance): Attempt the Granby Roll during transitions or scrambles, aiming for positional advantage. (3 attempts)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard by standing and moving around your legs.
    • When you are stuck in a bad position on the bottom and need to create immediate space and change your orientation.
    • As a dynamic transition from a failed submission attempt to regain guard or establish a better position.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass Defense: If the opponent attempts to stack you during the roll, immediately post your hand on their hip and bridge your hips away, creating a frame to prevent spinal compression.
    • Grip Break and Control: If the opponent breaks your collar grip during the roll, maintain your sleeve grip and use your free hand to post on the mat, controlling their posture and preventing them from capitalizing on the disengage.
    • Hip Escape and Re-Guard: If the roll stalls or the opponent anticipates it, immediately hip escape to regain guard or transition to a more dominant position like knee-on-belly.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try the Granby Roll in BJJ?

    Neck pain during the Granby Roll is typically caused by not tucking your chin properly. This allows your head to absorb the impact of the roll, leading to hyperextension or whiplash. Always keep your chin firmly tucked to your chest, allowing your upper back and shoulders to initiate the rotation, not your head.

    Q: How can I effectively use the Granby Roll against a much bigger and heavier BJJ opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on generating maximum momentum through hip extension and a strong leg drive. Use your grips to pull their weight into your roll, making them a part of your momentum rather than resisting it. Prioritize speed and fluidity; a hesitant roll will be easily stopped by a heavier opponent.

    Q: Is the Granby Roll considered a sweep or a transition in BJJ?

    The Granby Roll is primarily categorized as a transition. While it can lead to a sweep if executed perfectly to end up on top in a dominant position, its core function is to rapidly change your body's orientation and create space or recover guard. It's a movement technique rather than a direct submission or sweep.

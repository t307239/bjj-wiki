The Granby Roll is a transition used from bottom positions, most commonly closed guard or half guard, to achieve a more dominant position like side control or mount.

White belts often struggle by trying to muscle through the roll, leading to loss of position and potential injury.

The key is generating forward momentum and using your hips to create space and drive your body through, rather than just lifting your opponent.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (left sleeve) with your left hand. Your opponent is facing you.
    2. Contract your core and simultaneously drive your hips upwards and slightly forward, creating a slight gap between your pelvis and your opponent's.
    3. Extend your left leg, pushing off the mat to initiate forward momentum, while keeping your right leg hooked.
    4. Immediately transition your right hand grip from the collar to the mat beside your head, forming a stable base.
    5. As your hips clear your opponent's center of gravity, drive your body forward and to your left, aiming to land in side control.
    6. Your right shoulder and hip should be the first points of contact with the mat as you roll.
    7. Maintain your grip on the sleeve to control your opponent's posture and prevent them from posturing up during the transition.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to lift the opponent with your legs and arms instead of rolling: This can strain your lower back ligaments by hyperextending your spine.
    • Rolling directly onto your neck or head without establishing a mat hand: This puts immense pressure on the cervical spine, risking vertebral damage.
    • Not creating enough hip elevation before initiating the roll: This causes you to get stuck under the opponent, leading to knee or ankle entrapment and potential ligament tears from twisting forces.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Granby Roll: Practice the hip elevation, leg extension, and body rotation motion on the mat without resistance (10 reps).
    2. Granby Roll with Light Partner Assistance: Partner stands, you initiate the roll and they lightly guide their weight to allow the roll (25% resistance, 5 reps each side).
    3. Granby Roll from Closed Guard (No Resistance): Partner maintains a neutral posture, you execute the full Granby Roll to side control (50% resistance, 5 reps each side).
    4. Granby Roll from Half Guard (No Resistance): Partner maintains a neutral posture, you execute the Granby Roll to side control (50% resistance, 5 reps each side).
    5. Granby Roll with Active Defense (Light): Partner offers mild resistance, attempting to prevent the roll but not actively countering (75% resistance, 3 reps each side).
    6. Granby Roll in Light Sparring: Execute the Granby Roll during rolling with a partner who understands the technique and allows for attempts (90% resistance, 5 attempts).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent has strong hip control from closed guard, making traditional sweeps difficult.
    • To escape a tight half guard and transition to a more dominant position like side control or mount.
    • As a surprise transition when your opponent is focused on preventing a sweep or submission from the bottom.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Opponent extends their legs and pushes your hips away: This prevents hip elevation, stopping the roll before it starts. Maintain your base and look for another option.
    • Opponent attempts to sprawl or drive their weight forward as you roll: This can trap your head or shoulder. Maintain forward momentum and aim to clear their weight by driving your hips through.
    • Opponent attempts to grab your ankle or leg to prevent you from clearing: This is a common counter. Focus on maintaining the sleeve grip to control their posture and pull them closer as you roll, making it harder for them to grip.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try the Granby Roll from closed guard?

    Your neck likely hurts because you are not creating sufficient hip elevation and forward momentum. Instead of rolling, you are collapsing your body and landing on your neck. Ensure your hips rise significantly before initiating the roll, and drive your shoulder into the mat as you transition, not your neck.

    Q: How do I perform a Granby Roll against a much larger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on generating maximum forward momentum. Use your legs to create a strong push-off from the mat, and drive your hips aggressively through their center of gravity. Do not try to lift them; instead, use their weight against them by rolling under their mass. Your grip on their sleeve becomes even more critical to control their posture.

    Q: Is the Granby Roll a sweep or a transition in BJJ?

    The Granby Roll is primarily a transition technique. While it can result in you ending up in a dominant position (like side control or mount), it doesn't involve sweeping your opponent over your legs in the traditional sense. It's about using a dynamic roll to bypass their guard or position and land in a superior spot.

Open Guard: A White Belt's Guide to BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Open Guard starts from a seated or supine position with legs between you and your opponent, aiming to control distance and create attack angles.

White belts often struggle with an overly rigid structure, failing to use their legs dynamically for framing and sweeping.

The core principle is using your legs as flexible frames to manage distance and unbalance your opponent, not as rigid bars.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From supine position, place both feet on opponent's hips, knees bent to 90 degrees, shins vertical. This creates a primary frame.
    2. Grip opponent's sleeves, just above the elbow, with your palms facing up, thumbs inside the fabric. This controls their posture.
    3. Maintain a slight outward pressure with your feet on their hips, keeping your knees slightly wider than shoulder-width. This widens your base.
    4. Shift your hips laterally to your right (or left), creating an angle. This unbalances them and opens sweep opportunities.
    5. As they step to correct their base, use your right leg to extend and push their hip away, while your left leg hooks behind their knee.
    6. Simultaneously, pull with your sleeve grips, driving their posture forward and initiating a scissor-like leg motion to sweep.
    7. If they posture up, use your feet to push their hips away, creating space to re-establish your frames and transition to another attack or sweep.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Grasping opponent's wrists tightly without controlling their elbows: This allows them to easily pass your guard by straightening their arms and driving forward, potentially hyperextending your wrist joints.
    • Keeping legs straight and rigid like posts: This makes your legs easily breakable and vulnerable to leg locks, and prevents dynamic movement for sweeps. Your knees and hips should remain flexible.
    • Allowing opponent to get inside your leg frames: This compromises your ability to maintain distance and control, leading to guard passes and potential injury to your knees or ankles if they stack or apply pressure incorrectly.
    • Insufficient hip movement when attempting sweeps: A static base with no hip angle change will not generate the necessary leverage to unbalance the opponent, leading to wasted energy and missed opportunities.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice extending and retracting legs, hip shimmies, and leg frame placement without resistance (10 reps each movement).
    2. Partner drill (static): Opponent stands, you are in open guard. Focus on establishing frames and grips (5 reps per side).
    3. Partner drill (light pressure): Opponent applies minimal forward pressure. Practice maintaining frames and creating hip angles (5 reps per side).
    4. Sweep attempts (controlled): Opponent resists lightly. Focus on the sweep mechanics from step 5-7 of biomechanics (5 reps per side).
    5. Guard retention drill: Opponent attempts a simple pass (e.g., knee-slice). You focus solely on maintaining distance and re-establishing frames (3 mins).
    6. Live rolling (controlled): Spar with a partner at 50% intensity, focusing on implementing open guard techniques and sweeps. (3 rounds x 3 mins).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When opponent is posturing up and trying to pass your guard from a standing or kneeling position.
    • When opponent is attempting to establish a strong base on top of your closed guard, forcing you to open it.
    • When you are on your back and need to create space to initiate sweeps or submissions from a less dominant position.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Against Knee-Slice Pass: Immediately extend your leg on the side of the pass, creating a frame with your shin and foot against their hip. Simultaneously, pull their arm across your body with a sleeve grip to break their posture and create an angle for a sweep or transition.
    • Against Toreando Pass: As they attempt to circle their hips, extend your legs to maintain distance. Use your feet to push their hips away and your sleeve grips to pull their upper body towards you, forcing them to re-engage their base.
    • Against Double Under Pass: As they establish double underhooks, use your feet to push their hips away forcefully. Simultaneously, bring your knees towards your chest, creating a "shell" defense, and look to invert or transition to a single leg X-guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my hip hurt when I try to do Open Guard?

    Hip pain in Open Guard often stems from excessive internal rotation or a lack of pelvic control. When you extend your legs to frame, ensure your knees track over your toes and avoid forcing your legs outwards beyond a comfortable range of motion. Focus on using your gluteal muscles to control the hip's position, rather than just relying on leg extension.

    Q: How can I use Open Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, Open Guard's strength is distance management. Use your feet on their hips as powerful frames to prevent them from closing the distance. Focus on subtle hip movements and precise timing for sweeps, rather than trying to muscle them. Prioritize maintaining space to avoid being crushed.

    Q: Is Open Guard only for sweeps, or can I attack from here as a white belt?

    While sweeps are a primary goal, white belts can also learn basic attacks from Open Guard. Focus on simple submissions like triangle chokes or armbars that leverage the space created by your leg frames. The key is to maintain control of their posture and limbs before committing to an attack.

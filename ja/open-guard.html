Open Guard starts from the bottom position, where your opponent is inside your guard, but you break the closed guard connection. It aims to create space and angles for sweeps and submissions.

White belts often struggle by keeping their legs too straight or too close, negating the space needed for this guard.

The key insight is maintaining a constant, dynamic frame with your legs to control distance and create off-balancing opportunities.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting Position: On your back, knees bent, feet on opponent's hips or biceps.
    2. Grip: Secure opponent's sleeves (e.g., right sleeve with your left hand, left sleeve with your right hand) or collar grips.
    3. Leg Extension: Extend one leg (e.g., left leg) to push opponent's hip away, creating a 30-degree angle between your hips and theirs.
    4. Frame Creation: Simultaneously, bring your other leg (e.g., right leg) up, hooking opponent's hip or thigh with your shin/instep, creating a lever.
    5. Weight Distribution: Keep your base wide and your hips active, shifting your weight slightly to the side you are extending your leg from.
    6. Dynamic Movement: Continuously adjust the angle of your hips and the tension in your legs to control opponent's posture and balance.
    7. Space Management: Use your extended leg to maintain a safe distance, preventing them from collapsing your guard.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Hyperextension: Bending knees inwards and straightening legs too forcefully can lead to the hyperextension of the knee joint, damaging the ACL or PCL.

    Correct: Maintain a slight bend in the knees and focus on pushing with the hips and shins, not just the feet.

    • Ankle Sprains: Gripping opponent's pants too tightly with your feet while your ankles are flexed can cause a sharp inward or outward twist, leading to ligament damage.

    Correct: Use a light, controlled grip on pants or focus on hooking hips/thighs with your shins.

    • Lower Back Strain: Arching your lower back excessively to create space or reach grips puts undue stress on lumbar vertebrae and discs.

    Correct: Engage your core and use your hips and legs to create leverage, keeping your back relatively neutral.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Frame Drill: Practice extending one leg and hooking the other on an imaginary opponent. Focus on hip angle and leg tension. (50 reps each side)
    2. Solo Grip and Frame Drill: With no resistance, practice securing sleeve grips and immediately establishing the open guard frame. (30 reps)
    3. Partner Drill (No Resistance): Partner stands, you practice establishing open guard from closed guard or mount transition. Focus on grip and frame. (5 reps each position)
    4. Partner Drill (Light Resistance): Partner attempts to posture up. You practice maintaining frame and controlling distance. (5 reps, 25% resistance)
    5. Flow Rolling (Open Guard Focus): Roll with a partner, emphasizing the use of open guard for sweeps and submissions. Focus on maintaining the guard. (3 rounds, 50% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling: Integrate open guard into your sparring sessions, actively looking for opportunities. (5 rounds, 75-90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When opponent has broken your closed guard and is posturing up.
    • When you want to create space to set up sweeps like the scissor sweep or tripod sweep.
    • When opponent is attempting to pass your guard and you need to regain control and distance.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Guard Pass (e.g., Torreando Pass): If opponent steps around your leg, immediately bring your legs together to form a closed guard again or transition to half guard.
    • Stack Pass: If opponent drives forward to stack you, use your frames to push their hips away and create space to shrimp out or re-guard.
    • Knee Slice Pass: If opponent secures a knee slice, use your free leg to hook their hip or thigh and maintain distance, or attempt a technical stand-up.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do Open Guard in BJJ?

    Wrist pain in Open Guard often stems from gripping your opponent's sleeves too tightly and holding your wrists at an extreme angle. Instead of squeezing, focus on a light, controlling grip. Ensure your elbows are slightly bent, creating a stable frame, and avoid letting your wrists bend backward or inward excessively.

    Q: How can I use Open Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, Open Guard's strength is creating and managing space. Use your legs to push their hips away and prevent them from closing the distance. Focus on controlling one of their legs with your hook and using the other to create angles. Prioritize sweeps that off-balance them, rather than trying to lift their weight.

    Q: Is Open Guard considered an offensive or defensive position for white belts in BJJ?

    For white belts, Open Guard should be approached with a balanced mindset. While it offers defensive capabilities by maintaining distance, its true power lies in creating offensive opportunities for sweeps and submissions. Focus on mastering the defensive frame first, then gradually explore offensive options as your comfort and control improve.

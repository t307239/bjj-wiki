Open Guard: A Biomechanically Accurate BJJ Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Open Guard begins from a supine position, aiming to control distance and create attack angles. It utilizes the legs to create frames and initiate sweeps or submissions.

White belts often struggle by neglecting the foundational hip and leg mechanics, leading to a collapsed structure. They focus on gripping rather than body positioning.

The core principle is using your legs to create a consistent, adjustable frame that prevents your opponent from closing the distance and establishing control.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Assume supine position, knees bent, feet flat on the mat. Initiate by placing your feet on your opponent's hips, toes pointed slightly outwards.
    2. Maintain a slight bend in your knees, creating a spring-like tension. Your hips should be slightly elevated off the mat, creating space.
    3. Grip your opponent's sleeves (e.g., right hand on their left sleeve, left hand on their right sleeve) with a thumb-in grip, ensuring your knuckles are facing upwards.
    4. Simultaneously, grip their pants just above the knee with your other hand. This grip should be firm, preventing them from easily advancing.
    5. To create a frame, push your opponent's hips away by extending your legs, while simultaneously pulling their sleeves towards you. This action uses your hip flexors and hamstrings.
    6. Adjust your hip angle to 45 degrees to one side, allowing you to create space and prepare for a sweep. This asymmetry is crucial for generating momentum.
    7. As you initiate a sweep, drive your hips towards the ceiling while continuing to extend one leg and pull the corresponding sleeve, creating a rotational force.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Gripping only the sleeves without establishing a hip frame: This can lead to your opponent easily collapsing your guard by driving their weight forward, potentially causing hyperextension of your elbows or shoulder impingement.
    • Forcing leg extension without hip elevation: This puts excessive strain on your knee ligaments (ACL/MCL) as the knee joint becomes the primary point of pressure, rather than the controlled extension of the entire leg from the hip.
    • Allowing the opponent to get inside your knee line without adjusting hip angle: This can result in a stacked position where your spine is compressed, and you risk knee-on-neck or facet joint injuries in the neck.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Drilling: Practice opening your guard, establishing grips, and creating frames against an imaginary opponent. Focus on smooth hip movement and leg extension. (10 reps per side)
    2. Partner Drill (0% Resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the initial guard opening and grip establishment. Partner remains still. (10 reps per side)
    3. Partner Drill (25% Resistance): Partner lightly pushes forward. Focus on maintaining your frames and preventing them from closing the distance. (10 reps per side)
    4. Partner Drill (50% Resistance): Partner attempts to advance their hips. Practice adjusting your hip angle and leg frames to create space. (10 reps per side)
    5. Partner Drill (75% Resistance): Partner actively tries to pass. Focus on using your frames to create sweep opportunities or transition to a more dominant guard. (5 reps per side)
    6. Live Rolling (90% Resistance): Implement Open Guard during rolling. Focus on maintaining structure and looking for sweep opportunities, accepting that passes will occur. (3 x 2-minute rounds)
    7. Live Rolling (100% Resistance): Apply Open Guard with full intention during live rolling, aiming to execute sweeps and submissions. (5 x 3-minute rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent attempts to establish a tight closed guard and you want to create space for sweeps or submissions.
    • When your opponent is posturing up from your guard, giving you opportunities to attack their legs or create angles.
    • When you are defending a pass and need to re-establish guard control with active leg positioning.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass Defense: If your opponent stacks, immediately use your hands to push their shoulders away while driving your hips up and to the side, creating space to recover guard.
    • Leg Pummeling: If your opponent tries to pass by stepping over your legs, use your feet to pummel them out, returning to a closed or half-guard structure.
    • Hip Escape to Re-Guard: If your opponent successfully penetrates your frames, perform a strong hip escape to the side and use your legs to re-establish a controlling guard position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I do Open Guard?

    Wrist pain in Open Guard often stems from gripping too tightly without a solid base or frame. When your opponent applies forward pressure, your wrists absorb the entire force. Ensure your elbows are slightly bent and your shoulders are engaged to create a stable base. Also, focus on gripping the fabric of the sleeves and pants with your fingers, not just wrapping your palm around. This distributes pressure more effectively through your forearm.

    Q: How do I use Open Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, Open Guard's effectiveness relies on maximizing distance and leverage. Use your legs to create a strong, consistent frame on their hips and biceps, preventing them from collapsing your guard. Focus on off-balancing them with precise hip movements and leg extensions. Instead of trying to 'pull' them, think of 'pushing' them away with your legs while using your grips to control their posture and create angles for sweeps.

    Q: When should I transition from Closed Guard to Open Guard?

    You should consider transitioning from Closed Guard to Open Guard when your opponent is posturing up, making it difficult to maintain control or execute submissions from closed guard. This posture creates an opportunity for you to open your guard, establish frames with your feet on their hips, and initiate sweeps or leg attacks. It's also a good transition if they are successfully defending your closed guard attacks and you need to create new angles of attack.

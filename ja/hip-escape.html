Hip Escape: Your First BJJ Defense Technique

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Hip Escape, known as 'Shrimping', is a fundamental defensive movement from the guard or bottom half-guard. It creates space to re-establish guard or improve your position.

White belts often fail by pushing with arms or legs inefficiently, leading to a loss of posture and control. They also tend to move their whole body as one unit.

The core principle is using your hips to 'peel' away from pressure, leveraging your feet on the mat for controlled movement, not just flailing.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting Position: Guard or bottom half-guard, opponent's weight is directly on top of your hips.
    2. Initial Movement: Flex your knees, bringing your heels closer to your glutes. Your feet remain flat on the mat.
    3. Initiate Hip Lift: Engage your core and gluteal muscles to lift your hips slightly off the mat, creating a small gap.
    4. Drive with Feet: Press your feet firmly into the mat. Use your left foot to drive your hips to the right, and your right foot to drive to the left.
    5. Pelvis Rotation: As you drive with your feet, rotate your pelvis in the opposite direction of your desired movement. If moving right, rotate your pelvis left.
    6. Head and Shoulder Alignment: Keep your head and shoulders aligned with your hips. Avoid letting your upper body lead or lag excessively.
    7. Space Creation: Continue the driving and rotating motion until you have created enough space to bring your knee between you and your opponent.
    8. Re-establish Guard: Once space is made, tuck one knee towards your chest and extend the other leg to re-establish closed guard or a safer guard position.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Error: Pushing with arms against the opponent's hips or shoulders. Risk: Shoulder impingement or rotator cuff tear due to uneven force and joint strain. Correction: Drive with your feet, keeping arms framing the opponent's hips/shoulders to maintain distance, not to push.
    • Error: Extending legs straight out to 'kick' the opponent away. Risk: Knee hyperextension or ligament tears (ACL/MCL) from sudden uncontrolled extension. Correction: Keep knees flexed and drive through the soles of your feet.
    • Error: Lifting hips too high and arching the back excessively. Risk: Lumbar spine strain or disc compression from excessive lordosis. Correction: Focus on hip rotation and driving through the feet, keeping the spine relatively neutral.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Escape (0% Resistance): Lie on your back and practice the hip escape motion repeatedly to the left and right. Focus on the foot drive and hip rotation. (50 reps each side).
    2. Against a Wall (0% Resistance): Lie on your back with your feet against a wall. Practice hip escaping away from the wall, using it as a reference for distance. (50 reps each side).
    3. With a Pillow/Dummy (25% Resistance): Place a pillow or soft dummy on your hips. Practice hip escaping away from it, feeling the pressure. (25 reps each side).
    4. With a Partner (Light Pressure) (50% Resistance): Have a partner lie on top of you, applying light pressure. Practice hip escaping. Partner should not resist much. (25 reps each side).
    5. Partner Resisting Slightly (75% Resistance): Your partner applies moderate pressure and offers slight resistance. Focus on generating enough power to create space. (25 reps each side).
    6. Live Rolling (90-100% Resistance): Incorporate the Hip Escape into sparring. Focus on using it when your guard is being passed or when you are stuck in side control. (As needed during rolls).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your guard is being passed and the opponent's knee is inside your guard.
    • When stuck in bottom half-guard and the opponent is crushing your hips.
    • When an opponent is attempting to pass your guard from a knee-on-belly or side control position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Re-establishment: After successfully creating space, immediately drive a knee towards your chest and extend the opposite leg to secure closed guard.
    • Sweeping: If the opponent overcommits to pressure after a failed hip escape, use the newly created space to pivot and execute a sweep like a scissor sweep or tripod sweep.
    • Re-guarding: If you cannot fully re-establish closed guard, aim to get back to your knees and form a defensive shell or turtle position, then work to get back to your feet or a more advantageous guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I do the Hip Escape?

    This often occurs when you try to lift your hips too high off the mat by excessively arching your lower back, creating a 'swayback' posture. Instead, focus on driving through the soles of your feet and rotating your pelvis. Keep your core engaged to maintain a more neutral spine and avoid overextending your lumbar region.

    Q: How do I Hip Escape against a much bigger and heavier opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, you must be more efficient. Instead of trying to lift your hips high, focus on the 'peeling' action. Use your feet to drive your hips laterally, creating just enough space to slip your knee in. Your core and gluteal engagement are paramount to generate the force needed to move their weight.

    Q: No, you should not use your arms to actively push the opponent away. Your arms should be used for framing and maintaining distance. The primary force for the hip escape comes from driving with your feet and rotating your hips. Pushing with your arms can lead to loss of posture and ineffective movement.

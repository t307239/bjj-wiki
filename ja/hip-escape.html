BJJ Hip Escape: The Essential White Belt Technique

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Hip Escape, or 'Shrimping', is a fundamental defensive movement. It starts from being controlled, often in guard or side control, and aims to create space.

White belts often struggle because they try to push with their feet or lift their hips without a clear base, leading to inefficiency and injury.

The key is to pivot on your shoulders and knees, using your hips to drive away in a controlled, linear fashion, like a shrimp swimming backward.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting from closed guard, with opponent's knees inside your thighs. Your hands frame on opponent's hips, thumbs inside their gi pants at the hip bone.
    2. Flex your knees to a 90-degree angle, feet flat on the mat, aiming to bring your heels close to your glutes.
    3. Arch your lower back slightly, lifting your hips just off the mat, creating a small gap.
    4. Pivot on your left shoulder and left knee, keeping your right leg bent and foot on the mat.
    5. Simultaneously, drive your hips to the right, away from the opponent's chest, extending your left leg slightly.
    6. Your right knee should drive forward, clearing the opponent's knee that is inside your guard.
    7. Maintain pressure with your hands on the opponent's hips, creating a frame to prevent them from collapsing back into you.
    8. Continue this linear motion, moving your hips away until you create enough space to recompose guard or move to a safer position.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Trying to 'scoot' backward by straightening legs: This can hyperextend the knees, leading to ACL or MCL tears. Always keep knees flexed and pivot.
    • Pushing off the opponent's chest with hands: This can cause shoulder impingement or rotator cuff strain. Use a framing action on the hips, not a pushing one.
    • Lifting hips too high without a pivot: This creates an unstable base and can lead to lower back strain or sacroiliac joint dysfunction. Focus on the hip drive and shoulder pivot.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Escape: Lie on your back on a mat. Practice the hip escape motion without any resistance. Focus on the hip drive and shoulder pivot. (50 reps per side, 0% resistance).
    2. Mat-Based Hip Escape with Partner's Light Pressure: Partner lies on top, lightly controlling your shoulders and hips. Practice hip escapes, creating space. (30 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    3. Closed Guard Hip Escape (No Gi): Partner sits in your closed guard. Practice hip escapes to create space to recompose guard. Focus on frames. (20 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    4. Closed Guard Hip Escape (Gi): Partner sits in your closed guard with light gi grips. Practice hip escapes to create space. Focus on controlling the frames. (20 reps per side, 75% resistance).
    5. Side Control Escape Hip Escape: Partner is in side control. Practice hip escaping to create space to bring your knees back in for guard. (15 reps per side, 90% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling Hip Escape: In sparring, consciously look for opportunities to use the hip escape to defend or create space when in a disadvantageous position. (Full rolling time, 100% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When opponent has established closed guard and is pressuring forward.
    • When opponent is in side control and attempting to flatten you out.
    • When you are on your back and opponent is trying to pass your guard.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Maintain frames on opponent's hips with forearms, thumbs inside pants, to prevent them from collapsing back in.
    • Keep your core engaged and drive your hips linearly away, not in an arc, to maintain a consistent distance.
    • Use your shoulder as a pivot point, driving your opposite hip away, creating a shrimp-like motion.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my hip hurt when I try to hip escape in BJJ?

    Hip pain during hip escapes often stems from a lack of proper biomechanical alignment. Instead of a fluid hip drive, you might be twisting your pelvis awkwardly or pushing with your feet, straining the hip flexors and joint capsule. Focus on pivoting on your shoulder and knee while driving your hips directly away, keeping your knees bent and core engaged.

    Q: How do I hip escape against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, the principle of creating space becomes even more critical. You need to be precise with your frames on their hips, using your forearms to create a solid barrier. Your hip escape must be a powerful, linear drive, utilizing your entire core and the pivot on your shoulder and knee. Don't try to lift them; focus on moving yourself away from their weight.

    Q: When is the best time to hip escape in BJJ, and what if I can't create space?

    The best time to hip escape is immediately when you feel pressure or control that you want to escape. If you can't create space, it means your frames are not solid, or you're not driving with enough hip power. Re-evaluate your hand positions on their hips, ensure your knees are tucked, and practice the explosive hip drive. Sometimes, a slight adjustment in your shoulder pivot can unlock the movement.

Precise Outside Heel Hook Technique for BJJ White Belts

BROWN★★★★★Expert
Brown帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 茶帯昇格要件

The Outside Heel Hook is a powerful leg lock targeting the opponent's heel to hyperextend the knee and ankle, forcing a submission.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of control, leading to uncontrolled movements that risk injury to themselves and their training partners.

The key is isolating the heel and creating a fulcrum with your body, applying rotational pressure through your hips, not just leg strength.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a 50-50 guard or outside ashi garami position, secure your opponent's leg with your arms, creating a "figure four" grip around their ankle, ensuring your knuckles are pressed against their Achilles tendon.
    2. Position your hips so they are perpendicular to your opponent's leg, with your right hip (if attacking the opponent's right leg) driving into their hamstring.
    3. Drive your left knee towards the opponent's hip to prevent them from turning into you or escaping.
    4. Place your right foot's sole firmly on the opponent's hip crease, creating a strong base and preventing their hip from moving towards you.
    5. Arch your back and lift your hips off the mat, creating space between your torso and the opponent's leg.
    6. Begin to extend your legs away from your body, straightening your knees while maintaining the "figure four" grip.
    7. Simultaneously, rotate your hips inward, driving your right hip towards your own left shoulder, applying torque to the opponent's heel.
    8. Control the opponent's hip with your left knee and right foot to maintain your angular advantage and prevent them from posturing up.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Grabbing the opponent's foot with only your hands without establishing a "figure four" grip: This can lead to the opponent's foot slipping out and can cause hyperextension or twisting injuries to your own wrist and elbow if they resist forcefully.
    • Not using your hips to create rotational pressure, instead relying on straightening your legs: This directly stresses the opponent's knee and ankle ligaments, risking ACL or MCL tears and fibular collateral ligament damage.
    • Allowing the opponent to turn into you or create a "dead leg" situation by not controlling their hip with your knee or foot: This can result in a "stack" position where the opponent's weight crushes your chest and abdomen, potentially leading to rib injuries or asphyxiation if a choke is also applied.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the "figure four" grip and hip rotation motion on your own leg without a partner (50 reps).
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): From 50-50 guard, partner allows you to establish the grip and hip position. You practice the hip drive and leg extension motion (20 reps per side).
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers slight resistance to grip establishment and hip control, you execute the mechanics (20 reps per side).
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to prevent the grip and maintain posture, you work to secure the position and apply the submission mechanics (15 reps per side).
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner defends actively, you focus on isolating the heel and applying controlled pressure (10 reps per side).
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Attempt the Outside Heel Hook from appropriate positions, focusing on proper execution and control, tapping early if caught.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • From 50-50 guard when your opponent is on their back with their legs entangled with yours.
    • When you have achieved an outside ashi garami position and can isolate one of your opponent's legs.
    • During scrambles or transitions where you can secure a dominant leg entanglement.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Straightening the leg: The opponent can attempt to straighten their leg by extending their hips and driving their heel away, relieving pressure from the ankle and knee joint.
    • Turning the heel: The opponent can rotate their heel inward (towards their own body), which can sometimes disengage the rotational pressure applied by the attacker.
    • "Stacking" the opponent: If the attacker is not controlling the opponent's hip, the opponent can drive their hips forward and stack the attacker, relieving pressure and potentially creating an escape opportunity.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try to do an Outside Heel Hook?

    Your ankle likely hurts because you are not properly isolating your opponent's heel with a "figure four" grip. Instead, you might be gripping their foot or shin, and when you apply rotational pressure, the force is not directed correctly at the heel. Ensure your knuckles are pressing into their Achilles tendon and your hips are driving into their hamstring to create a stable fulcrum for the heel.

    Q: How can I safely practice the Outside Heel Hook against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on precision and control over brute force. Emphasize establishing a strong "figure four" grip around their heel and using your hips to drive into their hamstring, creating a solid base. Your secondary leg should firmly control their hip to prevent them from using their weight to stack you. Tap early and often to learn the mechanics without risking injury.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt an Outside Heel Hook in a BJJ match?

    The Outside Heel Hook is best attempted when you have achieved a dominant leg entanglement, such as 50-50 guard or outside ashi garami, and you have successfully isolated one of your opponent's legs. This typically occurs after a failed guard pass, a sweep, or a transition where you can secure a strong position before your opponent can re-establish their defense or posture up.

The Outside Heel Hook is a powerful leg lock that targets the opponent's heel bone, aiming to hyperextend the knee and ankle joints. It is typically initiated from positions where you can isolate one of the opponent's legs, such as the 50-50 guard or when they attempt to pass your guard.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of the precise body mechanics required, leading to ineffective application and increased injury risk. Fear of injuring themselves or their partner can also cause hesitation, preventing them from committing to the necessary movements.

The single most crucial mechanical insight is to create a straight line of force through the opponent's leg, from their hip to their heel, by controlling their hip and then applying a rotational force to their ankle. This isolates the ankle joint and maximizes leverage.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting Position: Opponent's leg is trapped between your legs, ideally in a 50-50 guard configuration or similar leg entanglement.
    2. Grip 1 (Heel Control): Cup your hand firmly around the opponent's heel, with your palm against their heel bone and fingers wrapping over the top of their foot. Your thumb should be on the outside of their foot.
    3. Grip 2 (Ankle Control): Place your other hand on the opponent's Achilles tendon, just above the heel cup grip. Your thumb should point towards their shin.
    4. Hip Angle: Angle your hips slightly away from the opponent's trapped leg, creating a slight gap between your hips and their thigh.
    5. Weight Distribution: Shift your weight onto your hips, driving them down towards the mat. Your base should be stable with your non-trapped leg extended for balance.
    6. Leg Drive: Extend your non-trapped leg forcefully towards the opponent's hip, creating a fulcrum and preventing them from easily extracting their leg.
    7. Rotation and Extension: Keeping your grips firm, rotate your hips and torso away from the opponent's trapped leg while simultaneously driving your hips down. This creates a simultaneous rotational force on their ankle and a hyperextension pressure on the knee.
    8. Pressure Application: Apply continuous, controlled pressure. The finish comes from the combined rotational torque on the ankle and the hyperextension pressure on the knee. Do not 'snap' or 'yank'.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Error: Grabbing the opponent's foot or shin instead of the heel bone, and applying a 'pulling' motion with your arms. Injury: This can lead to ligamentous sprains in the ankle and knee due to indirect and uncontrolled force. Correct: Grip the heel bone directly and use your hips and body rotation to generate force, not just your arms.
    • Error: Allowing your hips to be too close to the opponent's thigh, creating a 'pinched' position where you cannot generate leverage. Injury: This can cause anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) or medial collateral ligament (MCL) tears in your own knee if the opponent bridges or twists forcefully. Correct: Maintain a slight hip angle and space between your hips and their thigh.
    • Error: Trying to 'crank' the submission by violently twisting your wrists or arms. Injury: This puts immense, uncontrolled stress on the opponent's knee and ankle, risking severe ligament tears (ACL, MCL, PCL, meniscus) and fractures. Correct: Apply steady, increasing pressure through your body's rotation and hip drive.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Gripping and Positioning Drill: Practice isolating a training dummy's leg and securing the heel hook grips. Focus on hip angle and leg placement. (50 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    2. Static Control Drill: With a partner lying on their back, practice entering the 50-50 guard and securing the heel hook position. Partner remains passive, focusing on maintaining the position. (30 reps per side, 10% resistance)
    3. Controlled Leg Drive Drill: From the static position, practice driving your non-trapped leg into the opponent's hip and holding the position. Partner gently attempts to resist the drive. (20 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Hip Rotation and Extension Drill: Practice the hip rotation and body extension motion without full pressure. Partner remains completely still. Focus on the feeling of the mechanics. (30 reps per side, 30% resistance)
    5. Slow, Controlled Submission Drill: Apply the Outside Heel Hook very slowly and with minimal pressure. Partner taps immediately at the first sign of discomfort or pressure. Focus on smooth transitions and controlled force. (15 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling with Submission Focus: During live rolling, actively look for opportunities to enter the Outside Heel Hook. If the position is achieved, apply controlled pressure. If the opponent defends effectively, transition out safely. (5 minutes of rolling, 75-90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When you have successfully entered the 50-50 guard and have control of the opponent's heel.
    • When the opponent attempts a guard pass that leaves their leg exposed and vulnerable to entanglement.
    • When you are in a submission defense where you can invert and attack the opponent's leg, such as escaping a knee-on-belly.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • The 'Heel Peel' Escape: If your heel is caught, and the opponent is trying to extend their hips, the primary counter is to aggressively 'peel' your heel out by driving your hips forward and rotating your knee inward, creating space to free your foot. This requires immediate and explosive hip movement.
    • The 'Stack and Turn' Defense: If the opponent is applying pressure and has control of your leg, the defender can attempt to 'stack' by driving their weight forward onto the attacker's hips and shoulders, while simultaneously turning their body towards the attacker's head. This disrupts the attacker's base and leverage.
    • The 'Bridge and Roll' Counter: From certain entanglements, the defender can attempt to bridge their hips powerfully and roll their body to face the attacker, thereby escaping the leg lock and potentially reversing the position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do an Outside Heel Hook on someone?

    Your knee is likely hurting because you are not creating a direct line of force through the opponent's leg. Instead of isolating their ankle and knee joints with a controlled rotational force from your hips, you might be trying to 'pull' with your arms or apply pressure incorrectly. This indirect force can torque your own knee ligaments. Focus on gripping the heel bone, maintaining a proper hip angle, and using your body's rotation to apply pressure.

    Q: How do I apply an Outside Heel Hook against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision become paramount. You cannot rely on brute strength. Ensure you have excellent hip control to prevent them from posturing up and using their weight. Focus on getting a deep heel hook grip and creating a strong fulcrum with your non-trapped leg against their hip. Your body rotation and hip drive are your primary weapons; use them to generate torque that their size cannot easily resist.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt an Outside Heel Hook in a BJJ match?

    The Outside Heel Hook is best attempted when you have achieved a dominant leg entanglement, such as the 50-50 guard, or when you have successfully defended a guard pass and can isolate a single leg. It is also a strong option when your opponent is trying to escape a submission or transition aggressively, as their focus on escaping can create openings. Avoid attempting it from disadvantageous positions where your own leg is vulnerable.

Outside Heel Hook: A White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

BROWN★★★★★Expert
Brown帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 茶帯昇格要件

The Outside Heel Hook is a powerful leg lock targeting the opponent's ankle and knee. It's typically initiated from positions where you can isolate a leg, such as the 50-50 guard or when the opponent attempts a guard pass.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of joint mechanics and a tendency to rely on brute force, increasing injury risk. Focus on precise positioning and controlled pressure, not raw power.

The key to a successful Outside Heel Hook is creating a strong rotational force on the opponent's knee and ankle while preventing their escape through hip positioning and grip integrity.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting Position: Opponent's leg is trapped between your legs, ideally in a 50-50 guard scenario where both parties have a heel hook grip on the same leg. Alternatively, from a failed guard pass attempt where you secure the opponent's leg.
    2. Grip 1 (Attacking Foot): Place your forearm across the top of the opponent's foot, behind the ankle bone. Your hand grips your own bicep, creating a "figure-four" grip around the ankle.
    3. Grip 2 (Controlling Hip): With your other hand, secure a "S-grip" on the opponent's pants just above the knee. This prevents them from turning their hip away.
    4. Hip Angle: Drive your hips forward and slightly to the side, perpendicular to the opponent's trapped leg. Your hips should be higher than the opponent's hip on the trapped leg side.
    5. Weight Distribution: Shift your weight onto your hips, pressing your chest into the opponent's thigh. This anchors you and creates leverage.
    6. Leg Drive: Extend your legs slightly, keeping them straight but not locked out. This creates tension on the opponent's ankle.
    7. Execution: Apply pressure by arching your back and driving your hips forward. Simultaneously, pull your S-grip upwards while maintaining the figure-four grip on the ankle. The rotation comes from your hips and core, not just arm strength.
    8. Rotation: The primary force is a rotational twist applied to the opponent's foot and lower leg, forcing their ankle and knee to hyperextend internally.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hip Drive: Driving hips straight back instead of forward and to the side. This allows the opponent to escape by turning their hip and can cause hyperextension of the opponent's knee if they are much stronger or faster to escape.
    • Excessive Knee Flexion: Bending your own knees too much, bringing your heels too close to your glutes. This compromises your base and can lead to a "knee-bar" effect on yourself if the opponent turns into you, damaging your own knee ligaments.
    • Loose Ankle Grip: Gripping the opponent's foot loosely or only with your fingers. This allows the ankle to slip out, and if you then try to "pull harder," you risk tearing your own biceps or shoulder ligaments.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Grip Practice: Hold your own ankle and practice forming the figure-four grip. Repeat 20 times per leg. (0% resistance)
    2. Solo Hip Positioning: Lie on your back and practice the hip drive motion, simulating the weight transfer and hip angle without a partner. Repeat 20 times. (0% resistance)
    3. Partnered Static Position: With a partner lying down, practice entering the 50-50 guard and securing the Outside Heel Hook grips and hip positioning. Partner offers no resistance. Repeat 10 times per side. (25% resistance)
    4. Controlled Entry and Grip: With a partner, practice entering the position from a controlled scenario (e.g., they offer a leg). Focus on securing grips and hip angle. Partner gently resists basic escapes. Repeat 10 times per side. (50% resistance)
    5. Controlled Submission Attempt: From a secured position, apply slow, controlled pressure. Partner taps immediately when they feel significant pressure. Focus on the correct execution of the hip drive and rotation. Repeat 5 times per side. (75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling (Limited): During rolling, identify opportunities to attempt the Outside Heel Hook only when the position is clearly established and safe. Focus on securing the position and applying submission with control. Partner may resist more actively but should tap early. (90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When you have achieved a dominant 50-50 guard position where you control the opponent's heel.
    • During a guard pass attempt where the opponent's leg is exposed and you can isolate it.
    • When transitioning between leg lock entries and the opponent's leg becomes available for trapping.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape and Re-Guard: The opponent escapes by hip-escaping away from the pressure, breaking the ankle lock. They then re-establish guard or a neutral position.
    • Straightening Leg and Stacking: The opponent attempts to straighten their trapped leg and stack their weight onto you. You counter by adjusting your hip angle and continuing to drive forward.
    • Turning into the Lock: The opponent turns their body into the direction of the lock, attempting to relieve pressure on the knee. You must maintain your grip and hip position, possibly shifting your own body to maintain the rotational force.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try to do the Outside Heel Hook?

    Your ankle is likely hurting because you are gripping the opponent's foot too loosely, causing it to slip. When this happens, you instinctively try to "pull harder" with your arms, which puts direct strain on your own ankle ligaments and tendons. Instead, focus on securing a tight "figure-four" grip around their ankle with your forearm and bicep, and ensure your hips are driving forward to create the rotational pressure.

    Q: How can I effectively apply an Outside Heel Hook against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, brute strength is your enemy. Focus on precise biomechanics. Ensure you have a very tight grip on their ankle and pants. Drive your hips forward and up, creating a strong lever. The key is to use their weight against them; when they try to posture up or resist, use that movement to enhance your hip drive and rotational force, rather than trying to overpower them.

    Q: When should I transition to an Outside Heel Hook instead of an Inside Heel Hook?

    The choice between Outside and Inside Heel Hooks depends on how the opponent's leg is positioned relative to your body. An Outside Heel Hook is generally applied when you can isolate the opponent's leg on the outside of your body, allowing you to drive your hips forward and create a rotational force. An Inside Heel Hook is typically used when the opponent's leg is trapped on the inside, closer to your body, and you create pressure by pulling their heel towards your hip.

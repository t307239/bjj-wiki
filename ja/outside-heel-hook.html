BJJ Outside Heel Hook: A White Belt's Biomechanically Accurate Guide

BROWN★★★★★Expert
Brown帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 茶帯昇格要件

The Outside Heel Hook is a powerful leg lock that attacks the opponent's heel with rotational force, aiming to force a submission via knee or ankle hyper-extension.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body awareness and a tendency to force the submission rather than using leverage.

Mastering the hip rotation and the precise angle of attack is the single most crucial element for success and safety.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a strong 50-50 Guard position, ensuring your opponent's leg is trapped between your knees, with your hips aligned perpendicular to their body.
    2. Secure the opponent's heel with your inside arm, wrapping your forearm around the Achilles tendon and gripping your own bicep or wrist.
    3. Your outside arm grips the opponent's foot, specifically around the instep and toe area, creating a "figure four" lock.
    4. Drive your hips forward and slightly to the side of the opponent's trapped leg, creating a fulcrum point at your own hip.
    5. Apply pressure by extending your hips away from the opponent, while simultaneously pulling the heel towards your chest with your inside arm's grip.
    6. Crucially, rotate your torso counter-clockwise (if attacking the opponent's right leg) to generate the torque that stresses the knee and ankle.
    7. Maintain a straight line from your hips to the opponent's heel; any bend at your own knee will absorb the power and increase your risk of injury.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly gripping the opponent's foot, often with an open palm, can lead to the foot slipping and the opponent escaping, while your own knee is exposed to their counter-attacks.
    • The correct grip is a firm hold around the instep and toes, with your fingers interlocked or overlapping, providing a secure anchor.
    • Applying pressure with bent knees instead of hip extension will not create sufficient torque, leading to a weak submission attempt and potential hyperextension of your own knee joint.
    • Maintain straight legs, using your hips and core to generate the power, transferring force directly to the opponent's heel.
    • Allowing your hips to be too close to the opponent's hips creates a "crushing" scenario where they can easily bridge or stack you, risking a knee injury for yourself.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Drill (0% resistance): Practice the hip rotation and body positioning without a partner, focusing on the correct sequence and angles for 10 reps per side.
    2. Partner Assist (25% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice securing the heel and initiating the hip drive. Partner offers minimal resistance to grip. 10 reps per side.
    3. Controlled Grip Entry (50% resistance): Partner allows you to establish the 50-50 guard and the heel grip. You practice the initial hip drive and leg extension. Partner resists slightly to the grip. 10 reps per side.
    4. Controlled Hip Rotation (75% resistance): Partner allows the 50-50 and heel grip. You execute the full sequence, including the torso rotation. Partner offers slight pressure to prevent the submission, but allows the movement. 5 reps per side.
    5. Light Sparring (90% resistance): In a controlled sparring environment, attempt the Outside Heel Hook from the 50-50 guard. Partner may attempt to defend but will not actively try to injure you. Focus on execution and timing. 5 attempts per side.
    6. Live Sparring (100% resistance): Integrate the Outside Heel Hook into live sparring, with full understanding and respect for the submission. Tap early if caught. 3-5 attempts per side.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When you have successfully established a 50-50 Guard with control over the opponent's leg.
    • When the opponent is attempting to pass your guard and their leg becomes accessible for a heel hook.
    • When you have secured a deep half-guard and can transition to the 50-50 guard with the opponent's leg.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • The "Heel Hook Defense" (Leg Escape): If the opponent attempts an Outside Heel Hook, immediately turn your hip away from the attacking leg. This reduces the rotational pressure. Simultaneously, pull your heel out by extending your knee and ankle. The goal is to create space and disengage the attacking grip.
    • The "Stomp Defense" (Counter-Attack): If the opponent has a weak grip or is slow to apply pressure, stomp down on their attacking arm with your free leg's foot. This can break their grip and create an opportunity to escape or transition. Be mindful of not getting your own foot caught.
    • The "Bridge and Roll" (Positional Escape): If the opponent is attempting to secure the heel hook from an unstable position, bridge your hips sharply and attempt to roll your body towards the attacking leg. This can disrupt their base and allow you to untangle your leg.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do an Outside Heel Hook?

    Your knee is likely hurting because you are trying to force the submission with bent legs or by pulling with your arms alone. This creates shearing forces on your own knee joint. Instead, focus on straightening your legs and driving your hips forward to create a powerful lever, transferring the rotational force directly to the opponent's heel and ankle.

    Q: How do I do an Outside Heel Hook against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Ensure you are in a perfect 50-50 position with their leg trapped securely. Focus on generating maximum torque through precise hip extension and torso rotation. Do not try to overpower them; instead, use their weight against them by creating a strong fulcrum with your hips and attacking the ankle's vulnerability with a tight heel grip.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt an Outside Heel Hook in BJJ sparring?

    The optimal time to attempt an Outside Heel Hook is when you have established a dominant control position like the 50-50 guard, or when your opponent is actively trying to pass your guard and their leg becomes exposed. Avoid attempting it when you are in a compromised position or when your opponent has strong control over your hips, as this can lead to dangerous counter-attacks.

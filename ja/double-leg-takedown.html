Precision Guide to the Double Leg Takedown in BJJ

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

This technique aims to bring your opponent from a standing position to the mat, establishing a dominant control position.

White belts often struggle by trying to muscle the takedown without understanding the kinetic chain, leading to wasted energy and potential injury.

The core principle is to use your body's mass and forward momentum to drive through your opponent's center of gravity, not just pull them down.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Stance and Initial Approach: Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent. Initiate forward movement with a slight forward lean, keeping your base stable. Your opponent should be within striking distance.
    2. Penetration Step: Execute a deep, forward step with your lead leg (e.g., right leg). This step should land past your opponent's lead foot, aiming to get your knee level with or slightly behind their knee.
    3. Head Position and Grip: As you step, immediately drop your level. Place your head on the outside of your opponent's torso, typically on their right side if you are stepping with your right leg. Simultaneously, secure a double-underhook grip: wrap both arms around your opponent's thighs, hands clasped or interlocking on the back of their thighs.
    4. Hip Drive and Weight Transfer: Drive your hips forward and down, creating a powerful forward and upward force. Your hips should be lower than your opponent's hips. The weight transfer is from your rear leg through your hips to your lead leg and into your opponent.
    5. Leg Drive and Foot Placement: Use your lead leg (the one that penetrated) to drive forward and slightly upward. Your rear leg will naturally follow, driving off the mat to propel your body forward.
    6. Lifting and Finishing: Maintain the double-underhook grip and continue driving your hips. As your opponent's base is compromised, use your forward momentum and leg drive to lift their hips and drive them to the mat.
    7. Mat Control: Once your opponent is on the mat, maintain pressure and control. Do not release the grip prematurely. Drive your opponent to the mat in a controlled manner, aiming to end up in a dominant position like side control or mount.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Hyperextended Knee: Attempting to drive forward with a locked, straight lead leg can cause hyperextension. The knee joint is forced backward beyond its normal range of motion, damaging ligaments like the ACL or MCL. Always keep a slight bend in your lead knee and drive through your hips, not just your leg.
    • Lumbar Spine Strain: Trying to lift the opponent solely by rounding your back and pulling upwards with your arms strains the lumbar spine. The spinal erector muscles are overloaded, leading to potential disc compression or muscle tears. Maintain a neutral spine and use your legs and hips to generate power.
    • Neck Injury (Whiplash): Rushing the penetration step without establishing head position can lead to your head snapping backward if the opponent resists. This sudden flexion-extension movement can injure the cervical spine. Ensure your head is firmly placed on your opponent's body before driving.

    ドリル段階

    1. Shadow Drilling (0% Resistance): Practice the penetration step, hip drive, and grip without a partner. Focus on the body mechanics and weight transfer. (50 reps)
    2. Partnered Static Drill (0% Resistance): Stand opposite a willing partner. Practice the penetration step and securing the double-underhook grip. Partner remains passive. (30 reps)
    3. Controlled Entry (25% Resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance. Practice the penetration step and grip. Partner may take a small step back but does not actively defend. (30 reps)
    4. Assisted Takedown (50% Resistance): Partner stands passively but allows you to drive through them. Focus on driving your hips and finishing the takedown. Partner helps absorb the fall. (30 reps)
    5. Live Entry with Verbal Cue (75% Resistance): In live rolling, verbally cue your intention to attempt the double leg. Partner reacts defensively but doesn't fully resist. Focus on timing and execution. (5 rounds, 30 seconds each)
    6. Live Rolling (90% Resistance): Attempt the double leg takedown in live rolling with a resisting opponent. Focus on applying the learned biomechanics and adapting to their defense. (10 rounds, 1 minute each)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up and slightly off-balance forward.
    • When you have closed the distance and established a solid collar-and-sleeve or collar-and-collar grip, allowing you to pull them slightly forward.
    • When your opponent is attempting a shot themselves and you can intercept their forward momentum.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Sprawl and Hip Pressure: When the opponent attempts a double leg, immediately drop your hips to the mat and push your legs back. Maintain strong hip pressure into their shoulders to prevent them from driving through. This requires a quick reaction and maintaining a wide base.
    • Whizzer (Overhook): As the opponent penetrates, secure an overhook (whizzer) with your arm on their head/neck side. Use this to off-balance them and prevent them from driving forward, potentially turning them or creating an angle to circle out.
    • Head Up and Circle Out: If the opponent's grip is not fully secured or their drive is weak, keep your head up and actively circle your hips away from their drive. This breaks their angle and can lead to a scramble or opportunity to escape.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try the double leg takedown in BJJ?

    Lower back pain typically stems from rounding your spine during the drive phase. Instead of hinging at the hips and driving with your legs, you're using your back muscles to pull or lift. To fix this, focus on keeping your spine neutral and actively driving your hips forward and down, using the power generated from your legs and glutes.

    Q: How can I effectively perform a double leg takedown against a much larger BJJ opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, raw power is less effective. You must prioritize speed and leverage. Focus on a deep penetration step that gets your hips lower than theirs. Use a powerful hip drive to off-balance them, rather than trying to lift them. Secure your double-underhook grip firmly and drive your shoulders into their chest, using your entire body weight to propel them forward.

    Q: When is the best time to shoot for a double leg takedown in BJJ sparring?

    The ideal time to shoot for a double leg takedown is when your opponent is slightly off-balance forward, perhaps after they've attempted a strike or a movement that shifts their weight. You can also create opportunities by pulling them forward slightly with your grips before initiating your shot. Avoid shooting when they are perfectly balanced or leaning back.

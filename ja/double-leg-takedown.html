Precise Double Leg Takedown Technique for BJJ White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

The Double Leg Takedown initiates from a standing position and aims to bring your opponent to the mat, establishing top control.

White belts often fail by lunging with their head up or relying on brute strength rather than proper body mechanics.

The key insight is to drive through your opponent's center of mass using your hips and legs as a lever, not just your arms.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Stance: Assume a staggered stance, left foot forward, right foot back, approximately shoulder-width apart. Maintain a low center of gravity, knees bent.
    2. Entry: From approximately 1-2 meters away, perform a level change by bending your knees and hips, bringing your head to a position below your opponent's shoulders.
    3. Grip: Secure a 'double underhook' grip by reaching your arms underneath your opponent's armpits and gripping your own wrists or forearms firmly. Your grip should be tight, preventing their upper body from escaping.
    4. Hip Angle: Drive your hips forward and slightly to your opponent's left side, creating a 45-degree angle between your torso and theirs. Your left hip should be closer to their right hip.
    5. Head Position: Keep your head up and to the side of your opponent's body, ideally driving into their chest or shoulder. Never tuck your chin to your chest.
    6. Leg Drive: Simultaneously, explosively drive forward and upward with your legs, extending your hips and knees. Your rear (right) leg will act as the primary driving force.
    7. Weight Transfer: As you drive, transfer your weight forward onto your opponent's center of mass. Your body should move as one unit, propelling them backward and downward.
    8. Finish: Maintain your grip and drive. As your opponent falls, secure a 'mat return' by keeping your chest connected to their chest and walking them down to the mat. Aim to finish in a stable position like side control or a dominant guard pass position.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Lunging with head down: This puts excessive pressure on the cervical spine, risking neck injury (herniated discs, whiplash) if the opponent sprawls effectively. The correct action is to maintain an upright head position, driving into the opponent's chest.
    • Gripping too loosely on the wrists: This allows the opponent to easily break your grip and escape, leading to a loss of control and potential imbalance. Secure a firm grip by interlocking your fingers or using a gable grip.
    • Driving with arms only: This strains the shoulders and elbows, risking rotator cuff tears or ligament damage. The power should originate from the legs and hips, with arms providing the connection and control.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Stance and Level Change: Practice sinking your hips and bending your knees to a low level, maintaining a stable base. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Solo Double Underhook Grip and Drive: From a static position, practice achieving the double underhook grip and simulating the hip drive and leg extension. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Partner Assisted Entry and Grip: With a partner standing passively, practice your level change, entry, and securing the double underhook grip. Partner offers minimal resistance to your grip. (20 reps per side, 10% resistance)
    4. Partner Assisted Drive (No Fall): Partner stands, you secure the double underhook and drive. Partner takes small steps to allow you to feel the weight transfer. (20 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    5. Controlled Takedown Drill: Partner stands, you execute the double leg takedown. Partner offers controlled resistance on the sprawl and fall. Focus on maintaining control throughout. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling - Takedown Focus: In rolling, explicitly attempt the double leg takedown when the opportunity arises. Partner is fully resisting but not actively attempting to counter aggressively. (5 minutes, 75% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is postured up and their weight is forward, creating an opening at their hips.
    • When your opponent initiates a grip fight and their hands are within your reach for a quick entry.
    • When you have successfully defended a takedown attempt and are in a stable standing position with an angle on your opponent.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Sprawl: When the opponent initiates the double leg, immediately flatten your hips to the mat, extending your legs backward. Keep your weight over their shoulders to prevent them from lifting you. This biomechanically prevents them from driving through your base.
    • Whizzer (Overhook): As the opponent enters, secure an overhook on their far arm and drive your shoulder into their head and neck. This disrupts their body alignment and creates an angle to disengage or counter-attack, preventing them from establishing a solid grip and drive.
    • Foot Sweep/Trip: If the opponent's base is unstable during their entry, use a quick foot sweep (e.g., Osoto Gari) on their lead leg or trip their trailing leg as they drive. This exploits their forward momentum and unbalanced posture.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why do my knees hurt when I try the Double Leg Takedown?

    Knee pain during the double leg takedown often stems from driving your knees directly into the mat or hyperextending them. This occurs when you don't achieve a proper level change or your drive is too focused on pushing with your legs rather than driving your hips through. Ensure your knees are bent and your drive comes from hip extension, keeping your torso upright and head out of the line of fire.

    Q: How can I perform a Double Leg Takedown against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on speed, angles, and exploiting their weight distribution. You cannot simply out-muscle them. Your level change needs to be more pronounced, getting your head significantly lower than theirs. Drive your hips forward and slightly off-center to unbalance them before initiating the leg drive. A tight double underhook grip is crucial to prevent them from using their size to simply push you away.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Double Leg Takedown in BJJ?

    The optimal time is when your opponent's weight is forward, and their base is slightly compromised, such as when they are reaching for a grip or attempting to advance their position. Avoid attempting it when they are squared up and have a strong, stable base. Look for openings created by their offensive movements or when you have a clear line of entry with your head and shoulders positioned lower than theirs.

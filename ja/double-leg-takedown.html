The Double Leg Takedown initiates from a standing position, aiming to bring your opponent to the mat to establish top control.

White belts often struggle with insufficient penetration or incorrect weight transfer, leading to ineffective attempts.

The key is to lower your level and drive forward with your hips, using your entire body as a single unit.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Stance: Feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, weight balanced on the balls of your feet.
    2. Initial Contact: As opponent circles, step forward with your lead foot and simultaneously reach for their thighs with both hands, securing a "Pancake Grip" (cupping the back of the thighs).
    3. Penetration Step: Simultaneously, step your rear foot forward to a position behind your lead foot, bringing your lead knee to the mat or just above it, ensuring your thigh is parallel to the ground.
    4. Hip Drive: Drive your hips forward and down, tucking your chin to your chest and keeping your back straight.
    5. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight from your rear foot onto your lead leg and hips, creating forward momentum.
    6. Lifting/Driving: Maintain the pancake grip and continue to drive your hips forward. If lifting, use your legs and hips to elevate their center of gravity.
    7. Finishing: Once their base is broken, drive them to the mat in the direction of your penetration step, maintaining control of their legs and securing a dominant position like side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to "grab" the legs without penetrating: This often leads to your opponent easily posturing up and can cause hyperextension of your elbows and shoulder joint if they resist forcefully.
    • Driving with the upper body only: This puts immense strain on your lower back, risking lumbar strain or disc injury, as your core is not engaged to transfer force from your legs.
    • Not lowering your level sufficiently: This results in a "standing grab" and leaves your head exposed to headbutts or whiplash if the opponent reacts defensively.
    • Lunging with straight legs: This can lead to knee hyperextension injuries, tearing the anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) or meniscus.
    • Incorrect head position (head up): This can result in a head clash with your opponent, causing concussions or facial injuries.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling (0%): Practice the footwork, penetration step, hip drive, and head position without a partner. Focus on smooth transitions. (20 reps)
    2. Mirror Drilling (25%): Face a partner. They stand passively. You execute the entire takedown sequence against them, focusing on penetration and hip drive. Partner offers minimal resistance. (10 reps each side)
    3. Controlled Entry Drilling (50%): Partner offers light resistance to your initial grip. You focus on securing the pancake grip and executing the penetration step. Partner doesn't actively defend the takedown but may brace. (15 reps)
    4. Penetration and Drive Drilling (75%): Partner offers more active defense, trying to sprawl or resist your drive. Focus on maintaining your base and driving through their defense. (15 reps)
    5. Takedown to Position Flow (90%): Execute the double leg takedown and immediately transition to a dominant position (e.g., side control). Partner offers realistic defense but the focus is on completing the takedown and securing control. (10 reps)
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Attempt the double leg takedown during live sparring. Focus on timing and execution rather than solely on success. (5-minute rounds, 3 rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is postured slightly forward, making their legs accessible.
    • During a brief moment of imbalance or when they are circling away from you.
    • As a proactive measure when you feel them attempting a guard pass or a takedown of their own.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Sprawl: As you penetrate, the defender sprawls their hips back and down, creating a strong base to prevent you from lifting or driving their legs. Their weight shifts directly backward.
    • Whizzer/Overhook: The defender secures an overhook on the side you are driving towards. This allows them to create a strong frame and off-balance you, potentially leading to a trip or escape.
    • Framing with Arms: The defender uses their arms to create space between their hips and yours, preventing a tight grip and driving forward momentum. They push against your shoulders or head.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the double leg takedown?

    Knee pain during a double leg takedown often stems from not lowering your level sufficiently. When you penetrate with your lead leg, if your knee is not parallel to the ground or is bent too sharply, it can lead to excessive pressure on the patellofemoral joint or collateral ligaments. Ensure your lead thigh is parallel to the mat and your drive comes from your hips and rear leg.

    Q: How can I do a double leg takedown against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on precision and leverage over brute force. Your penetration step must be deep, bringing your lead knee very close to the mat. Drive your hips forward aggressively, using their size against them by unbalancing them. Aim to secure a "pancake grip" on the back of their thighs to control their base and prevent them from easily sprawling.

    Q: When is the best time to shoot for a double leg takedown in BJJ?

    The optimal time to shoot for a double leg takedown is when your opponent shows a momentary lapse in their stance or posture. This can occur when they are circling, shifting their weight, or momentarily leaning too far forward. Avoid shooting when they are perfectly balanced or actively defending your entry. Look for opportunities where their base is compromised.

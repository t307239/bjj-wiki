This technique initiates from a standing position and aims to bring your opponent to the mat, securing a dominant position.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body awareness and a tendency to rely on brute force rather than leverage.

The core principle is to penetrate deeply and use your body as a lever, driving through your opponent's center of mass.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Stance: Adopt a balanced stance with feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent. Maintain a neutral spine.
    2. Entry: Initiate by lowering your level and stepping forward with your lead leg, aiming to place it beside or slightly ahead of your opponent's lead foot.
    3. Penetration: As you step, simultaneously drive your hips forward and down, lowering your torso until your lead shoulder is below your opponent's hips.
    4. Grip: Secure a "double-under-hook" grip by reaching both arms around your opponent's thighs, interlocking your hands behind their legs. Ensure your thumbs are locked over your fingers.
    5. Hip Drive: Drive your hips forward, creating a powerful upward force through your opponent's base. Imagine pushing your pelvis into their groin area.
    6. Leg Drive: Simultaneously, drive forward with your rear leg, extending your knee and pushing off the mat. This propels your body mass forward.
    7. Head Position: Keep your head up and to the side of your opponent's body, preventing them from using it to resist or counter.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to grab the opponent's ankles from a high stance: This can lead to a hyperextended knee in your own leg if the opponent pulls away or if you fail to drive forward, damaging the ACL or MCL.
    • Driving with the upper body only, without a deep hip penetration: This puts excessive strain on your lower back, risking lumbar disc herniation.
    • Rounding your back during the penetration step: This makes you vulnerable to throws and strains your spine, potentially leading to a pulled muscle or disc injury. Keep your spine neutral.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo movement: Practice the penetration step and hip drive motion without a partner. Focus on lowering your level and driving your hips forward. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the entire takedown sequence. Focus on correct grip and hip/leg drive. (20 reps)
    3. Light resistance drill: Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the push and pull of the movement. (30 reps)
    4. Controlled resistance drill: Your partner actively tries to defend, but not with full force. Focus on maintaining your drive and grip. (40 reps)
    5. Scenario drilling: Practice the takedown from specific starting positions (e.g., opponent attempting a shot). (50 reps)
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the double leg takedown into sparring with 90% resistance, focusing on timing and execution.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is standing in a wide, unbalanced stance.
    • When your opponent attempts to shoot for a takedown themselves.
    • When you have secured a strong collar or sleeve grip and can use it to off-balance them before the shot.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Whizzer (overhook): If the opponent penetrates, secure an overhook on their lead arm and bridge your hips to the side, driving your elbow down. This creates a strong base to resist their forward pressure.
    • Sprawl: If the opponent penetrates and drives forward, quickly drop your hips to the mat and extend your legs back, creating a wide base and preventing them from securing the grip.
    • Footwork and Angle Change: If the opponent initiates the shot but is not deep, quickly pivot your hips and step to the side, breaking their line of attack and creating an escape angle.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do a Double Leg Takedown against a bigger opponent?

    Knee pain often occurs when you don't penetrate deeply enough, causing you to grab too high and rely on extending your own leg for leverage. This puts immense stress on your knee joint. Ensure you drive your hips forward and down, getting your shoulder below their hips, and then drive through with your rear leg, not by pushing with your lead leg.

    Q: How do I perform a Double Leg Takedown in No-Gi BJJ when grips are harder to secure?

    In No-Gi, the core mechanics remain the same: deep penetration and hip drive. Focus on securing a strong grip around the thighs or hips. Since collar and sleeve grips are absent, your penetration must be even more explosive. Your head position becomes critical for controlling their balance and preventing escapes. Use your chest pressure to drive them backward once you have the grip.

    Q: Is it better to shoot for a Double Leg Takedown from a high or low stance?

    For white belts, a slightly lower, more athletic stance is biomechanically superior for a Double Leg Takedown. This allows for easier penetration beneath your opponent's center of gravity. A high stance forces you to bend your back excessively or makes your penetration shallow, leading to weak grips and increased injury risk. Aim to be able to comfortably touch your knees with your hands without rounding your back.

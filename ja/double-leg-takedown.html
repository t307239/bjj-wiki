Master the Double Leg Takedown in BJJ: A White Belt's Guide

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

The double leg takedown is a fundamental wrestling and BJJ technique initiated from a standing position, aiming to bring your opponent to the mat.

White belts often struggle by relying solely on raw strength, leading to poor balance and ineffective attempts.

Success hinges on creating a strong, stable base and driving forward with your hips and legs, not just your arms.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Start in a staggered stance, left foot forward, right foot back, opponent facing you. Maintain a neutral torso, approximately 2-3 feet apart.
    2. Grip the opponent's right pant leg with your left hand, securing the fabric just above the ankle. Simultaneously, grip the opponent's left pant leg with your right hand, also above the ankle.
    3. Lower your level by bending your knees and hips, bringing your chest closer to your opponent's torso. Your head should be to the outside of their body, ideally on their right side.
    4. Plant your lead (left) foot forward, aiming it directly at the opponent's center of mass. Your rear (right) foot remains planted, providing a stable base.
    5. Initiate the drive by extending your hips forward and slightly upward, transferring your weight from your rear foot to your lead foot. Simultaneously, slightly lift the opponent's legs by pulling upwards on the secured pant legs.
    6. Drive your lead shoulder into the opponent's hip or thigh, using your body as a wedge. Maintain a tight grip on the pant legs throughout the movement.
    7. As you drive forward, your rear leg will naturally come forward to maintain balance. Continue the forward momentum to complete the takedown, finishing with the opponent on their back.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to 'pull' the opponent down with arm strength alone: This can strain the biceps and triceps, and often results in losing balance as the opponent remains upright. The correct movement is a forward drive with the hips and legs, using the arm grips for control and lift.
    • Driving forward with a straight back and hips too high: This puts immense pressure on the lumbar spine, risking disc injury. Maintain a neutral spine by bending your knees and hips, keeping your hips lower than your shoulders.
    • Not establishing a secure grip on the pant legs: Loose grips allow the opponent to easily escape or defend, leading to wasted energy and potential for the opponent to counter with a sprawl. Secure a firm grip on the fabric above the ankle, ensuring you can control the leg's movement.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the stance, level change, and forward drive motion without a partner. Focus on hip drive and leg extension. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a willing partner, practice the entire takedown sequence, focusing on correct grips and body positioning. Partner remains passive. (20 reps per side)
    3. Light resistance drill: Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the connection and drive. Focus on maintaining grips and driving through. (30 reps total)
    4. Controlled sparring (0% resistance): Practice the takedown against a partner who is trying to defend but not actively attacking. Focus on timing and execution. (5 minutes)
    5. Controlled sparring (25% resistance): Partner actively attempts to defend and resist the takedown, but without aggressive counter-offense. (5 minutes)
    6. Live rolling (50% resistance): Integrate the double leg takedown into live sparring, with both partners rolling with controlled intensity. (5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is in a staggered stance, offering a clear target for a double leg.
    • When the opponent overcommits to a strike or forward movement, creating an opening.
    • When you have secured a dominant collar or sleeve grip and can use it to create an angle for the takedown.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Sprawl: When the opponent initiates the double leg, immediately drop your hips to the mat, extending your legs back and flattening your body to prevent them from securing your legs. Your chest should be on their back.
    • Whizzer (overhook): As the opponent drives in, secure an overhook on their arm on your side and use your shoulder to push into their head/neck, creating a strong base and preventing them from driving through. This allows you to circle out or initiate your own offense.
    • Footwork and Angle Change: If the opponent's entry is slow or off-balance, quickly step to the side and pivot, creating a new angle and making their takedown attempt ineffective. This can lead to a single leg or a sweep.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the double leg takedown in BJJ?

    Knee pain often occurs when you drop too low and your lead knee collapses inward during the drive. This happens when your base is too narrow or you're not driving with your hips. Ensure your lead knee tracks over your toes and your hips are actively driving forward, keeping your spine neutral.

    Q: How can I do a double leg takedown against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on speed and leverage rather than brute force. Use a low level change and drive your hips forward aggressively. Secure tight grips on the pant legs and drive your shoulder into their hip or thigh to destabilize them. Aim to get under their weight and use your momentum to bring them down.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a double leg takedown in BJJ sparring?

    The ideal time is when your opponent is in a staggered stance or has just moved forward, creating an opening. Avoid attempting it when they are perfectly balanced or are actively defending your advance. Look for moments of imbalance or commitment to forward movement from their side.

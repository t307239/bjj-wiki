The double leg takedown is initiated from a standing position, aiming to secure both of the opponent's legs and drive them to the mat.

Many white belts rush this, forgetting the foundational mechanics and prioritizing speed over control.

The key is to lower your level and drive with your legs, using your head as a wedge to control their hips.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Stance: Maintain a balanced stance with feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, and hips low. Your lead leg should be slightly forward.
    2. Initial Penetration: As your opponent is slightly off-balance or reacting to a feint, step forward with your lead leg, bringing your rear leg to meet it, achieving a squared stance.
    3. Head Position: Immediately place your head on the outside of their hip, specifically on the side of your lead leg. Your head should be angled slightly upwards.
    4. Grip: Reach under their armpits with both arms, gripping the back of their thighs or the inside of their pants just above the knee. Your grip should be tight, pulling them towards you.
    5. Hip Angle and Drive: Lower your hips below their center of gravity. Your hips should be angled forward, and your lead leg's knee should be driving forward and slightly down.
    6. Weight Transfer: Drive your opponent forward and slightly upward by extending your hips and straightening your legs. Your bodyweight should be transferred through your hips and into their torso.
    7. Finish: As you drive, lift their legs off the mat and bring them to the ground, maintaining control by keeping your head on their hip and your hips driving.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect head placement: Driving your head directly into their chest or stomach can cause neck hyperextension, leading to cervical spine strain or disc injury. Always aim for the outside of the hip, angled upwards.
    • Over-reliance on arms: Pulling with your arms alone without driving with your legs and hips will lead to shoulder impingement and rotator cuff tears. Focus on driving with your entire body's power.
    • Knee valgus collapse: Stepping too wide or driving without proper hip alignment can cause your lead knee to cave inwards (valgus), leading to ACL or MCL tears. Keep your knee tracking over your foot.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Stance and Penetration Drill: Practice the initial stance, level change, and leg penetration steps without a partner. Focus on hip low and head position. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Partnered Penetration and Grip Drill: With a compliant partner, practice stepping in, placing your head, and securing the thigh/pant grips. Partner remains static. (30 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Controlled Drive Drill: With a compliant partner, secure the grips and practice the driving motion. Partner offers slight resistance. (20 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Drive and Lift Drill: Focus on driving and lifting the opponent's legs slightly off the mat. Partner offers moderate resistance. (20 reps, 50% resistance)
    5. Takedown to Mat Drill: Execute the full takedown motion, driving the partner to the mat. Partner offers significant resistance but allows the takedown. (15 reps, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Takedown Attempts: Attempt the double leg takedown in a live rolling scenario, focusing on timing and execution. (5-10 attempts per roll, 90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is reaching for grips or initiating a tie-up from standing.
    • When your opponent takes a wide stance or leans too far forward, creating an opening at their hips.
    • When you have successfully controlled their posture and they are looking to establish a dominant grip.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Whizzer (Overhook): As the opponent penetrates, secure an overhook on their lead arm. Use your hips to pivot away and off-balance them, or use it to defend the takedown and transition to a sweep or submission.
    • Sprawl and Hip Escape: When the opponent drives in, quickly extend your hips back and post your hands on their shoulders. As they lose forward momentum, hip escape to create space and potentially transition to a guard.
    • Foot Sweep/Trip: If the opponent's base is too narrow or they are driving with their lead leg forward, a well-timed foot sweep or trip on their lead leg can disrupt their balance and negate the takedown.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do a double leg takedown?

    Knee pain often stems from improper knee tracking during the drive. If your lead knee collapses inward (valgus) or your foot is angled incorrectly, you're placing excessive stress on the ACL and MCL. Ensure your knee stays aligned over your foot and your drive comes from your hips, not a forceful knee bend.

    Q: How can I perform a double leg takedown against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and speed are paramount. Focus on hitting their legs at a lower level than they expect, aiming to get your head on the outside of their hip. Drive with your legs and hips, using their own weight against them. A strong grip on the thighs is crucial to prevent them from posturing up.

    Q: Should I always aim for the thigh grip on a double leg takedown, or is gripping the pants okay for BJJ?

    For BJJ, gripping the pants just above the knee or the thigh is generally acceptable and often more practical than a deep thigh grip that can be easily defended. The key is a secure, tight grip that prevents them from posturing up and allows you to drive effectively. Focus on securing both legs firmly.

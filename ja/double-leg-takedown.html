Precision Double Leg Takedown Guide for BJJ White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

This technique starts from a standing position and aims to take your opponent to the mat, establishing a dominant top position.

Many white belts struggle by lunging with their head or failing to commit their hips, resulting in ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key insight is using your hips to drive forward and your head to guide, creating a powerful lever for the takedown.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Stance: Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, maintaining a balanced base.
    2. Initial Entry: Lower your level by bending your knees and hips, keeping your back straight.
    3. Grip: Secure a "collar-and-sleeve" grip on the opponent's gi, or a "two-on-one" grip on their arms if in no-gi. Alternatively, grip both of their pants just above the knees.
    4. Penetration Step: Step your lead leg deep between the opponent's legs, aiming to place your knee slightly behind their knee line.
    5. Hip Drive: As you step, drive your hips forward and down, aligning your pelvis with the opponent's center of mass.
    6. Head Position: Position your head on the opponent's chest or shoulder, driving forward, not downward.
    7. Lifting and Driving: Simultaneously, use your arms to secure the grip and drive your hips, lifting the opponent's base and propelling them forward and down.
    8. Finish: Maintain control, drive through to their back, and follow them to the mat, securing a dominant position like side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Lunging forward with your head: This can cause hyperextension of the neck or cervical spine injury. The correct movement is to drive your hips and chest forward, using your head as a guide, not a battering ram.
    • Bending at the waist without bending knees: This puts excessive strain on the lower back and can lead to disc injuries. Always bend your knees and hips to lower your center of gravity.
    • Overextending your arms to "pull" the opponent: This can lead to shoulder joint injuries like rotator cuff tears. Focus on driving with your hips and using your arms to maintain control and guide the opponent's movement.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling: Practice the footwork, penetration step, and hip drive motion without a partner (10 reps).
    2. Partner Static Drill (0% Resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the entry, grip, and hip drive, partner stands still (5 reps per side).
    3. Partner Controlled Movement Drill (25% Resistance): Partner offers slight resistance, focus on timing the penetration step and hip drive (5 reps per side).
    4. Partner Controlled Movement Drill (50% Resistance): Partner actively tries to defend, focus on maintaining your base and driving through (5 reps per side).
    5. Live Takedown Attempts (75% Resistance): Attempt the double leg takedown in a controlled sparring situation, focusing on execution and hip drive (5 attempts).
    6. Live Rolling (90-100% Resistance): Integrate the double leg takedown into live rolling, aiming for successful attempts and maintaining top position (5 minutes rolling).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured upright and within striking distance.
    • When your opponent overextends their base while attempting a sweep or guard pass.
    • When you have secured a strong grip on their gi or body that allows you to control their posture.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Whizzer (Overhook): If the opponent secures a double leg, immediately bring your arm over their back and hook their far armpit, driving your shoulder into their head to prevent them from driving forward. This uses your shoulder and chest to create a strong frame.
    • Sprawling: As the opponent penetrates, quickly snap your hips back and extend your legs, placing your weight on top of their shoulders and head. This creates a bridge-like structure with your body to flatten them out.
    • Foot Sweep/Trip: If the opponent is off-balance during their penetration, use your free leg to sweep their lead leg or trip their trailing leg. This exploits their compromised base by using a lever action with your foot.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the double leg takedown?

    Knee pain often occurs when you penetrate too deeply with your lead leg or if your knee strikes the mat directly without proper padding or support. Ensure your penetration step places your knee slightly behind the opponent's knee line, not directly beside it. Drive your hips forward and down, keeping your lead knee from taking the full impact of the mat.

    Q: How can I effectively perform a double leg takedown against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are crucial. Focus on a lower penetration step, ensuring your head is on their chest and your hips are driving forward and under their center of gravity. Instead of trying to lift them, aim to off-balance them by driving them forward and down, then using your body weight to follow them to the mat and secure position.

    Q: When is the best time to shoot for a double leg takedown in BJJ?

    The ideal time to shoot for a double leg is when your opponent is slightly off-balance, perhaps leaning forward to attack or defend. Look for moments when their weight is not firmly planted, or when they have committed their base forward. A common opportunity arises when they are reaching for a grip or attempting to close distance.

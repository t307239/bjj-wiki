Back Mount is a dominant position in BJJ where you secure control from behind your opponent, offering superior leverage for submissions and control.

White belts often struggle by being too high or too low, compromising their base and allowing escapes.

The key insight is understanding hip-to-hip connection and maintaining a tight seatbelt grip for optimal leverage.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Secure a "Seatbelt Grip": Right hand grabs opponent's left collar, left hand grabs opponent's right collar. Thumbs are inside the collar.
    2. Hook Legs: Insert your shins inside opponent's thighs, with your feet crossed at the ankles (not on the hips).
    3. Hip Alignment: Your hips should be directly behind opponent's hips, creating a "hip-to-hip" connection.
    4. Maintain "S" Shape with Body: Arch your back slightly, creating a concave shape that mirrors opponent's convex back.
    5. Weight Distribution: Apply downward pressure through your chest and hips onto opponent's upper back and glutes.
    6. Head Position: Keep your head on or slightly below opponent's shoulder, creating a strong frame.
    7. Control Posture: Use your hooks and seatbelt to prevent opponent from posturing up or turning into you.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hook Placement: Placing feet on opponent's hips or crossing shins too high can lead to knee hyperextension or hip dislocation if opponent bridges or turns forcefully. Always keep hooks inside the thighs, crossed at the ankles.
    • Over-Reliance on Arms: Gripping only the collar without maintaining hip-to-hip connection allows opponent to create space and escape. Focus on driving your hips forward and maintaining a strong base.
    • Neck Strain: Reaching too high for the head or allowing opponent to tuck their chin can lead to neck strain or injury. Keep your head position low and controlled, on the shoulder line.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Drill: Practice getting to Back Mount from a controlled position (e.g., opponent on hands and knees) with no resistance. Focus on hip-to-hip and seatbelt grip. (5 reps)
    2. Solo Drill with Light Resistance: Have a partner stand and gently resist your attempt to secure Back Mount. Focus on maintaining the grip and hooks. (10 reps)
    3. Partner Drill: Start from a neutral position. One partner attempts to get Back Mount while the other offers mild resistance (e.g., framing). (20 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled Scenario: Start with one partner in a compromised position (e.g., on hands and knees). The other partner attempts to secure Back Mount and hold for 10 seconds. (30 reps, 50% resistance)
    5. Positional Sparring: Begin in Back Mount. The defender tries to escape or turn. The attacker tries to maintain Back Mount and prevent escape for 30 seconds. (40 reps, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling: Incorporate Back Mount attempts and defenses naturally within rolling. Focus on maintaining the position and executing submissions. (5 minutes rounds, 100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT

    • When opponent turns their back to you during transitions or scrambles.
    • After successfully taking an opponent down and they attempt to turtle or escape.
    • When opponent is on their hands and knees, offering an opportunity to climb.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS

    • Bridging: If the attacker is too high, opponent bridges their hips up and to the side, driving their hips into the attacker's. This creates space to roll or turn.
    • Forearm Frame: If the attacker's hooks are too deep, opponent uses their forearm to create a wedge between their thigh and the attacker's shin, breaking the hook.
    • Head and Arm Defense: Opponent tucks their chin and drives their head into the attacker's chest, simultaneously trying to bring their arm across to frame the attacker's face or neck.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my back hurt when I try to get Back Mount in BJJ?

    Your back pain likely stems from an anterior pelvic tilt and over-arching your lower back. This happens when you try to 'sit on' the opponent instead of connecting your hips directly behind theirs. Focus on tucking your tailbone slightly and maintaining a neutral spine, allowing your opponent's back to support your weight, not your own lumbar muscles.

    Q: How can I get Back Mount against a much bigger BJJ opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is key. Don't try to muscle your way to Back Mount. Instead, focus on precise timing as they turn or expose their back. Use their momentum against them by circling your hips to their hip line and securing the seatbelt grip firmly. Your hooks become crucial; ensure they are deep inside the thighs to prevent them from shucking you off.

    Q: When should I stop attacking for Back Mount and transition to another position?

    If your opponent successfully defends your hooks or seatbelt grip for more than 3-5 seconds, or if they manage to create significant space by framing with their arms or elbows, it's often better to disengage and reset. Continuing to force the position when it's compromised can lead to you falling off or giving up your own position, so transition to a guard pass or a different control.

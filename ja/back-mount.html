Back Mount: The Ultimate Position in BJJ for White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

Back Mount is a dominant position in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu where you control your opponent from behind, facing their back.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of body mechanics and control.

The key is to establish control over the opponent's hips and maintain a strong base, preventing them from turning in or escaping.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From opponent's back, secure both of your opponent's hips with your legs, creating a 'body triangle' by placing your right foot behind your opponent's left knee and your left foot behind your opponent's right knee.
    2. Your left shin should be across your opponent's abdomen, and your right shin should be across their lower back, creating a tight, constricting grip.
    3. Place your chest firmly against your opponent's upper back, driving your weight downwards and slightly forward.
    4. Secure a "seatbelt grip" by placing your right arm over your opponent's left shoulder and your left arm under their right armpit.
    5. Interlock your hands in front of your opponent's chest, with your right hand gripping your left wrist or bicep.
    6. Keep your hips low, directly behind your opponent's hips, to prevent them from creating space.
    7. Maintain constant pressure by driving your chest into their back and keeping your legs tight.
    8. Use your head to lightly pressure one side of your opponent's head, encouraging them to face the opposite direction.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect leg entanglement: Extending legs too far apart or not securing the hook behind the knee can lead to the opponent escaping by bridging or turning.
    • Risk: Knee joint (ACL/MCL) hyperextension or sprain due to sudden twisting forces.
    • Correction: Ensure your foot is firmly hooked behind the opponent's knee and your shin is across their abdomen, maintaining a tight, connected structure.
    • Lifting hips too high: This creates a gap between your hips and the opponent's, allowing them to shrimp or roll.
    • Risk: Lumbar spine strain or facet joint compression from excessive arching.
    • Correction: Keep your hips low and tight, ensuring your pelvis is directly behind the opponent's.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling (0%): Practice securing the body triangle and seatbelt grip on a stationary object (e.g., a dummy or a willing training partner lying on their stomach). Focus on the feel of the leg and arm placement. (5 minutes, 10 reps)
    2. Controlled entry (25%): Partner lies on their stomach. You practice climbing to their back and securing the body triangle and seatbelt grip. Partner offers minimal resistance, just allowing you to practice the mechanics. (5 minutes, 10 reps)
    3. Passive resistance (50%): Partner attempts to defend by lightly shrugging shoulders or trying to create slight space, but does not actively resist being taken or controlled. Focus on maintaining your grip and base. (5 minutes, 10 reps)
    4. Active defense without escape attempt (75%): Partner actively tries to prevent you from establishing the seatbelt grip or body triangle, but does not attempt to escape the position once you have it. Focus on maintaining control against mild pressure. (5 minutes, 10 reps)
    5. Escape prevention (90%): Partner actively attempts to escape once you have Back Mount, while you focus on maintaining the position and preventing the escape. Partner is not actively attacking, just defending. (5 minutes, 10 reps)
    6. Live rolling (100%): Full sparring, focusing on achieving and maintaining Back Mount. Do not focus on submissions yet, just the positional control. (5 minutes, 1 round)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • After a successful takedown where you land behind your opponent.
    • When your opponent turtles defensively, exposing their back.
    • During scrambles where you can secure a dominant back position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Elbow-to-knee defense: Opponent brings their knees to their elbows and tucks their chin to their chest, creating a compact defensive shell. This makes it difficult to secure grips and apply pressure.
    • Bridging and rolling: Opponent attempts to bridge their hips upwards and roll to their side, attempting to dislodge you and escape the position.
    • Hand fighting: Opponent uses their hands to peel off your grips, specifically targeting your seatbelt grip and leg hooks, to create space and escape.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try to get Back Mount?

    Neck pain often occurs when you're trying to force the Back Mount by leaning your head into your opponent's head or neck. Instead, use your head as a light guide, applying pressure with your chest and shoulders. Your goal is to control their upper body with your torso and arms, not to engage in a head-to-head battle. Keep your chin tucked to protect your own neck.

    Q: How can I get Back Mount against a bigger opponent who is strong?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on leverage and precision over brute strength. Instead of trying to lift them, use their weight against them. When climbing to the back, ensure your hips are glued to their hips, preventing them from using their size to push you away. Secure your leg hooks tightly and use your chest pressure to maintain control, limiting their ability to generate power.

    Q: When is the best time to go for a submission from Back Mount as a white belt?

    As a white belt, your primary focus from Back Mount should be establishing and maintaining the position. Submissions like the Rear Naked Choke can be attempted once you have a secure body triangle and seatbelt grip, and your opponent is showing signs of fatigue or resignation to the position. However, prioritize control. Rushing a submission without solid control often leads to losing the position entirely.

Back Mount: The Ultimate Position in BJJ

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

Back Mount is a dominant positional control in BJJ achieved when you are behind your opponent, with your chest connected to their back. It allows for high-percentage submissions and control, minimizing their offensive options.

White belts often struggle with Back Mount due to a lack of understanding of body mechanics, leading to poor weight distribution and easily escapable holds. They focus on simply 'holding on' rather than actively controlling the opponent's structure.

The key mechanical insight is to create a stable 'seatbelt' grip and drive your hips into their hips, using your legs as hooks to control their base and prevent them from turning into you.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Secure the Seatbelt Grip: Right hand grips opponent's left collar, left hand grips opponent's right collar. Opponent's head should be on your right side.
    2. Place your right hip firmly against opponent's right hip, creating a solid connection.
    3. Drive your hips forward and slightly downward into opponent's hips, preventing them from creating space.
    4. Hook your right leg over opponent's left hip, with your shin across their body.
    5. Hook your left leg over opponent's right hip, with your shin across their body. Your feet should be 'squeezed' together, creating a stable 'body triangle' if possible, or at least maintaining tight hooks.
    6. Ensure your chest remains connected to opponent's upper back, preventing them from turning their head or shoulders towards you.
    7. Maintain a low center of gravity by keeping your hips down, making you harder to unbalance or remove from their back.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly hooking legs: Placing feet on opponent's hips without driving hips into their hips. This strains the knee ligaments (MCL/LCL) by allowing the knee joint to collapse inward or outward under pressure.
    • Gripping too high on the collar: Holding the collar near the shoulder. This allows opponent to easily create space by shrugging their shoulders, potentially injuring your wrist and elbow joints with hyperextension.
    • Letting hips fall back: Allowing your hips to drift away from opponent's hips. This disconnects your base, making your hooks less effective and opening up the possibility of opponent turning into you, risking your shoulder and neck joints if you are not in a stable position.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Grip & Hook Drill: Practice securing the seatbelt grip and placing hooks without a partner. Focus on hip connection. (50 reps each side, 0% resistance)
    2. Partnered Grip & Hip Drive Drill: With a compliant partner lying on their stomach, practice securing the seatbelt and driving your hips into theirs. (20 reps, 10% resistance)
    3. Hook Placement Drill: With partner on stomach, practice securing the seatbelt and then focusing on the precise placement and squeeze of your hooks. (30 reps, 15% resistance)
    4. Controlled Back Mount Transition: Start from a position where you can achieve Back Mount (e.g., opponent gives you their back). Practice securing the Back Mount without resistance. (15 reps, 25% resistance)
    5. Light Resistance Back Mount Hold: Partner offers light resistance to prevent you from establishing perfect Back Mount, but does not actively escape. Focus on maintaining control. (10 reps, 50% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling - Back Mount Focus: In sparring, dedicate rounds to achieving and maintaining Back Mount. Your partner will actively try to escape. (5 rounds x 3 minutes, 90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • After a failed takedown where your opponent ends up on their hands and knees.
    • When your opponent turtles to defend a submission or sweep, exposing their back.
    • During scrambles where you manage to get behind your opponent's hips.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Elbow-Knee Escape: When opponent has Back Mount, immediately bring your elbow and knee on the same side together to create a barrier. Then, drive your hips away to create space and turn into them, relieving pressure.
    • Bridging and Turning: If opponent's hooks are not fully locked, try to bridge your hips upwards and simultaneously turn your shoulders towards the side of their dominant hook. This can disrupt their base and allow you to escape.
    • Arm-In Defense: If opponent attempts a choke, tuck your chin to your chest and bring your arms across your neck, creating a barrier. Simultaneously, try to create space by shrugging your shoulders and turning your body to face your opponent.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my back hurt when I try to get to Back Mount in BJJ?

    Your back likely hurts because you are using your lower back muscles excessively to 'pull' yourself onto your opponent. Instead, focus on driving your hips forward into their hips and using your legs to 'hook' and control their base. This shifts the work to your glutes and hamstrings, providing a more stable and less strenuous base for Back Mount.

    Q: How do I get to Back Mount against a bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are critical. Focus on securing the seatbelt grip and immediately driving your hips into their hips to disrupt their base. Use your legs to hook their hips, preventing them from posturing up. Your weight should be on your hips and chest, not trying to 'climb' their back, which would allow them to use their size advantage.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from a failed takedown to Back Mount in BJJ?

    The ideal time is immediately after your takedown attempt fails and your opponent is on their hands and knees, or has exposed their back by turning away. As they are stabilizing their base, you should be moving your hips to get behind them. Prioritize securing the seatbelt grip and controlling their hips before they can fully recover their defensive posture.

Back Mount Position: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide to BJJ Dominance

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

Back Mount is a dominant position in BJJ where you control your opponent from behind, securing their back. It offers superior leverage for submissions and control, minimizing their ability to defend or escape.

White belts often struggle by focusing on 'holding on' rather than establishing proper biomechanical connections. This leads to them being easily peeled off or losing the position due to insufficient body alignment.

The key is to establish a secure 'seatbelt' grip and connect your hips to their hips, creating a unified structure that is difficult to break. Your weight should be distributed to anchor them.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Secure the 'Seatbelt' Grip: Place your left arm over your opponent's right shoulder, grabbing your right bicep with your left hand. Your right arm goes under their left armpit, grabbing your left bicep with your right hand. This grip distributes your force across their upper body.
    2. Hook Legs Inside: With your arms secured, drive your knees towards the mat, inside your opponent's thighs. Your shins should be pressing against the inside of their hamstrings, creating a 'hook' with your calves.
    3. Hip Connection: Lower your hips to connect them firmly with your opponent's hips. Your pelvis should be angled slightly forward, pushing your pubic bone into their lower back.
    4. Weight Distribution: Shift your weight onto your connected hips, driving your chest into their upper back. This pressure anchors them and prevents them from turning.
    5. Maintain Frame: Keep your elbows tucked in and your chest close to their back. Avoid leaving gaps that allow them to create space or escape.
    6. Head Position: Keep your head on one side of their neck, slightly above their shoulder, to maintain control and prevent them from turning into you.
    7. Control Escapes: If they try to peel your arms, reinforce your grip by squeezing your biceps. If they try to bridge, flatten your hips and drive your chest into them.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hooking: A white belt might flare their knees outwards instead of hooking inside. This can hyperextend the knees and damage the ACL/MCL ligaments by forcing the knee joint into an unnatural valgus or varus stress.
    • Over-Reaching for Grips: Grabbing loosely around the neck or shoulders without securing the bicep. This leaves the opponent's arms free to defend, and if they pull hard, the white belt's shoulder joint can be dislocated or the rotator cuff torn.
    • Losing Hip Connection: Allowing the hips to lift off the opponent's hips while trying to maintain an upright posture. This creates a gap, allowing the opponent to turn into you, potentially leading to a knee injury for the white belt if the opponent forcefully drives their hip into the white belt's knee.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Grip and Hook Practice: Lie on your back on the mat. Practice achieving the seatbelt grip and hooking your legs inside your own imaginary opponent. Focus on the feeling of the connection. (50 reps, 0% resistance).
    2. Partnered Grip and Hook Practice (No Movement): Have a partner lie on their stomach. Practice securing the seatbelt grip and hooking their legs without any resistance. Focus on making the connection tight. (25 reps, 0% resistance).
    3. Static Back Mount Control Drill: With a compliant partner in front of you, take the back mount position. Hold the position, focusing on maintaining hip connection and seatbelt grip for 30 seconds. Partner attempts to lightly resist but not actively escape. (10 reps, 25% resistance).
    4. Controlled Back Mount Entry and Hold: Start from a neutral position. Practice securing the back mount from a controlled entry (e.g., from a failed sweep or takedown defense). Hold for 30 seconds. Partner offers minimal resistance. (10 reps, 50% resistance).
    5. Dynamic Back Mount Entry and Control: Practice entering back mount from a simulated scramble or transition. Partner offers moderate resistance to your entry and escape attempts. Focus on maintaining control after entry. (5 reps, 75% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling - Back Mount Focus: During live rolling, actively seek to establish and maintain back mount. Your primary goal is to hold the position for as long as possible, rather than immediately going for a submission. (2 rounds, 90% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is on their hands and knees, presenting their back.
    • After a successful sweep or takedown where you end up behind them.
    • During scrambles where you can secure a dominant rear position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Elbow-Knee Escape: If the opponent has a loose seatbelt, bring your elbow to your knee on the side they are controlling, creating a frame to space out and turn into them.
    • Bridging and Turning: If the opponent's hips are not connected, bridge your hips upwards and turn your body towards their hips to face them.
    • Peeling Grips: Actively work to peel the opponent's arms off your body by creating frames with your own arms and shins, then turning towards the side of the loose grip.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my back hurt when I try to get Back Mount in BJJ?

    Your back likely hurts because you are arching your own back excessively to try and 'force' the position, instead of using your hips and core. Ensure your hips are connected to your opponent's hips, and drive your chest into their upper back. This allows you to use your skeletal structure for support, reducing strain on your lower back.

    Q: How do I get Back Mount against a bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is key. Focus intensely on securing a tight 'seatbelt' grip and connecting your hips to theirs. Drive your chest into their upper back to prevent them from using their weight to simply push you off. Use your legs to hook their thighs and maintain that connection, minimizing their ability to create space or drive forward.

    Q: When should I let go of Back Mount if I can't get a submission?

    You should only let go of Back Mount if you are about to lose the position entirely, or if it leads to a significantly worse position for you (e.g., you are swept). If you have a secure Back Mount, focus on maintaining the control and waiting for the opportunity. It's better to hold a dominant position with no submission than to give up the back and be in a defensive situation.

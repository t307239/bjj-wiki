Mastering the Americana in BJJ: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Americana is a shoulder lock typically initiated from side control or mount, aiming to hyperextend the opponent's shoulder joint. White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength instead of leverage and proper body mechanics.

The key to a successful Americana is isolating the opponent's arm and controlling their posture, creating a stable platform from which to apply the lock with minimal force.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side control, secure a 'figure-four' grip on the opponent's arm: place your right arm (if on their right side) under their armpit, gripping your own left wrist. Your left hand grips your right wrist.
    2. Ensure your chest is pressed firmly into the opponent's chest, preventing them from bridging or shrimping away. Your hips should be low and tight.
    3. Place your left forearm across the opponent's neck, creating a crossface to control their head and prevent them from turning in.
    4. Your right knee should be positioned near the opponent's head, acting as a strong base and preventing them from posturing up.
    5. Your left knee should be tucked close to the opponent's hip, further limiting their movement and maintaining your base.
    6. Begin to lift the opponent's wrist towards their head, while simultaneously keeping their elbow pinned to the mat. This begins to isolate the shoulder joint.
    7. With your figure-four grip, slowly and deliberately begin to extend your arms, pushing their wrist further away from their body and slightly upwards. This creates internal rotation and hyperextension of the shoulder.
    8. Maintain constant pressure with your chest and crossface. The lock is applied by the rotation of their arm, not by you pulling their arm up forcefully.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying excessive upward force with your arms without controlling the opponent's posture: This can lead to a torn rotator cuff or biceps tendon as the shoulder is forced into a vulnerable position.

    The correct movement is to maintain control of their posture and then rotate their arm, using your body as a lever.

    • Letting the opponent's elbow lift off the mat during the lock: This allows them to 'hide' the pressure and can strain the elbow joint instead of the shoulder.

    Keep their elbow firmly pinned to the mat throughout the execution of the lock.

    • Gripping too loosely or incorrectly on the figure-four: This allows the opponent to escape their arm or creates a weak connection, leading to ineffective pressure and potential wrist injury for you.

    Ensure a tight figure-four grip with your wrist against their bicep and your forearm across their forearm.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling of the figure-four grip and weight distribution: 10 repetitions per side, 0% resistance.
    2. Partner drilling of the Americana setup from side control without the submission attempt: 10 repetitions per side, 25% resistance (partner is passive).
    3. Partner drilling of the Americana submission attempt from side control, focusing on isolating the arm and initiating the lock: 10 repetitions per side, 50% resistance (partner offers light, controlled defense).
    4. Partner drilling of the full Americana submission from side control, with the defender tapping early: 10 repetitions per side, 75% resistance.
    5. Drilling Americana from mount, ensuring proper weight distribution and arm isolation: 10 repetitions per side, 75% resistance.
    6. Live rolling, focusing on creating opportunities to attack the Americana when the opportunity arises: 5 minutes, 90-100% resistance.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have established solid side control and the opponent is trying to shrimp or bridge, creating an opening for arm isolation.
    • When the opponent attempts to escape from mount, exposing their arm as they try to create space.
    • When you have secured a strong crossface and can prevent the opponent from turning into you, allowing you to secure their arm.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Shoulder Roll/Shrimp: If the opponent attempts the Americana while you are in side control, tuck your chin, keep your elbows tight to your body, and shrimp your hips away to create space and break their posture. Then, attempt to recover guard.
    • Bridge and Recover Guard: If the Americana is being applied and you feel pressure, bridge your hips to create a slight gap and immediately shrimp your hips to regain your guard. This disrupts their base and leverage.
    • Defend with the other arm: If the Americana is being applied, use your free arm to push against the opponent's elbow or forearm, creating a frame to prevent the rotation and potentially escape their grip.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Americana submission in BJJ?

    Your wrist likely hurts because you are using your wrist as the primary lever to apply the lock, rather than your entire body and shoulder girdle. This happens when you don't secure a proper figure-four grip, allowing the opponent's arm to move freely. Focus on gripping your own wrist securely and using your chest and hips to drive the rotation, keeping your wrist in a neutral, stable position.

    Q: How do I do the Americana BJJ submission against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise body positioning become even more critical. Ensure your chest is glued to their chest and your hips are low to prevent them from using their weight to flatten you. Use your crossface to control their head and prevent them from turning in. The key is to isolate their arm and use your body weight to create the rotational pressure, rather than trying to overpower them.

    Q: When is the best time to try the Americana in BJJ if I'm a white belt?

    As a white belt, the best time to attempt the Americana is when you have established a dominant position like side control or mount, and your opponent is making a clear mistake by leaving their arm exposed. Look for moments when they are trying to bridge or shrimp away, or when they are attempting to escape from mount. Avoid trying it when they have good posture or are actively defending, as this is when they can easily counter.

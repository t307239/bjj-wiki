Americana: The Essential BJJ Joint Lock for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Americana is a shoulder lock typically applied from side control or mount, aiming to hyperextend the opponent's shoulder joint for a submission.

White belts often rush the setup, neglecting control and creating openings for escapes or counter-attacks.

The key to a successful Americana is isolating the opponent's arm and controlling their body to prevent them from creating space, using leverage over strength.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side control, secure your opponent's right arm with your left arm, gripping their wrist with your left hand (S-Grip).
    2. Place your right arm under their elbow, creating a "figure-four" grip by interlocking your right fingers with your left wrist.
    3. Shift your weight forward, driving your chest into their chest, reducing their ability to bridge.
    4. With your left hand, control their right wrist, keeping it close to their head and slightly elevated.
    5. Your right arm acts as a fulcrum; keep your right elbow pointed towards the mat.
    6. Slowly lift your left hand (controlling their wrist) upwards, while simultaneously pushing your right elbow downwards.
    7. This movement creates a rotational force on the shoulder joint, leading to the submission.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying excessive downward pressure with your chest on the opponent's face/neck: This can cause cervical spine hyperextension or compression, leading to neck pain or injury.

    Correct alternative: Maintain chest-to-chest pressure, focusing on driving your hips forward and pinning their shoulder, not their head.

    • Lifting the opponent's arm too high or too far away from their body during the setup: This compromises your control and can strain the rotator cuff or cause shoulder dislocation.

    Correct alternative: Keep the opponent's arm tucked close to their body, with their elbow pointing towards the mat, maintaining a tight frame.

    • Jerking motions instead of a controlled lift and press: Sudden movements can surprise the opponent but also cause uncontrolled stress on their shoulder, risking ligament tears.

    Correct alternative: Execute the final submission mechanics with smooth, controlled pressure, increasing slowly until tap.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the grip changes and weight shifts from side control without a partner. (10 reps per side)
    2. Partnered drilling (0% resistance): Execute the entire Americana sequence from side control with a compliant partner. Focus on smooth transitions. (5 reps per side)
    3. Partnered drilling (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to practice establishing grips and controlling movement. (5 reps per side)
    4. Partnered drilling (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to defend, but you can still isolate the arm and initiate the Americana. (5 reps per side)
    5. Partnered drilling (75% resistance): Partner defends more actively, requiring you to maintain control and adjust your body positioning. (3 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Attempt the Americana during rolling, focusing on timing and proper execution under pressure. (Focus on 5 attempts)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have established a solid side control position and the opponent's arm is accessible.
    • When the opponent attempts to bridge or shrimp away, creating an opportunity to isolate an arm.
    • When the opponent has their arm extended or trapped between your body and theirs.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Escape by bridging and shrimping: As the opponent attempts the Americana, bridge your hips up and shrimp your body away, creating space to pull your arm out.
    • Posture up and create space: If the opponent's control is loose, sit up and pull your arm out while driving your elbow towards your hip.
    • Spin under: If the opponent has a loose grip, you can sometimes spin your body underneath their arm to escape the position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Americana submission in BJJ?

    Your wrist likely hurts because you are relying on wrist strength to apply the lock, or your grip is incorrect. The Americana uses the opponent's own arm as a lever. Ensure your S-Grip is on their wrist, not their fingers, and that your secondary grip (figure-four) is secure. Focus on driving your elbow down and lifting their wrist up, using your body's leverage, not just your wrist's power.

    Q: How do I finish the Americana BJJ submission against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a bigger opponent, leverage and control are paramount. Do not try to muscle it. Ensure you have their arm pinned securely to their side with your chest and hips. Your secondary grip (figure-four) should be tight, and your elbow pointed down. Focus on a slow, controlled elevation of their wrist while simultaneously driving your elbow down. Their size makes the shoulder joint more resistant to quick movement, so patience and precision are key.

    Q: When is the best time to go for the Americana in BJJ if I'm a white belt?

    The best time for a white belt to attempt the Americana is when you have comfortably established side control and the opponent's arm is not actively defended. Look for moments when they are trying to shrimp away or bridge, as this often leaves an arm exposed. Prioritize maintaining control of their hips and shoulder before attempting the grip and submission mechanics to avoid giving them an easy escape.

Anaconda Choke: A Biomechanically Precise Guide for BJJ White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

The Anaconda Choke is a submission applied from various positions, most commonly when the opponent is on their hands and knees or in your guard. It aims to constrict blood flow to the brain by compressing the carotid arteries. White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength rather than precise leverage and body positioning. The key mechanical insight is to use your shoulder and bicep to create a fulcrum against the opponent's neck and shoulder, driving your hips forward to complete the choke.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side control or a similar dominant position, secure a "figure-four" grip: your right arm wraps under the opponent's neck, and your right hand grips your own right bicep. Your left arm slides across the opponent's back, securing your right wrist. 2. Turn your body to face the opponent's hip, creating a perpendicular angle between your torso and theirs. 3. Drive your left shoulder into the opponent's chest, forcing them to lean forward and away from your choking arm. 4. Initiate a "hip drive" by lifting your hips and driving them forward towards the opponent's head. 5. Tuck your chin to your chest and slightly lift your head, preventing your own neck from being compromised. 6. Maintain constant pressure on the opponent's neck and shoulder with your figure-four grip. 7. Continue the hip drive and slight body rotation until the opponent taps. The pressure is created by the combined force of your hip drive and the tight figure-four grip constricting the carotid artery.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect grip: Grabbing your own wrist loosely instead of your bicep allows the opponent to escape the "figure-four" by posturing up, potentially leading to a strained shoulder joint on your part if you overextend trying to maintain the grip. The correct grip is a tight "figure-four" on your own arm, creating a stable fulcrum.
    • Poor body positioning: Turning your back completely to the opponent's head instead of facing their hip allows them to easily turn into you, creating a "stacking" pressure that can injure your neck and spine. Maintain a slight angle, facing their hip, and drive your shoulder into their chest.
    • Relying on arm strength: Trying to "squeeze" the choke with arm strength alone, without the hip drive, puts immense pressure on your own shoulder joint, risking a rotator cuff tear. The power comes from driving your hips forward, using your body weight as leverage.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the "figure-four" grip and hip drive motion without a partner, focusing on the body rotation and weight transfer (10 reps each side). Resistance: 0%.
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Secure the Anaconda Choke grip from a static position (e.g., opponent on hands and knees). Partner allows you to complete the hip drive and choke simulation (10 reps each side). Resistance: 10%.
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): From a dominant position, initiate the Anaconda Choke. Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the pressure build. Focus on maintaining the grip and hip drive (10 reps each side). Resistance: 25%.
    4. Partner drill (controlled resistance): Apply the Anaconda Choke from side control. Partner attempts to defend by posturing up or turning. You focus on maintaining your base and completing the choke with proper mechanics (10 reps each side). Resistance: 50%.
    5. Live rolling (opportunity-based): In live rolling, look for opportunities to set up the Anaconda Choke. Focus on correct execution and timing, but don't force it if the opportunity isn't there (5 minutes). Resistance: 75%.
    6. Live rolling (application focus): Dedicate rolling rounds to actively seeking and applying the Anaconda Choke, emphasizing the biomechanical principles learned. Focus on finishing the choke correctly when the opportunity arises (5 minutes). Resistance: 90%.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent turtles up, exposing their neck.
    • When you have side control and the opponent tries to bridge or shrimp away, creating an opening.
    • When applying from guard, and the opponent is posturing up or trying to pass your guard.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • "Stacking Defense": If the opponent attempts to stack you, immediately drive your hips into their chest and turn your body to face their hip, creating a strong base and preventing the choke from tightening. This uses your body weight to resist their pressure.
    • "Breaking the Grip": If the opponent has a tight "figure-four" grip, focus on peeling their choking arm away from your neck and shoulder by creating space with your free arm and shrugging your shoulders upwards. This disrupts the fulcrum.
    • "Turning Into the Choke": If the choke is being applied, try to turn your body *into* the choking arm and shoulder, creating a "wall" with your own body. This reduces the space for the choke to tighten and can create opportunities to escape.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try to do the Anaconda Choke?

    Your neck likely hurts because you are tucking your chin too far forward or relying on neck strength to finish the choke. The Anaconda Choke should constrict blood flow, not apply pressure directly to the trachea or neck muscles. Ensure you tuck your chin slightly to protect your own neck and drive your hips forward to create the choke's pressure, not by squeezing with your arms or straining your neck.

    Q: How can I finish the Anaconda Choke against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise body mechanics are paramount. Focus on creating a tight "figure-four" grip and then driving your hips forward with maximum force, aiming to get your hips over their shoulder. The key is to use their weight against them by creating a fulcrum with your shoulder and bicep, driving their head down and their shoulder into their own neck, rather than trying to overpower them with arm strength.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Anaconda Choke from guard?

    The Anaconda Choke from guard is most effective when the opponent is posturing up, trying to create space to pass, or when they are leaning forward. As they posture, you can snatch the "figure-four" grip around their neck and shoulder. Then, as they try to push down, you drive your hips up and backward, creating a counter-pressure that cinches the choke. Avoid attempting it when they are flat on their back, as it offers less leverage.

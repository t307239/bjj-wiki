The Anaconda Choke is a powerful submission initiated from various grappling positions, aiming to constrict the carotid arteries. It's often applied when an opponent attempts to pass your guard or during scrambles.

White belts often struggle because they rely on brute strength instead of leverage and precise body positioning. This leads to wasted energy and potential injury.

The key insight is to use your own body as a lever, creating a fulcrum with your shoulder and driving your hips forward to cinch the choke.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a front-headlock position (e.g., opponent's head is inside your armpit), secure a "S-grip" by placing your right hand under their chin, thumb pointing down, and grabbing your own right bicep with your left hand.
    2. Your right arm should be wrapped around the opponent's neck, with the back of your right hand pressing into their throat/trachea.
    3. Position your hips lower than the opponent's hips, creating a slight forward lean.
    4. Drive your left shoulder into the opponent's chest, forcing their head and shoulder to tilt away from your choking arm.
    5. As you drive your shoulder, simultaneously extend your hips forward and slightly upward, creating a pulling tension on the choke.
    6. Rotate your torso counter-clockwise, bringing your chest closer to the opponent's back.
    7. Maintain pressure by squeezing your biceps and driving your hips forward, ensuring your shoulder acts as the primary pressure point.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying pressure with just your arms: This can strain your shoulder joint and elbow ligaments, leading to rotator cuff tears or sprains. The correct method uses hip drive and body rotation for leverage.
    • Hyperextending the neck backwards: If the opponent defends by arching their back excessively, you might inadvertently force their neck into an unnatural and painful extension. Instead, focus on driving your shoulder forward and keeping your head position stable.
    • Gripping too loosely on the S-grip: A loose grip allows the opponent to escape their head. Always ensure a tight, secure S-grip by pulling your bicep firmly towards your chest.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the S-grip and shoulder-driving motion with a training dummy or pillow, focusing on hip placement. (50 reps)
    2. Solo drill: Practice the full sequence of body movement without a partner, imagining the opponent's resistance. (30 reps)
    3. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the grips and body positioning, receiving feedback. (20 reps each side)
    4. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the pressure and hip drive. (15 reps each side)
    5. Controlled rolling (50% resistance): Attempt the Anaconda Choke from the specified position, allowing your partner to defend lightly. (5 attempts)
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Attempt the Anaconda Choke during sparring, focusing on timing and proper execution. (Continuous attempts)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When an opponent is posturing up from your closed guard and you can isolate one arm and their head.
    • During a scramble where you end up in a front-headlock position.
    • When your opponent attempts a guard pass and their head and arm become vulnerable.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • "The Wall" defense: The opponent uses their free arm to create a "wall" by posting their hand on your hip or thigh, preventing your hip drive and creating space to escape.
    • "The Turtle": The opponent tucks their chin and rolls onto their hands and knees, relieving pressure from the choke. You can then transition to a back-mount.
    • "The Escape Grip": The opponent uses their free hand to grab your choking arm's wrist and pull it away, breaking the choke's integrity. You must maintain grip strength and continue hip drive.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Anaconda Choke?

    Wrist pain often stems from an incorrect S-grip. If your hand is angled too sharply or you're relying on wrist flexion, it strains the joint. Ensure your forearm is parallel to the ground and the pressure comes from your biceps and shoulder, with your wrist in a neutral position to maintain the S-grip securely.

    Q: How do I apply the Anaconda Choke against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on getting your hips significantly lower than theirs to create a better angle. Drive your shoulder into their chest with maximum force, using your entire body weight. Don't try to overpower them; instead, use their size against them by creating a tight fulcrum with your shoulder and driving your hips forward to constrict.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Anaconda Choke in BJJ?

    The optimal time is when you have established a dominant front-headlock position, often after a failed guard pass attempt by your opponent or during a scramble. Look for moments when their head is trapped and their posture is compromised, making it difficult for them to defend effectively. Timing is crucial; attempting it too early or too late can lead to an easy escape.

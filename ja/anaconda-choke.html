The Anaconda Choke is a powerful submission initiated from various grappling positions, often when the opponent's head and one arm are aligned. It aims to constrict blood flow to the brain by compressing the carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength rather than precise mechanics, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury. They may also fail to secure the proper grip and body alignment.

The key to the Anaconda Choke is creating a "figure-four" grip with your arms around the opponent's neck and arm, then using your body to drive forward and tighten the constriction.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side control or a similar dominant position, secure your opponent's head and the arm on that same side. Your opponent's head should be angled away from you.
    2. Wrap your right arm around the opponent's neck, bringing your hand to the inside of your left bicep. This creates the initial "figure-four" structure.
    3. Your left arm then snakes under the opponent's trapped arm and your left hand grips your right wrist. Ensure your left forearm is against the opponent's shoulder blade.
    4. Drive your hips forward and slightly to your left, angling your pelvis towards the opponent's hips. This creates leverage and prevents them from posturing up.
    5. Maintain a tight head control with your right arm's bicep and shoulder, preventing them from pulling their head out.
    6. Squeeze your elbows together, drawing your hands towards your chest. Simultaneously, arch your back and drive your chest into the opponent's shoulder.
    7. The pressure comes from your forearms and the constriction of your bicep and shoulder against their neck, amplified by your body's forward drive.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly applying pressure with your forearm directly across the windpipe instead of the carotid artery can cause discomfort and restrict breathing, not blood flow, and may damage the trachea. Always aim for the side of the neck. The correct application is to drive your bicep into the carotid artery.
    • Applying a "figure-four" grip with insufficient shoulder and bicep engagement, leading to excessive pressure on the opponent's shoulder joint or your own shoulder. This can cause rotator cuff tears or impingement in both individuals. Ensure your bicep is firmly against their neck and your forearm is behind their head.
    • Trying to force the choke by pulling upwards or sideways with your arms without proper hip drive and forward pressure. This can hyperextend your own elbows and shoulders, leading to ligamentous tears in the elbow or shoulder joint. Instead, focus on driving your hips forward and arching your back.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the grip sequence and body positioning without a partner. Focus on the "figure-four" arm placement and hip drive motion. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the full motion of securing the choke, focusing on correct grip and body alignment. (5 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    3. Static grip drill: With your partner in a static position, practice securing the Anaconda choke grips and applying controlled pressure. Ensure they tap early. (10 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled positional drill: Start from side control. Your partner offers minimal resistance as you work to establish the Anaconda choke. Focus on finishing the submission. (5 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Light sparring (flow rolling): Attempt the Anaconda choke during live rolling, but prioritize maintaining control and position over forcing the submission. Your partner can defend but not actively resist. (3 rounds, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the Anaconda choke into your sparring sessions, attempting it when opportunities arise. Your partner will actively defend. (5 rounds, 90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • From side control when the opponent turtles or tries to bridge away, exposing their neck and arm.
    • During transitions when the opponent attempts to escape from mount or side control, creating an opportunity to isolate their arm and neck.
    • From the guard when the opponent postures up aggressively or attempts to pass, allowing you to snatch their head and arm.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • When the Anaconda choke is being applied, the opponent can defend by: tucking their chin tightly to their chest, which prevents the attacker's bicep from reaching the carotid artery. The attacker must then adjust their grip or abandon the choke.
    • If the opponent's head is free, they can turn into the attacker's chest, creating space and relieving pressure on the neck. This requires a strong hip escape and forward drive from the defender.
    • The defender can also attempt to relieve pressure by posturing up and creating distance with their free arm. This is most effective if the attacker's grip is not fully secured or if they are not driving their hips forward effectively.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck feel pinched and not choked when I try the Anaconda Choke?

    This typically happens when your bicep isn't positioned correctly on the carotid artery. Instead of wrapping your arm around their neck and squeezing, ensure your bicep is pressing into the side of their neck, and your forearm is behind their head. The constriction comes from your bicep and shoulder against the artery, not just squeezing your arms together.

    Q: How can I use the Anaconda Choke against a much bigger and stronger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise mechanics are paramount. Focus on securing a tight "figure-four" grip and driving your hips forward aggressively to create a strong base. Do not rely on pulling power; instead, use your body weight and angle to apply pressure. Ensure their head is angled away from you to maximize the choke's effectiveness.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to an Anaconda Choke from the guard?

    The most opportune moment from guard is when the opponent is posturing up, attempting to pass, or has their head and one arm extended forward. You can then "catch" their head and arm as they move in, transitioning to the Anaconda Choke before they can fully establish their base or pass. A well-timed arm drag can also lead into this submission.

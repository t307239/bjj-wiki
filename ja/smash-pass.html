The Smash Pass is a dominant guard passing technique initiated from a standing or kneeling position against a closed guard, aiming to break the opponent's legs and establish side control.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of weight distribution and failing to create a stable base, leading to reversals.

The core principle is to use your body weight and angled pressure to collapse the opponent's guard structure, not brute force alone.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From kneeling, secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a cross-sleeve grip with your left hand on the opponent's gi.
    2. Angle your hips 45 degrees to your left, bringing your left knee close to the opponent's right hip.
    3. Drive your weight forward through your hips, creating a downward and forward pressure.
    4. Use your right hand to pull their left collar down and slightly across their body, breaking their posture.
    5. Simultaneously, use your left hand to pull their right sleeve towards your left hip, further disrupting their balance.
    6. Step your right foot forward, placing your knee on the mat inside their left thigh, creating a strong base.
    7. Drive your right shoulder into the opponent's chest, keeping your hips low and your base wide, and advance your left knee towards their hip.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Extension Injury: Attempting to 'push' their legs open with extended knees can hyperextend the knee joint, potentially tearing the ACL or MCL.

    *Correct:* Keep knees bent and drive forward with your hips and shoulder pressure.

    • Wrist Sprains/Fractures: Grabbing sleeves with an open palm and twisting forcefully can wrench the wrist, leading to ligament damage.

    *Correct:* Use a firm grip on the gi fabric, not just the wrist, and control the arm's movement.

    • Lower Back Strain: Arching your back excessively to create pressure instead of using hip drive can cause lumbar disc issues.

    *Correct:* Maintain a neutral spine and drive pressure from your hips and core.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling: Practice the footwork, hip angles, and grip placements without a partner (5 reps).
    2. Partnered Isolation (No Resistance): Execute the pass with a compliant partner, focusing on exact body mechanics (10 reps per side).
    3. Passive Guard Resistance: Partner attempts to maintain closed guard but offers no active defense, focus on breaking the guard (15 reps per side).
    4. Light Resistance (25%): Partner offers mild resistance, focusing on maintaining your base and pressure (20 reps per side).
    5. Moderate Resistance (50%): Partner actively tries to maintain guard and hip escape, focus on adapting your pressure (30 reps per side).
    6. Live Rolling (75-90%): Implement the Smash Pass during live rolling, aiming for successful passes and avoiding reversals (5-minute rounds).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent establishes a tight closed guard and their hips are relatively still.
    • When the opponent attempts to open their guard but hasn't fully transitioned.
    • When you have established a strong kneeling base and control of both collar and sleeve.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Recovery: The opponent can attempt to shrimp their hips away as you drive forward, creating space to re-establish guard.
    • Leg Pummeling: The opponent can try to pummel their legs inside yours, attempting to create a butterfly guard or regain guard.
    • Hip Bump Sweep: If the passer overcommits and becomes too upright, the opponent can use a hip bump sweep by bridging and turning.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to Smash Pass?

    Your wrist likely hurts because you are gripping the opponent's sleeve with an open palm and twisting forcefully. This puts direct stress on the wrist joint. Instead, grip the fabric of the sleeve firmly with your fingers and control the arm's movement by pulling it towards your hip, not by twisting your wrist.

    Q: How do I Smash Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a bigger opponent, focus more on the hip angle and weight distribution than purely on pulling. Drive your hips into them at a 45-degree angle, using your shoulder to create a wedge. Secure a deep cross-collar grip and pull their head down and across their body to break their posture before attempting to collapse their legs.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Smash Pass from a kneeling position?

    The optimal time is when the opponent has their legs locked in a closed guard and their hips are relatively static, preventing easy escapes. Look for moments when they try to open their guard or adjust their position, as this creates a slight structural weakness you can exploit with precise pressure.

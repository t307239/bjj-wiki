Smash Pass: A White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Smash Pass is a powerful guard passing technique initiated from a standing or kneeling position against an opponent's guard, aiming to break their legs apart and secure side control.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of precise body mechanics, relying on brute force instead of leverage and weight distribution.

The core principle is to use your body weight and leverage to collapse the opponent's legs inward, creating a direct path to their torso.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand, palm facing your own face, and your left hand on the opponent's left hip.
    2. Position your left knee on the mat, directly to the outside of the opponent's left hip, creating a strong base.
    3. Drive your right shoulder into the opponent's left hip socket, initiating forward pressure.
    4. Simultaneously, rotate your torso counter-clockwise, angling your hips towards your right.
    5. Use your left hand to pull the opponent's left hip forward and inward, towards your chest.
    6. Shift your weight forward, driving your right shoulder deeper into their hip, forcing their legs to collapse inward.
    7. As their legs collapse, step your right foot forward and around their hips to establish side control, maintaining pressure.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly driving your shoulder into the opponent's knee joint instead of the hip socket can cause ligament tears (ACL, MCL) due to shearing forces.
    • Failing to maintain a solid base with your left knee and hip will lead to you being swept or losing pressure, potentially causing knee hyperextension.
    • Grabbing the opponent's ankle or foot with your left hand and yanking it outwards creates excessive torque on their ankle and knee, risking sprains and ligament damage.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip angle, shoulder drive, and leg collapse motion without a partner (10 reps per side).
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Focus on precise grip placement and weight transfer, with the partner passively allowing the pass (20 reps per side).
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): The partner offers minimal resistance, focusing on maintaining guard; you execute the pass (30 reps per side).
    4. Partner drilling (medium resistance): The partner actively tries to maintain guard and prevent the pass, but not to counter aggressively (40 reps per side).
    5. Live rolling (controlled): Attempt the Smash Pass against a resisting opponent for 2-minute rounds, focusing on execution over success (5 rounds).
    6. Live rolling (full): Integrate the Smash Pass into your regular rolling practice, aiming for successful passes (ongoing).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent has a closed guard and you have established a strong cross-collar grip and hip control.
    • When the opponent attempts to transition to an open guard and their legs are slightly separated.
    • When you have successfully broken down their posture and are in a kneeling position on their guard.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape and Frame: If the opponent is pressing into your shoulder, immediately hip escape away and create a frame with your forearm between your hips and their shoulder.
    • Re-guarding with Legs: As they drive their shoulder, use your free leg to hook their hip and pull it away, then bring your legs back to establish guard.
    • Shucking the Grip and Sweeping: If they overcommit to the shoulder pressure, use a strong grip on their arm and hip to shuck their pressure off and initiate a sweep.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try the Smash Pass?

    Your shoulder likely hurts because you are driving your shoulder directly into the opponent's knee or shin instead of their hip socket. This misalignment creates shearing forces on the shoulder joint and surrounding ligaments. Ensure your shoulder is aimed at the bony prominence of their hip, and your body is angled to use your body weight to collapse their legs, not to force your shoulder through their leg.

    Q: How do I Smash Pass against a significantly larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise biomechanics are even more critical. Focus on breaking their posture first, then establish a deep cross-collar grip and control their hip. Instead of driving straight forward, emphasize the inward rotation of your hips and the inward pull of their hip with your free hand. This leverages their weight against them, allowing you to collapse their legs rather than trying to overpower them.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from a Smash Pass attempt to another pass?

    You should switch if the opponent successfully frames against your shoulder drive, preventing their legs from collapsing, or if they manage to maintain a strong base and prevent your hip rotation. If they create a strong barrier with their legs and hips, and you cannot create an inward collapse, continuing to force the Smash Pass can lead to you being swept or ending up in an unfavorable position. At this point, transition to a knee-slice or leg-drag pass.

スマッシュパス：白帯必須のBJJテクニック

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Smash Pass is a pressure-based guard pass initiated from a standing or kneeling position against an opponent's guard. Its goal is to break their leg structure and establish side control.

White belts often struggle by trying to force the pass with raw strength, neglecting the crucial alignment and leverage required.

The key insight is to use your body weight and hip pressure to collapse the opponent's base, not just push their legs away.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Secure a deep cross-collar grip with your right hand and a strong sleeve grip on the opponent's left sleeve with your left hand.
    2. Stance: Adopt a low, wide base with your knees bent and hips below your opponent's hips. Your weight should be forward, leaning into them.
    3. Hip Angle: Drive your right hip towards the opponent's left hip, creating a perpendicular angle between your torso and their legs.
    4. Pressure Application: Simultaneously, pull the collar grip down and across your body while pushing the sleeve grip forward and slightly down.
    5. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your right foot and right hip, driving forward and down through the opponent's legs.
    6. Leg Collapse: Use your left knee to drive into the opponent's left hip crease, further collapsing their guard.
    7. Pass and Control: As their guard breaks, step your right leg over their body and bring your left leg through, securing side control with your hips low and chest tight.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Movement: Grabbing the opponent's pants and attempting to rip them off while standing upright.

    Injury Risk: Twisting your own knee or ankle ligaments due to unstable base and lack of rotational control, especially if the opponent resists.

    Correct Movement: Maintain a low base, use collar and sleeve grips, and drive forward with hip pressure to collapse the guard.

    • Incorrect Movement: Pushing directly on the opponent's knees to break their guard.

    Injury Risk: Ligament tears in your own knees or ankles from absorbing the opponent's resistance and potential counter-sweeps.

    Correct Movement: Focus on collapsing the hip structure with your own hip and knee pressure, not just pushing the legs.

    • Incorrect Movement: Arching your back and lifting with your arms to create space.

    Injury Risk: Lumbar spine strain and potential disc injury from improper spinal loading.

    Correct Movement: Keep your core engaged and use your legs and hips to generate power and drive.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Grip and Stance Drill: Practice acquiring the correct collar and sleeve grips and sinking into the low, wide base. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Static Guard Collapse Drill: Opponent holds a static closed guard. Practice the hip angle, weight transfer, and leg collapse motion without moving the opponent significantly. (20 reps per side, 10% resistance)
    3. Controlled Guard Break Drill: Opponent offers minimal resistance. Practice the full sequence, focusing on smooth transitions and maintaining pressure. (30 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Semi-Resisted Guard Pass: Opponent attempts to maintain guard or make minor adjustments. Focus on driving through their attempts and securing side control. (20 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Resisted Guard Pass (Light): Opponent actively tries to defend and reset their guard. Focus on maintaining forward pressure and adapting to their movements. (15 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling (Focus): Implement the Smash Pass against a resisting opponent, dedicating rounds to its successful execution. (5-minute rounds, 90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is holding a tight closed guard and is not actively attempting to shrimp or create space.
    • When the opponent's hips are relatively flat on the mat, making it harder for them to generate upward or lateral momentum.
    • When you have established a strong collar and sleeve grip, preventing them from easily breaking your posture.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Reset: If the opponent feels the pressure, they can immediately attempt to shrimp their hips away and re-establish their guard or distance.
    • Technical Stand-up: If the Smash Pass is failing and the opponent is gaining control, they can initiate a technical stand-up to create space and return to a neutral standing position.
    • Hip Escape to Guard: The opponent can use a strong hip escape combined with their legs to create enough space to bring their knees back to their chest and reform their guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my elbow hurt when I try to Smash Pass from closed guard?

    Your elbow pain likely stems from overextending your arm while trying to push the opponent's leg away. This puts excessive strain on the elbow joint. Instead of pushing directly, focus on driving your shoulder and chest into their hip and using your collar grip to pull their head and shoulder towards you, collapsing their base biomechanically.

    Q: How can I Smash Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, raw strength is less effective. Prioritize precise biomechanics: get your hips low and perpendicular to theirs, creating a leverage advantage. Use your collar grip to break their posture and pull their upper body towards you as you drive your shoulder into their hip, collapsing their base rather than trying to push their mass.

    Q: When is the best time to use the Smash Pass versus a Knee Slice Pass?

    The Smash Pass is most effective against opponents who maintain a relatively flat and static guard, where their hips are not actively bridging or shrimping. The Knee Slice Pass is generally better against opponents who are more active in framing and attempting to create space, as it allows you to isolate a leg more directly and drive through their guard.

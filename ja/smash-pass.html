The Smash Pass is a dominant guard passing technique initiated from the opponent's guard, aiming to break their leg structure and establish side control.

White belts often struggle by trying to force movement rather than understanding leverage, leading to stalled passes and potential injury.

The core principle is to use your weight and shoulder pressure to collapse their base, creating an opening for your hips to drive through.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand, palm facing down, thumb inside the lapel. Your left hand secures their left sleeve grip, palm facing up.
    2. Angle your hips at approximately 45 degrees to your opponent's left hip, creating a slight off-balancing force.
    3. Drive your right shoulder down towards their left hip bone, simultaneously pulling their left sleeve towards your right hip.
    4. As you apply pressure, step your right foot outside their left hip, keeping your knee bent and foot planted.
    5. Shift your weight forward and down, driving your shoulder into their hip and continuing the sleeve pull. This collapses their guard.
    6. With their guard broken, immediately step your left foot forward, bringing your left knee to the mat inside their leg structure.
    7. Drive your hips forward and down, flattening your chest onto their torso, securing a cross-face with your left arm and establishing side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly driving your head forward instead of your shoulder: This can hyperextend your cervical spine, leading to neck pain or injury.
    • Trying to 'muscle' the pass by pulling with your arms alone: This puts excessive strain on your shoulder and elbow joints, risking tears or sprains.
    • Not angling your hips sufficiently, leading to a head-on push: This can cause knee valgus stress on your own knees, potentially damaging ligaments.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip angle, shoulder drive, and arm movements without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Partner maintains a neutral closed guard. Execute the Smash Pass steps slowly, focusing on body mechanics. (20 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the guard collapse. (30 reps per side)
    4. Controlled passing drill: Partner actively defends with light pressure, but allows you to progress through the pass. (40 reps per side)
    5. Flow rolling (25% resistance): Focus on executing the Smash Pass when opportunities arise, without prioritizing speed. (5 minutes)
    6. Live rolling (50% resistance): Apply the Smash Pass in a more dynamic environment, focusing on timing and reaction. (5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent has a tight closed guard and is posturing up.
    • When you can isolate one of their legs with your own leg.
    • When your opponent attempts a sweep and creates an opening.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Recovery: If the opponent feels their guard collapsing, they can immediately bring their free leg back across their body to re-establish guard.
    • Hip Escape to Open Guard: As the pass begins, the defender can shrimp their hips away and open their guard, creating distance.
    • Knee Shield Setup: The defender can use their knee to create a frame against the passer's shoulder, preventing the drive and allowing them to recompose guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to Smash Pass?

    Shoulder pain often occurs when white belts use their arms to pull the sleeve and collar instead of using their body weight and hip rotation. Ensure your shoulder is driving downwards into their hip, and your grip on the sleeve is used to help collapse their base, not to yank them over.

    Q: How can I Smash Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on achieving a deep cross-collar grip and controlling one of their legs. Use your hip angle to create a leverage point, driving your shoulder into their hip rather than trying to overpower them. The goal is to break their base, not their strength.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate the Smash Pass from closed guard?

    The optimal time is when your opponent is posturing up or trying to create space. Look for moments when their weight is forward, making their base less stable. If they are flat on their back with hips close, other passes are usually more effective.

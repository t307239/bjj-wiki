The Smash Pass is a dominant guard passing technique initiated from a standing or kneeling position, aiming to break down and flatten the opponent's guard.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of weight distribution and leverage, leading to stalled passes or injury.

The key insight is using your shoulder and hip to drive downwards, creating a heavy base to stifle their leg retention.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From kneeling guard pass stance: secure a "collar grip" with your right hand on their left collar and a "same-side sleeve grip" with your left hand on their left sleeve.
    2. Shift your weight onto your right knee, placing it on the mat slightly behind and to the side of their hip.
    3. Drive your right shoulder downwards into their hip, creating a fulcrum.
    4. Simultaneously, extend your left arm, pulling their sleeve towards your left hip to control their arm and prevent frames.
    5. Rotate your hips counter-clockwise, aligning your left hip with their left hip.
    6. Drive your body weight forward and down, flattening their guard against the mat with your shoulder pressure.
    7. Once their legs are flattened, step your right leg forward to establish side control, maintaining head and arm control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly driving your shoulder into their knee can hyperextend the knee joint, risking ACL or MCL tears. Always aim for the hip or upper thigh.
    • Grabbing the pants too high on the thigh and pulling upwards can torque the opponent's knee or ankle, causing ligament damage. Instead, use a firm sleeve grip and control their posture.
    • Trying to force a pass by simply pushing with your hands without proper hip and shoulder engagement can strain your lower back. Focus on driving your body weight through your shoulder and hips.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo movement: Practice shoulder driving and hip rotation with no partner (50 reps each side).
    2. Static partner drill: Opponent lies flat, you perform the Smash Pass steps with no resistance (20 reps each side).
    3. Light resistance drill: Opponent offers minimal resistance (e.g., lightly holding grips), you execute the pass (30 reps each side, 25% resistance).
    4. Controlled resistance drill: Opponent actively tries to maintain guard but doesn't counter aggressively (20 reps each side, 50% resistance).
    5. Moderate resistance drill: Opponent uses standard guard retention techniques (15 reps each side, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the Smash Pass into sparring situations (5 rounds, 90-100% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is actively trying to close their guard and you have established a kneeling passing stance.
    • When the opponent attempts a spider guard or lasso guard and you can secure a strong collar and sleeve grip.
    • When the opponent's hips are slightly elevated, creating an opening for your shoulder to penetrate.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Framing with forearm: The opponent uses their free arm to push against your shoulder and hip, creating space to re-establish guard. Maintain continuous pressure and keep your head down.
    • Leg escape: The opponent pulls their leg back and steps over your head to transition to a back take. Be ready to disengage and recompose your guard or defend the back take.
    • Hip escape and re-guard: The opponent hips away and attempts to bring their knees back to center. Use your grips to control their posture and maintain your pressure.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try the Smash Pass?

    Shoulder pain often arises from improper weight distribution and a lack of connection between your shoulder and hip drive. You might be using your shoulder as a lever against their knee or hip directly, instead of using it as a pressure point while driving your entire body weight down and forward. Ensure your shoulder is driving into their hip crease or upper thigh, and your hips are rotating to support that pressure. Focus on a 'smashing' motion that originates from your core and travels through your shoulder.

    Q: How can I Smash Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing become paramount. Instead of relying solely on brute strength, focus on generating angles and using their own weight against them. Secure a strong collar grip to break their posture and pull them slightly forward. Then, use your shoulder and hip to drive downwards into their hip, creating a fulcrum. Your goal is to flatten them out, making their size less of an advantage. Prioritize controlling their hips and head to prevent them from using their mass effectively.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Smash Pass from a standing guard pass?

    The Smash Pass is most effective from a standing guard pass when the opponent's guard is slightly open or when they are trying to initiate a guard recovery. As you step in to pass, look for an opportunity to secure a deep collar grip and a sleeve grip. Immediately drive your shoulder into their hip as you begin to flatten them out. Avoid attempting it if their guard is completely closed and they have a strong, stable base, as this will require more static pressure and less dynamic movement.

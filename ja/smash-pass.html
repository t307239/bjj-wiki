Smash Pass Technique for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Smash Pass is a powerful guard passing technique initiated from top half-guard or side control, aiming to flatten the opponent and establish side control. White belts often struggle by relying on raw strength rather than leverage, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The core principle is to break the opponent's posture and create a clear path to pass their legs, collapsing their base by driving their hips and shoulders in opposite directions. This requires precise weight distribution and controlled pressure, not just brute force.

Understanding the mechanics of hip rotation and shoulder pressure is crucial. By driving your shoulder into their hip and your opposite hand into their shoulder, you create a powerful lever that can break their guard structure. This allows for a clean pass.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting Position: Top half-guard, opponent's left knee inside your right thigh.
    2. Grip: Secure a collar grip with your right hand on the opponent's far collar (left side) and your left hand on their left sleeve near the elbow.
    3. Hip Angle: Lower your hips so they are below the opponent's hips, creating a strong base and driving force.
    4. Weight Transfer (Initial): Drive your right shoulder into the opponent's left hip, compressing it towards the mat.
    5. Grip Adjustment: Shift your left hand from the sleeve to their left shoulder or upper back, creating a cross-face pressure.
    6. Weight Transfer (Pass): As you drive your shoulder into their hip, simultaneously pull their shoulder towards you and slightly across your body.
    7. Leg Movement: Step your right leg over their hips, clearing their legs as your weight shifts fully to the mat on your right side.
    8. Final Control: Immediately establish side control, keeping your chest tight to their chest and your hips low.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hip Drive: Driving your hips forward and up instead of down and into the opponent's hip. This can lead to your own knee or ankle hyperextension, damaging the ACL or ATFL.
    • Swaying Upper Body: Leaning back with your upper body to compensate for lack of hip pressure. This exposes your neck to chokes and your arms to submissions like the armbar.
    • Gripping Too Wide: Grabbing the collar from too far away or the sleeve with an open hand. This reduces your leverage and allows the opponent to easily escape the grip, often leading to wrist sprains from the sudden release.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Drive: Practice lowering your hips and driving your shoulder into an imaginary opponent's hip, focusing on the weight transfer. (50 reps)
    2. Solo Grip and Drive: Add the collar and shoulder grips, simulating the movement without resistance. (50 reps)
    3. Partner Resistance (25%): With a compliant partner, practice the entire sequence with minimal resistance, focusing on timing and hip movement. (5 sets of 5 reps)
    4. Partner Resistance (50%): Increase partner's resistance slightly, focusing on breaking their posture and clearing their legs. (5 sets of 5 reps)
    5. Partner Resistance (75%): Apply the pass with more intent, focusing on finishing the pass to side control. (3 sets of 3 reps)
    6. Live Rolling (90%): Attempt the Smash Pass during rolling with a focus on execution, not necessarily success. (5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is in half-guard and their hips are angled away from you.
    • When the opponent's knees are close together, limiting their ability to create frames.
    • When you have secured a strong collar and sleeve grip, allowing you to control their posture.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Retention: If the opponent attempts the Smash Pass, immediately re-establish your guard by bringing your knees between you and them, creating frames with your forearms.
    • Shrimping to Guard: As the opponent drives their shoulder into your hip, shrimp your hips back to create space and bring your legs back to closed guard.
    • Re-Guard: If the Smash Pass is initiated, focus on connecting your feet to create a frame and then use your hips to push away and re-establish guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try the Smash Pass?

    Your shoulder likely hurts because you are shrugging your shoulder upwards and forward, rather than driving it down and into the opponent's hip. This puts undue stress on your rotator cuff. To fix this, focus on keeping your shoulder blade retracted and driving your *humerus* (upper arm bone) into their hip, using your lats for power. Ensure your grip is secure and your weight is transferring through your hips and core.

    Q: How do I Smash Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision become even more critical than raw strength. Focus on getting your hips *below* their hips to generate upward lifting pressure before driving your shoulder into their hip. Use your grips to break their posture, pulling their collar and shoulder towards you to create a slight angle. Then, drive your shoulder into their hip at that angle to start collapsing their base.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate the Smash Pass from half-guard?

    The optimal time to initiate the Smash Pass from half-guard is when the opponent's hips are slightly turned away from you, and their knees are not fully locked out to create a strong base. If they are squared up and actively framing, you will have a harder time generating the necessary leverage. Look for moments where their posture is slightly compromised or their hip mobility is restricted.

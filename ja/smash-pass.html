Smash Pass Technique Guide for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Smash Pass is a top-tier guard passing technique initiated from standing or kneeling positions against an opponent's guard. Its goal is to break down their leg structure and establish side control.

White belts often struggle because they rely on brute strength rather than precise body mechanics, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The core insight is using your shoulder to drive their knee downwards, collapsing their base and opening a path to pass their legs.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand, palm facing your own face, thumb inside the collar.
    2. Secure a cross-waist grip with your left hand, gripping the opponent's gi pants near the hip bone.
    3. Position your right shoulder directly over the opponent's knee on the same side as your collar grip (their left knee if you have a right collar grip).
    4. Drive your weight forward and slightly to your right, creating a forward pressure.
    5. Simultaneously, use your right shoulder to push the opponent's knee downwards and inwards towards their body.
    6. As their knee collapses, step your left foot outside their hip, creating a wide base.
    7. Drive your hips forward and down, bringing your chest close to their torso while maintaining head pressure.
    8. Transition your right hand from the collar to secure a cross-face grip or establish side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly applying pressure with your head: This can hyperextend the cervical spine or cause neck pain, especially if the opponent attempts a guillotine choke.
    • Correct: Maintain a neutral head position, driving with your shoulder and hips, not your head directly.
    • Lunging forward with straight legs: This exposes your knees to potential hyperextension or direct impact if the opponent shucks you.
    • Correct: Keep a slight bend in your knees and drive forward with your hips, maintaining a balanced base.
    • Over-extending your arm for the collar grip: This can lead to shoulder impingement or tears if the opponent resists strongly or counters with an armbar.
    • Correct: Keep your elbow slightly bent and your shoulder engaged, ensuring a controlled grip.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling: Perform the entire sequence of grips and body movements without a partner. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Partnered Isolation (No Resistance): Practice the grip establishment and the shoulder drive to collapse the knee, with a compliant partner. (25 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    3. Partnered Knee Collapse and Base Step: Focus on driving the knee down and stepping the foot outside the hip. (25 reps per side, 10% resistance)
    4. Partnered Pass to Side Control: Execute the full pass, aiming to secure side control. Partner offers minimal resistance to establishing the pass. (20 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    5. Controlled Passing with Light Resistance: Your partner actively tries to maintain guard but offers no aggressive counters. Focus on maintaining pressure. (15 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling - Focused Attempt: During rolling, dedicate specific rounds to attempting the Smash Pass when the opportunity arises. (5 rounds, 75% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent has their knees close to their chest in closed guard, creating a clear target for the shoulder drive.
    • When the opponent attempts to open their guard and create space, providing an opening for your pressure.
    • When you have secured a strong grip on their pants and collar, allowing you to control their base.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Recovery (Knee Shield): If the opponent attempts to regain guard, quickly bring your knee between your chest and their shoulder to re-establish distance.
    • Sweep Attempt (Hip Bump): If the opponent overcommits to the pass and their weight is too far forward, use your hips to bump them off-balance and attempt a sweep.
    • Counter-Pass (Leg Drag): If the opponent's shoulder drive is weak, you can sometimes transition to a leg drag by securing their leg and pulling it across your body.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to Smash Pass their knee?

    Your shoulder may hurt due to improper weight distribution and a lack of core engagement. Instead of driving directly with your shoulder, think of it as a fulcrum. Your weight should be driven forward through your hips and torso, with your shoulder acting to guide their knee downwards. Ensure your elbow is not fully locked out when applying pressure.

    Q: How can I Smash Pass a bigger opponent who has strong legs?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on leveraging their own weight against them. Secure your grips first, ensuring you have a strong collar and waist grip to break their posture. Then, instead of a direct shoulder drive, use your shoulder to slightly lift and then drive their knee downwards. This small elevation combined with the downward pressure can be more effective than sheer force.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate a Smash Pass against closed guard?

    The ideal time to initiate the Smash Pass from closed guard is just as the opponent begins to open their guard or hip escape. As they try to create space, their base is momentarily compromised. This is when your shoulder drive can be most effective in collapsing their legs before they can fully re-establish their guard.

X-Guard Sweep: A Biomechanically Precise Guide for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The X-Guard Sweep starts from an open guard position where one of your legs is between the opponent's legs, creating an X-shape. Its goal is to off-balance and invert the opponent to secure a dominant position.

White belts often struggle by not establishing a strong base or by attempting to muscle the sweep without proper leverage, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key mechanical insight is using your hips to create a fulcrum and your legs to drive the opponent's base away, generating rotational force for the sweep.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip opponent's right ankle with your left hand (palm facing up, thumb inside). Simultaneously, grip opponent's left pant leg just above the knee with your right hand.
    2. Position your right leg hook deep on the opponent's left hip crease, with your knee pointing towards their left shoulder.
    3. Drive your left foot flat on the mat, slightly behind your right hip, creating a stable base.
    4. Shift your weight onto your left foot and right hip, angling your pelvis towards the opponent's left side.
    5. Initiate the sweep by extending your right leg forcefully, pushing their left hip away from your body.
    6. Simultaneously, pull their right ankle towards your chest while 'kicking' your right leg to drive their base outwards.
    7. As they fall, pivot your hips and drive your shoulder into their hip to secure a dominant top position, transitioning to side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to lift the opponent's ankle directly upwards without hip extension: This places extreme torque on the opponent's knee and ankle, risking ACL tears or ankle sprains.
    • Using only arm strength to pull the ankle: This can lead to shoulder impingement or biceps tears due to improper leverage and force application.
    • Allowing your supporting leg to collapse inward: This compromises your base, leading to a loss of balance and potential knee ligament strain if the opponent counters with weight.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice leg placement, grip acquisition, and hip angle shifts without a partner (10 reps each side).
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): Partner holds a neutral stance. Practice the full sweep motion, focusing on grip and body mechanics (5 reps each side).
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers slight resistance to your leg hook and ankle grip. Focus on overcoming minimal pushback (5 reps each side).
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to post their foot or maintain balance. Practice maintaining your structure and drive (5 reps each side).
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner attempts basic guard pass defenses. Focus on maintaining control and executing the sweep under light pressure (5 reps each side).
    6. Live rolling (90-100% resistance): Attempt the sweep during rolling, prioritizing safety and learning over successful execution (5 attempts total).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is posturing up and their weight is forward, creating an opening.
    • When the opponent is attempting a guard pass and their weight is distributed between their legs.
    • When the opponent is on their knees or in a turtle position, with one leg extended.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Posting the foot: If the opponent posts their foot on your hip, maintain your ankle grip and use your free leg to push their posted foot away or shrimp your hips to create space.
    • Reguarding: If the opponent manages to bring their legs back in, transition to a closed guard or knee shield to reset your position.
    • Stacking: If the opponent stacks you, keep your hooks tight and use your hips to create a wedge, preventing them from collapsing your structure.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the X-Guard Sweep?

    Your knee might hurt if your right leg hook isn't deep enough in the opponent's hip crease, or if you're not using your hips to drive their base away. Ensure your knee points towards their shoulder and your extension comes from your hips, not just your leg. This prevents hyperextension and reduces stress on your knee joint.

    Q: How can I X-Guard Sweep a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on precise timing and leverage rather than brute force. Secure a deep ankle grip and a strong pant grip. Use your hip angle to create a fulcrum and drive their weight away; the goal is to rotate them, not lift them. Maintain a tight base with your supporting leg to prevent them from crushing you.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from X-Guard Sweep to a different sweep?

    If the opponent successfully defends your initial sweep attempt by posting their foot strongly or regaining their base, it's wise to switch. Immediately transition to a closed guard or a knee shield to reset. Alternatively, if they sprawl heavily, you might consider transitioning to a leg drag attempt if the opportunity arises.

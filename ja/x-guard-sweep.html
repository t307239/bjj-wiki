Mastering the X-Guard Sweep in BJJ: A White Belt's Precise Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The X-Guard Sweep originates from a specific open guard position where you control one of your opponent's legs with both of yours, forming an 'X' shape. Its goal is to elevate and unbalance your opponent to transition to a dominant position.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of leverage and frame creation, leading to pushing rather than using proper body mechanics. This results in wasted energy and potential injury.

The core mechanical insight is using your hips as a fulcrum and your legs as levers to create a sweep by controlling the opponent's base and lifting their center of gravity.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Secure a strong collar grip with your right hand, thumb inside the gi, and a sleeve grip on your opponent's left sleeve with your left hand. Ensure your elbow is tucked to your side.
    2. Leg Placement: Place your right foot on your opponent's left hip crease, with your knee bent at 90 degrees. Your left foot hooks behind your opponent's left knee pit.
    3. Body Angle: Lower your hips to the mat, creating a slight angle with your torso relative to your opponent. Your left shoulder should be close to your opponent's left hip.
    4. Weight Distribution: Shift your weight onto your left hip, keeping your hips elevated off the mat. Your right leg acts as a post, not for pushing.
    5. Leverage Creation: Use your right foot on the hip to create a frame and prevent your opponent from posturing up. Your left leg controls their knee angle.
    6. Initiate Sweep: Simultaneously, drive your hips forward and upward while pulling their collar grip towards your opposite shoulder (left shoulder). Extend your left leg to push their knee away.
    7. Completion: As their base breaks, drive your opponent over your right leg, which acts as a pivot point. Immediately transition to mount or side control as they fall.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Injury: Attempting to push the opponent's leg outwards with your foot on their hip while your knee is also providing support can cause your knee to buckle. This can lead to ACL or MCL tears.
    • Correction: Maintain a strong frame with your foot on the hip and use your hips to drive *through* the opponent, not push their leg directly. Keep your supporting knee stable.
    • Ankle/Foot Sprain: Gripping too tightly around the opponent's knee pit with your foot and then having them step or twist unexpectedly can hyperextend your ankle or foot.
    • Correction: Use a hooking motion behind the knee pit, not a crushing grip. Focus on controlling the *angle* of their leg, not squeezing.
    • Lower Back Strain: Trying to lift the opponent by arching your back and using arm strength alone, rather than hip drive, puts excessive strain on your lumbar spine.
    • Correction: Ensure your hips are actively driving forward and upward. Your arms are for control and unbalancing, not brute force lifting.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Escape & Frame Drill: Practice hip escapes and driving your hips up while maintaining the X-guard leg configuration (no opponent). 50 reps.
    2. Partnered Frame Drill: With a willing partner in a seated position, practice establishing the X-guard grips and leg positions, focusing on creating a strong frame with your right foot on their hip. Partner offers minimal resistance. 25% resistance. 10 reps per side.
    3. Controlled Sweep Drill: Partner lies on their back. You establish X-guard. Focus on the coordinated hip drive and pull to execute the sweep, with the partner gently falling. 50% resistance. 5 reps per side.
    4. Assisted Sweep Drill: Partner actively tries to resist by posturing up or moving their leg slightly. You execute the sweep, and they react to the movement. 75% resistance. 5 reps per side.
    5. Live Rolling - X-Guard Focus: Roll with a partner, specifically aiming to enter X-guard and attempt sweeps. Focus on timing and body mechanics. 90% resistance. 3 minutes.
    6. Full Live Rolling: Integrate X-guard sweeps into regular sparring. Focus on recognizing opportunities and executing the sweep efficiently. 100% resistance. 5 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is postured up and their weight is forward, creating an opportunity to unbalance them.
    • When your opponent attempts a guard pass, and you can intercept their leg to establish X-guard.
    • When you are in your own closed guard and can transition to an open guard like X-guard to initiate a sweep.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS (Defenses Against X-Guard Sweep):
    • The "Stack" Pass: When the X-guard is established, the defender can drive their hips forward and down, stacking their weight onto the X-guard player's hips and shoulders. This crushes the X-guard frame and can transition to a pass.
    • "Knee to Chest" Defense: The defender can bring their free leg (the one not controlled by the X-guard) to their chest and use it to push away the X-guard player's hips, breaking the frame and creating space to escape.
    • "Leg Release" Sweep: The defender can attempt to pull their trapped leg out by straightening it and driving their foot into the X-guard player's hip, forcing a release and potentially creating an opportunity for a sweep themselves.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my hip hurt when I try the X-Guard Sweep?

    Hip pain often arises from attempting to push the opponent's leg away with your foot on their hip, rather than using your hips as the primary driving force. This leads to your hip joint absorbing excessive lateral force. Ensure your hips are elevated and driving *through* the opponent, using your right leg as a stable post, not an active pushing lever.

    Q: How do I X-Guard Sweep against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paramount. Focus on securing a strong collar grip and controlling their posture to prevent them from using their weight advantage. Use your hips to create a tight frame on their hip and drive *upward* and *forward*, aiming to lift their center of gravity. Don't try to muscle them; use precise timing and biomechanics.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from X-Guard Sweep to a different sweep?

    You should consider switching if your opponent successfully stabilizes their base by flattening their weight, or if they manage to stack you effectively. If their knee is too far away from your hook, or if they are defending the collar grip well, it might be time to transition. Recognizing these defensive postures is key to effective sweep selection.

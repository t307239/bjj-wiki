X-Guard Sweep: A Biomechanically Accurate BJJ Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The X-Guard Sweep originates from a specific open guard position, aiming to off-balance your opponent and transition to a dominant position. It's a fundamental sweep for controlling leg entanglements.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength or incorrect angles, leading to loss of balance and potential injury. They forget the core principle of leverage.

The single most critical mechanical insight is creating an 'X' shape with your legs around one of the opponent's legs, using your hips and core to generate upward and forward momentum.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Opponent is in your guard, ideally with one knee on the mat or their weight shifted forward.
    2. Secure a collar grip with your right hand on their left collar, and a sleeve grip with your left hand on their left sleeve.
    3. Place your left foot on their left hip bone, ensuring your knee is bent at approximately 90 degrees and your shin is parallel to the mat.
    4. Slide your right leg *between* their legs, hooking behind their left knee with your shin and foot, creating an 'X' shape with your legs.
    5. Your right hip should be angled slightly towards your left, creating a base.
    6. Simultaneously, pull their left collar and sleeve towards your chest while driving your left foot *into* their hip.
    7. Shift your weight forward and slightly upward, using your left foot as a pivot and your right leg's hook to elevate their leg and disrupt their base, causing them to fall forward.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee hyperextension: Applying the sweep with straight legs and attempting to 'push' the opponent over can hyperextend your own knee if they resist heavily, potentially tearing the ACL or MCL.
    • Correct: Maintain a slight bend in your knees throughout the movement, focusing on the 'pull' and hip drive.
    • Ankle sprain: Gripping the opponent's leg too low and attempting to 'kick' it out can lead to your ankle rolling inwards or outwards if their weight shifts unexpectedly.
    • Correct: Hook behind the knee with your shin and maintain control of the upper thigh/hip area.
    • Hip impingement: Driving your hips forward too aggressively without proper framing can cause your own hip joint to jam, leading to pain or cartilage damage.
    • Correct: Ensure your left foot is firmly planted on their hip and use your core to drive upwards, not just your hips.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill (0% resistance): Practice entering the X-Guard position and achieving the 'X' leg configuration, focusing on grip placement and hip angle. (10 reps).
    2. Partner drill - static (25% resistance): Partner stands or kneels, you enter X-Guard. Practice the leg hook and hip placement. Partner offers minimal resistance to entry. (10 reps).
    3. Partner drill - controlled push (50% resistance): You enter X-Guard. Partner gently pushes forward with their hands. Practice the pull-and-drive motion without attempting the sweep. (10 reps).
    4. Partner drill - controlled sweep attempt (75% resistance): You enter X-Guard. Partner offers light resistance as you attempt the sweep. Focus on the exact timing of the pull and drive. (15 reps).
    5. Partner drill - dynamic sweep (90% resistance): Partner actively tries to resist or posture up. You execute the sweep, focusing on the biomechanical chain reaction. (20 reps).
    6. Live rolling (100% resistance): Attempt the X-Guard Sweep in sparring, prioritizing technique and safety over successful completion. (1-minute rounds, focus on sweep attempts).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • Opponent is postured up, creating space between your legs.
    • Opponent is trying to pass your guard by stepping over your legs.
    • Opponent has one knee on the mat and their weight is forward, making them susceptible to a forward sweep.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Posture Up and Base: Opponent immediately drives their hips back and down, widening their base and pulling their arms to their chest, negating the lever.
    • Knee Slice Defense: Opponent brings their free leg (the one not hooked) forward to create a strong frame against your hip or shoulder, preventing the upward drive.
    • Leg Push Out: Opponent forcefully straightens their hooked leg and pushes their foot away from your body, breaking the 'X' and creating space to regain base.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the X-Guard Sweep?

    Your knee pain likely stems from hyperextension or impingement. If you are straightening your legs to 'push' the opponent, or if their resistance causes your knee to bend beyond its natural range of motion, you risk injury. Always maintain a slight bend in your knees and focus on using your hips and core to drive, not your leg strength alone.

    Q: How do I X-Guard Sweep against a much bigger and heavier opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes even more critical. Ensure your left foot is firmly planted on their hip bone, creating a solid pivot point. Focus on timing your pull and drive when they are slightly off-balance or leaning forward. Use your entire body weight to generate the sweep, rather than trying to lift them with just your legs.

    Q: When is the right time to switch from an X-Guard Sweep attempt to another move?

    If your opponent successfully defends by posting a strong base or pulling their leg out, do not force the sweep. This is the opportune moment to transition. If they create space, you can transition to a single leg takedown attempt, or if they've defended by posturing, you might be able to enter into a triangle choke or armbar setup from your guard.

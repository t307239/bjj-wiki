X-Guard Sweep: A White Belt's Guide to BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The X-Guard Sweep is initiated from an open guard position, aiming to unbalance and elevate your opponent to achieve a dominant position.

White belts often struggle because they lack the foundational understanding of weight transfer and leverage, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The core principle is creating a strong 'X' shape with your legs, using your ankles as hooks to control their base and drive your hips upwards.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a standard open guard, with both your feet on your opponent's hips. Your knees should be bent at approximately 90 degrees, creating a strong base.
    2. Select one leg to initiate the 'X'. Hook your ankle behind your opponent's knee on the same side, ensuring your shin is perpendicular to their thigh.
    3. Bring your other leg to the outside of your opponent's thigh on the opposite side. Your foot should be placed high on their thigh, near the hip crease.
    4. Create a strong frame with your arms by gripping their biceps or the inside of their elbows. Keep your elbows tucked to your body to maintain connection.
    5. Drive your hips forward and slightly upwards, simultaneously extending the leg that is hooked behind their knee. This action starts to lift their base.
    6. As you extend the hooked leg, pull your opponent's opposite leg towards your chest using your arm grip. This breaks their posture and shifts their weight.
    7. Use your leg on the outside of their thigh to drive them away from you, creating space to elevate your hips further and initiate the sweep.
    8. As their weight shifts, drive your hips upwards and over your opponent's base, rolling them to the side and following to a dominant position like side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Leg Extension: Extending your hooked leg too forcefully without proper hip drive can hyperextend your knee. This can damage the anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) or medial collateral ligament (MCL).
    • Over-Rotation of the Spine: Trying to generate sweep power solely through thoracic spine rotation, instead of hip drive, can strain or sprain spinal ligaments.
    • Gripping Too Wide: Gripping the opponent's pants too low or wide reduces your ability to control their base. This can lead to your opponent posturing up and collapsing your guard, potentially causing your knees to impact the mat forcefully.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Elevation Drill: From a seated position, practice driving your hips up and forward, mimicking the extension of the hooked leg. Perform 20 repetitions.
    2. Static X-Guard Entry: Partner sits on the mat. Practice entering the X-Guard position, placing your hooks and outside leg without any movement. Hold for 10 seconds, repeat 10 times.
    3. Controlled Sweep Entry: Partner offers minimal resistance (0% resistance). Practice the full sequence of entering X-Guard and performing the hip drive and leg extension. Perform 10 repetitions.
    4. Light Resistance Sweep: Partner provides light, controlled resistance (25% resistance). Focus on timing your hip drive with their attempts to posture up. Perform 15 repetitions.
    5. Simulated Sweep: Partner actively tries to defend by posturing (50% resistance). Practice maintaining your X-Guard structure and executing the sweep mechanics. Perform 20 repetitions.
    6. Live Rolling Application: Apply the X-Guard Sweep in live rolling situations (75-90% resistance). Focus on recognizing opportunities and executing the fundamental mechanics under pressure. Perform 5-10 attempts per roll.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up and leaning forward in your closed guard.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard by stepping their legs inside your own.
    • When your opponent is kneeling directly in front of you, creating an easy target for your hooks.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass Defense: If your opponent stacks you, flatten your hips to the mat and drive your knees towards your chest, creating space to escape. Simultaneously, use your arms to push their hips away.
    • Knee-on-Belly Transition: If your opponent stabilizes their base and prevents the sweep, transition your hips to the side and bring your knee on the mat to their hip, establishing a knee-on-belly position.
    • Guard Retention: If the sweep is failing, immediately reset your guard by bringing your feet back to their hips and re-establishing your frames. Avoid getting stuck in a compromised position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try the X-Guard Sweep?

    Ankle pain often stems from the hooked leg. If you're not driving your hips forward and extending your leg properly, you might be 'bending' your ankle backwards to create tension. Ensure your shin is perpendicular to their thigh and your hip drive is the primary force, not ankle flexion.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paramount. Focus on using your hips to elevate their base, rather than trying to muscle them. Ensure your 'X' is tight, with your hooks deep behind their knees. Drive your hips upwards and slightly forward, creating a fulcrum point to lift their weight.

    Q: If your initial X-Guard setup isn't leading to a sweep, you can switch legs. As you drive your hips forward, if you feel your opponent's weight is not shifting optimally, you can release the hook on one leg and immediately re-establish it on the other, while simultaneously repositioning your outside leg for a new angle of attack.

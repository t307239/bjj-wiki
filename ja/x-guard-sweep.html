Precise X-Guard Sweep Technique for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The X-Guard Sweep initiates from a specific open guard position where your legs are positioned to attack the opponent's legs. Its goal is to unbalance and invert the opponent, creating an opportunity to take the top position.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of precise limb control and understanding of leverage points. They try to force the sweep with raw strength instead of coordinated body mechanics.

The key insight is using your legs as levers, specifically your shin to create pressure on the opponent's hip and your foot to control their ankle, while your body acts as the fulcrum.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Secure an overhook on the opponent's far leg at the hip crease with your near-side arm. Simultaneously, place your other hand on the opponent's ankle of the same leg, fingers pointing towards their toes.
    2. Leg Placement: Insert your outside leg (the one not overhooking the hip) between the opponent's legs, placing your shin across their hip joint, perpendicular to their torso.
    3. Base Adjustment: Your inside leg (the one controlling the ankle) should be bent, with your foot flat on the mat, providing a stable base. Your hips should be slightly off the mat, creating space.
    4. Head Position: Drive your head into the opponent's hip on the side of the leg you are attacking. This prevents them from posturing up and creating a strong base.
    5. Shin Pressure: Apply upward pressure with your shin against the opponent's hip. This pressure should be directed slightly towards their centerline, causing them to lean away.
    6. Ankle Control: Maintain a firm grip on the opponent's ankle. As you apply shin pressure, use your ankle grip to slightly pull their leg across your body.
    7. Hip Elevation and Rotation: Simultaneously, elevate your hips off the mat and rotate your torso away from the opponent. This action drives their weight onto the leg you are controlling.
    8. Sweep Execution: As their weight shifts, extend your outside leg (the one on the hip) while simultaneously pulling their ankle across your body. This creates a powerful lever that sweeps their leg out from under them.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Hyperextension: Attempting to drive your shin forward forcefully without proper hip angle can cause your knee to buckle inwards, leading to ACL or MCL tears. Correct: Focus on driving your hip *up* and *towards* the opponent's centerline, using your shin as a pivot point.
    • Ankle Sprain/Tear: Gripping the ankle too loosely and allowing the opponent to twist their foot can lead to ankle inversion or eversion injuries. Correct: Maintain a secure grip on the entire foot, cupping the heel and controlling the toes, keeping your wrist firm.
    • Hip Flexor Strain: Pushing off the mat with excessive force while keeping your hips too low can overstretch your hip flexors. Correct: Ensure your hips are elevated off the mat *before* initiating the sweep, creating a strong base and leverage.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Repetition (0% resistance): Practice the grip changes, leg placements, and hip movements without an opponent. Focus on smooth transitions and correct body angles. (50 reps)
    2. Partnered Isolation (25% resistance): Your partner lies on their back. You practice entering X-guard, establishing grips, and performing the leg sweep motion. Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the movement. (30 reps per side)
    3. Controlled Entry (50% resistance): Partner from closed guard. You break their grip, enter X-guard, and attempt the sweep. Partner offers light resistance to posture up or defend slightly, allowing you to practice your timing. (20 reps per side)
    4. Guard Retention Focus (75% resistance): Partner attempts to pass your guard from X-guard. You focus on maintaining your X-guard position and executing the sweep when they overcommit to a pass. Partner actively tries to pass. (15 reps per side)
    5. Sweep Completion Focus (90% resistance): Partner attempts to defend the sweep actively. You focus on executing the sweep to completion, aiming to land in a dominant position. Partner uses defensive posture and limb control. (10 reps per side)
    6. Live Rolling (100% resistance): Integrate the X-Guard Sweep into live rolling scenarios. Focus on recognizing opportunities to enter X-guard and execute the sweep. Allow for natural defense and counters. (5 minutes, focus on sweep attempts)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When opponent is posturing up from your guard, creating space for your legs to enter.
    • When opponent attempts a guard pass, especially a knee-slice or toreando, as their weight distribution becomes predictable.
    • When opponent is kneeling or has one knee on the mat, making their base unstable.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass Defense: If the opponent stacks you, immediately widen your base by pushing their hips away with your free leg and bringing your hips back to the mat. If you have the ankle grip, pull it across your body to disrupt their stack.
    • Knee Shield/Frame: If the opponent tries to drive their knee into your hip to break your guard, use your free foot to push their knee away, creating space and re-establishing your shin-on-hip frame. If you have the ankle grip, use it to pull them forward.
    • Hip Escape and Re-Guard: If they successfully break your leg control, immediately hip escape to create distance and re-establish a strong guard position, such as closed guard or a basic open guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try the X-Guard Sweep?

    Your ankle is likely hurting because you are gripping the opponent's foot too loosely, allowing their ankle to twist unnaturally as you attempt the sweep. The pressure should be on their tibia and fibula, not the delicate bones of the ankle joint itself. Ensure your grip cups the heel and controls the toes, and that your shin is the primary lever, not a twisting force on their foot.

    Q: How do I X-Guard Sweep against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes even more critical. Focus on precise hip elevation and rotation to create the imbalance. Instead of trying to lift them, think of driving their weight onto the leg you control by creating a strong lever with your shin and ankle grip. Use your head to anchor to their hip to prevent them from posturing up and using their weight against you.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to X-Guard for a sweep?

    The optimal time to transition to X-Guard for a sweep is when the opponent is actively posturing up, creating space between your legs, or when they are committing to a guard pass. These moments offer a predictable weight distribution that you can exploit. Avoid entering X-Guard when they are already in a very stable, low base, as it will be much harder to generate leverage.

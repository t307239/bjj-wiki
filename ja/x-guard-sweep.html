This sweep originates from the X-guard, aiming to off-balance and elevate your opponent to achieve a superior position.

White belts often struggle due to insufficient understanding of leverage and framing, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The core concept is to create a strong leg-over-leg structure and use your hips to drive upwards, breaking their base.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish an Open Guard: Opponent is in your closed guard. Break their posture by gripping both sleeves with your hands, palms facing down.
    2. Transition to X-Guard: As opponent attempts to pass, drop one leg (e.g., right leg) between their legs, hooking their right thigh from the inside with your shin. Keep your left foot on their left hip.
    3. Secure the Ankles: Grip both of your opponent's ankles with your hands, palms facing each other. Your forearms should press against their shins.
    4. Create the 'X': Cross your legs so your right shin is over their left thigh and your left shin is over their right thigh. Your knees should be slightly bent, creating tension.
    5. Hip Elevation and Drive: With your grip on their ankles, sit up and drive your hips upwards, lifting them off the mat. Simultaneously, push off their hip with your left foot.
    6. Leg Extension and Sweep: Extend your legs to drive your opponent forward and over your body. Maintain the ankle grips throughout the motion.
    7. Transition to Top Position: As they fall, immediately bring your left leg over their body and secure a dominant top position, such as side control or mount.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect leg crossing: Pushing your leg across their groin can hyperextend your knee or cause a groin injury. Always aim to hook their thigh from the inside.
    • Forcing hip elevation with arms: Relying on arm strength to lift them strains your shoulders and elbows. Use your hips and legs to drive upwards.
    • Neglecting ankle grips: Losing your grip on their ankles allows them to easily regain balance or posture. Maintain a firm, consistent grip throughout the sweep.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Mobility Drill: Practice entering X-guard from closed guard and establishing the leg-over-leg structure. Focus on hip movement and leg placement. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Static X-Guard Hold: Have a partner hold your ankles while you practice the leg-over-leg structure and hip elevation. (30 reps, 10% resistance)
    3. Assisted Sweep (No Posture): Partner stands with minimal posture. Practice the full sweep motion, focusing on hip drive and leg extension. (20 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Assisted Sweep (Light Posture): Partner offers light resistance and tries to maintain posture. Focus on breaking their posture before initiating the sweep. (20 reps, 50% resistance)
    5. Controlled Sweep (Partner Assists Recovery): Partner actively tries to defend but allows you to complete the sweep motion. Focus on transitioning to top position. (15 reps, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling (Focus on X-Guard Entry and Sweep): Implement X-guard and attempt sweeps during rolling. Focus on proper entry and execution. (5 rounds, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • Opponent is posturing down, trying to pass your closed guard.
    • Opponent is attempting to stack you from your closed guard.
    • You have successfully broken your opponent's posture and have control of their sleeves.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass Defense: If opponent stacks, maintain ankle grips and drive hips up to prevent flattening. Push off their hip with your free foot.
    • Knee Slice Pass (from X-Guard): If opponent attempts a knee slice, use your free leg to push their knee away and maintain distance. Transition to a different guard or sweep.
    • Posture Control: If opponent maintains strong posture, focus on breaking it down with collar grips or sleeve pulls before attempting the sweep.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the X-Guard Sweep?

    Knee pain often stems from incorrectly crossing your legs or forcing them into an unnatural position. Ensure your right shin hooks their right thigh from the inside, and your left shin hooks their left thigh from the inside. Avoid pushing your leg directly across their groin, which can cause hyperextension. Focus on creating a stable 'X' structure with your shins against their thighs.

    Q: How can I X-Guard Sweep against a much bigger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paramount. Focus on breaking their posture by pulling their sleeves and using your hips to create space. Ensure your ankle grips are tight and your shin-to-thigh connection is solid. The key is the upward hip drive; use your entire body's weight and momentum to lift and off-balance them, rather than trying to overpower them with brute strength.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from closed guard to X-Guard for a sweep?

    The ideal moment to transition to X-Guard is when your opponent is actively trying to pass your guard and their posture is somewhat broken. If they are standing tall and stable, attempting the X-Guard entry might expose you to a pass. Look for opportunities when they step in to pass, creating an opening for your leg to enter between theirs.

The X-Pass is a guard passing technique initiated from a standing or kneeling position against an opponent's open or closed guard. Its goal is to bypass the opponent's legs and establish side control.

White belts often struggle with the X-Pass due to a lack of foundational body mechanics and fear of getting swept. They tend to rely on brute force rather than precise weight distribution and leverage.

The core mechanical insight is to use your hips to drive through the opponent's legs while simultaneously controlling their upper body to prevent them from establishing frames or hip escapes.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a kneeling position facing your opponent's open guard, secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (same side as collar) with your left hand.
    2. Place your right knee on the mat, slightly outside the opponent's left leg, creating a 45-degree angle between your hips and theirs.
    3. Drive your left knee forward, aiming to place it between the opponent's legs, close to their hip.
    4. Extend your hips forward and downward, creating a strong base and driving pressure through their legs.
    5. Simultaneously, pull their collar towards your chest and push their sleeve away, breaking their posture and balance.
    6. Shift your weight onto your right hip as you swing your left leg across and behind their hips, securing a hip-to-hip connection.
    7. Drive your chest into their chest, continuing to apply forward pressure, and transition to side control by clearing their legs and establishing your knee-on-belly or mount.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect knee placement: Driving your knee directly into the opponent's hip socket can cause a hip impingement injury. The knee should be placed *between* their legs, creating space and leverage.
    • Overextending your back: Arching your lower back excessively to gain depth can lead to lumbar strain. Maintain a neutral spine and use your hips for driving power.
    • Allowing opponent to frame with arms: If you don't secure a strong collar and sleeve grip, the opponent can frame with their arms, potentially injuring your elbow or shoulder if you force the pass.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip drive and leg swing motion without a partner. Focus on hip mobility and coordination. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the entire X-Pass sequence, focusing on grip acquisition and precise body positioning. (20 reps per side)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the pressure and movement. Focus on breaking posture. (15 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner drilling (moderate resistance): Partner actively tries to defend by framing or shrimping. Focus on maintaining your base and driving through. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Live rolling (controlled): Attempt the X-Pass during sparring, but prioritize technique over success. Stop if a clear submission or sweep is available for either party. (5 attempts, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling (full): Integrate the X-Pass into your sparring rounds with full intent and resistance. (Ongoing, 100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent has an open guard and their legs are slightly separated.
    • When you have established a strong cross-collar and sleeve grip, allowing you to control their posture.
    • When the opponent is leaning too far forward, creating an opportunity to drive through their legs.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip escape (Shrimping): If the passer drives too far forward, the defender can quickly shrimp their hips away, creating space to re-establish guard.
    • Framing with forearms: The defender can place their forearms between their hips and the passer's chest to create a frame and prevent the forward drive.
    • Leg entanglement: The defender can attempt to re-hook the passer's legs with their own, turning it into a scramble or a potential sweep opportunity.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the X-Pass?

    Knee pain often stems from driving your knee directly into the opponent's hip joint or socket. The correct biomechanics involve placing your knee *between* their legs, near their hip crease, to create leverage and avoid direct impact. Ensure your hips are angled correctly to facilitate this.

    Q: How can I X-Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise timing are paramount. Focus on securing a strong cross-collar grip to break their posture and prevent them from using their weight to crush you. Your hip drive needs to be explosive, aiming to get underneath their base rather than trying to overpower them directly.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt an X-Pass?

    The X-Pass is most effective when the opponent's guard is slightly open and they are leaning forward, creating a momentary gap in their defense. You need to have a solid grip on their collar and sleeve to control their posture before initiating the hip drive. Avoid attempting it when they have a tight closed guard or are actively framing.

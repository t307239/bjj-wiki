Xパス BJJ テクニックガイド（初心者向け）

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック

The X-Pass is a dynamic guard pass used to break through an opponent's closed guard or open guard. It aims to secure a dominant side-control position by advancing past their legs.

White belts often struggle because they lack the precise hip and shoulder control needed to bypass the legs effectively. They tend to rely on raw strength instead of leverage.

The key insight is using your hips to drive forward while simultaneously controlling the opponent's legs with your arms, creating a "trapped" posture that allows you to advance.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip opponent's ankles with your palms facing inwards, thumbs outside. This is an "ankle grip".
    2. Place your knees outside the opponent's hips, maintaining a wide base, approximately shoulder-width apart.
    3. Lower your hips towards the mat, creating a low center of gravity, with your pelvis slightly angled forward.
    4. Drive your right shoulder forward and slightly down, aiming towards the opponent's left hip pocket.
    5. Simultaneously, use your left arm to hook behind the opponent's right knee, pulling it towards your chest.
    6. Shift your weight forward onto your right foot, transferring your mass over your opponent's legs.
    7. As you drive forward, your left leg will naturally swing past their legs, and your right foot will plant to establish side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly driving your head forward and down: This can strain your neck ligaments (e.g., cervical spine sprain) if the opponent shrimps or posts. The correct movement is to drive your shoulder, not your head, maintaining a neutral neck position.
    • Overextending your knee joint when swinging your leg: This can lead to a hyperextended knee or ACL/MCL tear. Swing your leg with a controlled bend, keeping the knee slightly flexed.
    • Allowing your opponent to trap your arm during the pass: This can lead to shoulder joint dislocation or rotator cuff tears. Always maintain a strong frame with your hooking arm and be aware of their counter-grips.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the entry, hip drive, and leg swing motion without a partner. Focus on hip angle and shoulder drive. (10 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Partner lies on their back, you practice the X-Pass entry and movement. Focus on grip and hip positioning. (20 reps)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the pressure and hip movement. (30 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner drill (medium resistance): Partner actively tries to maintain guard or recover. Focus on driving through their attempts. (40 reps, 50% resistance)
    5. Live rolling (controlled): Attempt X-Pass during rolling with a focus on applying the technique. Do not force it. (5 minutes, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling (full): Integrate X-Pass into your game during regular rolling. (5 minutes, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent has a deep closed guard and is posturing up slightly.
    • When the opponent attempts to open their guard and creates a small space.
    • When the opponent is flat on their back and their legs are not actively framing against your hips.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): If the opponent drives their hips away as you initiate the pass, creating space to re-establish guard. The counter is to maintain your frame and continue the pressure, or transition to a knee-slice.
    • Leg Pummeling: If the opponent tries to pummel their leg inside your hooking arm, they are attempting to recover guard. The counter is to maintain your grip and drive your shoulder deeper, or switch to a leg drag pass if they expose that option.
    • Posting and Framing: If the opponent uses their hands to post on your shoulders or hips to create distance, they are preventing your forward drive. The counter is to use your free hand to clear their frames and continue the hip pressure.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try the X-Pass?

    Your shoulder likely hurts due to improper arm positioning and excessive reliance on arm strength. When hooking the opponent's knee, ensure your elbow is slightly bent and your forearm is pressing against their hamstring, not their calf. Drive with your hips and shoulder, not by yanking with your arm. This prevents hyperextension and protects your rotator cuff.

    Q: How do I X-Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise hip mechanics are even more critical. Focus on getting your hips significantly lower than theirs before initiating the drive. Use your shoulder to create a strong wedge against their hip, preventing them from using their weight to stifle your movement. Aim to get your hooking arm deep behind their knee to control their base.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from X-Pass to another pass?

    You should consider switching if the opponent successfully frames your face or shoulders to create significant distance, or if they manage to pummel their leg inside your hooking arm and establish a strong connection. At this point, the X-Pass entry is compromised. You can then transition to a leg drag or a knee-slice pass by adjusting your angle and grips.

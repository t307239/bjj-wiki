Russian Tie: The Essential BJJ Takedown for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Russian Tie is a fundamental takedown initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to break your opponent's posture and create an angle for a sweep or takedown.

White belts often struggle by forcing the movement with upper body strength alone, neglecting the crucial hip and leg mechanics.

The core principle is to use your head and shoulder to control their posture while your hips drive forward and to the side, unbalancing them.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grips: Establish a strong over-under grip. Your right arm goes over their left shoulder, and your left arm goes under their right armpit, securing their left collar with your left hand.
    2. Head Position: Drive your head into their right pectoral muscle, creating a secure connection and blocking their forward movement.
    3. Hip Angle: Simultaneously, angle your hips approximately 45 degrees to their right, bringing your right hip closer to their body.
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto the balls of your feet, creating a forward pressure that pushes them onto their toes.
    5. Leg Drive: Step your left foot back slightly, creating a base, and begin to drive your right hip forward and into their body.
    6. Leverage Creation: As you drive your hips, your head and shoulder act as a fulcrum, forcing their upper body to twist and their base to become unstable.
    7. Completion: Continue the hip drive and slight pull on the collar with your left hand to complete the unbalancing and initiate your takedown.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Neck Strain: Applying the Russian Tie by forcefully pulling your opponent's head down with your arms, instead of using your head and shoulder to control their posture. This can lead to severe neck sprains or disc injuries by hyperextending or compressing the cervical spine.
    • Knee and Ankle Twisting: Attempting to force the takedown by aggressively turning your opponent while their feet are planted. This can cause a valgus or varus stress on the knee, leading to ACL or MCL tears, or ankle sprains due to uncontrolled rotation.
    • Back Injury from Poor Posture: Leaning back excessively to compensate for a lack of hip drive, creating an excessive lumbar curve. This puts immense strain on the lower back, potentially causing muscle strains or disc herniation.

    ドリル段階

    1. Static Grip and Head Placement: Partner stands still, you practice establishing the over-under grip and head connection. 10 reps each side.
    2. Controlled Hip Drive (0% Resistance): Partner stands still, you focus on the hip angle and forward drive without any resistance. 10 reps each side.
    3. Assisted Angle (25% Resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the unbalancing effect as you drive your hips. 10 reps each side.
    4. Snap Down Integration (50% Resistance): Incorporate a slight snap down motion with the collar grip as you drive your hips. Partner provides slight upward pressure. 10 reps each side.
    5. Takedown Entry (75% Resistance): Partner actively tries to resist, but you focus on the initial unbalancing mechanics leading into a basic double leg or sweep. 5 reps each side.
    6. Live Rolling Application (90-100% Resistance): Attempt the Russian Tie in live rolling, focusing on the mechanics learned, accepting that success rate will be low initially.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up and leaning forward slightly, creating an opening for your head and shoulder.
    • When your opponent attempts a collar tie or headlock, and you can secure the over-under grip to counter.
    • During transitions, when your opponent is momentarily off-balance or adjusting their stance.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Sprawl and Grip Break: If the opponent drives forward, sprawl your hips back and use your arms to break their collar grip, creating space.
    • Whizzer Counter: If they secure the over-under grip, use your arm to create a 'whizzer' over their arm, preventing them from establishing head control and driving your hips forward to counter their angle.
    • Hip Escape and Re-Grip: If they start to drive, immediately hip escape to the side and attempt to secure a single leg or butterfly guard to reset.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try the Russian Tie?

    Your neck likely hurts because you are using your arms to pull your opponent's head down, rather than using your head and shoulder to control their posture. The Russian Tie's effectiveness comes from using your body's structure as a lever. Ensure your head is firmly planted into their shoulder/chest and drive your hips forward, allowing your head and shoulder to naturally guide their upper body's movement.

    Q: How can I use the Russian Tie against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, the Russian Tie becomes less about brute force and more about precise timing and leverage. Focus on their base and posture. Look for moments when they are slightly off-balance or reaching. Use a strong head and shoulder connection to disrupt their center of gravity, and drive your hips forward with a slight upward angle to off-balance them rather than trying to overpower them directly.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate a Russian Tie in a BJJ match?

    The optimal time to initiate a Russian Tie is when your opponent is posturing up, especially if they are leaning forward slightly, or when they attempt a collar tie on you. These moments provide the ideal structural alignment for you to secure the over-under grip and drive your hips to create an unbalancing angle. Avoid attempting it when your opponent has a very solid, upright stance or is actively pressuring forward.

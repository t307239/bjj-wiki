Russian Tie: A Biomechanically Sound BJJ Takedown for Beginners

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Russian Tie is a fundamental takedown initiated from a collar-grip tie-up, aiming to off-balance and drive an opponent to the mat.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength, leading to poor posture and ineffective, energy-wasting movements.

The key is to use your opponent’s posture against them, creating a leverage point with your shoulder to initiate the drive.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Opponent maintains a standard collar grip (e.g., right hand on your left collar).
    2. You establish a strong cross-collar grip with your left hand on their right collar, thumb inside.
    3. Your right arm snakes under their left arm and secures a grip on their right elbow or tricep, palm facing inward.
    4. Lower your level by bending your knees, keeping your back straight and hips slightly back, creating a stable base.
    5. Initiate a slight pull with your collar grip while simultaneously pushing their elbow/tricep grip forward and down, breaking their posture.
    6. Drive your left shoulder into their chest, near the sternum, aligning your body with their center of mass.
    7. Transfer your weight forward by stepping your left foot forward and driving through your hips, maintaining the shoulder pressure and collar pull to execute the takedown.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to pull the opponent's head down with the collar grip while simultaneously pushing their arm can hyperextend your own neck and shoulder ligaments, leading to strains.

    Correct: Maintain a stable collar grip and focus on driving your shoulder into their chest while using the arm grip for control and posture breaking.

    • Stepping too far forward with your right leg (the leg on the same side as the arm grip) can cause your knee to buckle inward under your body weight, risking medial collateral ligament (MCL) damage.

    Correct: Drive off your back leg (left leg) and step with your front leg (left foot) to maintain a balanced, forward drive.

    • Rounding your back to reach for the collar grip or to drive into the opponent can compress your lumbar spine and lead to disc injuries.

    Correct: Maintain a neutral spine, bend your knees to lower your level, and use your shoulder to drive through their chest.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the grip changes and body positioning (lowering level, shoulder placement) 10 times each side (0% resistance).
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Practice the full sequence of grips, posture break, and drive with a compliant partner (25% resistance), focusing on correct weight transfer.
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Focus on the initial posture break and shoulder drive. Partner resists slightly by maintaining posture (50% resistance), 5 reps each side.
    4. Partner drilling (controlled takedown): Execute the full Russian Tie with a partner who offers light resistance and allows the takedown (75% resistance), 5 reps each side.
    5. Controlled sparring: Apply the Russian Tie during live rolling, focusing on timing and execution, allowing the partner to defend and recover if needed (90% resistance), 3 minutes.
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the Russian Tie into your rolling game with full resistance, aiming for successful takedowns (100% resistance), 5 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent establishes a collar grip on you, creating an immediate opportunity for the tie-up.
    • When you are in a standing clinch and can secure the cross-collar and elbow/tricep grips.
    • When the opponent attempts to pull you forward, creating an opening to tie them up and drive.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Sprawl and recover: If the opponent drives forward, quickly sprawl your hips back and widen your base to resist the forward pressure. Maintain a strong base and look to transition to a guard.
    • Grip break and re-establish posture: If they secure the grips, focus on breaking the collar grip by prying it open with your thumb, and simultaneously frame with your free arm to create space and regain upright posture.
    • Circle out and reset: If you feel the drive coming, pivot your hips and step your outside leg in a circular motion to create distance, breaking their line of attack and resetting your position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Russian Tie?

    Wrist pain during the Russian Tie often stems from gripping the opponent's collar too tightly with your fingers extended or hyperextending your wrist backward. Ensure your collar grip is firm but controlled, with your thumb inside the lapel and your wrist in a neutral position. When gripping their elbow or tricep, maintain a strong forearm connection rather than relying solely on your wrist.

    Q: How can I use the Russian Tie against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paramount. Focus on getting your collar grip *before* they establish a strong tie-up. Lower your level significantly, getting your hips below theirs, to create a better leverage angle. Drive your shoulder into their sternum with your entire body weight, using their size against them to propel them forward and down.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Russian Tie in a BJJ match?

    The Russian Tie is most effective when initiating from a standing clinch or when you feel the opponent leaning into you. It's also a good option when you've just broken their posture or when they are attempting to close the distance aggressively. Avoid attempting it when you are off-balance or have a weak grip, as this can lead to being taken down yourself.

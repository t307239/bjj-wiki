Beginner's Guide to the BJJ Heel Hook

BROWN★★★★★Expert
Brown帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 茶帯昇格要件

The Heel Hook is a leg lock applied to the opponent's heel, targeting the knee joint's ligaments. It's typically initiated from positions like the 50-50 guard or outside leg entanglement.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of control and understanding of joint mechanics, leading to sloppy application and increased injury risk.

The key to a successful Heel Hook is isolating the opponent's heel and applying rotational torque to the knee, creating pressure without compromising your own structure.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Entry (e.g., from 50-50 Guard): Secure both of the opponent's legs in your own legs. Your outside leg should cross over their hip to prevent them from turning out.
    2. Grip: Place your arm nearest to the opponent's heel *underneath* their heel, with your elbow pointing towards their knee. Your hand cups the opponent's heel, thumb facing upwards.
    3. Secondary Grip: Your other hand grips your own bicep, creating a "figure-four" grip around the opponent's ankle. Ensure your grip is tight on their heel, not their foot.
    4. Hip Angle: Drive your hips forward and slightly to the side, away from the opponent's hips. This creates leverage and prevents them from stacking you.
    5. Knee Alignment: Your knee on the same side as the trapped heel should be pointed slightly upwards, creating a "wall" to prevent them from straightening their leg. Keep your knee cap aligned with your ankle.
    6. Weight Distribution: Shift your weight onto your hips and lower back, not directly onto your knees. This protects your own knee joint.
    7. Finishing Pressure: To finish, extend your hips and simultaneously pull your figure-four grip towards your chest, while driving your head into their hip. This applies a twisting force to the knee.
    8. Control: Maintain control of the opponent's hip with your crossed leg. This prevents them from creating space or turning their hips away.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Heel Grip: Grabbing the opponent's foot instead of their heel. This allows their heel to slip, potentially leading to a hyper-extension of your own knee if you try to force the lock, damaging your ACL/MCL.
    • Knee Direction: Allowing your knee to bend inwards towards your body while applying pressure. This puts shear force on your own knee joint, risking meniscus tears or ligament damage.
    • Stacking Pressure: Not driving your hips forward and allowing the opponent to stack you by driving their hips into yours. This can cause your own knee to bend past its natural range of motion, risking ligamentous injury.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Drill (0% Resistance): Practice the grip and hip movement without an opponent. Focus on isolating the heel and achieving the figure-four grip. (50 reps)
    2. Static Partner Drill (25% Resistance): With a compliant partner lying on their back, practice securing the heel and establishing the figure-four grip. Partner offers minimal resistance to leg movement. (20 reps per side)
    3. Controlled Entry Drill (50% Resistance): Start from a position where the opponent's leg is accessible (e.g., 50-50 guard). Practice the entry and grip-fighting, with the opponent passively allowing the leg entanglement. (15 reps per side)
    4. Controlled Finish Drill (75% Resistance): From the established position, practice applying the finishing pressure. The opponent actively resists the turn but does not attempt to escape the position. Tap early. (10 reps per side)
    5. Positional Sparring (90% Resistance): Spar with the goal of achieving the leg entanglement and attempting the Heel Hook. The opponent defends the submission but can escape the position. Focus on clean technique over speed. (3 rounds, 3 minutes each)
    6. Live Rolling (100% Resistance): Incorporate the Heel Hook into live rolling. Be mindful of your partner's safety and tap early if caught. Focus on recognizing opportunities. (5 rounds, 5 minutes each)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard and their legs are exposed.
    • When you have achieved a strong inside leg entanglement (e.g., 50-50 guard).
    • When you have successfully trapped one of your opponent's legs and can isolate their heel.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Straightening the Leg: If the opponent is attempting a Heel Hook, actively try to straighten your leg by pushing your heel away. This elongates the lever arm and reduces the torque on your knee.
    • Turning Hips Away: Rotate your hips away from the direction of the torque. This can create space and relieve pressure on the knee joint.
    • "Bridging" or "Hiking" the Hip: If the opponent has a strong grip, try to "hike" or "bridge" your hips upwards and away from their pulling motion. This can loosen their grip and allow you to escape.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do a Heel Hook on someone?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are applying torque to your own knee joint. This happens when you don't properly isolate the opponent's heel or when your own hips are not angled correctly, causing your knee to bend in an unnatural direction under pressure. Ensure your figure-four grip is tight on the heel itself, and drive your hips forward and away from the opponent's hips to create a stable base.

    Q: How can I safely apply a Heel Hook against a much larger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise mechanics are even more critical. Focus on isolating *their* heel cleanly, rather than trying to force the submission. Use your hips to drive into their hip, creating a stable base and preventing them from using their weight to stack you. A tight figure-four grip on their heel is paramount; a loose grip will allow them to escape and potentially injure you.

    Q: When is the right time to go for a Heel Hook as a white belt?

    As a white belt, the safest and most effective time to attempt a Heel Hook is when you have achieved a dominant leg entanglement like the 50-50 guard. This position provides control over both of your opponent's legs, significantly reducing their ability to escape or counter. Avoid attempting Heel Hooks from less controlled positions where there is a higher risk of injury to yourself or your partner.

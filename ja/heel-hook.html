BJJ Heel Hook: A White Belt's Biomechanically Precise Guide

BROWN★★★★★Expert
Brown帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 茶帯昇格要件

The Heel Hook is a leg lock attacking the opponent's knee joint by applying rotational torque to the ankle and foot.

White belts often fail because they lack the positional control and understanding of joint mechanics, leading to sloppy attempts.

Success hinges on isolating the leg and applying a controlled, perpendicular flexion at the ankle, not raw strength.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Secure an 'Inside Heel Hook' grip: Opponent's foot is trapped between your legs. Your right hand grips the opponent's heel, thumb pointing upwards. Your left hand cups the opponent's instep, fingers pointing towards their toes.
    2. Establish 'Hip Angle': Your hips should be perpendicular to the opponent's attacking leg. Drive your hips into the opponent's thigh, creating a strong base.
    3. Isolate the Leg: Ensure the opponent's free leg cannot easily escape or post. Your legs should form a tight 'figure-four' around their attacking leg.
    4. Control the Knee: Use your left knee to pin the opponent's thigh to the mat, preventing them from straightening their leg.
    5. Position your 'Finishing Leg': Your right leg should be behind the opponent's knee, creating a fulcrum.
    6. Initiate the Lock: Extend your hips away from the opponent while simultaneously pulling their heel towards your chest.
    7. Apply Torque: Rotate your hips outwards, forcing the opponent's foot to rotate internally. This creates supination at the ankle and pronation at the knee.
    8. Maintain Control: Keep your grips firm and your body tight, ensuring the opponent cannot escape the lock's pressure.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Rushing the finish: Attempting to 'rip' the heel hook with excessive force before proper positioning is achieved. This can lead to hyperextension or tearing of the Anterior Cruciate Ligament (ACL) in the opponent's knee, or a knee-on-knee impact injury for the attacker.
    • Incorrect grip application: Gripping too high on the calf or ankle, preventing proper rotational leverage. This allows the opponent to escape or leads to a calf slicer if pressure is applied incorrectly, potentially causing muscle tears.
    • Allowing leg escape: Failing to maintain a tight 'figure-four' around the opponent's leg, enabling them to spin out. This can result in the attacker's own knee or ankle being put in a vulnerable position if they are not careful.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling (0% resistance): Practice the grips and hip movement without a partner. Focus on achieving the correct 'figure-four' and hip angle. (10 reps)
    2. Static partner drill (0% resistance): Partner lies on their back. You practice the entire sequence of entering the heel hook, focusing on grip and body positioning. Partner remains completely still. (10 reps)
    3. Controlled entry drill (25% resistance): From a controlled position (e.g., your open guard), practice attacking the heel hook. Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to secure the initial grips. (10 reps per side)
    4. Positional sparring - Guard (50% resistance): Start in closed guard. You attempt to transition to a position where you can attack the heel hook. Partner defends the leg lock attempt but does not actively try to escape the guard. (3 minutes)
    5. Positional sparring - Turtle/Side Control (75% resistance): Start in side control or from turtle. You practice transitioning to attack the heel hook. Partner actively defends and attempts to escape. (3 minutes)
    6. Live rolling - Leg Lock focus (90% resistance): During rolling, focus specifically on attacking leg locks. Both participants actively attempt to secure and defend leg locks, with an emphasis on controlled submissions. (5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent attempts a guard pass and their leg is extended away from their body.
    • When you have established a strong open guard and can isolate one of your opponent's legs.
    • When your opponent is turtling and you can secure a strong grip on their heel.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The "Spinning Escape": If the heel hook is not fully locked, the defender can spin their hips away from the attacking force, attempting to untangle their leg.
    • The "Stomp Defense": The defender can attempt to stomp their attacking foot down onto the mat, creating space and reducing the torque on their knee.
    • The "Body Lock": The defender can attempt to secure a strong body lock or cross-face, preventing the attacker from creating the necessary hip angle and leverage.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try to do a Heel Hook?

    Your ankle pain likely stems from incorrect torque application. Instead of focusing on rotating the opponent's foot internally (supination), you might be twisting your own ankle in an attempt to force the lock. The heel hook should generate torque at the knee, not your own ankle. Ensure your hips are perpendicular and you are driving your hips into their thigh while pulling the heel towards you.

    Q: How can I Heel Hook a much bigger opponent with my smaller frame?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paramount. Focus on isolating their leg and preventing them from using their mass to power out. Your 'figure-four' leg entanglement must be extremely tight. Prioritize getting your hips perpendicular to their attacking leg and use your body weight to drive into their thigh, creating a stable base before initiating the lock.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to a Heel Hook from my Closed Guard?

    The ideal time to transition to a heel hook from closed guard is when your opponent attempts to pass your guard and extends one of their legs away from their body, creating an opening. Look for moments where they are off-balance or have committed to a specific pass. Simultaneously, you need to break their posture and secure a strong control of the leg you intend to attack before they can recover their base.

The Heel Hook is a leg lock targeting the knee joint, typically initiated from positions like the 50-50 guard or Ashi Garami variations. It aims to hyperextend or rotate the knee, forcing a submission.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body awareness and fear, leading to uncontrolled movements and increased injury risk. They may grip incorrectly or apply pressure in the wrong direction.

The key mechanical insight is controlling the opponent's hip and using your own body to create a stable base, isolating the heel and applying torque to the knee without compromising your own structure.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Initiation from Ashi Garami: Secure your opponent's leg by placing your shin across their ankle and your foot on the outside of their hip, creating a figure-four with your legs. Your hips should be angled slightly towards your opponent's head.
    2. Securing the Heel: Grip the opponent's heel with your arm that is on the same side as the leg you are attacking. Your forearm should be on the outside of their heel, and your hand cupping the heel itself. Your other hand grips your own bicep, creating a strong, locked frame.
    3. Hip Control: Drive your hips forward and slightly to the side, perpendicular to your opponent's leg. This prevents them from turning their hips away and escaping the lock.
    4. Weight Distribution: Shift your weight onto your hips, pinning your opponent's attacked leg between your thighs. Your base should be wide enough to maintain stability but not so wide that you lose connection.
    5. Applying Pressure: With your forearm locked on the outside of their heel and your other arm gripping your bicep, begin to extend your hips and simultaneously pull your opponent's heel towards your own hip. This creates a rotational force on the knee.
    6. Knee Angle: Ensure your opponent's knee is slightly bent, not locked straight, to maximize leverage and direct the pressure effectively. Your own knee should be slightly bent to absorb any unexpected movements.
    7. Finishing Pressure: Continue to drive your hips forward and pull the heel. The pressure should be felt on the outside of the knee joint, targeting the ligaments. Maintain a stable base throughout the submission.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hip Alignment: Applying pressure by simply pulling the heel without controlling the opponent's hips can lead to the opponent's knee hyperextending unnaturally, potentially tearing the ACL or PCL.
    • Over-Rotation without Control: Gripping the heel but not establishing a solid base or hip control allows the opponent to spin out. This can result in your own knee being twisted, risking MCL or LCL tears.
    • Jerking Motion: Attempting to finish with a sudden, uncontrolled pull puts excessive stress on the ankle and knee joints of both practitioners. Focus on a smooth, controlled increase in pressure instead.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Leg Control Drill (0% Resistance): Practice isolating one of your own legs and simulating the Ashi Garami and heel grip. Focus on the correct angles and hand placement. (5 minutes, 10 repetitions per side).
    2. Partnered Leg Control (25% Resistance): With a partner lying on their back, practice entering Ashi Garami and securing the heel grip. Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to focus on grip and body positioning. (5 minutes, 5 repetitions per side).
    3. Controlled Sweep and Lock (50% Resistance): From a controlled guard or half guard, transition to Ashi Garami, secure the heel grip, and apply slow, controlled pressure. Partner taps immediately when they feel any pressure. (5 minutes, 3 repetitions per side).
    4. Heel Hook Entry from Guard Pass Attempt (75% Resistance): Partner attempts a simple guard pass (e.g., knee slice). You react by entering Ashi Garami, securing the heel grip, and applying controlled pressure. Partner defends by trying to create space or escape. (5 minutes, 3 repetitions per side).
    5. Live Rolling - Defensive Focus (90% Resistance): During live rolling, actively look for opportunities to secure the heel hook without forcing it. Focus on maintaining control and applying pressure gradually. Partner is actively defending and attempting to escape. (2 minutes rounds, 5 rounds).
    6. Live Rolling - Submission Focus (100% Resistance): During live rolling, actively seek to finish the heel hook. Both practitioners are working at full intensity, with the defender using all their skills to escape and the attacker applying the submission effectively. (2 minutes rounds, 5 rounds).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard and their leg is extended and accessible.
    • When you have established a strong Ashi Garami or 50-50 guard.
    • When your opponent is posturing up and their weight is shifted away from the leg you intend to attack.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The Spin-Out Escape: When the heel hook is being applied, the defender can attempt to spin their hips towards the attacker's head while keeping their knee bent. This rotates the attacking leg and often breaks the grip, allowing for an escape.
    • The Knee-Tap Defence: If the attacker is reaching for the heel, the defender can try to tap their own knee to the mat on the side of the attacked leg. This flattens the leg and makes it difficult to secure a strong heel grip, often forcing the attacker to abandon the submission.
    • The Leg Release: The defender can attempt to pull their heel out of the attacker's grip by creating a space between their heel and the attacker's forearm. This requires a sharp, explosive movement and often involves bridging or shrimping to create the necessary distance.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my heel hook feel weak and my opponent doesn't tap, even when I pull hard?

    Pulling 'hard' without proper biomechanics is inefficient. The weakness stems from incorrect hip alignment and insufficient control of the opponent's hip. Ensure your hips are driving forward, perpendicular to their leg, and your forearm is firmly cupping their heel, creating a lever against their knee. Lock your own arm by gripping your bicep to prevent the opponent from creating space.

    Q: How can I effectively apply a heel hook against a much larger and stronger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on precision and leverage over brute strength. Establish a dominant Ashi Garami or 50-50 guard to control their leg's movement. Use your hips to drive forward and create a stable base, preventing them from using their weight to crush you. Secure a tight heel grip and apply the rotational pressure smoothly and consistently, aiming for their knee ligaments rather than trying to 'bend' their leg.

    Q: When is the right time to go for a heel hook, and how do I avoid getting swept or giving up a better position?

    The ideal time is when your opponent is actively trying to pass your guard and exposes their leg, or when you have already established a strong leg entanglement like Ashi Garami or 50-50. To avoid giving up position, maintain a solid base with your hips and control their hips to prevent them from turning. If the submission isn't there, be ready to transition to a safer position or continue to improve your leg entanglement rather than forcing a weak attempt.

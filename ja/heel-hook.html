Precise Heel Hook Technique Guide for BJJ White Belts

BROWN★★★★★Expert
Brown帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 茶帯昇格要件

The Heel Hook is a leg lock applied to the opponent's heel, targeting the knee joint's ligaments through rotational force.

White belts often fail by trying to force the submission without understanding the biomechanical leverage, leading to uncontrolled movements and potential injury.

The key to a successful Heel Hook is isolating the opponent's heel and creating a fulcrum with your own body to apply torque, not brute strength.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a 50-50 Guard or similar control position, secure the opponent's leg with both arms. Your right arm's bicep should be inside the opponent's thigh, and your left arm should hook behind their knee.
    2. Place your right foot on the opponent's hip, creating a strong base and preventing them from easily escaping or stacking.
    3. Your left leg should slide down and hook the opponent's ankle, specifically trapping their heel with your left foot's instep.
    4. Drive your hips forward and slightly to the left, aligning your pelvis perpendicular to the opponent's leg.
    5. With your right arm, pull the opponent's knee towards your chest, and with your left arm, pull their heel towards your left hip.
    6. Simultaneously, extend your hips away from the opponent while maintaining the heel grip, creating rotational stress on their knee.
    7. Apply pressure by squeezing your legs and driving your hips, focusing on the torque around the heel, not a straightening motion.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying excessive upward or downward force on the opponent's leg instead of rotational torque: This can cause a hyperextension or hyperflexion injury to the knee, tearing the ACL or PCL.
    • Gripping the opponent's foot too loosely or allowing their heel to slip: This compromises the leverage needed for the submission and can result in a failed attempt with potential knee strain for the attacker.
    • Attempting the Heel Hook from an unstable position without proper hip alignment: This creates an uncontrolled application of force, increasing the risk of knee ligament damage for both parties.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Drill (0%): Practice the grip acquisition and leg isolation on a dummy or air, focusing on the hip alignment and foot placement. 10 reps per side.
    2. Controlled Partner Drill (25%): With a compliant partner, practice the initial leg capture and hip placement. Focus on smooth transitions and understanding the feel of the opponent's leg. 5 reps per side.
    3. Assisted Submission Drill (50%): Partner allows you to secure the heel hook grip and hip position. You gently apply pressure, and the partner taps early to reinforce the feeling of the submission. 5 reps per side.
    4. Positional Sparring (75%): Start from a position where the heel hook is viable (e.g., 50-50 guard). Focus on securing the position and applying the submission without excessive force. Tap immediately if the opponent defends effectively. 3 rounds, 2 minutes each.
    5. Limited Submission Sparring (90%): Allow heel hooks from specific positions. Focus on proper setup and execution, but prioritize safety and tapping. 5 rounds, 3 minutes each.
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Integrate heel hooks into regular sparring, always prioritizing your partner's safety and your own. Tap early and often to reinforce good habits.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have established strong control in the 50-50 guard, with your hips aligned and the opponent's foot secured.
    • When the opponent attempts to escape a leg entanglement by turning their body away from you, exposing their heel.
    • As a counter to an opponent's failed guard pass attempt where they leave a leg exposed and within your reach.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Straightening the Leg: If the opponent is attempting the heel hook, immediately try to straighten your leg and create distance with your hips. This reduces the torque applied to your knee.
    • Re-Guard or Escape: If your heel is caught, focus on bringing your free leg back to establish a guard or immediately hip out to create space and relieve pressure. The goal is to break the attacker's hip alignment.
    • Spinning Out: While risky, a quick spin away from the direction of the pressure can sometimes relieve the tension on the knee. This requires excellent body awareness and should only be attempted if you can do so rapidly.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee feel a sharp pain when I try to do a Heel Hook, even if my partner doesn't tap?

    The sharp pain indicates you are likely applying force incorrectly. Instead of isolating the heel and creating rotational torque, you might be inadvertently hyperextending or hyperflexing the knee joint. Ensure your hips are perpendicular to the opponent's leg and focus on pulling their heel towards your hip while driving your hips forward, creating a twisting motion, not a bending or straightening one.

    Q: How can I apply a Heel Hook effectively against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paramount, not strength. Focus on establishing a dominant 50-50 guard where you can control their leg's ankle and heel securely. Use your hips to drive forward and create the necessary rotational torque. Ensure your foot is firmly hooked on their heel to prevent slippage, and use your free leg to control their hip to prevent them from posturing up or creating space.

    Q: Is it okay to use my shin across my opponent's thigh to help secure the Heel Hook?

    Using your shin across the opponent's thigh, as sometimes seen in more advanced techniques, is not recommended for white belts learning the basic Heel Hook. This technique often requires a specific understanding of hip pressure and body mechanics to avoid injuring your own knee or your partner's. Stick to the fundamental technique of hooking the heel with your instep and using your hips for leverage.

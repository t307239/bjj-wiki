This technique aims to hyperextend the opponent's knee by attacking the heel bone. It's often initiated from leg entanglement positions.

White belts often fail due to fear and improper mechanics, leading to uncontrolled pressure or missed opportunities.

The key is isolating the ankle and using your hip to drive into the heel, creating rotational force on the knee joint.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Secure an Outside Heel Hook grip: With your opponent on their back, place your arm under their attacked leg, cupping the heel bone with your forearm. Your bicep should be against their calf.
    2. Create a perpendicular angle: Shift your hips so they are perpendicular to your opponent's hips. Your body should be positioned to the side of their leg.
    3. Lock your legs: Hook your non-attacking leg's foot behind your opponent's knee on the attacked leg. This prevents them from easily rotating out.
    4. Control the foot: With your hooked arm, pull the opponent's heel towards your chest. Your hand should grip their heel with fingers pointing upwards.
    5. Drive your hips: Extend your hips forward and slightly upwards, applying pressure directly into the opponent's heel bone. Your bodyweight should be behind the attack.
    6. Maintain knee angle: Keep your opponent's knee bent at approximately a 90-degree angle. Avoid letting their knee straighten completely, as this reduces leverage.
    7. Apply pressure: Squeeze your arms and drive your hips to create a rotational force on the knee. Listen for the tap.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly gripping the foot and pulling the heel towards your own head: This can lead to a knee-bar if the opponent's leg straightens, risking ACL or MCL tears.
    • Allowing the opponent's knee to straighten completely while applying pressure: This puts excessive torque on the anterior and posterior cruciate ligaments, leading to tears.
    • Not establishing a strong hip connection and instead relying on arm strength: This can result in the opponent escaping or you losing control, potentially injuring your own shoulder or elbow if you overextend.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill - Grip and hip angle: Practice securing the Outside Heel Hook grip and achieving the perpendicular hip angle without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill - Static position: With a compliant partner, practice securing the grip and establishing the hip angle. Partner offers no resistance. (30 reps)
    3. Partner drill - Controlled pressure: Practice the full Heel Hook mechanics with a partner who gently resists, allowing you to feel the pressure and control. (20 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill - Gentle resistance: Your partner offers light resistance to your attempts, simulating a realistic scenario without full commitment. (5 minutes per side)
    5. Live rolling - Controlled attempts: During rolling, you can specifically target the leg lock opportunity but with very light pressure, prioritizing safety. (2 rounds)
    6. Live rolling - Full commitment (with tap): Once comfortable, engage in live rolling where both participants are actively trying to achieve and defend the Heel Hook. Emphasize tapping early. (5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is on their back and you are in a leg entanglement position like the 50-50 guard or have secured a single leg.
    • When you have successfully isolated one of your opponent's legs and can control their foot.
    • When your opponent attempts to stand up from a grounded position and you can secure their leg.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Escape: If the heel hook is being applied, the primary counter is to immediately try and untangle your leg and create space by pushing off your opponent's hip or shoulder.
    • Straighten the Leg: If possible, and without causing further injury, try to straighten your leg to reduce the torque on the knee joint. This also makes it harder for the attacker to maintain their grip.
    • Turn Towards the Attacker: Instead of resisting the pull, turning your body towards the attacker can often relieve pressure and allow you to escape the grip or even transition to a better position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do a Heel Hook?

    Your knee is likely hurting because you are not isolating the heel correctly or are applying pressure before your hips are at the optimal angle. Ensure your arm is cupping the heel bone and your hips are perpendicular to your opponent's. Drive your hips forward into the heel, not by pulling with your arms, to create rotational force on the knee instead of direct pressure.

    Q: How can I Heel Hook a bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a bigger opponent, leverage is even more crucial. Focus on achieving a tight grip on their heel and establishing a strong perpendicular hip angle. Use your bodyweight by driving your hips into their heel, rather than relying on brute strength. Ensure your non-attacking leg is securely hooked behind their knee to prevent them from turning out.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Heel Hook from Closed Guard?

    Heel Hooks are generally not recommended directly from Closed Guard due to the risk of your opponent stacking you and the difficulty in achieving the necessary hip angle. It's more effective to transition from Closed Guard to a position like the 50-50 guard or to open your guard and secure a leg entanglement that allows for proper biomechanics for the Heel Hook attack.

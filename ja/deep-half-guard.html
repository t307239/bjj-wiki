Deep Half Guard: The Essential BJJ Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Deep Half Guard is a position initiated from bottom half guard where one leg is trapped between the opponent's legs and the other is outside. It aims to secure a strong control position for sweeps and submissions.

White belts often struggle by creating a flat back or extending their hips too far, exposing them to passes and compromising their base. Maintaining a tight, coiled structure is crucial.

The single most important concept is framing with your forearm and actively using your hip to create space and drive your opponent's weight away from your center line.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From bottom half guard, your opponent is typically in a knee-on-belly or stacked position. Secure a "collar grip" with your right hand on the opponent's left collar, pulling their head towards you.
    2. Simultaneously, with your left hand, secure a "pant grip" on the opponent's left pant leg, just above the knee.
    3. Insert your right leg (the one on the same side as the trapped leg) deeply between the opponent's legs, aiming to get your shin across their hip.
    4. Drive your hips forward and to your left, creating a strong "frame" with your right forearm against the opponent's left hip.
    5. Your left leg should be "hooking" the opponent's right thigh from the outside, preventing them from easily posturing up or stepping out.
    6. Maintain a "tight" structure by keeping your knees close to your body and your back rounded, avoiding any flat back angles.
    7. Continuously "pressure" your right forearm into the opponent's hip, creating a wedge that limits their movement and sets up sweeps.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending your hips outwards while trying to frame: This can hyperextend your lower back, leading to muscle strains or disc injuries. The correct movement is to drive your hips forward and slightly to the side, creating a diagonal line of pressure.
    • Allowing the opponent to "stack" you by driving their weight straight down on your chest: This can compress your rib cage and neck, potentially causing rib fractures or spinal injury. The correct action is to use your forearm frame to push their hip away and maintain a rounded back.
    • Releasing the pant grip prematurely and losing the leg hook: This allows the opponent to easily step out and pass your guard, and can lead to awkward limb entanglement and potential knee or ankle sprains if your leg gets caught incorrectly.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the entry and framing motion without a partner. Focus on hip movement and forearm placement. (10 reps per side)
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): Start from bottom half guard. Your partner allows you to establish the deep half guard position without resistance. Focus on grip and frame. (5 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to your entry, allowing you to get the frame. Focus on maintaining the frame and hip drive. (5 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to posture up or stack. Practice maintaining your frame and hip pressure. (5 reps per side)
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner attempts a basic guard pass from the deep half guard. Focus on maintaining control and setting up a sweep. (3 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Spar with a partner, attempting to implement Deep Half Guard from bottom half guard. Focus on understanding timing and transitions. (2 minutes)
    7. Live rolling (100% resistance): Full sparring, integrating Deep Half Guard into your game. (3 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent establishes a strong side control or knee-on-belly from your half guard, and you can isolate one of their legs.
    • When your opponent is posturing up directly over your half guard, creating an opportunity to dive under their base.
    • When you are being "stacked" in half guard and need to create a strong frame to prevent further pressure.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • "Stack Pass Defence": If the opponent stacks, keep your frame strong, drive your hips into their hip, and use your hooked leg to "kick" their base out to the side, creating space to recover guard.
    • "Knee Slice Pass Defence": If the opponent attempts a knee slice, maintain your frame, "pull" their leg across your body with your hook, and use your hips to "trap" their knee, preventing them from passing.
    • "Head and Arm Control": If the opponent tries to "smash" you from deep half guard, maintain your collar grip and use your free arm to "hug" their head or shoulder, preventing them from advancing their position and setting up a sweep.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to get into Deep Half Guard?

    This is usually due to arching your back excessively when driving your hips forward. Instead of extending your spine, focus on driving your hips *forward and slightly to the side* while maintaining a rounded upper back. Think of it like pushing a heavy box with your hips, not your spine. Keep your core engaged to protect your lower back.

    Q: How do I maintain Deep Half Guard against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision become even more critical. Focus on creating a very strong frame with your forearm against their hip to prevent them from "smashing" or "stacking" you. Use your hooked leg to actively control their thigh and prevent them from posturing up. Prioritize hip movement to generate off-balancing forces rather than relying on raw strength.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from Half Guard to Deep Half Guard?

    The optimal time is when your opponent is posturing up directly over your half guard, or when they are leaning heavily to one side attempting a pass. Look for the moment their weight is forward and their base is somewhat compromised. Diving in when they are stable and postured high makes it much harder to secure the deep half guard position effectively.

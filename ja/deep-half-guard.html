Deep Half Guard: A White Belt's Biomechanically Accurate Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Deep Half Guard is a position where your opponent is on top, with one of your legs trapped between their legs, and your hips are significantly lower than theirs.

It is designed to create opportunities for sweeps and transitions, but white belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of body positioning and leverage.

The key is to create a strong underhook and use your hips to drive forward and up, rather than simply pulling.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Opponent is in your guard, attempting to pass. Your hips are on the mat, knees bent.
    2. Secure a deep underhook with your arm around your opponent's thigh, gripping your own bicep or shoulder.
    3. Place your free hand on their hip or thigh to control distance and prevent them from posturing up.
    4. Drive your hips forward and slightly to the side, creating a wedge with your trapped leg.
    5. Your trapped leg should be bent at the knee, with your foot pointed towards their knee to create a frame.
    6. Swing your free leg back and around their leg, aiming to get it behind their knee line.
    7. Simultaneously, drive your shoulder into their hip and use your underhook to pull them down and forward, initiating your sweep.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to stand up while in Deep Half Guard without proper hip control: This can lead to knee hyperextension injuries as your opponent's weight is on your leg.
    • Grabbing the opponent's ankle with your free hand instead of controlling their hip: This leaves your head exposed and can result in a guillotine or head-and-arm choke.
    • Not maintaining hip pressure and instead pulling the opponent towards you: This can strain your lower back and make it impossible to generate sweep momentum.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice entering Deep Half Guard from a seated guard position, focusing on hip movement and underhook placement. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    2. Solo drill: Practice the hip drive and leg swing motion of the sweep without an opponent. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    3. Partner drill: Opponent lies on their back, you get into Deep Half Guard and practice the underhook and hip drive. (5 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner drill: Opponent attempts a simple pass, you secure Deep Half Guard and attempt the sweep. (5 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Partner drill: Opponent actively tries to pass, you secure Deep Half Guard and execute the sweep. (5 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Incorporate Deep Half Guard entries and sweep attempts into rolling. (3 x 2-minute rounds, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent establishes a strong top control and you are unable to maintain a standard guard.
    • When your opponent is posturing up and you can isolate one of their legs.
    • When your opponent is trying to pass your guard from a knee-on-belly or side control position.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Knee-on-Belly: If your opponent achieves knee-on-belly, and you trap one leg, you can use the underhook to pull them into your guard and re-establish a stronger defensive position.
    • Stack Pass: If your opponent attempts a stack pass, you can use the deep half guard position to invert and potentially sweep or transition to a leg entanglement.
    • Facing Upwards: If your opponent is on top and you have a deep half guard, you can use your hips to drive them forward and then transition to your back, creating space to re-guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get into Deep Half Guard?

    This is likely due to your knee joint taking direct pressure from your opponent's weight or being forced into hyperextension. Ensure you are framing with your foot pointed towards their knee and actively driving your hips forward to create space, rather than letting your knee buckle under their weight.

    Q: How can I use Deep Half Guard against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on securing a deep underhook and using your hips to drive them forward, creating an off-balance situation. Do not try to match their strength; instead, use their weight against them by angling your body and driving them in the direction they are already moving.

    Q: I keep getting stuck in Deep Half Guard and can't sweep. What am I missing?

    You are likely missing the critical hip drive and upward momentum. Simply holding the position is not enough. You need to actively drive your hips forward and up, using your underhook to pull them down and create an angle for your sweep. Think of it as a powerful hip bump combined with a leg escort.

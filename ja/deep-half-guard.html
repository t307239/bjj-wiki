Deep Half Guard is a position initiated from being on your back, often after a failed guard retention or a sweep attempt. It aims to create a strong base to prevent passes and set up sweeps or transitions.

White belts often struggle by over-relying on strength or by incorrectly framing, leading to passes or submission attempts.

The core principle is to control your opponent's base and off-balance them by creating leverage with your legs and hips, using their own weight against them.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Opponent is in your half guard, controlling one of your legs with their knee and chest pressure.
    2. Secure a deep underhook with the arm on the same side as the trapped leg, reaching past their hip bone.
    3. Grip their far-side pants with your free hand, specifically the inseam just above the knee.
    4. Drive your hips forward and flatten your back to the mat, creating a wedge with your trapped leg's thigh against their hip.
    5. Your trapped leg's knee should point towards their armpit, while your other leg extends to create a frame.
    6. Shift your weight slightly to your trapped leg side, initiating a slight lift of your hips off the mat.
    7. Use your underhook to pull their upper body towards you, while simultaneously pushing their hips away with your extended leg to create an angle.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to 'muscle' your way out by pushing their head back directly: This can strain your neck and shoulders, and allows them to easily stack you. Instead, focus on hip and leg leverage.
    • Keeping your trapped knee too close to your body: This allows them to easily pass your guard by driving their knee over your hip. Instead, extend your trapped leg to create a wedge against their hip.
    • Not securing a deep underhook: A shallow underhook offers no control and allows them to posture up and pass. Instead, reach deep past their hip bone and secure a firm grip.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip and leg movement of establishing the deep half guard position without a partner (5 reps each side).
    2. Static partner drill: Partner lies flat on their back, you get into the deep half guard position and hold it (10 reps each side, 0% resistance).
    3. Controlled entry drill: Partner offers minimal resistance as you attempt to establish deep half guard from a neutral half guard (20 reps each side, 25% resistance).
    4. Sweep setup drill: From deep half guard, practice initiating a basic sweep like the 'hook sweep' (20 reps each side, 50% resistance).
    5. Transition drill: Practice transitioning from deep half guard to a sweep attempt or a guard pass attempt (15 reps each side, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Apply deep half guard during rolling, focusing on maintaining the position and attempting sweeps or transitions (2 x 5-minute rounds, 90% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent establishes a strong half guard on you and is pressuring forward.
    • When you attempt a sweep from half guard and your opponent defends by stacking or posturing up.
    • When you are caught in a bad position and need to reset to a more controlled guard.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass Defense: If they attempt to stack, widen your base with your free leg and drive your hips into them, creating space to shrimp or recover guard.
    • Knee Slice Pass Defense: If they try to knee slice, use your trapped leg to create a frame against their shin and your underhook to pull their upper body away, preventing them from flattening you.
    • Posture Up and Pass: If they manage to posture up and create distance, use your underhook to pull them back down or transition to a different guard like closed guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get into Deep Half Guard?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are allowing your opponent's knee to drive directly into the inside of your knee joint. To prevent this, when establishing the deep half guard, focus on trapping their leg higher up with your thigh and creating a wedge between your own thigh and their hip, rather than letting their knee dig into your own. This utilizes your stronger structures and avoids direct pressure on the vulnerable knee joint.

    Q: How can I use Deep Half Guard against a much larger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is even more critical. Focus on securing a deep underhook and using your hips to create an angle, rather than trying to lift them. Drive your hips forward to wedge their base, and use your extended leg to push their hips away, off-balancing them. Your goal is to use their weight and momentum against them, not to overpower them.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a sweep from Deep Half Guard?

    The optimal time to attempt a sweep from Deep Half Guard is when your opponent is committing their weight forward to pass or is trying to regain their posture. Look for moments where they are slightly off-balance or have less base. This is when your hip drive and the leverage created by your legs can most effectively off-balance them and initiate a sweep attempt.

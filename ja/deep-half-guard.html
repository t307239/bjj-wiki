Deep Half Guard: Your First Step in BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Deep Half Guard is a dynamic position initiated from bottom half guard, aiming to invert the body and attack the opponent's legs or sweep.

White belts often struggle by creating too much space or by not committing to the inversion, leading to passes.

The core principle is controlling the opponent's weight distribution by hooking their leg and hip, then using your own hips to create leverage for sweeps or transitions.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From bottom half guard, secure your opponent's right leg with your left arm, hooking behind their knee (knee-to-hip grip).
    2. Simultaneously, use your right arm to grip their right hip or belt.
    3. Drive your hips towards the mat, creating a strong frame with your left forearm against their right thigh.
    4. Begin to invert your body by bringing your left knee towards your chest while keeping your right knee tucked under their right thigh.
    5. Shift your weight onto your left shoulder, angling your hips perpendicular to your opponent's base.
    6. Use your hooked left leg to elevate their leg and your right hand on their hip to drive forward, initiating the sweep.
    7. As they post to defend, continue to drive your hips and roll to your left, bringing your right leg free to secure a better position or transition.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending your hips outwards while inverted: This can hyperextend your knee joint, risking ACL or MCL tears.
    • Grabbing the opponent's ankle instead of the knee-to-hip area: This often leads to losing the hook and allows the opponent to step over your head, risking a knee-on-belly pass or even an armbar.
    • Forcing the sweep by pushing with your hands on the hip: This can strain your shoulder joint and wrist, especially if the opponent is significantly heavier; instead, drive with your hips and core.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo movement: Practice the hip inversion and knee tuck motion without a partner. Focus on hip angle and knee placement. (50 reps)
    2. Static partner drill: With a compliant partner in half guard, practice securing the grips and performing the inversion without applying pressure. (10 reps per side)
    3. Controlled sweep drill: Partner allows the initial inversion and hip drive, resisting slightly as you attempt the sweep. Focus on connecting the grips to the hip drive. (5 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Sweep progression drill: Partner offers more resistance to the sweep, allowing you to feel the weight transfer and hip pressure needed. (5 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Sweep attempt drill: Partner actively defends the sweep, allowing you to practice recovering the position if the sweep is initially unsuccessful. (5 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Integrate Deep Half Guard attempts into sparring, focusing on recognizing opportunities and executing the mechanics correctly. (10 minutes, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent has established a strong cross-face and is pressuring heavily into your half guard.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your half guard by stacking or driving their weight forward.
    • When you have secured a deep hook on your opponent's leg and can feel their weight shift forward.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass: The opponent drives their weight directly down onto your hips. To counter, maintain your hip frame and use your free leg to create space and shrimp out, or transition to a technical stand-up.
    • Knee Slice Pass: The opponent brings their knee across your body to pass. To counter, keep your hips tight and use your arm on their hip to push them away, creating space to bring your legs back into a full guard or another half guard.
    • Hip Escape and Re-guard: If the opponent is posturing up and you lose the deep hook, immediately shrimp your hips away and try to re-establish guard or transition to a different guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why do I feel a sharp pain in my knee when I try to invert in Deep Half Guard?

    This pain is often due to over-extending your knee joint. When inverting, your left knee should tuck towards your chest, and your right knee should remain bent under your opponent's thigh, creating a stable pocket. Avoid letting your knee go straight or hyperextend; focus on driving your hips forward and up, not just your leg.

    Q: How can I use Deep Half Guard against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are crucial. Focus on controlling their hip with your gripping arm and using your hooked leg to elevate their weight, not just to hold them. Drive your hips forward and perpendicular to their base, using your body weight to unbalance them rather than trying to muscle them over.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from regular half guard to Deep Half Guard?

    The ideal time is when your opponent is actively trying to pass by driving their weight forward or stacking. This forward pressure gives you the leverage to hook their leg and invert. If they are postured up and away from you, it's harder to establish the deep hook and secure the position effectively.

Deep Half Guard: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide to BJJ

Deep Half Guard is a position where your opponent has passed your guard and is now on top of your legs, with one of your legs trapped underneath theirs.

White belts often struggle because they lack the fundamental body mechanics to control their opponent's weight distribution, leading to ineffective grips and pressure.

The core insight is to use your trapped leg's ankle and your free leg's hip to create a powerful lever, controlling your opponent's base and enabling sweeps or transitions.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Opponent is in your guard, knee-on-belly or side control, and their weight is forward, driving into your hips.
    2. Your left leg (if opponent is on your right) is secured under their right thigh, with your left ankle hooked behind their right knee.
    3. Your right leg is free and positioned to "cup" the outside of their right hip, with your right foot flat on the mat or slightly angled forward.
    4. Your left arm secures a "collar grip" on their right gi-collar, pulling it towards your chest to break their posture.
    5. Your right arm secures an "underhook" on their right armpit, preventing them from posturing up or shrimping away.
    6. To initiate a sweep: actively drive your left ankle upward, creating upward pressure on their trapped leg.
    7. Simultaneously, extend your right leg and drive your right hip forward and slightly upward, creating a "wedge" against their hip.
    8. Maintain constant pressure with your left collar grip to keep their upper body pulled down and off-balance.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to "pull" your opponent down with only arm strength without hip engagement: This strains the shoulder joint's rotator cuff and can lead to tears due to excessive, uncontrolled torque.
    • Allowing your trapped leg's ankle to be "bent backward" by the opponent: This puts extreme stress on the ankle ligaments (talofibular and deltoid), risking sprains or even fractures.
    • Not cupping the opponent's hip with your free leg, instead letting it dangle: This allows the opponent to easily "stack" their weight onto your hips and spine, risking lumbar disc compression or facet joint injury.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the leg entanglement and hip cupping motion without a partner, focusing on the feel of the lever. 50 reps.
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): Opponent lies flat on their back, you establish Deep Half Guard. Focus on achieving the correct grips and hip angle. 5 reps per side.
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Opponent gently resists, allowing you to feel the pressure and control. Focus on maintaining the ankle hook and hip cup. 5 reps per side.
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Opponent actively tries to posture up or pass, you practice maintaining the position and initiating the sweep motion. 5 reps per side.
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Opponent attempts to pass or stack, you focus on the sweep mechanics and recovering the guard if the sweep is countered. 3 reps per side.
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Apply Deep Half Guard in rolling, focusing on executing the sweep or transitioning to other positions. 3 rounds.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent achieves a strong knee-on-belly, driving their knee into your hip.
    • Opponent's weight is significantly forward, making their base unstable.
    • Opponent has successfully "passed" your open guard and is on top of your legs.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Opponent stacks their weight directly onto your hips: Immediately drive your trapped ankle upwards and push your hips away, creating space to shrimp out.
    • Opponent attempts to "smash pass" by driving their chest into your face: Use your free leg's hip cup to create a frame and "kick out" their leg, transitioning to a single leg takedown or back to guard.
    • Opponent tries to "spin" out of the position: Maintain your collar grip and ankle hook, then use your free leg to "chop" at their base as they turn, potentially sweeping them.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try to get into Deep Half Guard?

    Your ankle likely hurts because you are not properly securing the ankle hook, allowing your opponent's shin to press directly against your ankle bone. Ensure your left ankle is actively "hooking" behind their knee, not just resting there. The pressure should be distributed through your shin and calf, not solely on your ankle joint.

    Q: How can I use Deep Half Guard against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on the "lever" aspect of Deep Half Guard. Your goal is to disrupt their base and make their weight feel lighter. Emphasize the upward drive of your trapped ankle and the forward "wedge" of your hip cup. Use your collar grip to break their posture and prevent them from simply "squashing" you.

    Q: You should transition to Deep Half Guard when your opponent has successfully passed your initial Half Guard and their weight is significantly forward, trapping one of your legs deep. If they are still allowing you to easily control their leg with a standard Half Guard grip, stay there. Deep Half Guard is for when the pass is already happening.

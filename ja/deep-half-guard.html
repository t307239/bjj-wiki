Deep Half Guard is a position initiated from a bottom half guard where your opponent has achieved a significant positional advantage, often with their knee on the mat. It aims to create immediate offensive opportunities by compromising your opponent's base and balance.

White belts often struggle because they lack the fundamental body mechanics to establish and maintain the correct angles, leading to them being easily passed or stacked. They try to muscle their way through instead of using leverage.

The single most crucial mechanical insight is to invert your hips and create a tight, underhooking grip to control your opponent's base, preventing them from posturing up and driving forward.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From bottom half guard, secure an underhook with your right arm, reaching deep past your opponent's right hip. Your forearm should be pressed against their side, and your hand gripping their left hip or the seam of their gi pants.
    2. Your left leg hooks your opponent's right leg behind the knee, creating a "hook" that prevents them from easily stepping out.
    3. Drive your left hip into your opponent's right thigh, creating a strong wedge. Your pelvis should be angled upwards, not flat on the mat.
    4. Tilt your body to your left, creating a strong "frame" with your right arm and shoulder against your opponent's thigh or knee to prevent them from driving forward.
    5. Use your hooked left leg to pull your opponent's leg towards you, simultaneously pushing your right hip away to create space and angle.
    6. Initiate a powerful "sit-up" motion, driving your hips upwards and forward while maintaining the underhook and leg hook. This is the primary movement to sweep or transition.
    7. As you sit up, your goal is to bring your left knee to your chest, creating a strong "seatbelt" grip around your opponent's waist, and transitioning to a sweep or submission.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to "pull" yourself forward with the underhook without simultaneously driving your hips into your opponent's leg: This can lead to your opponent easily "stacking" you, putting immense pressure on your spine and potentially causing a lumbar disc injury.
    • Letting your hooked leg become loose or "sloppy": If your left leg hook is not tight behind your opponent's knee, they can easily step out or drive their knee into your groin, causing a painful groin contusion or adductor strain.
    • Trying to "leg-drag" your opponent's leg instead of hooking it: This creates an opening for your opponent to easily pass your guard by stepping their leg over your head, potentially leading to a knee-on-belly position or a pass that strains your hip flexor.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Escape and Underhook Drill: Practice shrimping out and establishing the deep half guard underhook and hip wedge without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Static Partner Drill (0% resistance): Partner kneels, you establish deep half guard. Focus on grip and hip angle. Partner offers no resistance to the sweep. (5 sets of 1 minute)
    3. Controlled Sweep Drill (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to your sweep attempt, focusing on maintaining position. You execute the sweep. (5 sets of 1 minute)
    4. Deep Half Guard Entry and Sweep (50% resistance): Start from bottom half guard. Partner attempts to pass. You establish deep half guard and attempt the sweep. Partner defends passively. (5 sets of 1 minute)
    5. Sweep to Mount Transition (75% resistance): Execute the sweep. If successful, immediately transition to mount. Partner offers moderate resistance to the sweep and transition. (5 sets of 1 minute)
    6. Live Rolling (90% resistance): Focus exclusively on entering and attacking from deep half guard for a set time. Opponent actively defends and attempts to pass. (3 rounds of 3 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is posturing up and driving their weight forward from your half guard.
    • When your opponent's knee is on the mat and their weight is distributed forward, creating an opportunity for a hip-in.
    • When you feel your opponent is about to "stack" you from your half guard, and you can invert to create a frame.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass Defense: If your opponent attempts to stack you, continue to drive your hips into their thigh and use your underhook to prevent them from collapsing your spine. Adjust your hip angle to absorb the pressure.
    • Knee Slice Pass Prevention: If your opponent tries to bring their knee over your body, use your underhook to control their hip and your hooked leg to block their knee from advancing. "Shrimp" your hips away to create space.
    • "Re-guarding" the Half Guard: If the deep half guard position is compromised, quickly transition back to a standard half guard by extending your legs and re-establishing your hooks. Focus on regaining your base.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my back hurt when I try to do Deep Half Guard?

    Your back pain is likely due to improper hip mechanics. When you try to initiate the sweep, you're probably arching your lower back instead of driving your hips forward with your glutes and core engaged. To fix this, focus on keeping your spine neutral and using your powerful hip flexors and abductors to generate the upward and forward drive, much like a powerful sit-up motion.

    Q: How do I get out of Deep Half Guard if my opponent is much bigger than me?

    Against a larger opponent, the key is to use their weight against them and focus on creating off-angles. Instead of trying to lift them, use your underhook to "pull" their weight forward and to your side, while simultaneously driving your hips into their base. Your goal is to destabilize them and make them feel like they're falling, forcing them to react and give you an opening to transition or sweep.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a sweep from Deep Half Guard?

    The ideal time to attempt a sweep from Deep Half Guard is when your opponent commits their weight forward and their hips are relatively close to yours. Look for moments when they are posturing up to improve their position or when they are trying to drive a knee through your guard. These moments create the biggest opportunity to invert your hips and leverage their forward momentum against them.

Harai Goshi: The Sweeping Hip Throw for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Harai Goshi is a dynamic hip throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to sweep your opponent's leg and bring them to the mat.

Many white belts struggle by focusing on brute strength over coordinated movement, leading to failed attempts and potential injury.

The core insight is using your hips as a fulcrum and your leg sweep as the lever to unbalance your opponent's base.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Kumi Kata (Grip): Right-handed attacker: Right hand grips opponent's left collar, thumb inside. Left hand grips opponent's right sleeve at the elbow crease.
    2. Stance and Footwork: Adopt a staggered stance, left foot slightly forward. Your body should be angled 45 degrees to your opponent.
    3. Pull and Enter: With your collar grip, pull your opponent slightly forward and to your left. Simultaneously, step your left foot forward, placing it outside your opponent's right foot.
    4. Hip Placement: Rotate your hips inward (counter-clockwise for right-handed attacker), bringing your right hip bone into contact with your opponent's right abdomen.
    5. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight from your back foot to your front foot, leaning your upper body slightly forward and down.
    6. Leg Sweep: As your opponent's weight shifts onto their right leg, extend your right leg backward in a sweeping motion, aiming to contact their right shin or ankle area.
    7. Execute the Throw: Continue the hip rotation and leg sweep while pulling with your collar grip and pushing with your sleeve grip. Your hips should drive upward as your opponent's base is compromised, causing them to fall over your hip.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hip Rotation: Trying to throw by lifting the opponent with arms alone or rotating hips too late can strain the lower back (lumbar spine) by hyperextension. Always ensure your hip is a stable pivot point before initiating the sweep.
    • Over-extension of Knee: Sweeping with a straight, locked leg while the opponent resists can cause a knee hyperextension or ligament tear (ACL/MCL). Keep a slight bend in your sweeping leg and focus on sweeping the opponent's foot, not forcing your leg through theirs.
    • Arm-Dominant Pulling: Relying solely on pulling with the arms without proper hip and body connection can lead to shoulder impingement or rotator cuff tears. Your pull should complement the hip and leg action, not be the primary force.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling (0%): Practice the footwork and hip rotation motion without a partner. Focus on smooth transitions between steps. (20 reps)
    2. Solo with Object (0%): Use a training dummy or a sturdy pole to practice the entry, hip contact, and sweep motion. (20 reps)
    3. Partner Static Drill (25%): With a compliant partner, practice the grips, entry, and hip placement. Partner offers minimal resistance. (15 reps)
    4. Partner Assisted Throw (50%): Partner helps with the throw by leaning into you slightly. Focus on the timing of the hip and leg sweep. (15 reps)
    5. Controlled Takedown Practice (75%): Work with a partner who understands the technique. Focus on executing the throw with controlled speed and follow-through. (10 reps per side)
    6. Live Takedown Situations (90%): Apply Harai Goshi in a live rolling scenario, focusing on recognizing the opportunity and executing the technique. (5 attempts per side)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is slightly off-balance and leaning forward.
    • When you have established a strong collar and sleeve grip.
    • When your opponent attempts a forward-moving attack or a weak grip break.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Uchi Mata (Inner Thigh Throw): If the opponent attempts Harai Goshi, block their sweeping leg with your inner thigh and step in for an Uchi Mata. This involves stepping your right foot across their body and sweeping their inner thigh as they commit to their throw.
    • Sumo Stance/Low Base: If you anticipate the Harai Goshi, widen your stance, lower your hips, and widen your base. This makes it difficult for them to get their hip in and sweep your leg effectively.
    • Break Grip and Disengage: As the opponent initiates their pull and entry, break their collar grip and step back or to the side, creating distance. This immediately disrupts their throwing mechanics and prevents them from completing the throw.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my hip hurt when I try to do Harai Goshi?

    Hip pain during Harai Goshi often stems from a lack of proper hip rotation and relying too much on arm pulling. Instead of allowing your hip to act as a pivot, you might be twisting your lower back or jamming your hip bone into your opponent forcefully without the correct body alignment. Ensure your hips are rotating inward and your weight is transferring smoothly before initiating the sweep.

    Q: How can I do Harai Goshi against a much bigger and heavier opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing become paramount. You cannot rely on strength. Focus on their balance; look for moments when they shift their weight forward or to one side. Your entry needs to be precise, getting your hip deep and using their forward momentum against them. A strong, sharp sweep of their supporting leg is crucial, combined with a tight pull on their collar to unbalance them over your hip.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt Harai Goshi in BJJ?

    The optimal time to attempt Harai Goshi is when your opponent is slightly off-balance, particularly if they are leaning forward or have committed their weight to one leg. This often occurs when they are attempting to advance their position, break your grips, or after they have just applied a weak pressure. Having a secure collar and sleeve grip is essential, allowing you to control their upper body and guide them into the throw.

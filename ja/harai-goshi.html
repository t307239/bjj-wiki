Harai Goshi: The Sweeping Hip Throw for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Harai Goshi is a dynamic hip throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to unbalance and off-balance your opponent to the side and behind.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of coordinated hip and foot movement, relying too much on arm strength and causing them to be easily countered.

The key to Harai Goshi is the synchronized rotation of your hips and the sweeping action of your leg, using your entire body as a lever.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grips: Right-handed thrower: Left hand grips opponent's right lapel (collar grip), right hand grips opponent's left sleeve near the elbow (sleeve grip).
    2. Stance: Adopt a staggered stance, right foot slightly forward, left foot back. Maintain a hip-width base.
    3. Initial Pull: With your left hand, pull your opponent's collar towards your upper chest. Simultaneously, with your right hand, pull their left sleeve down and across their body.
    4. Foot Placement: Step your right foot forward, angling your body 45 degrees to your opponent's left. Your right foot should be roughly in line with their left foot, creating a slight gap.
    5. Hip Insertion: Rotate your hips inward, bringing your right hip into contact with your opponent's abdomen. Your hips should be lower than theirs.
    6. Body Rotation & Sweep: As your hips connect, rotate your torso to your left, keeping your back straight. Simultaneously, sweep your right leg outward and upward in an arc, aiming to catch your opponent's left leg just above the ankle.
    7. Weight Transfer: Transfer your weight from your back foot onto your right leg and into your opponent. Your pulling hands drive them forward and downward over your hip.
    8. Completion: Continue the sweep and rotation, driving your opponent over your hip and to the mat. Maintain control with your grips throughout.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Hyperextension: Attempting to 'kick' the opponent's leg with a straight leg, instead of a sweeping motion. This can cause hyperextension and ligament damage (ACL, MCL) in your own knee.
    • Correct: Maintain a slight bend in your sweeping leg and focus on a smooth, arcing motion that lifts and sweeps their leg.
    • Lower Back Strain: Rounding your back significantly when inserting your hip, especially when pulling hard with your arms. This can lead to muscle strains and disc compression.
    • Correct: Keep your back straight and engage your core, allowing your hips to drive the rotation and unbalancing.
    • Ankle/Foot Injury: Planting your foot too flat or too far forward when stepping in, leading to an awkward angle when sweeping. This can result in ankle sprains or fractures.
    • Correct: Step with the ball of your foot, allowing for better mobility and a more natural sweeping action.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Footwork & Hip Rotation: Practice the stepping, angling, and hip insertion motion without a partner. Focus on the body rotation. (10 reps each side, 0% resistance).
    2. Solo Sweep Motion: Add the leg sweep motion after the hip insertion. Focus on the arc and timing. (10 reps each side, 0% resistance).
    3. Mirror Drill (No Resistance): With a partner standing passively, practice the full sequence of grips, steps, hip insertion, and sweep. Partner offers no resistance. (5 reps each side, 10% resistance).
    4. Assisted Sweep (Light Resistance): Partner stands passively but provides a slight static resistance, allowing you to feel the weight transfer and sweep. (5 reps each side, 25% resistance).
    5. Controlled Entry & Sweep: Partner offers light, reactive resistance. Focus on breaking their posture and entering cleanly, then executing the sweep. (10 reps each side, 50% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling (Controlled): Attempt Harai Goshi during live rolling with a partner who understands you are drilling it. Focus on proper entry and execution, not necessarily the finish. (5 attempts per round, 75% resistance).
    7. Live Rolling (Full): Attempt Harai Goshi in regular sparring. Focus on timing, grip fighting, and the biomechanics of the throw. (As opportunities arise, 90-100% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up and you have good control of their collar and sleeve.
    • When you've successfully pulled your opponent forward, creating an opening to step in.
    • During a stand-up exchange where you can establish dominant grips and control their balance.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Uchi Mata (Inner Thigh Throw): If the opponent attempts Harai Goshi, as they step in with their right leg, you step your left leg slightly forward and inward, blocking their right leg with your inner thigh while pulling them forward and down.
    • Osoto Gari (Major Outer Reaping): As the opponent commits to the Harai Goshi entry, step your left leg back and wide, using your right leg to reap their extended right leg while pulling them backward and to their right.
    • Defensive Posture & Grip Release: If you feel the opponent entering, immediately stiffen your base, widen your stance, and break their dominant grips by pulling your arms apart and slightly upward, creating space to disengage.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my right wrist hurt when I try to do Harai Goshi?

    Your wrist pain likely stems from gripping too tightly with your fingers alone and over-relying on arm strength. When you pull the sleeve, instead of just using your fingers, make sure your entire forearm and even your shoulder are involved in the pull. The sleeve grip should feel like a connection through your arm, not just a finger clamp. This distributes the force and reduces strain on your wrist joint.

    Q: How can I use Harai Goshi effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, the key is to use their size and momentum against them by focusing on their balance and your hip insertion. Don't try to lift them; instead, use your grips to pull them forward and off-balance. Your hip insertion needs to be lower and tighter, creating a strong pivot point. The sweeping leg should aim to catch their base, and the rotation of your hips and torso will do the work, not brute strength.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate Harai Goshi in a BJJ match?

    The ideal time to initiate Harai Goshi is when your opponent is slightly off-balance forward, perhaps after they've attempted a failed pass or a weak attack. You need to establish dominant grips first, ideally controlling their collar and sleeve to break their posture. Look for moments of stillness or slight forward momentum from them, which creates the perfect window to step in and execute the throw before they can react defensively.

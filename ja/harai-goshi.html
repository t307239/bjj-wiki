Harai Goshi is a dynamic hip throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to off-balance and sweep your opponent to the mat.

White belts often struggle by relying on raw strength rather than proper body mechanics, leading to missed throws and potential injury.

The key insight is to use your hip as a fulcrum and your leg as a sweeping lever, synchronized with a strong pulling action.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grips: Right-handed attacker: Left hand grasps opponent's right collar, right hand grasps opponent's right sleeve. Left foot steps slightly forward, right foot parallel behind it.
    2. Entry: Step your left foot forward and to the side of your opponent's right foot, creating a 45-degree angle between your centerlines.
    3. Hip Engagement: Rotate your hips inward, bringing your right hip bone into contact with your opponent's right abdomen or lower rib cage.
    4. Body Rotation: Simultaneously, rotate your upper body to your left, keeping your back straight and your head to your opponent's right side.
    5. Pulling Action: Pull your opponent forward and down with your sleeve grip, and create a downward pulling tension with your collar grip.
    6. Leg Sweep: As your opponent's weight shifts forward onto their right leg, lift your right leg and sweep it across the front of their right thigh, from bottom to top.
    7. Weight Transfer: Drive your hips into your opponent and transfer your weight forward, completing the sweep as their balance is broken.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Knee Bend: Bending knees too deeply and letting the hips drop too low can hyper-extend the attacker's knee or cause a direct knee impact injury if the throw is missed.
    • Jerking Head: Pulling only with the head and neck muscles without engaging the core and hips leads to neck strain and potential whiplash injuries.
    • Stiff Leg Sweep: Attempting to sweep with a straight, locked leg without proper hip rotation and timing can result in ankle sprains or knee ligament damage.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Footwork and Hip Rotation: Practice the stepping and hip rotation motion without a partner. 50 reps.
    2. Grip Fighting and Body Positioning: Practice securing grips and establishing the correct body angle. 50 reps.
    3. Controlled Hip Contact and Pull: With a compliant partner, practice bringing your hip in and initiating the pulling action. 25% resistance. 20 reps.
    4. Full Sweep Motion (No Resistance): Practice the entire Harai Goshi motion with a partner, focusing on the sweep without resistance. 50% resistance. 20 reps.
    5. Controlled Throwing (Light Resistance): Practice Harai Goshi with a partner offering light resistance, focusing on timing and execution. 75% resistance. 15 reps.
    6. Live Rolling (Application): Attempt Harai Goshi during live rolling, prioritizing safety and technique over success. 90-100% resistance. 5 attempts per round.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is leaning forward, creating an opening for a forward throw.
    • When you have established strong collar and sleeve grips, allowing for control.
    • When your opponent is off-balance or has a narrow base.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Osoto Gari Entry: If the attacker commits to Harai Goshi, step your right leg back and outside their right leg, and execute an Osoto Gari to disrupt their balance.
    • Snap Down Defense: As the attacker pulls, use a snap down motion with your sleeve grip to break their posture and create space to disengage or counter.
    • Uchi Mata Entry: If the attacker's hip is not fully engaged, step your left foot inside their right leg and execute an Uchi Mata, using their forward momentum against them.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do Harai Goshi in BJJ?

    Wrist pain often occurs if you are gripping too tightly with your fingers and not distributing the tension through your forearm and shoulder. Ensure your collar grip is firm but not tense, and your sleeve grip uses your entire forearm to pull, not just your fingers. Focus on pulling your opponent's elbow towards your hip.

    Q: How can I perform Harai Goshi against a much bigger BJJ opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes even more critical. Focus on getting your hip deeper into their abdomen and using your entire body to rotate and lift. Your leg sweep must be precise and timed perfectly as they shift weight. Do not try to overpower them; instead, use their size and momentum against them by breaking their posture and balance.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt Harai Goshi from a standing clinch in BJJ?

    The optimal time to attempt Harai Goshi is when your opponent has a slightly forward lean or has just committed to a forward movement, creating an imbalance. Look for opportunities when they are reaching for a grip or trying to advance their position. Avoid attempting it when they are actively resisting and firmly planted with a wide base.

Harai Goshi: The Sweeping Hip Throw for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Harai Goshi is a dynamic hip throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to sweep your opponent off their feet and to the mat.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of coordinated body movement, relying on brute force instead of leverage.

The key is to use your hip as a fulcrum, generating rotational force through your entire body.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Right-handed grip: Your left hand secures a deep collar grip on the opponent's gi lapel, pulling them towards your center. Your right hand secures a strong sleeve grip on their right arm, slightly extended.
    2. Footwork: Step your left foot forward and slightly to the side of the opponent's right foot, creating a narrow base.
    3. Hip entry: Rotate your hips inward, aligning your right hip against the opponent's right side. Your pelvis should be angled approximately 45 degrees to their line of force.
    4. Weight transfer: Shift your weight from your right leg onto your left leg, creating a counterbalance.
    5. Lifting and sweeping: As you lower your hips, use your right sleeve grip to pull their extended arm across your body. Simultaneously, elevate your right hip and sweep their right leg backward with your right thigh.
    6. Body rotation: Maintain a tight connection and rotate your torso counter-clockwise, driving your hips forward and upward.
    7. Completion: Continue the sweep until the opponent is airborne and falls to the mat. Maintain control of grips throughout the throw.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Hyperextending the elbow: Grabbing the sleeve with excessive force and attempting to yank can cause the opponent's elbow to bend backward beyond its natural range, leading to ligament tears in the elbow joint. Instead, maintain a controlled grip and use your body's rotation to initiate the sweep.
    • Knee valgus stress: Stepping too wide or entering the throw with the knee caving inward (valgus collapse) can place excessive stress on the medial collateral ligament (MCL) of the knee, leading to sprains. Maintain a strong, stable knee alignment and a narrower base.
    • Lower back hyperextension: Attempting to lift the opponent solely with the back muscles instead of using hip and leg drive can result in lumbar spine hyperextension and strain. Engage your glutes and hamstrings to drive your hips forward and upward.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo stance and hip movement: Practice entering the Harai Goshi stance and rotating your hips without a partner. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Grip and hip entry drill: With a compliant partner, practice securing grips and entering your hip position. (20 reps per side, 10% resistance)
    3. Sweep motion without lift: Practice the sweeping motion of the leg and hip drive without lifting the partner. (20 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled sweep with partner support: Execute the full Harai Goshi with a partner offering minimal resistance and support. (15 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Dynamic entry and sweep: Practice the entire Harai Goshi sequence with a slightly more resistant partner, focusing on timing. (10 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling attempts: Attempt Harai Goshi during live rolling, focusing on successful entry and execution. (5 attempts per round, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is in a staggered stance with their weight forward.
    • When you have established a strong collar and sleeve grip, pulling them into your center.
    • When you are able to achieve a deep hip connection and break their balance forward.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Osoto Gari (Major Outer Reaping): If the opponent initiates Harai Goshi, step your left foot back and outside their right leg. As they commit their hip, step your right foot back and perform an Osoto Gari to off-balance them in the opposite direction.
    • Snap Down: If the opponent attempts to enter for Harai Goshi but their grip is weak, use a strong snap down with your collar grip to pull their head down and break their posture, transitioning to a dominant position.
    • Uchi Mata (Inner Thigh Reap): As the opponent enters with their hip, if you can maintain your structure, step your left foot between their legs and perform an Uchi Mata to disrupt their balance and throw.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do Harai Goshi?

    Wrist pain during Harai Goshi often stems from gripping the opponent's sleeve with your fingers extended too far backward, especially when they resist. This puts excessive strain on your wrist extensor muscles and ligaments. To prevent this, maintain a more neutral wrist position, keeping your fingers slightly curled and engaging your forearm muscles for a stronger, more stable grip. Focus on using your body's rotation to initiate the throw, rather than solely relying on wrist strength.

    Q: How can I do Harai Goshi effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paramount. Focus on getting *under* their center of gravity by sinking your hips lower than theirs. Your hip entry must be precise, making solid contact with their side. Instead of trying to lift them, emphasize the sweeping motion with your leg and the rotational force from your core. Use their forward momentum against them by timing your entry as they step in.

    Q: I keep failing Harai Goshi and ending up on my back. What am I doing wrong?

    Ending up on your back usually means you've committed to the throw without breaking the opponent's balance or establishing a strong enough connection. If their base is too solid, your hip entry will simply be absorbed. Ensure you're pulling them forward with your collar grip to create an opening. Also, check that your own base isn't too wide; a narrow, stable stance allows for better hip insertion and prevents you from being easily unbalanced. Practice the initial pulling and entry phases diligently.

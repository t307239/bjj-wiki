Harai Goshi: The Sweeping Hip Throw for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Harai Goshi is initiated from a standing position, typically when your opponent is facing you or slightly angled. It aims to unbalance and throw them over your hip.

White belts often struggle because they lack precise body control and focus on brute strength over leverage and timing.

The key insight is to use your pulling and stepping actions to break their posture and create a dynamic imbalance that your hip rotation exploits.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grips: Right-handed attacker: Left hand on opponent's right lapel, near the collarbone. Right hand on opponent's left sleeve, near the elbow.
    2. Stance: Feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, maintaining a stable base.
    3. Entry: Step your left foot forward and to the side of your opponent's right foot, creating a diagonal line between your bodies.
    4. Pulling Action: Simultaneously pull your opponent's left sleeve towards your right hip and their right collar towards your left shoulder. This breaks their posture and shifts their weight forward.
    5. Hip Placement: Rotate your hips inward, turning your back towards your opponent. Your right hip bone should be in contact with their lower abdomen or upper hip.
    6. Lifting and Rotating: As you rotate your hips, lift your opponent slightly by extending your left leg and slightly bending your right knee. Your pulling arms continue to guide their upper body over your hip.
    7. Throwing Motion: Complete the throw by continuing the hip rotation and forward pull, guiding your opponent to the mat. Maintain your base and control throughout the movement.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Step and Hip Contact: Stepping too close or not rotating the hips enough can cause your opponent to fall awkwardly on your knee or ankle, leading to hyperextension or ligament tears (ACL, MCL).
    • Pulling with Arms Only: Relying solely on arm strength without hip rotation leads to inefficient force transfer and potential shoulder or elbow strain for both parties.
    • Losing Balance During Entry: A wide or unstable stance during the initial step can result in you falling over your opponent, risking a twisted ankle or knee injury.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Footwork and Hip Rotation: Practice the stepping and hip turning motion without a partner, focusing on a stable base and a full rotation (10 reps).
    2. Grip Fighting and Posture Break: With a compliant partner, practice establishing grips and the pulling action to break their posture (20 reps, 25% resistance).
    3. Entry and Hip Sweep (No Throw): Practice entering, establishing hip contact, and performing the hip sweep motion without completing the throw, allowing the partner to fall naturally (10 reps, 50% resistance).
    4. Controlled Harai Goshi with Light Resistance: Execute the full Harai Goshi with a partner who offers minimal resistance, focusing on smooth transitions (15 reps, 75% resistance).
    5. Harai Goshi with Dynamic Resistance: Practice against a partner who actively tries to resist and maintain their balance, requiring more precise timing and force application (10 reps, 90% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling Application: Attempt Harai Goshi during live rolling, focusing on identifying opportunities and executing the technique with intent (5 attempts per roll).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT

    • When your opponent is off-balance and leaning forward.
    • During a grip-fighting exchange where you can secure dominant grips.
    • When your opponent attempts a forward-moving technique, creating an opening for a counter-throw.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS

    • Osoto Gari: If your opponent attempts Harai Goshi, step your right foot back and to the side, widening your base. As they rotate their hip, use your left leg to sweep their right leg out and behind them, driving them forward into your Osoto Gari.
    • Snap Down: When your opponent initiates the pulling motion, resist by pulling their collar grip down sharply and forward. As their head comes down, follow with a forward pressure to their upper back to break their posture further and potentially create a takedown opportunity.
    • Ankle Pick: If your opponent commits heavily to their hip rotation, quickly drop your level and shoot for their nearest ankle. This disrupts their throwing mechanics and allows you to secure a single-leg takedown.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to Harai Goshi?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you're gripping too tightly with your fingers extended, or if you're pulling with your arms instead of using your body's rotation. Ensure your grips are firm but not tense, with your thumb wrapped around the collar and sleeve. Focus on driving the movement from your hips and core, using your arms primarily to guide and control the opponent's posture.

    Q: How can I Harai Goshi against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paramount. Focus on breaking their posture and off-balancing them significantly before committing to the throw. Use your movement to get your hip in deep, and ensure your pulling grips are strong to draw their weight onto your hip. A well-timed entry and a powerful hip rotation are more effective than trying to match their strength.

    Q: When is the best time to use Harai Goshi in BJJ?

    Harai Goshi is most effective when your opponent is slightly off-balance and moving forward, or when you've established strong grips and have successfully broken their posture. It's an excellent choice when you have a good angle on your opponent and can create a dynamic imbalance that your hip rotation can exploit. Avoid attempting it when your opponent is firmly planted and squared up.

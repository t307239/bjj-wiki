Harai Goshi: The Sweeping Hip Throw for BJJ White Belts

Harai Goshi is a dynamic hip throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to unbalance your opponent and project them to the mat. It's an excellent way to transition from standing to grappling.

White belts often struggle because they lack the precise hip engagement and weight transfer necessary, leading to energy leaks and poor results. Over-reliance on brute strength without proper mechanics is a common pitfall.

The key mechanical insight is to use your hips as a pivot point while simultaneously sweeping your opponent's leg with your own, creating a fulcrum for their fall. This leverages your entire body's mass efficiently.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Right-Handed Harai Goshi: Establish a strong right lapel grip (near the opponent's collarbone) and a left sleeve grip (near the opponent's wrist). Maintain a staggered stance, left foot slightly forward.
    2. Kuzushi (Unbalancing): Pull the opponent's left lapel towards your right chest and simultaneously pull their left sleeve towards your right hip. Lower your center of gravity by bending your knees slightly, keeping your back relatively straight.
    3. Tsukuri (Entry): Step your left foot forward and to the side of the opponent's left foot. Rotate your hips to face the opponent's left side, turning your hips inward to create a strong blocking surface.
    4. Hip Engagement: Drive your right hip into the opponent's lower abdomen/hip area. Your right hip bone should be slightly higher than your left hip bone, creating a wedging action.
    5. The Sweep: As your hips engage, sweep your right leg across the front of the opponent's left leg, aiming to contact their thigh or calf area with the back of your thigh. This sweep should be a lifting and outward motion.
    6. Projection: Continue the rotation of your torso and hips, driving your right shoulder towards the opponent's left shoulder. Your pulling arm (left sleeve grip) continues to guide their upper body down.
    7. Weight Transfer: All your weight should transfer onto your right leg as you pivot, with your left leg acting as the sweeping lever. The opponent's weight should be directed over your right hip and off their base.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hip Rotation: Attempting to throw without fully rotating your hips, leading to a "dead arm" throw. This can strain your shoulder and elbow ligaments as you rely solely on arm strength, risking rotator cuff tears or elbow hyperextension. The correct movement involves a full hip turn to drive your mass into the throw.
    • Leg Sweep Too Low or Too High: Sweeping the opponent's leg too low (at the ankle) can cause your own ankle to twist, risking a syndesmosis sprain (high ankle sprain). Sweeping too high (at the knee) can lead to your leg getting trapped, risking a knee ligament tear if the opponent resists. Sweep at the mid-thigh to upper-calf area with a lifting motion.
    • Forcing the Pull: Pulling solely with your arms without engaging your hips and legs. This leads to lower back strain and ineffective throws. Focus on driving your hips into the opponent and using your leg sweep to create the leverage.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Rotation and Sweep: Practice the hip turn and leg sweep motion without a partner. Focus on the feel of your hips turning and your leg sweeping across. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance).
    2. Shadow Throwing: Stand in front of a mirror or imaginary opponent. Execute the full Harai Goshi motion, focusing on grip, unbalancing, entry, hip engagement, sweep, and projection. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance).
    3. Partner Static Entry: With a compliant partner, practice the grips and the entry step. Your partner stands still. Focus on getting your hips into position and initiating the sweep. (10 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Controlled Sweep Drill: With a compliant partner, focus on the hip engagement and leg sweep. Your partner offers slight resistance to the sweep. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Controlled Throwing: Execute Harai Goshi with a partner who offers controlled resistance. Focus on timing and proper weight transfer. (10 reps per side, 75% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling Application: Attempt Harai Goshi during live rolling, focusing on creating opportunities and executing the mechanics learned. Don't force it; be ready to transition if it's not there. (5 attempts per round, 90-100% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is leaning forward slightly, creating an opening for kuzushi.
    • When you have established a strong lapel and sleeve grip and can control their posture.
    • When you are in a stable standing clinch position with a good base.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Uki Otoshi (Floating Drop): If the opponent attempts Harai Goshi and you feel their hip coming in, quickly drop your weight, extend your legs, and guide their throwing arm down to create a counter-throw. This involves lowering your base and using their momentum against them.
    • Ouchi Gari (Major Inner Reaping): If the opponent commits to the Harai Goshi sweep, step your right leg slightly back and inside their sweeping leg, then reap their right leg with your left leg, pulling them down. This intercepts their sweep and creates a new attack.
    • Hand Fighting and Posture Control: If you anticipate the Harai Goshi, break their grips aggressively and immediately regain your upright posture. Create distance by stepping back and to the side, disrupting their ability to generate leverage. This involves active hand fighting and maintaining your base.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to grip for Harai Goshi?

    Wrist pain often stems from gripping too tightly with straight fingers or forcing the pull with only your wrist. For a secure grip, use a "finger-curled" grip on the lapel and a "cupped" grip on the sleeve, allowing your knuckles to point slightly upwards. Ensure your pulling motion originates from your shoulder and torso, not just your wrist, to distribute the force and protect the joint.

    Q: How can I effectively use Harai Goshi against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on using their size against them by exploiting their forward lean. Your goal is to get *under* their center of gravity. Use your hips to get deeper into their base, and your leg sweep needs to be aggressive and timed precisely to their forward momentum. Prioritize breaking their posture and balance before initiating the throw.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt Harai Goshi in a BJJ match?

    Harai Goshi is most effective when your opponent is actively trying to advance their position or is posturing up. Look for moments when they are reaching for your guard or trying to establish control from a standing position. It's also a good option when you've successfully broken their posture and they are momentarily off-balance, creating an opening for your throw.

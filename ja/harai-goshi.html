Harai Goshi: The Essential BJJ Takedown for White Belts

Harai Goshi is a dynamic hip throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to unbalance your opponent and sweep them to the mat.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of coordinated body mechanics and a tendency to rely on brute strength, leading to inefficiency and injury.

The core principle is using your hip as a fulcrum while simultaneously pulling their posture down and forward, creating a powerful lever action.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grips: Right-handed thrower: Left hand grips opponent's right lapel (collar grip), right hand grips opponent's right sleeve (sleeve grip).
    2. Stance: Adopt a staggered stance, left foot forward, about shoulder-width apart. Your weight should be balanced on the balls of your feet.
    3. Break Posture: Pull down on the lapel grip and slightly back on the sleeve grip, forcing their head towards your chest. Simultaneously, step your left foot forward, aligning your left hip with their center line.
    4. Hip Placement: Rotate your hips inward, driving your right hip into their lower abdomen/pelvic area. Your right hip bone should be slightly higher than theirs.
    5. Leg Sweep: As your hip connects, lift your right leg (the leg closest to them) and sweep it in a wide arc behind their right leg. The sweep should be initiated from the hip, not just the knee.
    6. Body Rotation and Pull: Simultaneously, rotate your torso and pull their lapel grip downwards and slightly across your body. Your right arm acts as a support and pulling point.
    7. Weight Transfer: Transfer your weight onto your right hip and right leg as you complete the sweep and pull, driving them over your hip and down to the mat.
    8. Control: As they fall, maintain your grips and follow them down, securing a dominant position like side control or mount.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Hyperextension: Attempting to sweep with a stiff, straight right leg and failing to bend the knee during the sweep arc can cause hyperextension of the knee joint, leading to ligament damage (ACL, MCL).
    • Correct: Keep a slight bend in your right knee throughout the sweep, allowing for dynamic movement and shock absorption.
    • Lower Back Strain: Trying to throw by solely pulling with your arms and twisting your back without engaging your hips and legs can lead to lumbar spine strain or disc injury.
    • Correct: Initiate the movement by sinking your hips and driving them forward, using your legs and core for power, not just your back.
    • Shoulder Impingement: Over-extending your pulling arm while gripping the sleeve, especially if the opponent resists strongly, can cause shoulder impingement or rotator cuff tears.
    • Correct: Maintain a strong, stable posture with your elbow slightly tucked, using your body's rotation to generate power rather than just arm strength.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Footwork & Hip Rotation (0%): Practice stepping and rotating your hips without a partner. Focus on the hip-to-hip connection feel. 50 repetitions.
    2. Grip & Posture Break Drill (0%): Stand facing a wall or heavy bag. Practice breaking posture with your grips and simulating the hip connection. 50 repetitions.
    3. Shadow Harai Goshi (25%): Perform the entire Harai Goshi motion without a partner, focusing on smooth transitions and correct body mechanics. 20 repetitions.
    4. Controlled Partner Drill (50%): With a cooperative partner, practice the grip, posture break, and hip connection. Partner offers minimal resistance. 5 sets of 5 repetitions each side.
    5. Semi-Live Takedown (75%): Practice Harai Goshi against a partner who is actively resisting but not fully resisting. Focus on timing and commitment. 5 sets of 3 repetitions each side.
    6. Live Rolling (90-100%): Integrate Harai Goshi attempts into live rolling, focusing on opportunities and proper execution. Aim for 3-5 attempts per round.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is off-balance, leaning forward, or has a weak base.
    • When you have established strong lapel and sleeve grips.
    • When you have successfully broken their posture and are close to their center of gravity.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Osoto Gari (Major Outer Reaping): If the opponent attempts Harai Goshi, step your left leg back and outside their right leg, placing your right foot behind their right ankle. Sink your hips and pull their upper body forward to execute Osoto Gari.
    • Inside Reaping (Uchi Mata): As they drive their hip in, step your right foot slightly forward and to the side of their right leg. Lift your right leg and sweep their inner thigh while pulling their upper body down and away.
    • Counter-Grip and Pull: If they grip your lapel and sleeve, immediately grip-fight to break their grips. If they commit to the throw, focus on maintaining your base and using your free hand to block their hip or push their shoulder.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do Harai Goshi?

    Wrist pain often occurs from gripping too tightly with a straight wrist or from the opponent resisting your sleeve grip by pulling back with excessive force. Ensure your wrist remains slightly bent, creating a more stable and powerful grip. Instead of just pulling the sleeve, use your body's rotation and hip drive to create the unbalancing force, reducing the reliance on pure wrist strength.

    Q: How can I do Harai Goshi against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on timing and breaking their posture effectively before committing to the throw. Use your grips to pull their head down and their base forward, making them feel lighter. Your hip contact needs to be precise and driving, using their forward momentum against them. Elevating your hips slightly higher than theirs can create a better lever point.

    Q: When is the best time to try Harai Goshi from the clinch in BJJ?

    The optimal time to attempt Harai Goshi is when your opponent is leaning forward, has a compromised base, or is actively trying to advance their position. If they are too upright or stable, the throw will be much harder to execute. Look for opportunities after a failed submission attempt or when they are reaching for a grip.

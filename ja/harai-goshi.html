Harai Goshi: A Precise BJJ Takedown Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Harai Goshi is a hip throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to unbalance your opponent and sweep them to the mat.

White belts often fail by muscling the throw or failing to generate proper hip rotation, leading to inefficient attempts and potential injury.

The key insight is to use your hips as a pivot point, sweeping their leg with your own while simultaneously pulling and pushing their upper body to create a rotational force.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Right-handed attacker: Right hand grips opponent's left lapel with thumb inside the fabric, fingers outside. Left hand grips opponent's right sleeve at the elbow crease.
    2. Step forward with left foot: Place left foot slightly ahead of opponent's right foot, heel-to-toe alignment.
    3. Turn your hips: Rotate your pelvis 90 degrees to your left, bringing your right hip into contact with the opponent's right hip.
    4. Lower your base: Bend your knees deeply, creating a stable, wide base, with your center of gravity low.
    5. Sweep with right leg: Extend your right leg, hooking the opponent's right thigh or calf with the back of your knee, simultaneously lifting it upwards.
    6. Pull and Push: Pull down on the opponent's left lapel with your right hand while pushing their right sleeve/arm towards your right side with your left hand.
    7. Execute the sweep: As their leg is lifted and their upper body is rotated, drive your hips forward and slightly upward, completing the sweep and throwing them over your right hip.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect leg sweep: Attempting to lift opponent's leg with a bent knee or by kicking, which can strain the knee joint ligaments (ACL, MCL) due to hyperextension or twisting. The correct method is a controlled sweep with the back of your knee, driving it up their thigh.
    • Over-rotating upper body: Twisting your spine excessively without proper hip engagement, leading to potential lumbar disc injury or muscle tears. Maintain a stable core and let your hips lead the rotation.
    • Dropping weight too soon: Lowering your hips too far or too quickly without proper connection to the opponent's hip, causing your own knee to buckle under your weight and risking a meniscus tear. Keep your hips engaged and in contact with theirs throughout the throw.

    ドリル段階

    1. Shadow drilling (0%): Practice the footwork, hip turn, and arm movements without a partner. Focus on precise body alignment. (20 reps)
    2. Static partner drill (25%): Partner stands still. You practice grips, step, hip turn, and the sweeping motion without significant resistance or movement from them. (15 reps)
    3. Assisted movement drill (50%): Partner offers slight resistance and takes a small step as you initiate the throw. Focus on coordinating your hip drive and arm pulls. (10 reps)
    4. Controlled throw drill (75%): Partner cooperates by allowing the throw once initiated, but offers some balance and resistance. You focus on completing the sweep and rotation. (8 reps)
    5. Light resistance rolling (90%): Attempt Harai Goshi during light rolling exchanges. Partner will actively defend, but the goal is to practice timing and application. (5 attempts per round)
    6. Live rolling (100%): Attempt Harai Goshi during live rolling with full resistance. Focus on reading your opponent's posture and balance. (As opportunities arise)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • Opponent's weight forward: When they are leaning into you, making their balance unstable.
    • Opponent's stance is wide: A wider stance can make it harder for them to recover from the sweep.
    • After a failed guard pass attempt: When you have secured a good grip and they are off-balance from their own movement.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Uchi Mata (Inner Thigh Throw): If the attacker attempts Harai Goshi, the defender can step their supporting leg across the attacker's body and perform an inner thigh sweep, disrupting the throw.
    • Counter-step and sprawl: If the attacker's hip connection is weak, the defender can step their leg back and sprawl their weight onto the attacker's hips, negating the sweep.
    • Break grip and posture: The defender can actively break the attacker's grips on the lapel and sleeve, then quickly regain upright posture to prevent the throw's initiation.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my right knee hurt when I try to do Harai Goshi?

    Your right knee likely hurts because you are not properly turning your hips to meet the opponent's. Instead of your hip acting as a pivot, your knee is taking the rotational force. Ensure your right hip is firmly against your opponent's, and your right leg is sweeping their thigh with the back of your knee, not hyperextending.

    Q: How can I use Harai Goshi against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on timing and leverage rather than brute strength. Use their forward momentum against them by stepping in quickly and deeply. Your hip contact must be solid, and your sweep needs to be precise, lifting their leg high enough to disrupt their base. Your pulling and pushing grips are crucial for creating the rotational force they cannot easily resist.

    Q: I can't seem to get my opponent off balance for Harai Goshi, what am I doing wrong?

    You are likely not establishing a strong enough initial unbalancing action. Before initiating the throw, use your grips to pull their weight forward and slightly to their right (for a right-handed attack). This pre-unbalancing makes the subsequent hip turn and leg sweep much more effective, as they are already struggling to maintain their base.

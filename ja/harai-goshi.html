Harai Goshi is a dynamic hip throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to unbalance your opponent and take them down. It requires precise timing and body mechanics to execute effectively.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of coordinated hip movement and a tendency to rely on brute strength, leading to missed opportunities and potential injury. Understanding the core mechanics is crucial for success.

The single most important insight for Harai Goshi is to use your hip as a fulcrum and your body's rotation to generate the throwing power, rather than just pulling with your arms.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grips: Right-handed attacker: Left hand grips opponent's right lapel, approximately at the sternoclavicular joint. Right hand grips opponent's right sleeve, near the elbow joint.
    2. Stance and Entry: Adopt a staggered stance, left foot forward. Your opponent should be facing slightly to your right. Your body is approximately 30-45 degrees offset from your opponent.
    3. Hip Placement: Rotate your hips inward, aligning your right hip bone with the front of your opponent's right hip bone. Your hips should be lower than your opponent's hips.
    4. Kuzushi (Unbalancing): Pull the opponent's lapel grip downwards and slightly to your left. Simultaneously, pull their sleeve grip across their body towards your left shoulder, creating a diagonal pull.
    5. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight from your back (right) leg to your front (left) leg. Your right leg will then pivot.
    6. Body Rotation and Hip Contact: Rotate your torso counter-clockwise, bringing your right hip into firm contact with your opponent's right hip. Your right leg pivots on the ball of your foot, sweeping behind your opponent's right leg.
    7. The Sweep Action: As your hips make contact, extend your right leg in a sweeping motion, contacting your opponent's right thigh or calf with the back of your calf. Your hips remain low and drive upward.
    8. Completion: Continue the rotation and sweep, driving your hips upward and forward, using your lapel and sleeve grips to guide their fall. Maintain pressure to prevent them from regaining balance.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hip Position: Trying to throw with hips too high or not making contact. This can lead to hyperextension of the attacker's lumbar spine (lower back) and potential disc injury. The correct action is to sink your hips low and ensure direct contact.
    • Arm-Only Pulling: Relying solely on arm strength to pull the opponent down. This can cause shoulder impingement or rotator cuff tears due to the unnatural angles and excessive force applied. The correct action is to integrate the hip and body rotation with a controlled pull.
    • Knee Flexion Without Hip Drive: Bending the knees deeply without driving the hips forward. This can strain the knee ligaments (ACL/MCL) as the force is absorbed by the knees instead of the larger muscle groups and core. The correct action is to use the hips as the primary lifting and throwing mechanism.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling (0% Resistance): Practice the footwork, hip rotation, and sweeping motion without a partner. Focus on the smooth transition between steps. (50 reps)
    2. Mirror Drilling (0% Resistance): Stand facing a partner who mimics your movements. Practice the entry, hip placement, and Kuzushi without any resistance. (50 reps)
    3. Assisted Entry (25% Resistance): Partner stands passively. You practice the Kuzushi and the initial hip engagement. Partner offers minimal resistance to your pull and rotation. (30 reps)
    4. Controlled Throwing (50% Resistance): Partner actively cooperates by allowing you to guide their weight during the Kuzushi and throw. Focus on making solid hip contact and completing the sweep. (30 reps)
    5. Semi-Live Entry (75% Resistance): Partner offers light resistance to your Kuzushi and entry. You must generate the power through your body mechanics. Focus on adapting to their slight resistance. (20 reps)
    6. Live Rolling (90-100% Resistance): Attempt Harai Goshi in sparring. Focus on timing, grip fighting, and executing the technique under dynamic pressure. Do not force the throw; look for openings. (Practice for 5 minutes, multiple attempts)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is slightly off-balance forward or to their right.
    • You have secured strong lapel and sleeve grips.
    • Opponent is stepping forward with their right foot.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Uchi Mata (Inner Thigh Throw): If the attacker's hip is too low, step your left foot inside their right leg and perform a Uchi Mata by lifting their thigh with your leg and turning into them.
    • Counter-Rotation and Foot Block: If the attacker attempts the sweep, immediately step your right foot back and to the side, blocking their sweeping leg with your own leg while rotating your hips away from their throw.
    • Break Grip and Base: If the attacker's Kuzushi is weak, immediately break their lapel grip and step your feet wider to establish a strong base, preventing them from unbalancing you.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to do Harai Goshi?

    Your lower back pain likely stems from attempting to generate throwing power solely with your back muscles instead of your hips and legs. When you don't sink your hips low and make solid contact, your spine becomes the primary lever, leading to hyperextension and strain. Focus on bending your knees, lowering your hips below your opponent's, and driving your hip bone into their side as you rotate.

    Q: How can I perform Harai Goshi against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paramount. Do not try to match their strength; instead, focus on subtle Kuzushi to create their imbalance. Use your grip fighting to break their posture, then swiftly enter with a low hip and a powerful body rotation. Your smaller size allows for quicker hip movement, which you must exploit.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt Harai Goshi if my opponent is actively resisting my grips?

    The ideal time to attempt Harai Goshi is when your opponent is momentarily off-balance, perhaps after they've attempted a step or a forward movement. This is often when they are slightly leaning forward or their weight has shifted. If they are actively resisting and maintaining a strong base, focus on grip fighting and creating that initial imbalance before committing to the throw. A strong lapel grip that allows you to pull their weight forward is key.

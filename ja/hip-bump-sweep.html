Hip Bump Sweep: A White Belt's Biomechanically Accurate Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This technique originates from closed guard, aiming to reverse position and establish top control.

White belts often struggle by relying on upper body strength and neglecting hip mechanics.

The core insight is using your hips as a lever to displace your opponent's base.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish an overhook on your opponent's far arm with your near arm (e.g., right arm over their left arm).
    2. Secure a grip on your opponent's far sleeve (e.g., their left sleeve) with your free hand (e.g., left hand).
    3. Simultaneously, place your feet on your opponent's hips, maintaining a close guard.
    4. Shift your hips to your right by arching your back and bringing your left knee towards your chest.
    5. Drive your right hip upwards and towards your opponent's left side, creating a wedge.
    6. As your right hip elevates, extend your left leg sharply, pushing off their hip.
    7. Maintain pressure with your grips to control their upper body movement as you bridge.
    8. Rotate your body to land in side control, trapping their arm if possible.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Pushing with arms alone: This can strain your shoulder and elbow joints by hyperextension, especially if the opponent resists with significant weight. Correct by using hip drive as the primary force generator.
    • Arching back without hip elevation: This puts excessive stress on the lumbar spine, risking facet joint impingement. Correct by ensuring your hips are actively lifting off the mat.
    • Letting legs fall limp: This removes the crucial push-off point from the opponent's hips, leading to a loss of leverage and potential knee hyperextension. Correct by maintaining a strong, active push with your feet against their hips.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip movement and leg extension without a partner, focusing on hip elevation and spinal arch (10 reps).
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the full sequence, focusing on grip acquisition and hip displacement (5 reps per side).
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to your hip bump, focusing on timing and weight transfer (10 reps per side).
    4. Controlled rolling (25% resistance): Attempt the sweep against a partner who understands to lightly resist and allow the sweep to occur (5 attempts).
    5. Controlled rolling (50% resistance): Partner actively defends but allows opportunities for the sweep (5 attempts per side).
    6. Live rolling (75-90% resistance): Execute the sweep in a live roll, focusing on commitment and adapting to defense (3-5 attempts per round).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is posturing up high, creating space between your bodies.
    • Opponent is leaning heavily forward, their weight is distributed onto their hands.
    • Opponent is attempting to pass your guard from a close distance.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass Defense: If the opponent stacks, immediately tuck your chin and drive your hips back to create space, preventing their weight from crushing you. Continue to look for hip bump opportunities as they reset.
    • Knee Slice Defense: If the opponent attempts a knee slice, maintain your guard and use your foot on their hip to create distance, then re-establish grips for the sweep.
    • Re-guarding: If the sweep fails and the opponent creates space, focus on closing your guard or transitioning to a different guard like half guard to prevent the pass.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my hip hurt when I try the Hip Bump Sweep in BJJ?

    Hip pain often results from trying to force the sweep with your upper body or from an incorrect hip angle. Instead of pushing with your arms, focus on actively lifting your hips off the mat and driving your entire pelvis towards your opponent's side. This uses your glutes and core more effectively, reducing strain on your hip joint.

    Q: How do I Hip Bump Sweep against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes even more critical. Ensure your grips are tight and you're controlling their posture. The key is to create a significant angle with your hips; don't just lift, but also pivot. Drive your hip into their side, then use your extended leg to create a strong lever, pushing them off balance before they can effectively use their weight.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Hip Bump Sweep from closed guard?

    The Hip Bump Sweep is most effective when your opponent is posturing up and their weight is forward, often when they are trying to break your guard or create space to pass. Look for moments when their base is compromised and they are leaning into you. Avoid attempting it when they are flat on their back or have significant weight on their heels.

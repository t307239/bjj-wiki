Hip Bump Sweep: A White Belt's Guide to BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This sweep starts from closed guard and aims to reverse the position, placing you on top. White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of leverage and weight distribution.

The key insight is using your hips to create an off-balancing force, not just pushing with your arms.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a strong closed guard: both hooks inside opponent's thighs, shins crossed behind their back.
    2. Grip opponent's collar with your right hand, pulling their head towards your chest.
    3. Grip opponent's right sleeve with your left hand, keeping their arm bent at the elbow.
    4. Turn your body 90 degrees to your left, bringing your right hip off the mat towards your left shoulder.
    5. Drive your right hip upwards and to the left, simultaneously pushing with your left hand on their sleeve and pulling with your right hand on their collar.
    6. As your opponent's weight shifts forward, extend your left leg outwards, creating space.
    7. Execute the hip bump by driving your right hip into their side, forcing them to fall.
    8. Immediately bring your left knee to the mat and drive your right knee into their hip to establish side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Trying to push opponent's entire body away with only arms: This can strain the shoulder joint (rotator cuff) or elbow (ligament damage) due to excessive, uncontrolled force against a stable base. Instead, focus hip drive.
    • Arching your lower back excessively to create space: This can lead to lumbar spine strain or disc injury. Keep your core engaged and use hip movement.
    • Letting opponent's arm posture up during the push: This can lead to a shoulder lock (armbar) if your arm is extended improperly. Maintain control of the bent sleeve grip.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip bump motion without a partner, focusing on hip elevation and rotation (20 reps).
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the full sequence of grips, hip bump, and transition to side control (10 reps per side).
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers light resistance to the hip bump. Focus on generating enough force with your hips (10 reps per side).
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to maintain base but allows the sweep to happen with proper mechanics (10 reps per side).
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner defends more actively, requiring precise timing and hip pressure (5 reps per side).
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Attempt the sweep in a controlled sparring situation, focusing on execution over success (5 attempts).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When opponent is posturing up in your closed guard, creating space between their hips and yours.
    • When opponent attempts to pass your guard by stacking you or driving forward.
    • When you have established strong collar and sleeve grips, controlling their posture.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stacking pass: If opponent recognizes the hip bump and drives their weight forward and down, immediately bring your knees together to form a tight guard and try to reguard or transition to a technical stand-up.
    • Hip escape: If opponent tries to prevent the hip bump by escaping their hips in the opposite direction, follow their hip with your own and maintain your grips to prevent them from establishing a strong base.
    • Knee slide defense: If opponent attempts to slide a knee inside to pass, use your free leg to block their knee and immediately re-establish your guard or transition to a sweep.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my hip hurt when I try the Hip Bump Sweep in BJJ?

    Your hip may hurt if you are pushing your opponent's entire body with your arms and legs, rather than using your hips as the primary driving force. This can cause hyperextension or twisting of the hip joint. Focus on elevating your hip off the mat and rotating it towards your shoulder, then driving that elevated hip into your opponent's side.

    Q: How do I Hip Bump Sweep against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on minimizing their base and leverage. Ensure your collar grip is tight and you are pulling their head down, breaking their posture. Use your hip bump to create a sharp, unexpected angle of attack rather than a sustained push. The smaller the base they have, the easier it is to sweep.

    Q: When is the best time to try a Hip Bump Sweep in BJJ?

    The Hip Bump Sweep is most effective when your opponent is postured up and their weight is forward, especially if they are trying to stack you or drive into your guard. Look for opportunities where there is a slight gap between your hips and theirs, allowing you to initiate the hip rotation and drive.

This sweep starts from closed guard, aiming to transition to mount or side control by unbalancing your opponent. White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength instead of proper leverage. The key is to use your hips as a lever, creating a powerful upward and sideways motion.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a strong closed guard with both feet on the inside of your opponent's hips.
    2. Grip your opponent's collar with your right hand and their left sleeve with your left hand.
    3. Untuck your left leg, placing your left foot flat on the mat near your opponent's left hip.
    4. Simultaneously, bring your right knee to your chest, creating a strong frame.
    5. Drive your hips UPWARDS, creating space by pushing your opponent's upper body away with your arms and right knee.
    6. Swing your left leg over your opponent's left hip, aiming to hook their left thigh with your left foot.
    7. Explode your hips to your right, using your right foot on the mat as a pivot and your left leg to lift their hip.
    8. Transition to mount by bringing your right leg over their body as you complete the hip rotation.
    9. Maintain control by keeping your hips low and your opponent's weight on your chest.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Risk 1: Knee Hyperextension. When untucking your leg (step 3), if your left knee extends beyond its natural range of motion while pushing off the mat, you risk tearing your ACL or MCL. Correct: Keep a slight bend in your left knee and focus on pushing your hip UPWARDS, not just extending your leg.
    • Risk 2: Shoulder Impingement. During the hip explosion (step 7), if your opponent stacks you heavily and your right arm is trapped between your body and theirs, you can impinge your shoulder joint. Correct: Keep your right elbow tucked towards your ribs and use your left hand on their collar to maintain distance and control the angle.
    • Risk 3: Lower Back Strain. When driving your hips up (step 5), if you round your lower back and try to lift with your back muscles, you risk muscle strains or disc issues. Correct: Maintain a neutral spine and focus on using your glutes and core to generate the hip power, imagining you are bridging.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Drill (0% resistance): Practice the hip bump motion and leg untuck/re-tuck without a partner, focusing on hip movement and body positioning. (20 reps)
    2. Assisted Drill (25% resistance): Partner lies on their back, you practice the grips, hip lift, and leg sweep motion, with minimal resistance from partner. (15 reps)
    3. Controlled Sweep (50% resistance): From closed guard, partner helps initiate the sweep by allowing you to unbalance them. Focus on the hip drive and leg hook. (10 reps per side)
    4. Guard Pass Defense (75% resistance): Partner attempts a basic guard pass (e.g., standing up). You attempt the hip bump sweep from closed guard. (5 reps per side)
    5. Flow Rolling (90% resistance): Incorporate the hip bump sweep into open mat rolling, but with a clear intention to sweep and transition, stopping if the sweep is not successful. (5 minutes)
    6. Live Rolling (100% resistance): Attempt the hip bump sweep during regular sparring with no limitations. (10 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is postured up high, creating space between their hips and yours.
    • When your opponent is leaning heavily forward, making them susceptible to an upward hip drive.
    • When your opponent attempts to break your closed guard by stacking or pressuring down.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass Defense: If your opponent stacks you heavily, instead of hip bumping, immediately bring your knees to your chest and attempt to reguard or transition to a technical stand-up, protecting your spine. Correct: Keep your hips tucked, knees tight to your chest, and use your arms to push their shoulders away.
    • Leg Drag Counter: If your opponent tries to leg drag your leg as you untuck, immediately bring your untucked knee back in and re-establish closed guard or transition to a butterfly guard. Correct: Keep your untucked foot active and ready to pull back in, using your heel to create space.
    • Postured Up Defense: If your opponent stays tightly postured, the hip bump sweep loses its leverage. In this case, focus on controlling their posture with collar and sleeve grips and look for other sweeps like the scissor sweep or pendulum sweep. Correct: Maintain tight grips and use your legs to control their hip distance.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my hip bump sweep feel weak and my opponent just pushes me back down?

    Your hip bump sweep likely lacks the crucial upward hip drive. Instead of just pushing your opponent away, focus on bridging your hips aggressively towards the ceiling. This creates the necessary leverage by lifting their center of gravity before you attempt the sweep motion. Ensure your feet are firmly planted on the mat to generate power.

    Q: How do I Hip Bump Sweep against a much bigger and heavier opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, the hip bump sweep relies even more on precise timing and leverage. Focus on controlling their posture by pulling their collar down and keeping your knees tight to their hips. When you untuck your leg and drive your hips up, imagine you are trying to 'pop' their hips off the mat. Use your body weight efficiently to unbalance them, rather than trying to overpower them.

    Q: When is the best moment to try the Hip Bump Sweep from closed guard?

    The ideal moment is when your opponent is postured up, meaning their torso is leaning forward and their hips are relatively close to yours. This posture creates a gap you can exploit with your upward hip movement. Avoid attempting it when they are flat on their back or if they are already collapsing their weight onto you, as this removes the necessary leverage.

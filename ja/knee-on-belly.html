Knee on Belly is a dominant positional control in BJJ, achieved after a successful guard pass, aiming to pin the opponent and set up submissions or further positional advancement.

Many white belts fail because they place their weight too high or too low, losing balance and control, and allowing the opponent to escape easily.

The key mechanical insight is to create a stable tripod: your knee, your foot, and your opponent's hip, with your upper body providing downward pressure and control.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a passed guard position (e.g., side control), drive your lead knee (e.g., right knee) onto the opponent's abdomen, slightly to the side of their midline.
    2. Place the ball of your lead foot (right foot) firmly on the mat, creating a base with your knee and foot.
    3. Your trail leg (left leg) should be bent, with the foot flat on the mat behind you, creating a wider base and forward momentum.
    4. Position your hips low, almost touching the opponent's hip on the side of your lead knee, distributing your weight directly over their center of mass.
    5. Your chest should be pressed down onto the opponent's upper chest or shoulder, creating forward pressure.
    6. Your arms should frame the opponent's head and far shoulder, preventing them from bridging or shrimping away.
    7. Maintain a slight head-on-head connection or chin-to-chest posture to prevent the opponent from turning into you.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly placing your lead knee too high on the sternum can cause rib fractures or intercostal muscle tears from direct pressure.
    • Failing to keep your trail leg foot planted can lead to a loss of base, causing your knee to hyper-extend or your ankle to roll, risking ACL or ankle ligament damage.
    • Allowing your hips to drift too far away from the opponent's body creates a gap, making it easy for them to shrimp and escape, and can strain your lower back from overextension.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice transitioning from side control to Knee on Belly, focusing on hip placement and weight distribution without a partner (10 reps).
    2. Mirror drilling: With a compliant partner, practice holding Knee on Belly for 10 seconds, focusing on maintaining pressure and base (5 reps per side).
    3. Light resistance drilling: Partner provides minimal resistance, practice transitioning and holding Knee on Belly for 15 seconds (5 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Controlled movement drilling: Partner actively tries to escape from Knee on Belly, you focus on maintaining the position for 30 seconds (5 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Positional sparring: Start from Knee on Belly and attempt to maintain it for 1 minute while partner attempts to escape or sweep (5 reps per side, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Integrate Knee on Belly as a transitional position during rolling, focusing on securing and maintaining it for 2 minutes (5 reps, 90% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • Immediately after successfully passing the opponent's guard and before settling into side control.
    • When the opponent attempts to bridge or shrimp out of side control, you can transition to Knee on Belly to re-establish control.
    • As a transitional control point to set up submissions like the armbar or baseball choke from side control.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): When the opponent's knee is on your belly, bridge your hips towards their knee and shrimp your body away to create space and potentially recover guard.
    • Knee Shield: If they have Knee on Belly, bring your opposite knee up between your chest and their knee to create a barrier and prevent them from lowering their weight fully.
    • Transition to Guard Recovery: As they apply Knee on Belly, use their pressure against them by turning into them and attempting to re-establish a closed or open guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I do Knee on Belly in BJJ?

    Your knee pain during Knee on Belly likely stems from improper weight distribution and pressure. If you are placing the hard bone of your knee directly onto your opponent's abdomen without proper padding or a controlled landing, it can cause discomfort. Ensure your shin and the top of your foot are also making contact, distributing the pressure across a wider surface area, and keep your hips low to avoid hyperextending your knee.

    Q: How do I maintain Knee on Belly against a bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on leveraging your body mechanics for maximum pressure and control. Drive your hips low and forward, almost as if you are trying to sit on their hip. Use your chest and upper body to apply downward force, and keep your supporting foot firmly planted behind you to create a strong base. Your grips on their collar or shoulders become crucial for preventing them from posturing up and creating space.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from Knee on Belly to another position in BJJ?

    The optimal time to transition from Knee on Belly is when you feel your opponent's defense weakening or when you've successfully created an opening. For instance, if they react by turning away, you might transition to a S-mount. If they try to push your knee off with their hands, you can use that momentum to set up an armbar. Always look for their reaction and exploit the immediate space or imbalance they create.

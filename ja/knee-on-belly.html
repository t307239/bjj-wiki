Knee on Belly: The Essential BJJ Position for White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

Knee on Belly is a dominant control position achieved after passing the guard, aiming to pin the opponent's torso to the mat. White belts often struggle because they treat it like a static hold, forgetting the dynamic pressure required.

The key is understanding that it's not just about placing your knee; it's about actively driving your hips forward and using your shin to create a fulcrum for pressure.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a passed guard (e.g., side control transition), position your left knee on the opponent's sternum, perpendicular to their body. Your right foot remains flat on the mat, providing a stable base.
    2. Angle your hips so they are 45 degrees to the opponent's torso. This allows for maximum weight transfer onto their chest.
    3. Place your right hand on the opponent's hip on the same side as your knee (e.g., right hand on their right hip). This prevents them from shrimping away.
    4. Place your left hand on the opponent's far shoulder or collar (e.g., left hand on their left collar). This controls their upper body and prevents them from bridging.
    5. Drive your hips forward, transferring your weight onto the opponent's sternum via your left knee. Your body should form an 'L' shape.
    6. Keep your base leg (right foot) slightly wider than shoulder-width for stability. Your weight should be distributed 70% on the knee and 30% on the foot.
    7. Actively pull down on the far collar/shoulder with your left hand while simultaneously pushing into their hip with your right hand. This creates a strong pinning mechanism.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Placing the knee directly on the opponent's thigh or hip: This can cause hyperextension or twisting injuries to the opponent's knee/hip joint. The knee should always target the torso.
    • Arching your back excessively to create pressure: This strains your lumbar spine and makes you vulnerable to sweeps. Maintain a neutral spine and drive with your hips.
    • Letting your hips fall back, creating a gap: This allows the opponent to recover guard or escape. Keep your hips actively driving forward, maintaining constant pressure.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip angle, knee placement, and weight transfer without a partner. Focus on feeling the correct body positioning. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    2. Static partner drilling: With a compliant partner lying on their back, practice transitioning into Knee on Belly and holding the position. Partner offers no resistance. (10 reps per side, 10% resistance)
    3. Controlled transition drilling: Practice moving from side control into Knee on Belly, holding for 5 seconds, and then returning to side control. Partner offers slight resistance to hip escape. (10 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled pressure drilling: From side control, enter Knee on Belly. Partner attempts to resist pressure with body weight but not actively try to escape. Focus on maintaining hip drive. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Limited escape drilling: From Knee on Belly, partner attempts basic escapes (e.g., hip escape, bridging). You practice maintaining the position and resetting. (5 minutes, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Apply Knee on Belly during rolling, focusing on maintaining control and transitioning to other dominant positions. (Full rolling session, 90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • After successfully passing the opponent's guard and being in a transitional side control position.
    • When the opponent attempts to bridge or shrimp, creating an opening to transition to a more dominant control.
    • As a setup for other control positions or submissions, such as mount or armbar.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): As the knee lands, the opponent immediately hips away, creating space to bring their legs back in and recover guard. The attacker must maintain forward hip pressure to prevent this.
    • Bridging: The opponent uses their legs to bridge upwards, lifting the attacker's weight off them. The attacker counters by driving their hips down and forward, keeping their base wide.
    • Elbow Escape: The opponent tucks their elbow under their body and uses it as a fulcrum to turn into the attacker. The attacker must keep their chest tight and maintain collar/shoulder control to prevent the turn.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I do Knee on Belly?

    Your knee might hurt if you are placing the knee directly on the opponent's kneecap or shin, which is incorrect. The knee should land on the sternum or upper abdomen. Ensure your shin is perpendicular to their torso and your hips are angled, distributing weight across your shin and foot, not solely on the kneecap.

    Q: How can I maintain Knee on Belly against a bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on hip pressure and leverage rather than brute strength. Drive your hips forward aggressively to create a strong fulcrum with your knee on their sternum. Use your far-side hand to control their collar or shoulder, preventing them from posturing up, and your near-side hand to control their hip to stop escapes.

    Q: When is the best time to switch my Knee on Belly leg?

    Switching legs is typically done from a side control position to transition to the other side. If you are on their right side, you might transition to Knee on Belly on their left. This is usually initiated by moving your hips and bringing your opposite knee to the mat while your current knee lifts, always maintaining pressure and control throughout the transition.

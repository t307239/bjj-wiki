Knee on Belly: The Essential BJJ Position for White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

Knee on Belly is a dominant top control position, achieved after passing your opponent's guard, aiming to stifle their movement and set up submissions.

White belts often struggle by placing too much weight on the knee or failing to establish a strong base, leading to instability and missed opportunities.

The core mechanic is creating a stable fulcrum with your knee and hip, allowing you to pivot and maintain pressure without sacrificing balance.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a passed guard position (e.g., side control), drive your weight forward over your opponent's chest and shoulders.
    2. Place your dominant knee (e.g., right knee) firmly on the mat, just below their hip bone, perpendicular to their spine.
    3. Your hips should be low and slightly forward, creating a stable base directly over your supporting knee.
    4. Your non-dominant foot (e.g., left foot) should be flat on the mat, providing a secondary point of contact and balance.
    5. The shin of your dominant leg (right shin) should be angled across your opponent's torso, roughly from their hip to their sternum.
    6. Your chest should be pressed into their upper chest, creating upper body pressure and limiting their ability to bridge.
    7. Your hands can either frame against their hips to maintain distance or grip their collar and sleeve for control and submission setup.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Placing the knee directly on the opponent's hip bone: This can cause hyperextension of the opponent's hip joint, potentially leading to a hip flexor tear or labral injury.
    • Pressing weight solely through the knee: This puts excessive shear force on the knee joint, risking medial collateral ligament (MCL) sprains or meniscal tears.
    • Allowing the hips to rise too high: This compromises your base, making you susceptible to sweeps and increasing the risk of lumbar strain from overextension.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Knee on Belly Hold: Practice transitioning to the position and holding it for 30 seconds, focusing on hip placement and base. (10 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner Assisted Transition: Your partner lies passively. Practice establishing Knee on Belly from a side control entry. (10 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Light Pressure Knee on Belly: Partner remains passive. You apply light pressure, shifting weight. (10 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled Transitions with Light Resistance: Practice moving to Knee on Belly from side control, partner offers minimal resistance to the transition. (10 reps, 50% resistance)
    5. Knee on Belly to Submission Setup: Practice establishing Knee on Belly and then transitioning to a basic armbar setup (e.g., armbar from mount or side control). (10 reps, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling - Focus on Positional Control: During rolling, prioritize achieving and maintaining Knee on Belly for at least 10 seconds before attempting sweeps or submissions. (5 rounds, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • Immediately after successfully passing an opponent's guard, before they can re-establish their guard.
    • When transitioning from side control to a more dominant top position.
    • As a strategic pause to control an opponent who is actively trying to escape side control.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape and Re-Guard: As the opponent establishes Knee on Belly, immediately shrimp your hips away to create space and attempt to re-establish your guard.
    • Knee Shield/Posting: Place your shin across the opponent's hip or chest to create a barrier and prevent them from lowering their weight, then shrimp out.
    • Bridge and Roll: If the opponent is off-balance, a strong bridge can disrupt their base, creating an opportunity to escape or reverse the position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I do Knee on Belly in BJJ?

    Your knee pain likely stems from placing excessive direct pressure on the kneecap or the knee joint itself. The biomechanically correct way is to use your shin as a pivot point and distribute your weight through your hip and the ball of your supporting foot. Ensure your shin is angled across the opponent's torso and your hips are low, not directly on top of your knee.

    Q: How can I maintain Knee on Belly against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on leverage and creating a strong base rather than brute force. Drive your hips low and forward, ensuring your supporting knee is firmly planted just below their hip bone. Use your chest to apply constant pressure to their upper body, and grip their collar and sleeve to control their posture and prevent them from using their size to simply push you off.

    Q: When is the best time to switch my Knee on Belly leg in BJJ?

    Switching legs (e.g., from right knee to left knee) is most effective when your opponent attempts to escape by turning into you or bridging. As they shift their weight, you can use their movement as an opportunity to pivot your hips and drive your other knee onto the mat, maintaining pressure and controlling their escape attempt. It's a dynamic adjustment, not a static repositioning.

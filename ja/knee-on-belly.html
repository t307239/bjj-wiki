Precise Knee on Belly Technique for BJJ White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック

Knee on Belly is a dominant control position achieved from side control, aiming to inhibit opponent mobility and set up submissions or further positional advancements.

White belts often struggle by placing too much weight on their knee or failing to establish a strong base, leading to instability and easy escapes.

The core principle is using your shin and foot as a fulcrum, distributing weight onto the opponent's torso via your hip, not just your knee.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting from side control, establish a cross-face grip with your left hand on the opponent's right collar and your right hand gripping their left sleeve near the wrist.
    2. Shift your weight slightly onto your right elbow, creating space to bring your right knee towards the opponent's chest.
    3. Drive your hips forward and down, angling your pelvis so your right hip is directly over the opponent's sternum.
    4. Place your right knee on the ground directly over the opponent's solar plexus, ensuring your shin is perpendicular to their torso.
    5. Extend your left leg back, placing your left foot flat on the mat for a stable base, with your knee pointing slightly outwards.
    6. Maintain your cross-face grip and adjust your right hand grip to control their far hip or thigh to prevent bridging.
    7. Keep your chest close to the opponent's chest, applying downward pressure through your hips and maintaining a wide base with your left leg.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Placing the knee directly on the kneecap: This can cause severe ligament damage (ACL/MCL) to the opponent and hyperextension injuries to your own knee if they roll into it.
    • Distributing weight solely on the knee: This puts excessive pressure on the patellofemoral joint, leading to cartilage damage and pain, and makes you unstable.
    • Allowing the opponent to bridge effectively by not controlling their hips: This can lead to your knee being trapped or you being swept, risking ankle sprains or knee ligament tears from awkward falls.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip and leg placement in the air, focusing on the weight transfer and base. (50 reps, 0% resistance).
    2. Partner drill (static): Partner lies on their back, you practice entering Knee on Belly and holding the position with minimal pressure. (20 reps each side, 10% resistance).
    3. Partner drill (light pressure): Partner actively resists with slight framing, you focus on maintaining the position and hip pressure. (15 reps each side, 25% resistance).
    4. Partner drill (controlled movement): Partner attempts to escape slowly, you practice transitioning and maintaining Knee on Belly while they move. (10 reps each side, 50% resistance).
    5. Flow rolling (positional focus): Practice transitioning into Knee on Belly from side control and maintaining it for 30 seconds before transitioning to another position or allowing an escape. (5 rounds, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling (game focus): Incorporate Knee on Belly as a goal position during rolling, focusing on smooth transitions and effective control. (3 rounds, 90% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When you have established a dominant side control position with good hip connection.
    • When the opponent is turtled or trying to defend your side control by turning away.
    • As a transition to other dominant positions like mount or to set up submissions.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): If the opponent's knee is on your chest, bridge your hips and shrimp away to create space and potentially recover guard.
    • Framing and Bridging: Use your arms to frame against the opponent's hips and shoulders, then bridge to disrupt their base and create an angle for escape.
    • Re-guarding: As the opponent enters Knee on Belly, use your legs to create a frame and attempt to hook their leg or bring your guard back into play.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I do Knee on Belly for BJJ?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are placing too much direct pressure on the kneecap itself, or your shin is angled incorrectly, causing shear forces. To fix this, ensure your shin is perpendicular to the opponent's torso and that the weight is distributed through your hip onto their sternum, not solely on your knee. Your foot should be flat on the mat for base.

    Q: How do I maintain Knee on Belly against a bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on hip pressure and weight distribution rather than brute force. Drive your hips forward and down, making your pelvis the primary point of contact on their torso. Use your shin and foot as a stable base, and maintain a tight cross-face grip to control their upper body, preventing them from using their size to generate power.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from Knee on Belly to Mount in BJJ?

    The optimal time to switch to mount is when the opponent has committed to defending your Knee on Belly by turning away or trying to bridge, creating an opening. As they react, use your momentum to swing your back leg over their body, bringing your hips closer to their head and establishing the mount position with both knees on the mat.

Knee on Belly: Your First Dominant BJJ Position

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

Knee on Belly is a powerful positional advantage gained from side control or mount, aiming to control and submit your opponent.

White belts often struggle by placing too much weight on the knee or failing to establish a strong base, leading to instability and discomfort.

The key is distributing weight onto the knee and the foot of the opposite leg, creating a tripod of pressure that limits the opponent's movement.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side control, with your chest over the opponent's upper chest, drive your right knee (if controlling opponent's left side) towards their hip bone.
    2. Place the ball of your left foot flat on the mat, directly behind your right knee, creating a wide base.
    3. Your right knee should be positioned on the opponent's abdomen, slightly to the side of their sternum, not directly on the navel.
    4. Angle your hips at approximately 45 degrees relative to the opponent's torso, leading with your hips towards their head.
    5. Your left shin should be perpendicular to the opponent's body, and your left foot planted firmly.
    6. Apply downward pressure through your right knee and your left foot, driving your hips forward.
    7. Maintain a strong chest-to-chest connection, keeping your back relatively straight to prevent arching and losing pressure.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Placing the knee directly on the opponent's stomach and driving down: This can cause sharp pain and potentially injure the opponent's spleen or intestines. The correct placement is on the upper abdomen/rib cage area, distributing pressure with the foot.
    • Letting your hips fall back and losing the base: This hyperextends the knee joint of the supporting leg, risking ACL or meniscus tears. Always maintain forward hip pressure and a wide base with the supporting foot.
    • Arching your back to maintain pressure: This strains the lumbar spine and makes you vulnerable to sweeps. Keep your core engaged and your back straight, using hip drive instead of back arching.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo: Practice the knee placement and hip angle from a side control position on a dummy or mat. (50 reps each side)
    2. Solo: With your knee on the mat, practice placing the supporting foot and establishing the 45-degree hip angle. (50 reps each side)
    3. Partner Drill (0% resistance): From side control, move into Knee on Belly, focusing on precise placement and base. Partner remains passive. (10 reps each side)
    4. Partner Drill (25% resistance): Partner gently resists by trying to bridge or shrimp. Focus on maintaining pressure and base. (10 reps each side)
    5. Partner Drill (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to create space and counter. Focus on hip pressure and foot placement. (10 reps each side)
    6. Live Rolling (75-90% resistance): Attempt Knee on Belly during rolling, focusing on maintaining the position and not rushing submissions. (5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When transitioning from side control, especially after a failed pass or a slight opening.
    • When your opponent attempts to shrimp or bridge from side control, and you can capitalize on the space.
    • As a setup to transition to mount or other dominant positions.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Shrimp (Hip Escape): If the opponent has a weak base, they can shrimp their hips away, creating space to recover guard. The key is to have a strong base and immediate follow-up pressure.
    • Bridge and Roll: A powerful counter, but requires good timing. The opponent can bridge their hips upwards and attempt to roll you over. This is countered by maintaining forward pressure and a tight base.
    • Knee Tap/Guard Recovery: If the opponent's supporting knee is too far forward, they can attempt to tap your knee and recover guard. This is countered by keeping the supporting knee slightly behind the attacking knee and maintaining a strong hip connection.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: My knee hurts when I do Knee on Belly, what am I doing wrong?

    Your knee is likely experiencing direct, unsupported pressure. Ensure the ball of your foot on the supporting leg is firmly planted, creating a tripod with your knee and hips. This distributes your weight across a wider base, taking direct stress off the knee joint itself.

    Q: How do I maintain Knee on Belly against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on hip pressure and angling. Drive your hips forward and towards their head, creating a strong connection. Use your supporting foot as a solid anchor to prevent them from using their weight to push you off.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from Side Control to Knee on Belly?

    The optimal time is when your opponent attempts to shrimp or bridge, creating a momentary gap. As they move, quickly drive your knee into their abdomen and establish your base, preventing them from fully recovering their guard.

The North-South position is achieved when you are perpendicular to your opponent's head, facing their feet, with your chest on their chest.

It's a dominant control position, often transitioning from guard passes or scrambles, offering offensive opportunities.

White belts often struggle by over-relying on brute strength, leading to loss of position and potential injury.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side control, shift your hips forward until your chest is directly over your opponent's sternum. Your head should be near their feet.
    2. Place your left hand on your opponent's right hip bone, digging your fingertips into their gi or skin. Your right arm secures a cross-collar grip (thumb inside, fingers outside).
    3. Drive your chest down, creating a heavy, crushing pressure. Your hips should be slightly lower than your opponent's, creating a "pinching" effect.
    4. Keep your knees wide and firmly planted on the mat, creating a stable base. This prevents your opponent from bridging or shrimping out.
    5. Your left forearm should be extended along their right side, preventing them from turning into you. Your right arm maintains collar control, pulling their head towards your chest.
    6. Ensure your weight is distributed primarily onto their chest and hips, not solely on your knees or feet.
    7. Maintain a "frame" with your body; your shoulders should be aligned with their shoulders, preventing them from creating space.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect weight distribution: Placing too much weight on your knees, leading to knee hyperextension or ligament tears (ACL/MCL).

    Correct: Distribute weight onto your chest and hips, keeping knees slightly bent and stable.

    • Allowing opponent to create space: Not maintaining tight control of the collar grip or hip, allowing them to shrimp away.

    Correct: Keep the cross-collar grip tight, pulling their head towards your chest, and use your forearm to block hip movement.

    • Overextending your neck: Tilting your head too far back to "look" for submissions, straining your cervical spine.

    Correct: Keep your head tucked in a neutral position, using your peripheral vision and body awareness.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the weight shift and hip placement from a neutral position. 50 reps.
    2. Partner assisted: With a compliant partner, practice establishing the North-South position from a side control start. 5 reps per side.
    3. Static hold: Maintain North-South position against a resisting partner who is actively trying to escape for 30 seconds. 3 reps.
    4. Controlled transition: Drill transitioning to North-South from a successful guard pass. 10 reps per side.
    5. Light resistance drill: Practice North-South control against a partner offering 25% resistance, focusing on maintaining pressure and framing.
    6. Live rolling: Attempt to achieve and maintain North-South position during live rolling, focusing on positional control over submission. 5 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • After successfully passing the guard and landing in side control, but the opponent turns their hips towards you.
    • During a scramble where you end up perpendicular to your opponent's head and chest.
    • When your opponent attempts a weak guard recovery from side control and leaves their upper body exposed.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): If the opponent is not controlling your hips, shrimp your hips away and create space to recover guard.
    • Bridge and Roll: If the opponent's weight distribution is poor, bridge your hips and attempt to roll them over, reversing the position.
    • Elbow Escape: If the opponent is crushing your chest, try to "swim" your arm underneath and create an elbow frame to create space and recover guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try to hold North-South?

    Your neck pain likely stems from trying to overcompensate with your head to maintain control. Instead of craning your neck, focus on keeping your head tucked neutrally against your opponent's chest or shoulder. Use your chest and shoulders to pin their head down, and your cross-collar grip to control their upper body. This allows you to maintain pressure without straining your neck.

    Q: How do I maintain North-South against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, your leverage and pressure are key. Focus on driving your chest into their sternum and your hips slightly lower, creating a "pinching" effect. Use your cross-collar grip to pull their head towards your chest, disrupting their base. Keep your knees wide and planted to prevent them from bridging or shrimping effectively. Don't try to match their strength; instead, focus on precise weight distribution and tight control.

    Q: The optimal time to transition from North-South is when your opponent is actively struggling to escape and has exposed a limb or their head. Common transitions include the North-South choke (if you have a strong cross-collar grip and can flatten them out), or an armbar if they defend the choke by posturing up and exposing an arm. Always ensure you have a solid positional base before attempting any submission.

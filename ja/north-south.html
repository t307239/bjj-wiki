North-South Position: The Foundation of Dominant Control in BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

North-South is a dominant positional control where you are perpendicular to your opponent, facing their head. It offers strong control and excellent submission opportunities.

Many white belts struggle by treating it like a wrestling pin, leading to loss of position and frustration.

The key is understanding how to use your hips and weight to create a crushing pressure, not just your arms.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side control, transition your hips towards your opponent's head. Your chest should be over their sternum.
    2. Place your left knee on the mat, angled slightly towards their left armpit. Your right knee stays high, near their head.
    3. Your right hand secures a "collar grip" on the far side of their gi, pulling their head towards your chest.
    4. Your left hand secures a "sleeve grip" on their near-side sleeve, keeping their arm controlled.
    5. Drive your chest into their chest, creating a "smash" pressure. Keep your hips low and your base wide.
    6. Rotate your hips so they are perpendicular to your opponent's body. Your weight should be distributed over their chest and shoulders.
    7. Maintain a tight frame with your arms. Your elbows should be tucked, preventing them from creating space.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Forcing a "headlock" by wrapping your arms around their head without securing a collar grip. This can hyperextend your neck or theirs, risking cervical spine injury.

    Correct: Secure a collar grip on their far collar and use your chest to control their head.

    • Arching your back excessively while trying to "pin" them. This can strain your lumbar spine and open up your hips for escape.

    Correct: Keep your core engaged and drive your hips forward and down, creating pressure.

    • Letting your hips get too high, creating a "bridge" opportunity for your opponent. This can lead to your opponent escaping or sweeping you.

    Correct: Keep your hips low and close to the mat, maintaining a solid base.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip and weight transfer from side control to North-South without a partner. Focus on hip angle and chest pressure. (50 reps)
    2. Positional drilling (0% resistance): Start in side control. Your partner remains completely passive. Execute the transition to North-South and hold for 10 seconds. (10 reps)
    3. Positional drilling (25% resistance): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to focus on grip fighting and maintaining pressure. (15 reps)
    4. Positional drilling (50% resistance): Your partner actively tries to create space and escape, forcing you to maintain your grips and weight distribution. (20 reps)
    5. Controlled sparring (75% resistance): Implement North-South transitions and control during light sparring. Focus on maintaining the position and preventing escapes. (3 rounds)
    6. Live rolling (90-100% resistance): Apply the North-South position and transitions during live rolling, focusing on smooth execution and pressure. (5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have established dominant side control and your opponent is trying to shrimp away.
    • When your opponent is turtled up, and you have controlled their hips from side control.
    • As a transitional position from other dominant controls like mount or knee-on-belly.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): If the opponent's hips are not fully controlled, they can shrimp their hips away to create space and escape the position.
    • Bridge and Roll: If the opponent loses their base and their hips are too high, a well-timed bridge can disrupt their control and allow for an escape.
    • Turning Inwards: By turning their hips and shoulders towards you, the opponent can create a frame and potentially escape to their knees or guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try to get to North-South from side control?

    This usually happens when you try to "pull" your opponent's head down with your arms instead of "smashing" their head with your chest while maintaining a collar grip. This "pulling" motion can hyperextend your cervical spine or theirs. Focus on using your sternum to pin their head and your collar grip to control their posture, keeping your neck in a neutral, safe alignment.

    Q: How can I maintain North-South position against a much larger and stronger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, your weight distribution and hip control are paramount. Instead of relying on brute strength, focus on lowering your hips as close to the mat as possible, creating a wide base. Drive your chest into their sternum and use your collar grip to prevent them from posturing up. Keep your elbows tight to your body to maintain a strong frame and prevent them from creating space.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from North-South to a submission?

    The optimal time to transition to a submission from North-South is when your opponent is actively trying to escape or is stuck in a compromised position. Look for opportunities when they are turning into you, exposing their arm for an arm-triangle choke, or when their head is trapped for a North-South choke. Patience is key; secure your control first before attempting a submission to avoid losing your dominant position.

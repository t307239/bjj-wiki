ノースサウスポジション：白帯のための生体力学ガイド

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

North-South is a dominant positional control where your head is near your opponent's hips, and your body runs perpendicular to theirs.

White belts often struggle by being too loose or not understanding weight distribution, leading to escapes or loss of position.

The key is to create a strong, stable base with your hips low and chest connected, preventing your opponent from generating space.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side control, transition by moving your hips towards your opponent's head.
    2. Place your lead knee on the mat, creating a pivot point, while your trail leg extends back.
    3. Your chest should press down onto your opponent's upper chest or shoulder area.
    4. Your lead arm (closest to their head) secures a cross-collar grip on their gi lapel, pulling their head towards you.
    5. Your trail arm (closest to their hips) secures a far-side pants grip at the ankle or shin, preventing them from turning in.
    6. Keep your hips heavy and low, creating a "pancake" effect on their upper body.
    7. Your base should be wide enough for stability, with your knees and feet providing support.
    8. Ensure your head is not directly over their head; aim for their shoulder or upper chest area for better control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Head Placement: Placing your head directly over your opponent's head invites them to bridge or shrimp, potentially straining your neck if you lack a solid base.
    • Risk: Cervical hyperextension or compression.
    • Corrective Action: Keep your head angled towards their shoulder, maintaining a solid chest-to-chest connection.
    • Loose Hip Control: Allowing your hips to rise or drift away from your opponent's torso creates space for them to shrimp and escape.
    • Risk: Knee or hip joint hyperextension during escape attempts.
    • Corrective Action: Drive your hips down and forward, connecting your chest to theirs.
    • Over-reliance on Arm Strength: Trying to force the position with arm strength alone, neglecting hip and base pressure, will lead to fatigue and poor control.
    • Risk: Shoulder impingement or bicep tears.
    • Corrective Action: Focus on the weight of your hips and the stability of your base, using grips for control, not force.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Control Drill: Practice transitioning from side control to North-South position in the air, focusing on hip movement and chest connection (50 reps).
    2. Solo Grip Placement Drill: From a stable North-South position (opponent static, 0% resistance), practice securing the cross-collar and far-side pants grip precisely (30 reps).
    3. Partner Static Drill: With a partner lying passively in side control, practice transitioning to North-South and establishing grips, holding for 10 seconds each (10 reps per side).
    4. Light Resistance Drill (25%): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to practice maintaining the position as they subtly try to bridge or shrimp.
    5. Moderate Resistance Drill (50%): Partner actively attempts to escape by bridging and shrimping; focus on maintaining hip pressure and grip integrity.
    6. Live Rolling Scenario (75%): Practice achieving North-South from side control during rolling, focusing on establishing and holding the position against an aware opponent.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have successfully passed to side control and your opponent is attempting to bridge or turn into you.
    • When your opponent is turtled up from your side control, and you can establish a North-South grip.
    • As a transition from a failed submission attempt where you end up in a perpendicular position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Shrimping: When the opponent feels pressure, they can shrimp their hips away, creating space to recover guard. The North-South player must maintain hip pressure and adjust their base.
    • Bridging: The opponent can bridge their hips upwards, attempting to create space and roll you over. The North-South player must keep their hips low and use their chest connection to prevent the upward momentum.
    • Turning In: The opponent can try to turn their body towards your head. The far-side pants grip is crucial here to prevent this rotation, while the cross-collar grip helps control their head.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try to hold North-South?

    Neck pain often stems from placing your head directly over your opponent's head. This position creates a direct line of force for their bridges and twists. Biomechanically, your cervical spine is not designed for this sustained axial loading. To fix this, angle your head towards their shoulder, establishing a strong chest-to-chest connection instead of a head-to-head one, and ensure your hips are heavy and low.

    Q: How do I maintain North-South against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise weight distribution are paramount. Focus on using your hips to "pancake" their upper body, making it difficult for them to breathe or move. Secure a deep cross-collar grip to control their head and prevent them from turning in. Your far-side pants grip at the ankle is vital to prevent them from shrimping effectively. Prioritize a wide, stable base over brute strength.

    Q: When should I transition from Side Control to North-South?

    You should consider transitioning to North-South when your opponent actively tries to escape side control by bridging or turning into you. If you have established a strong cross-face or chest-to-chest pressure in side control and they are reacting by creating space towards your head, it's an opportune moment. This position offers superior control over their upper body and limits their ability to generate power for escapes.

The D'Arce Choke is a submission primarily initiated from dominant positions like side control or from the guard when the opponent attempts to pass.

White belts often struggle with the D'Arce Choke due to a lack of understanding of rotational mechanics and relying on brute strength instead of leverage.

The key to a successful D'Arce Choke lies in creating a tight shoulder-to-shoulder grip and using your body to drive through the opponent's neck, constricting blood flow.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side control, establish a cross-face with your left arm (if attacking the opponent's right side).
    2. Secure a "figure-four" grip: your right arm wraps around the opponent's neck, and your left hand grabs your own right bicep, creating a "lock" around their head and shoulder.
    3. Turn your hips towards the opponent's head, aligning your left hip bone with their neck.
    4. Drive your chest into the opponent's shoulder, flattening your base and creating pressure.
    5. Shift your weight forward, allowing your right shoulder to press into the side of their neck.
    6. Use your hips and core to rotate your body counter-clockwise, driving your right shoulder and the "figure-four" grip deeper into the choke.
    7. Maintain head control with your left arm to prevent them from posturing up and relieving pressure.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to "pull hard" with your arms without proper hip and chest drive: This can strain your biceps and shoulders, leading to muscle tears or tendonitis, as you're fighting against their structure instead of using leverage. The correct action is to drive your shoulder into their neck and rotate your hips.
    • Overextending your neck to "reach" for the grip: This puts excessive strain on your cervical spine and can lead to neck pain or even disc injury. Instead, focus on closing the distance with your hips and chest before securing the grip.
    • Allowing the opponent to "stack" you by not maintaining hip connection: If your hips lose connection, they can drive their weight onto your shoulders, potentially causing shoulder impingement or AC joint sprains. Always keep your hips driving forward and your base tight.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill (no partner): Practice the "figure-four" grip and hip rotation motion 10 times, focusing on the feel of the lock.
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a willing partner, practice the grip and hip drive from side control for 5 repetitions each side.
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Apply the D'Arce Choke with minimal resistance, allowing your partner to tap easily. Focus on correct body positioning for 10 repetitions.
    4. Controlled drilling (25% resistance): Your partner offers light defense, allowing you to work the mechanics of the choke. Perform 5 successful chokes.
    5. Controlled drilling (50% resistance): Your partner actively defends, but you can still isolate the movement. Aim for 5 successful chokes.
    6. Live rolling (75% resistance): Attempt the D'Arce Choke in a rolling situation against a resisting opponent. Focus on setting it up and executing the core mechanics for 3 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When you have established side control and the opponent is trying to shrimp away or frame.
    • When the opponent attempts a guard pass and you can catch their head and arm.
    • From turtle position when you have an underhook and can secure the grip.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Elbow-to-knee escape: If the attacker's hips aren't tight, bring your elbow to your knee on the choked side and bridge to create space and relieve pressure.
    • "Stacking" the opponent: If the attacker is not driving their hips forward, you can attempt to drive your weight forward onto their shoulders, flattening them out and breaking the choke.
    • Turning into the choke: By turning your body towards the direction of the choke (towards their armpit), you can often relieve the pressure by creating a more neutral position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try the D'Arce Choke?

    Your neck is likely hurting because you are trying to create the choke by crunching your neck muscles or pulling your head down. The D'Arce Choke is a blood choke that works by compressing the carotid arteries. The pressure should come from your shoulder and the "figure-four" grip driving into their neck, combined with hip rotation, not from your cervical spine. Ensure your shoulder is the primary point of contact on their neck and your hips are driving forward.

    Q: How do I D'Arce Choke a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise mechanics are even more critical than brute strength. Focus on getting your hips significantly lower than theirs when initiating the choke, creating a strong "figure-four" grip, and driving your shoulder into their neck with your body weight. The rotation of your hips and torso is key to generating the necessary force, rather than trying to muscle it. Ensure you are cutting off their head and shoulder with your arms.

    Q: When should I abandon a D'Arce Choke attempt?

    You should abandon a D'Arce Choke attempt if the opponent successfully defends by posturing up significantly, creating a large gap between your chest and their neck, or if they manage to turn their head away and relieve the pressure from your "figure-four" grip. Continuing to force a poorly secured choke can lead to injury for you or your partner, and it's better to transition to a different submission or positional control. Watch for them to create space by posturing up.

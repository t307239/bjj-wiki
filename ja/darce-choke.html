D'Arce Choke: A Biomechanically Precise BJJ Guide for White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

The D'Arce Choke is a powerful submission typically initiated from the side-control or head-and-arm control positions. It aims to constrict blood flow to the brain via pressure on the carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle because they rely on raw strength, leading to poor positioning and ineffective choke application. Understanding the mechanics is crucial for success.

The key insight is to use your body's leverage and weight distribution to create a crushing vise, not just to pull your arms.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side-control, secure a "frame grip" under the opponent's head with your left arm (if choking on their left side). Your right arm reaches across the opponent's neck, creating a "figure-four grip" by grabbing your own right bicep with your left hand.
    2. Turn your hips towards the opponent's head, angling your pelvis at approximately 45 degrees relative to their body. This hip rotation initiates the choke's power.
    3. Drive your chest into the opponent's chest, maintaining tight contact. This prevents them from creating space.
    4. Tuck your chin to your chest, creating a strong base and protecting your own neck.
    5. Begin to drive your hips forward and slightly upwards, sinking your chest deeper into their neck. This forward hip drive is the primary force generator.
    6. Your right forearm presses into the carotid artery on the opponent's neck, while your left arm's bicep and shoulder apply counter-pressure on the opposite side.
    7. Maintain a strong, connected posture throughout the choke application, keeping your back straight and core engaged.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly "pulling" with arms: This often leads to straining the rotator cuff and shoulder ligaments (e.g., supraspinatus tendon), as the force is applied through weak, isolated joint movements. The correct action is to drive with your hips and core.
    • Over-extending the neck: Attempting to force the choke by hyperextending your own neck can strain cervical ligaments and muscles. Maintain a neutral, tucked chin. The correct action is to use spinal extension from the hips.
    • Allowing opponent to create space: If you don't maintain chest-to-chest contact, the opponent can shrimp or bridge, potentially causing your own shoulder to hyperextend or leading to a loss of position. Maintain tight connection and frame with your hips.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo grip and hip-turn drill: Practice the figure-four grip and hip rotation against air. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Shadow drilling with dummy: Perform the entire D'Arce Choke sequence on a training dummy, focusing on hip drive and body connection. (30 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Positional drilling with light resistance: From side-control, have a partner hold a static position. Execute the D'Arce Choke with minimal resistance. (20 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled execution: Practice the D'Arce Choke with a willing partner, focusing on control and stopping immediately upon tap. (15 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Live rolling with specific focus: In rolling, actively look for opportunities to initiate the D'Arce Choke, but do not force it if the position is not ideal. Focus on correct setup and execution. (5 rounds, 75% resistance)
    6. Full rolling: Apply the D'Arce Choke as a natural part of your game during live rolling, focusing on seamless integration and finishing power. (10 rounds, 100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is turtled and you have head-and-arm control.
    • When you have established side-control and your opponent is trying to wall-walk away.
    • When transitioning from a failed guard pass and you can secure the head-and-arm grip.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • "The Escape": If the choke is being applied, the opponent should immediately try to turn their head towards the choking arm, tucking their chin. This reduces the pressure on the carotid. Simultaneously, they should try to drive their hips towards the choking arm to relieve pressure and potentially create space to escape.
    • "The Grip Break": The opponent can attempt to break the figure-four grip by prying the choking arm's elbow away with their free hand, or by actively pushing the choking arm's forearm off their neck.
    • "The Roll": A skilled opponent may attempt to roll through the choke, often towards the choking arm, to relieve pressure and reset the position. This requires precise timing and body awareness.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try to do the D'Arce Choke?

    Neck pain usually indicates you are trying to force the choke with your neck muscles or by craning your head. The D'Arce Choke is a blood choke, not a neck crank. Ensure your chin is tucked to your chest and you are driving your hips forward and sinking your chest into their neck. The pressure should be on their carotid arteries, not your own cervical spine.

    Q: How can I D'Arce Choke a much bigger opponent as a smaller white belt?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision become paramount. Focus on securing a tight figure-four grip and driving your hips into them to create a crushing pressure. Your body weight should be used to sink your chest deep into their neck, compressing the carotid artery. Avoid trying to muscle them; instead, use their size against them by creating a tight, inescapable vise.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to a D'Arce Choke from side-control?

    The optimal time to transition to a D'Arce Choke from side-control is when your opponent is trying to escape by turning away from you, or when they turtle up. This movement naturally presents the head-and-arm position needed for the D'Arce. If they are actively resisting and creating space, it's generally better to maintain control and look for a more stable submission or transition.

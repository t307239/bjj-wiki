The D'Arce Choke is a powerful submission applied from various positions, primarily to restrict blood flow to the brain via the carotid arteries. It's often initiated when an opponent attempts to pass your guard or when you secure a front headlock position.

White belts often struggle with the D'Arce Choke due to a lack of understanding of fundamental body mechanics, leading to inefficient pressure and potential injury. They tend to rely on brute strength rather than precise leverage.

The single most critical mechanical insight is creating a "bow and arrow" like tension by driving your hips forward while simultaneously pulling your opponent's head towards your chest, effectively cinching the choke.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a front headlock position (e.g., opponent trying to pass guard), secure a "figure-four" grip: your right arm goes over their left shoulder, and your left arm wraps around their head and under your right bicep, locking your hands together with your left palm facing your face.
    2. Immediately drive your left hip into your opponent's right hip, creating a strong perpendicular base.
    3. Shift your weight forward, driving your chest down onto your opponent's upper back.
    4. With your head tight against your opponent's head, begin to retract your left elbow backwards while simultaneously extending your hips forward.
    5. The pressure comes from the combined action of your hips driving forward and your arm pulling the head into the crook of your elbow and shoulder.
    6. Ensure your left shoulder is pressed firmly into the side of your opponent's neck, blocking the carotid artery.
    7. Continue to drive your hips forward and squeeze your arms together, maintaining constant tension until submission.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Elbow Position: Attempting to "pull hard" with the elbow flared outwards instead of tucked can strain the shoulder joint (rotator cuff) and elbow ligaments. The correct movement is to pull the elbow backward in a controlled arc.
    • Hyperextending the Neck: Forcing the head into the choke with excessive neck twisting can injure cervical vertebrae and spinal ligaments. The choke is applied by controlling the head's position with the arms and hips, not by twisting.
    • Over-Reaching for the Grip: Trying to grab the bicep too far away from the head creates a weak lock and puts excessive strain on the shoulder and elbow. The grip should be tight and close to the opponent's head.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Grip Practice: Practice the figure-four grip and hand lock 50 times on a dummy or pillow, focusing on tightness.
    2. Solo Hip Drive: From a seated position, practice driving your hips forward while simulating the arm position 50 times.
    3. Partner Resistance Drill (0%): With a compliant partner, practice the entire sequence with no resistance. Focus on hip angle and weight transfer. 10 reps each side.
    4. Partner Resistance Drill (25%): Partner offers minimal resistance to the initial setup. Focus on maintaining your base and grip. 10 reps each side.
    5. Partner Resistance Drill (50%): Partner actively tries to defend the setup. Focus on maintaining control and driving hips. 10 reps each side.
    6. Live Rolling (75%): Apply the D'Arce Choke during live rolling, aiming for the setup against a resisting opponent. Focus on execution, not necessarily submission. 5 minutes.
    7. Live Rolling (90%): Actively look for D'Arce Choke opportunities in live rolling, aiming for submission. 5 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When an opponent attempts a guard pass and their head is exposed to a front headlock.
    • When you secure a strong front headlock position after a scramble or takedown defense.
    • When an opponent turtles up and you can secure a head-and-arm control.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Defending the Grip: If the figure-four grip is not fully secured, immediately "frame" with your forearm between their chest and your neck, and "bridge" your hips away to create space and escape the headlock.
    • The "Stand Up" Defense: If the choke is being applied, the opponent can attempt to stand up by driving their feet forward and raising their hips, relieving the pressure. This requires significant hip mobility and forward momentum.
    • "Stacking" the Opponent: A strong opponent can "stack" by driving their weight forward and down onto your shoulders. This can break the choke's integrity. You must maintain your hip pressure to counter this.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try to D'Arce Choke?

    Neck pain often stems from trying to force the submission with neck twisting rather than proper biomechanical leverage. The D'Arce Choke relies on compressing the carotid arteries by trapping the head with your arm and driving your hips forward. Ensure your head is tight against their head and focus on the hip drive and arm retraction, not neck rotation.

    Q: How do I D'Arce Choke a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paramount. Focus on a tight figure-four grip, ensuring your arm is deep under their chin and your hand is locked securely. Drive your hips forward with maximum force to create the necessary pressure. Do not try to muscle it; use your entire body's weight and momentum.

    Q: When is the best time to go for a D'Arce Choke in BJJ?

    The D'Arce Choke is most effective when the opponent is committed to a forward motion, such as during a guard pass attempt or when they are in a turtle position. Securing a strong front headlock without allowing them to escape or "stack" you is crucial for a successful D'Arce setup.

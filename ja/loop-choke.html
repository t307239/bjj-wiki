Loop Choke: A White Belt's Biomechanically Sound Introduction

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Loop Choke is a powerful submission typically initiated from closed guard, aiming to cut off blood flow to the brain.

White belts often struggle by over-relying on brute strength rather than precise leverage and body mechanics.

The key is to create a tight, angled choke using your forearm and lapel, cinching the carotid artery with minimal effort.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish a "Cross-Collar Grip" with your right hand on the opponent's left lapel, thumb inside the weave.
    2. Simultaneously, secure a "Pinch Grip" with your left hand on the opponent's right lapel, thumb outside the weave, about 2-3 inches below the right collar bone.
    3. Adjust your hips to the opponent's left, creating a 45-degree angle between your torsos.
    4. Extend your right arm forward, pushing the opponent's left shoulder slightly away to create space for the lapel.
    5. "Loop" your right forearm behind the opponent's neck, ensuring the lapel is snug against their carotid artery.
    6. Bring your left hand across your body, connecting to your right wrist or forearm, forming a "Grip" that stabilizes the choke.
    7. Shift your weight onto your hips, leaning back slightly, while simultaneously pulling your elbows towards your navel to tighten the choke.
    8. Maintain hip pressure and keep your elbows tight to your body to prevent the opponent from escaping.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly reaching for the lapel with a straight arm can hyperextend the shoulder joint, leading to rotator cuff tears. Always maintain a slight bend in the elbow and use hip movement to create space.
    • Grabbing the opponent's gi too high on the neck can restrict the lapel's path to the carotid artery and strain the wrist. Aim for the collar bone area for optimal lapel placement.
    • Trying to "pull" the choke with arm strength alone will tire you out and be ineffective. Focus on hip scooping and body angling to drive the choke. Use your hips to create the angle, not just your arms to pull.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the grip sequences and body angles without a partner. (50 reps each side)
    2. Static partner drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice entering the position and establishing the grips and choke mechanics. Focus on correct body positioning. (10 reps)
    3. Light resistance drill (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to grip acquisition and body positioning. Focus on smooth transitions. (15 reps)
    4. Controlled entry drill (50% resistance): Partner attempts to defend slightly, allowing you to practice fighting for grips and maintaining the angle. (20 reps)
    5. Simulated choke drill (75% resistance): Partner acknowledges the choke position but does not actively resist the squeeze, allowing you to feel the pressure. (25 reps)
    6. Live rolling (90-100% resistance): Attempt the Loop Choke during sparring, prioritizing correct mechanics over completion. (5 attempts per round)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • From closed guard when the opponent is posturing up, creating space to insert your arm.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard and their head is within striking distance.
    • If the opponent is leaning heavily forward, making it difficult for them to defend.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • "Head and Arm Escape" (Defense): When the choke is being applied, tuck your chin to your chest and attempt to turn your head towards the choking arm. Use your free arm to push against the opponent's hip, creating space to step your leg over and "Disengage".
    • "Postural Defense" (Defense): Immediately break the opponent's grips on your collar and lapel. "Posture Up" by straightening your back and driving your hips forward, creating space to relieve pressure.
    • "Trap and Roll" (Defense): If the opponent's arm is trapped, use your free arm to grip their elbow. "Hip Bump" to create an angle and "Roll" them to the opposite side, escaping the choke.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Loop Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you are using excessive forearm supination or pronation to create the choking force. The correct biomechanics involve using your entire body, particularly your hips and torso rotation, to drive the lapel into the carotid artery. Ensure your grip is secure but not overly tight, and focus on pulling your elbows towards your navel while maintaining a neutral wrist position.

    Q: How can I successfully apply the Loop Choke against a much larger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on creating a significant hip angle, moving to their side by unhooking one of your legs and "Shucking" your hips out. Use your legs to "Squeeze" their posture down, making it harder for them to resist. The lapel needs to be placed precisely; don't be afraid to use your free hand to "Snug" it against their neck before initiating the choke.

    Q: I can't seem to get the lapel to choke effectively; it just feels loose. What am I doing wrong with the Loop Choke?

    The most common issue is not creating enough space or angle to properly "Loop" the lapel. Ensure your right arm is extending forward to push their shoulder slightly, creating the necessary gap. Furthermore, the grip on the choking lapel should be "Thumb Inside" the weave, and you must actively "Drive" your forearm into their neck while simultaneously "Scooping" your hips to tighten. The lapel must be snug against the carotid artery from the start.

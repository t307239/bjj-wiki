The Loop Choke is typically initiated from the closed guard, aiming to submit the opponent via strangulation. It's a powerful technique when executed correctly.

White belts often struggle with the Loop Choke due to a lack of understanding of leverage and pressure. They tend to rely on brute strength, which is inefficient and risky.

The core mechanical insight is using your opponent's own posture and your hip movement to create a tight, blood-choking grip that bypasses their defenses.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish a cross-collar grip with your right hand, thumb inside the lapel, reaching deep. Your left hand grips the opponent's left sleeve near the wrist.
    2. Immediately drive your hips to your left, creating a 45-degree angle relative to your opponent's torso. Your left knee should slightly lift off the mat.
    3. While maintaining the collar grip, swing your right arm *under* your opponent's chin and across their neck, aiming to secure a grip on their own collar on the right side.
    4. Once your right hand secures the opponent's right lapel (thumb inside), your right arm forms a loop around their neck.
    5. Simultaneously, pull your opponent's left sleeve (held by your left hand) towards your left hip, creating tension and breaking their posture forward.
    6. Drive your right elbow down and slightly back, while simultaneously arching your back and lifting your hips. This compresses the carotid artery.
    7. Maintain the sleeve pull with your left hand to prevent them from posturing up, and ensure your right forearm is snug against their neck.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to force the collar grip by over-extending your right arm and rotating your shoulder socket excessively can lead to a rotator cuff tear or labral tear.
    • Correct movement: Focus on hip movement and pulling the sleeve to bring the opponent's neck into position, rather than forcing the arm over.
    • Gripping too low on the lapel with your choking hand, or not getting a deep thumb-in grip, can result in a weak choke and strain on your wrist and elbow joint.
    • Correct movement: Aim for a deep grip, thumb inside the lapel, as close to the opponent's sternum as possible. Ensure the forearm is perpendicular to the neck.
    • Tensing your neck excessively to resist the choke can lead to muscle strain or minor cervical injuries. Your focus should be on executing the mechanics, not resisting the resistance.
    • Correct movement: Relax your neck and focus on the hip and arm mechanics to apply the choke effectively.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip movement and arm positioning without a partner. Focus on the sequence and feeling the leverage. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner in your closed guard, execute the technique slowly, focusing on grip and body positioning. (10 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to posture or sleeve control. Focus on driving hips and completing the choke. (10 reps per side)
    4. Controlled drilling: Partner actively tries to defend by posturing up or removing the collar grip. Work on maintaining control and re-establishing grips. (10 reps per side)
    5. "Flow roll" with specific focus: Attempt the Loop Choke when the opportunity arises during a flow roll, but don't force it. If it fails, transition smoothly. (5 minutes, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the Loop Choke during live rolling, understanding that it may not be successful against a resisting opponent. Focus on learning from attempts and failures. (10 minutes, 90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up in your closed guard, creating space for your arm to pass.
    • When your opponent tries to stack you from closed guard, often exposing their neck.
    • When you have established a strong cross-collar grip and their sleeve is within reach for control.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Defense by Posture Control: If the opponent initiates the choke, they must pull your sleeve and drive their hips. Immediately straighten your legs and drive your hips back to create distance, preventing the hip angle.
    • Defense by Collar Grip Break: As the choking arm attempts to secure the second collar grip, use your free hand to grab your own collar or the opponent's choking arm at the elbow and pull it away, breaking the loop.
    • Defense by Escaping the Guard: If the opponent has a strong grip and is driving their hips, focus on breaking the sleeve control and then immediately opening your guard to create space and potentially pass or reset.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Loop Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often stems from an incorrect sleeve grip or excessive force applied through the wrist. Ensure your left hand grip on the sleeve is firm but not rigid, and focus on pulling the sleeve towards your hip. The primary power should come from your hips and back arch, not a wrist-heavy pull. Realign your wrist to be in a neutral or slightly extended position when pulling.

    Q: How can I use the Loop Choke against a much larger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paramount. Focus on the hip angle: drive your hips hard to your left, creating a significant separation. Use your opponent's weight against them by pulling their sleeve sharply towards your hip as you swing your choking arm. This break in their posture is what allows the choke to be effective, not raw strength.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to a Loop Choke, and what if my opponent defends by posturing up?

    The best time is when your opponent is slightly hunched forward or trying to stack you, as this creates the necessary space for your arm. If they posture up, don't force the choke. Instead, use the sleeve pull to maintain control, and consider transitioning to a sweep or another submission that uses their upright posture, such as a triangle choke setup.

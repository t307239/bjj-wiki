Shrimp Escape: The Essential BJJ Escape for White Belts

WHITE★☆☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Shrimp Escape is initiated when an opponent has established control, typically from side control or mount, and you need to create space to regain guard or escape. White belts often fail by trying to push away with their arms or by lifting their hips too high, losing their base.

The key mechanical insight is using your feet on the mat to drive your hips away, pivoting on your shoulder, creating a 'shrimping' motion for space. This leverages your entire body's weight and structure efficiently.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting Position: Opponent is in side control, right arm over your chest, left arm under your head.
    2. Hip Angle: Your hips are flat on the mat, body aligned with the opponent's hips.
    3. Left Foot Placement: Place your left foot flat on the mat, knee bent to approximately 90 degrees, with the sole of the foot in contact.
    4. Right Elbow Frame: Place your right elbow on the mat, creating a frame between your torso and the opponent's chest. Your forearm should be angled slightly upwards.
    5. Hip Drive Initiation: Drive your left foot into the mat, pushing your hips laterally to your left. Simultaneously, slightly lift your head and torso off the mat.
    6. Shoulder Pivot: As your hips move left, pivot on your right shoulder, allowing your body to rotate. Your head should follow your hips.
    7. Space Creation: Continue the hip drive and shoulder pivot until you create approximately 6-12 inches of space between your hips and the opponent's chest.
    8. Guard Recovery/Escape: Immediately look to bring your knees into your chest to establish guard or to turn towards your knees to stand up.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hip Lift: Lifting hips excessively high off the mat without driving with the foot. This strains the lower back ligaments (lumbar spine) and makes you unstable, allowing the opponent to maintain pressure or transition. The correct movement is a lateral hip drive, keeping hips low.
    • Arm Pushing: Attempting to push the opponent's chest or head away with outstretched arms. This can hyperextend the elbow joint (ulnar collateral ligament) or strain the shoulder joint (rotator cuff). The correct movement uses the foot as the primary drive.
    • Neck Extension: Arching the neck backward while trying to create space. This puts excessive strain on the cervical spine (neck vertebrae) and can lead to whiplash-like injuries. The head should move with the torso's rotation, not independently extended.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Drive (0%): Lie on your back, practice the hip drive and shoulder pivot motion without any opponent. Focus on the feeling of pushing with the foot and rotating. (20 reps)
    2. Solo Frame and Drive (0%): Practice placing your elbow frame and then executing the hip drive and pivot. (20 reps)
    3. Partner Assisted (25%): Have a partner lie on top of you in side control, but with minimal weight. You practice the full Shrimp Escape motion. Partner offers light resistance. (10 reps each side)
    4. Controlled Partner Drill (50%): Partner applies light pressure in side control. You execute the Shrimp Escape. Partner does not actively resist the escape, but holds position. (10 reps each side)
    5. Semi-Resisted Drill (75%): Partner applies moderate pressure. You execute the Shrimp Escape. Partner will try to maintain position but not actively counter the escape. (5 reps each side)
    6. Live Rolling Scenario (90-100%): Incorporate the Shrimp Escape into sparring when in a bad position. Partner actively tries to prevent the escape. (Focus on successful escapes, not count)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When in side control and the opponent's weight is mostly on your chest or hips, creating a predictable pressure.
    • When the opponent attempts to transition from side control to mount or knee-on-belly, creating a momentary lapse in their base.
    • When trapped under mount and the opponent is posturing up, creating a small gap at their hips.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Opponent Maintains Strong Base: If the opponent is skilled and maintains a tight base, they can often prevent the hip drive by posting their weight heavily. The counter is to recognize this and transition to a different escape or recovery technique, like bridging or turning to your knees.
    • Opponent 'Regrips' Immediately: A common counter is the opponent immediately regripping or re-establishing control as you shrimp. The biomechanical counter is to be explosive and have a follow-up plan (e.g., immediately shooting your knees in to establish guard).
    • Opponent 'Steps Over' Hip: If the opponent steps their leg over your hips as you shrimp, they are attempting to secure mount or a more dominant position. The counter is to use your free arm and leg to block their stepping leg and immediately try to invert or turn into them.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to Shrimp Escape?

    Your shoulder pain likely stems from pushing with your arm instead of driving with your foot. When you push with your arm, you are directly transferring force through the shoulder joint, which is not designed for that type of leverage. Ensure your elbow is on the mat as a stable frame and focus on driving your hips away using your planted foot.

    Q: How do I Shrimp Escape against a much bigger and heavier opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, the Shrimp Escape requires more precise timing and biomechanical efficiency. Instead of trying to power through, focus on finding the moments when their weight shifts or their base is slightly compromised. Use your foot to drive your hips away at a sharp angle, and be prepared to immediately follow up with guard recovery or a turn to your knees as soon as even minimal space is created.

    Q: When is the best time to Shrimp Escape, and what if my opponent is too tight?

    The optimal time to Shrimp Escape is when you feel a slight shift in the opponent's weight or a momentary opening, often during their transition attempts. If your opponent is 'too tight' and you cannot create space by shrimping, it indicates their base is solid. In such cases, don't force the shrimp; instead, consider alternative escapes like bridging to create a quick pop or turning to your knees if they are not controlling your hips tightly.

Shrimp Escape: The Essential BJJ Technique for White Belts

WHITE★☆☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Shrimp Escape is a fundamental BJJ movement used to create space and escape from dominant positions like side control or mount.

White belts often struggle by pushing straight back or lifting their hips too high, negating the escape's effectiveness.

The core principle is hip dissociation: moving your hips laterally while keeping your base wide and low.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Assume a supine position with knees bent, feet flat on the mat, and hips close to the opponent. The opponent is in a dominant position (e.g., side control).
    2. Place your left forearm on the opponent's hip bone, creating a frame with your elbow and hand. Your right hand cups the back of their head or neck.
    3. Tuck your right knee towards your chest, bringing your foot to the mat on your right side, slightly behind your left hip.
    4. Simultaneously, extend your left leg straight, pushing your left hip away from the opponent's base.
    5. Shift your weight onto your left foot and forearm frame, creating a pivot point.
    6. Drive your hips laterally to your left, moving them away from the opponent's center of gravity.
    7. Maintain your forearm frame on the hip and hand on the neck to prevent them from re-establishing pressure.
    8. Continue to move your hips until you create enough space to bring your knees to your chest and establish a closed guard or transition to a safer position.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Pushing straight back with legs: This strains the lumbar spine and can lead to disc herniation by hyperextending the lower back.

    Instead, focus on lateral hip movement and creating space by driving hips away.

    • Lifting hips too high off the mat: This exposes the lower back to potential slams or hyperextension injuries, and makes the movement inefficient.

    Keep hips low and drive them sideways, using the forearm and foot as anchors.

    • Grabbing opponent's gi too tightly without framing: This can lead to wrist or elbow hyperextension if the opponent applies pressure or resists.

    Use your forearms to create a solid frame against the opponent's body, not just to pull their gi.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip movement without a partner. Focus on tucking one knee, extending the other leg, and laterally shifting the hips. (50 reps)
    2. Partnered static drill: With a partner in side control, practice the shrimp movement. Partner remains passive, focusing on your hip movement and framing. (25 reps per side)
    3. Partnered light resistance drill: Partner offers minimal resistance (e.g., light pressure on hips). Focus on driving through the resistance. (25 reps per side)
    4. Simulated escape drill: Partner maintains a light side control. You attempt the shrimp escape. Partner allows the escape if executed correctly. (5 rounds, 30 seconds each)
    5. Guard retention drill: Start from closed guard. Partner attempts to pass. You shrimp to create space and re-establish guard. (5 rounds, 1 minute each)
    6. Live rolling with focus: In rolling, specifically look for opportunities to shrimp from mount or side control escapes. Partner is aware and allows for the technique. (2 rounds, 5 minutes each, 75% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When trapped in side control with opponent's weight on your chest and hips.
    • When being mounted and needing to create space to hip escape and recover guard.
    • When your guard is being passed and you need to create distance to reset.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Elbow-knee connection: If the opponent tries to stack pass, connect your elbow to your knee on the same side and shrimp away.
    • Hip frame: Use your forearm to create a strong frame on the opponent's hip, preventing them from closing the distance as you shrimp.
    • Leg extension: As you shrimp, extend the leg furthest from the opponent to create maximum lateral distance and space.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to shrimp escape from mount?

    Your lower back is likely hurting because you are attempting to lift your hips too high off the mat and push straight back. This hyperextends your lumbar spine. Instead, keep your hips low and focus on driving them laterally away from the opponent's weight, using your foot on the mat as a pivot and your elbow as a frame.

    Q: How can I shrimp escape against a much larger and heavier opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes critical. Focus on creating a solid frame with your elbow and forearm against their hip or chest to stop their forward pressure. Then, use your foot as a strong base on the mat and drive your hips laterally with maximum force, aiming to move them off-center rather than just creating distance.

    Q: When is the best time to shrimp escape and not just try to bridge?

    Bridging is most effective when the opponent is directly on top of you and trying to flatten you out, aiming to create an escape angle by lifting them. The shrimp escape is better when the opponent is slightly to your side (side control) or when you need to create space to recover guard from mount, as it moves your hips away from their pressure.

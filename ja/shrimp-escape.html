Shrimp Escape: The Essential BJJ Technique for White Belts

WHITE★☆☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Shrimp Escape starts when your opponent has secured a dominant position like side-control or mount, with your hips flattened against the mat. It's designed to create space and allow you to re-establish guard or regain a more neutral position.

White belts often fail because they try to push their opponent away with brute force rather than using leverage and precise body mechanics. This leads to wasted energy and potential injury.

The key insight is to use your hips to 'swim' out, like a shrimp moving backward, creating a controlled expansion of space by pivoting on your shoulders and elbows.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting from side-control, your opponent has their chest on your chest, knees driving into your hips. Your head is on the mat, and your hips are pinned.
    2. Place your forearm (the one closer to the opponent's head) on their hip bone, creating a frame. Your other hand grips their opposite sleeve or collar.
    3. Bring your knees towards your chest, creating a compact shape. Your feet should be flat on the mat, toes pointing forward.
    4. To initiate the shrimp, drive your elbow on the mat (the one closer to the opponent's head) into the mat, creating a pivot point. Simultaneously, lift your hips off the mat, angling your pelvis upwards.
    5. With your hips lifted and angled, extend your legs away from the opponent's weight, pushing your hips backward. Your body should move laterally away from the opponent's center of gravity.
    6. As you extend, your framed forearm on their hip pushes them away, and your other hand maintains control of their sleeve/collar to prevent them from closing the distance again.
    7. Continue to 'swim' your hips backward until you create enough space to bring your knee between you and the opponent, re-establishing guard or moving to a safer position.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly pushing your opponent with straight arms: This can hyperextend your elbow or shoulder joint, causing ligamentous tears. The correct action is to use your forearm as a stable frame against the hip.
    • Arching your back excessively to lift hips: This puts extreme stress on your lumbar spine, potentially leading to disc injuries. Instead, engage your core and use the pivot of your elbow and shoulder.
    • Trying to 'crab walk' sideways with flat hips: This generates little space and strains your hip flexors and lower back. Focus on the backward 'swimming' motion with an angled pelvis.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Mobility Drill: Perform 20 hip extensions and 20 hip flexions, focusing on the shrimp motion without any resistance. (0% resistance)
    2. Mat Shrimp Drill: Lie on your back and practice the shrimp escape motion 20 times, ensuring your hips clear the imaginary opponent's center line. (0% resistance)
    3. Partnered Frame and Hip Push Drill: With a partner lying on top of you in side-control, practice framing their hip and pushing away to create space, without moving your hips backward significantly. 10 repetitions each side. (25% resistance)
    4. Full Shrimp Motion (Static Partner): Have a partner lie passively on top. Execute the full shrimp escape, focusing on creating space with hip movement. 15 repetitions each side. (50% resistance)
    5. Controlled Shrimp Escape Drill: Partner applies light pressure in side-control. You shrimp out and immediately work to establish guard. 10 repetitions each side. (75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling Application: During rolling, actively look for opportunities to shrimp out of bad positions like side-control or mount. Focus on the technique, not just escaping. 3-5 minute rounds. (90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent has established side-control with their chest on your chest, pinning your hips.
    • Opponent is in mount and you feel their weight pressing down, limiting your hip mobility.
    • Opponent is attempting to pass your guard and has flattened your hips to the mat.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Opponent Postures Up: If the opponent postures up and creates space, you can follow the shrimp motion to create even more distance and potentially transition to a guard recovery or sweep.
    • Opponent Drives Forward: If the opponent drives forward aggressively as you shrimp, your frame on their hip becomes stronger, pushing them further away and potentially off-balance, creating an opportunity for a sweep or submission.
    • Opponent Follows Your Hip Movement: If the opponent tries to follow your hip movement to maintain control, continue to extend your legs and create space. This can lead to them overextending, creating openings for armbars or triangle chokes.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why do my hips hurt when I try to shrimp escape in BJJ?

    Hip pain during the shrimp escape often stems from trying to force the movement with stiff, flat hips, leading to strain on the hip flexors and lower back. Ensure your knees are bent and drawn towards your chest, creating a compact base. Then, focus on a fluid backward motion driven by extending your legs and pivoting on your shoulder and elbow, rather than simply pushing with your feet.

    Q: How do I shrimp escape against a much bigger and stronger BJJ opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, brute force is ineffective and risky. Focus on precise biomechanics: establish a strong frame with your forearm against their hip bone, and use your other arm to control their posture (sleeve or collar grip). The key is leverage; lift your hips by driving your elbow into the mat, then extend your legs backward to create space. Think of 'swimming' your hips away rather than pushing.

    Q: When is the best time to shrimp escape vs. just bridging in BJJ?

    Bridging is most effective when the opponent is directly on top of you and you want to create vertical space to invert or escape to your side, often from mount or guard. The shrimp escape is ideal for creating horizontal space when your hips are pinned to the mat, such as in side-control or when your guard has been flattened. Using a shrimp when your hips are pinned is far more biomechanically efficient and safer than attempting a bridge.

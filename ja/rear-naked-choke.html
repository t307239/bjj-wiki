BJJ Rear Naked Choke: A White Belt's Complete Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Rear Naked Choke (RNC) is applied from the back control position, aiming to restrict blood flow to the brain. It's a fundamental submission for any BJJ practitioner.

White belts often struggle by trying to muscle the choke, leading to inefficiency and potential injury. Focus on technique over brute force.

The key to a successful RNC is creating a tight "figure-four" grip that compresses the carotid arteries, not the trachea.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Secure back control: Establish hooks with your feet inside opponent's thighs, keeping your hips connected to their back. Your chest should be firmly against their upper back.
    2. Bring your choking arm across opponent's neck: Place your bicep under their chin, aiming to touch your own shoulder with your elbow.
    3. Grip your own bicep: With your free hand, grab the bicep of your choking arm, creating a "figure-four" grip. Your thumb should be pointed upwards.
    4. Secure the "seatbelt" grip (optional but recommended): With your free hand, reach over opponent's shoulder and grab your own opposite shoulder. This prevents them from turning their head.
    5. Squeeze with your arms: Simultaneously, pull your choking arm's bicep towards your chest while pushing your elbow backwards. This creates a crushing pressure on the carotid arteries.
    6. Tuck your chin: Keep your chin tucked towards your chest, away from opponent's defensive movements.
    7. Apply hip pressure: Drive your hips forward into opponent's back, further tightening the choke and reducing space for escape.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying the choke by squeezing with the arm and forearm against the windpipe (trachea): This can cause severe throat damage and pain, potentially leading to vocal cord injury. Always aim for the sides of the neck where the carotid arteries are located.
    • Gripping too loosely with the "figure-four": This allows opponent to create space by shrugging their shoulders or turning their head, making the choke ineffective and risking shoulder impingement in your own arm. Ensure a tight grip with your forearm pressing into their neck.
    • Over-extending your neck backwards to "force" the choke: This exposes your cervical spine to injury from a potential counter-attack or can lead to neck strain. Keep your head stable and focus on the arm and hip pressure.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling of grip mechanics: Practice forming the "figure-four" grip and "seatbelt" grip on your own arm and shoulder. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner drilling of hook and body position: With a compliant partner, practice establishing hooks and maintaining chest-to-back connection without applying the choke. (20 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    3. Choke setup and grip drill: From back control, practice bringing the choking arm across and forming the "figure-four" grip. Partner offers minimal resistance to head movement. (30 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled choke application drill: Apply the RNC with controlled pressure, allowing partner to tap early. Focus on the squeeze and hip drive. (15 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Live drilling with focus on RNC: Roll with a partner, specifically looking for opportunities to take the back and apply the RNC. Partner defends with moderate effort. (5 x 3-minute rounds, 75% resistance)
    6. Full sparring: Implement the RNC as a regular submission option in live sparring. (10 x 3-minute rounds, 90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When you have successfully secured back control, establishing hooks and chest-to-back connection.
    • When opponent is turtled or trying to escape from the back, creating an opening for your choking arm.
    • When opponent attempts to bridge or roll, as this can sometimes present an opportunity to secure the choke.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Chin Tuck Defense: Opponent tucks their chin tightly to their chest, preventing the choking arm from reaching the neck. This requires the attacker to adjust their grip or abandon the choke.
    • Shoulder Shrug Defense: Opponent actively shrugs their shoulders upwards, creating space between their neck and the attacker's bicep. This breaks the choke's seal, requiring the attacker to re-adjust.
    • Head Turn Defense: Opponent attempts to turn their head sharply away from the choking arm. This requires the attacker to maintain a strong grip and potentially adjust their body positioning to follow the head.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Rear Naked Choke?

    Wrist pain during the Rear Naked Choke often occurs when you overextend your wrist in an attempt to create pressure. Instead of bending your wrist backwards, keep it straight and strong. Focus on squeezing with your forearms and driving your hips forward. Ensure your thumb is pointed upwards when gripping your bicep; this neutralizes the wrist position and allows for a more powerful, biomechanically sound squeeze.

    Q: How do I Rear Naked Choke a bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, brute force is ineffective. Focus on superior technique and leverage. Ensure your hooks are deep and your chest is glued to their back to minimize their size advantage. When applying the choke, prioritize creating a tight "figure-four" grip and driving your hips into their back. The squeeze should come from compressing their carotids, not from a massive arm strength battle.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Rear Naked Choke from the back?

    The optimal time to attempt the Rear Naked Choke is when you have secured solid back control with both hooks in and your chest firmly against their upper back. Look for moments when your opponent is actively trying to escape or is in a compromised position, such as when they are turtled or attempting a turn. Avoid attempting the choke if you lack control of their hips or back, as this makes the submission easy to defend.

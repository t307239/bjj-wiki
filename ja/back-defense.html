Back defense begins when your opponent has secured control of your back, typically with hooks. The goal is to break their control and regain a neutral or advantageous position.

White belts often panic, leading to uncontrolled flailing and exposing themselves to submissions rather than escaping.

The core principle is to use your body's structure to create space and disrupt their leverage, not brute force.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Escape the Body Triangle: If a body triangle is applied, immediately focus on the arm controlling your hip. Grip their arm with both hands, palms facing each other.
    2. Create Space: Arch your back and push your hips up and to the side, creating a small gap between your back and their torso. This is a hip extension and lateral flexion.
    3. Release the Hook: With the hip space created, bring your knee (the one on the same side as the trapped arm) towards your chest, tucking it in. This breaks the body triangle.
    4. Defend the Hooks: If hooks are present, immediately turn towards the side of their controlling arm. Place your forearm on their hip to create a frame.
    5. Hip Escape (Shrimping): Simultaneously, shrimp your hips away from them. Your leading leg will slide out from between their legs.
    6. Hand Fighting for Control: As you shrimp, reach for their arm that's around your neck. Grip their wrist or forearm with your opposite hand, creating a strong frame.
    7. Turn to Your Side: Continue to shrimp and turn onto your side, facing your opponent. Your goal is to get your hips to the mat before they can re-establish control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Neck Extension: Jerking your head backward when the opponent is applying a rear-naked choke can strain your cervical spine and neck muscles.
    • Solution: Instead, focus on protecting your carotid arteries by tucking your chin to your chest and framing with your arms.
    • Knee to Face/Head: In panic, a white belt might forcefully extend their leg upwards when the opponent is close, potentially striking their own face or head.
    • Solution: Always maintain control of your limbs and use controlled movements to create space, not wild flailing.
    • Elbow Hyper-extension: Trying to break a grip on your collar or arm by violently extending your elbow outwards can lead to ligament tears in the elbow.
    • Solution: Use your body weight and hip movement to create the space needed to break the grip, rather than isolating the elbow.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Isolation (Body Triangle Escape): Practice the hip extension and lateral hip movement to create space and release the body triangle. 10 reps per side.
    2. Solo Isolation (Hook Escape): Practice the frame and shrimp motion to create space and slide your leg out. 10 reps per side.
    3. Partner Drill (Static Position): Partner secures back control with hooks. You practice framing and shrimping to create space to turn. 5 reps, 25% resistance.
    4. Partner Drill (Dynamic Position): Partner attempts to maintain back control as you execute the frame and shrimp. 5 reps, 50% resistance.
    5. Live Rolling (Controlled): Start with partner in back mount. You focus solely on escaping to guard or side control. 2 minute rounds, 75% resistance.
    6. Live Rolling (Full): Integrate back defense into rolling scenarios. Focus on preventing the submission and escaping bad positions. 5 minute rounds, 100% resistance.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent has secured hooks and is attempting to flatten you out.
    • When the opponent has secured a body triangle and is looking to transition to a choke.
    • When the opponent is transitioning from back mount to a submission attempt.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The Shrimp and Frame: As described in biomechanics, use hip extension and lateral movement to create space, then frame with your forearm on their hip to prevent them from closing the distance. Continue shrimping until you can turn to your side.
    • The "Wall Walk" (if near a wall): If you are near a wall, use the wall for support to drive your hips up and away, creating space. Then, use your hands to push off the wall and turn into your opponent.
    • The "Elbow Escape" (for body triangle): Focus on creating space with your hips, then driving your knee towards your chest and elbowing outwards to break the body triangle grip. This is a more advanced variation.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt so much when defending the back in BJJ?

    Your neck likely hurts because you are over-extending or tensing your neck muscles in an attempt to fight the choke directly. The correct biomechanical approach is to tuck your chin tightly to your chest, creating a strong posture. Then, use your arms to frame against their choking arm or head, and use your hips to create space, rather than relying solely on neck strength.

    Q: How do I defend my back against a much larger BJJ opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, brute strength is not your ally. Focus on leverage and precise movement. When they have your back, immediately use your hip escape (shrimping) to create distance, and frame with your forearm against their hip to prevent them from collapsing into you. Your goal is to get to your side and face them, then use your legs to create a guard, rather than trying to overpower them.

    Q: When should I try to escape the back versus just surviving?

    You should always be looking for an escape. Surviving is a temporary measure. As soon as the opponent secures hooks, initiate your escape mechanics by framing and shrimping. If they are actively attacking with a choke, prioritize defending the choke by tucking your chin and protecting your arteries while simultaneously working to create space and turn. The objective is to transition out of the dangerous position as quickly as possible.

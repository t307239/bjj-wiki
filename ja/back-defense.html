BJJ Back Defense: The White Belt's Ultimate Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Back defense starts when an opponent has secured hooks and control from behind you. Its goal is to prevent them from establishing dominant submissions like the rear-naked choke.

White belts often struggle because they panic and flail, exposing their neck or losing their base entirely. This creates openings for the attacker.

The key is to create a strong defensive frame by connecting your elbows to your knees and hips, neutralizing the attacker's control.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. If opponent has hooks: Immediately bring your knees up to your chest, creating a tight defensive "shell" with your shins against their hips.
    2. Grip: Place both hands on the opponent's hips, fingers pointing towards their feet, to create a "frame."
    3. Hip Angle: Keep your hips low and tucked, preventing them from being lifted or manipulated.
    4. Weight Distribution: Distribute your weight evenly, ensuring you are not leaning too far forward or backward.
    5. Elbows to Knees: Actively press your elbows into your knees, creating a solid barrier that prevents the opponent from sinking their hooks deeper.
    6. Head Position: Keep your head up and look towards the ceiling, not down at the opponent, to maintain awareness and prevent neck cranks.
    7. Hip Escape: If the opponent shifts their weight, perform a hip escape (shrimping) to create space and realign your body, moving your hips perpendicular to their body.
    8. Transition: Once you have created sufficient space, transition to a guard recovery position like closed guard or half guard.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Panicking and arching your back excessively: This can lead to hyperextension of the lumbar spine, potentially damaging intervertebral discs.
    • Trying to "muscle" your way out by pulling arms or pushing shoulders: This often results in losing your base and exposing your neck, leading to a submission attempt.
    • Not connecting elbows to knees: Leaving a gap allows the opponent to improve their position, sinking hooks or transitioning to a body triangle, risking knee or hip joint strain if you resist improperly.
    • Turning your head to look at the opponent: This exposes your carotid artery and neck, making you vulnerable to a rear-naked choke and risking cervical strain if you twist unnaturally.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo "Shell" Drill: Practice bringing knees to chest and forming the elbow-to-knee frame without a partner. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Partnered "Hook Removal" Drill: Partner lightly attempts to secure hooks. You focus solely on forming the shell and frame. (20 reps per side, 10% resistance)
    3. Partnered "Frame and Hip Escape" Drill: Partner applies light pressure with hooks. You practice framing and executing hip escapes to create space. (15 reps per side, 20% resistance)
    4. Guard Recovery Drill: Partner applies hooks. You defend, create space, and then immediately transition to closed guard. (10 reps per side, 30% resistance)
    5. "Scramble to Defense" Drill: Partner attempts a takedown and then immediately tries to secure hooks. You focus on defending the takedown and then establishing back defense. (10 reps, 50% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling (Back Defense Focus): Roll with a partner, with the primary objective being to defend the back when it's taken. (5 minutes, 75% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent secures both hooks from the rear mount.
    • Opponent is actively trying to flatten you out from the back mount.
    • Opponent is attempting a rear-naked choke or other rear-mount submission.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The "Shell" Defense: Bring knees to chest, elbows to knees, creating a tight, impenetrable barrier. Focus on maintaining this frame and hip escaping to create space.
    • Guard Recovery to Closed Guard: Once space is created, use your legs to ensnare the opponent's hips and hips escape to establish a closed guard, regaining a more neutral position.
    • Transition to Turtle: If the opponent's control is too strong and guard recovery is difficult, tuck your chin, protect your neck, and turtle up. This forces the opponent to re-establish their attack from a less dominant position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to defend the back in BJJ?

    Your lower back pain likely stems from arching your back excessively in an attempt to escape. This hyperextends the lumbar spine. Instead, keep your hips tucked and press your elbows into your knees. This creates a strong frame and utilizes your core and leg strength, not just your back, to maintain position and escape.

    Q: How do I defend my back against a bigger BJJ opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, raw strength is not your ally. Focus on superior technique and leverage. Immediately create a tight 'shell' by bringing your knees to your chest and pressing your elbows into them. Use your hips to actively hip escape and create space. Don't let them flatten you out; maintain a compact posture.

    Q: When is the best time to try and escape the back, or should I just wait for help?

    You should actively attempt to escape the moment the opponent secures both hooks and begins to flatten you out. Waiting passively allows them to solidify their control and set up submissions. The 'shell' defense and immediate hip escape are your primary tools for creating an opportunity to recover guard or transition to turtle.

Back Defense for White Belts in BJJ

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Back defense begins when an opponent has secured hooks or a body triangle from your back.

It aims to prevent submission and regain a neutral or advantageous position.

White belts often panic and flail, exposing their neck and limbs.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Initial Reaction: If hooks are in, immediately flatten your hips to the mat, reducing their leverage.
    2. Protect the Neck: Create a tight "frame" with your forearms against their chest, keeping your chin tucked.
    3. Grip Fighting (if applicable): If they have a collar grip, use your opposite hand to peel their fingers off with a "thumb-in" grip on their wrist.
    4. Hip Escape (Shrimping): Drive your left knee towards your right shoulder while pushing your hips to the right, creating space.
    5. Lowering Base: As space is created, lower your base by bending your knees and driving your feet towards the mat.
    6. Framing with Arms: Extend your arms to push their chest away, creating distance to prevent them from re-establishing control.
    7. Turning Inward: Once space is sufficient, turn your body towards the side you shrimped to, facing your opponent.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Head Movement: Tucking the chin too tightly can compress the cervical spine, leading to neck injury.
    • Correct: Tuck chin, but maintain a slight gap for breathing and awareness.
    • Flailing Arms: Wildly swinging arms to dislodge grips can expose the elbow joint to hyperextension or shoulder to dislocation.
    • Correct: Use controlled framing and shrimping motions.
    • Arching Back Excessively: Arching the back under pressure can strain the lumbar spine and open up submissions like a rear-naked choke.
    • Correct: Keep the back relatively flat while shrimping and framing.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Escape & Frame: Practice shrimping away from a wall, extending arms to create a frame. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner Frame & Shrimp (No Hooks): Partner stands in front, you practice framing and shrimping away. (10 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    3. Partner Frame & Shrimp (With Hooks, No Submission): Partner establishes hooks, you practice framing and shrimping away. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    4. Partner Frame & Shrimp (With Hooks, Light Control): Partner establishes hooks and light chest pressure. You frame and shrimp to create space. (10 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    5. Escape to Guard: Partner establishes hooks, you frame, shrimp, and attempt to get to closed guard. (5 reps per side, 90% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling (Defensive Focus): Focus solely on defending the back and escaping to guard. (3 minute rounds, 100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent secures both hooks.
    • Opponent establishes a body triangle.
    • Opponent attempts a rear-naked choke.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Shrimp and Frame: Maintain frames, shrimp hips away, and turn into the opponent to face them.
    • Remove Hooks: Use your forearm to post on their hip and kick your leg out to remove a hook.
    • Stall and Wait: If escape is impossible, create a tight frame and wait for an opportunity or instruction from your coach.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try to defend the back in BJJ?

    Neck pain often occurs from tucking your chin too aggressively or by allowing your opponent to isolate your head with their arm. Ensure you maintain a slight air gap between your chin and chest for breathing and awareness, and focus on framing with your forearms against their chest rather than collapsing your neck.

    Q: How do I defend the back against a bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is key. Focus on creating frames with your forearms against their chest and hips, using your entire body weight to shrimp away. Never let them flatten you; always try to maintain a rounded shape to make it harder for them to apply pressure and submissions.

    Q: When is the best time to try and escape the back in BJJ?

    The ideal time to escape the back is immediately after hooks are established or if you feel their control weakening. The longer you allow them to settle into a dominant back-mount position, the more difficult it becomes to create space and defend submissions. Be proactive, not reactive.

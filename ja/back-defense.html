BJJ Back Defense: A White Belt's Guide

Back Defense begins when an opponent secures a dominant position on your back, threatening submissions like the rear-naked choke. It aims to prevent the choke and regain a neutral or advantageous position.

White belts often fail by panicking, flailing, or giving up their neck without attempting to create space or control limbs. This leads to immediate submission or positional disadvantage.

The core mechanical insight is to use your body as a shield and lever, creating frames and moving your hips to break the opponent's control and disrupt their posture.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Initial Reaction: When the opponent secures the "hooks" (their feet inside your thighs), immediately tuck your chin to your chest, creating a "chin-to-sternum" posture. This prevents them from easily lifting and controlling your head.
    2. Hip Escape: Drive your hips to the side, creating a small gap. Your goal is to move your hips perpendicular to your opponent's chest.
    3. Frame Creation: Use your forearms to create frames between your body and the opponent's torso. Place your bicep on their hip and your forearm on their ribcage.
    4. Elbow Connection: Bring your elbow on the side you are escaping towards your hip. This creates a "wall" that prevents them from sinking their chest further into your back.
    5. Hand Control (Optional but Recommended): If possible, secure a "collar grip" on the opponent's lapel with your non-framing hand. This limits their ability to posture up or drive forward.
    6. Leg Pummeling: Once a small space is created, "pummel" your legs. This means actively moving your legs to create an escape route, aiming to untangle your opponent's hooks.
    7. Hip Explosion: As you pummel your legs, explosively drive your hips away and to the side, aiming to end up on your side or even on your knees.
    8. Head and Arm Control: As you escape, prioritize protecting your neck and creating space to turn into your opponent or face them.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Chin Tuck: Tucking the chin too far down or to the side leaves the carotid arteries exposed, making you highly susceptible to a rear-naked choke. The correct movement is a firm "chin-to-sternum" tuck, protecting the front of the neck.
    • Over-Reliance on Arm Strength: Trying to "pull" the opponent off with arm strength alone strains the shoulder joint and often fails. Instead, use your hips and core to create leverage and space, keeping your arms in a "frame" position.
    • Twisting the Neck: Panicked neck twisting to escape can damage the cervical spine and ligaments. Focus on controlled hip movement and framing to create space, rather than violent head movements.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Escape Drill: Practice tucking your chin and performing hip escapes to the side, focusing on creating space. (50 reps each side, 0% resistance)
    2. Frame Creation Drill: From a seated position, have a partner lightly rest their chest on your back. Practice creating forearm frames against their hips and ribs. (30 reps, 10% resistance)
    3. Pummeling Drill: Lie on your stomach. Have a partner lightly place their feet (hooks) around your hips. Practice pummeling your legs to untangle them. (40 reps each side, 15% resistance)
    4. Combined Hip Escape and Frame Drill: Have a partner lightly apply pressure from your back. Practice tucking your chin, creating frames, and performing hip escapes. (30 reps each side, 20% resistance)
    5. Controlled Back Escape Drill: Partner starts in back mount with light control. You focus on chin tuck, framing, and hip escapes to turn into them or escape to your side. (20 reps each side, 40% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling with Focus: During rolling, specifically look for opportunities to practice Back Defense when you are taken down. Focus on executing the learned mechanics. (5 rounds, 75% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent secures "hooks" on your back and begins to drive their chest into you.
    • When the opponent has a "seatbelt grip" and is threatening to pull you forward.
    • When you feel the opponent's weight shifting to "climb" for a choke.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape and Frame: As described in biomechanics. Tuck chin, escape hips to the side, create forearm frames, and pummel legs to create space and turn into the opponent.
    • "Shrimping" to Guard: If you can create enough space, "shrimp" your hips away and bring your legs up to establish closed guard. This immediately neutralizes their back control.
    • Turtle Position Conversion: If escape to guard is not immediately possible, drive your hips down and forward to turtle up. Protect your neck and attempt to regain a base on your knees.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I defend the back in BJJ?

    Neck pain often occurs from either not tucking your chin effectively, allowing the opponent to torque your head, or from excessive and uncontrolled twisting of your neck when trying to escape. Ensure a firm "chin-to-sternum" tuck and focus on using your hips and frames to create space, rather than violently jerking your head.

    Q: How do I defend the back against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus intensely on creating small frames with your forearms to disrupt their pressure. Explode your hips to create even minimal space, and immediately use your legs to pummel and escape, aiming to get to your side or turtle. Do not try to overpower them; use precise, explosive movements.

    Q: When is the best time to try and escape the back in BJJ?

    The optimal time to attempt Back Defense is as soon as you feel the opponent establish hooks and begin to apply pressure. Don't wait until they have a strong grip or are actively attacking a choke. The initial moments of losing the back are when your escape attempts have the highest chance of success.

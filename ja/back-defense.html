Back defense is the critical process of preventing an opponent from securing dominant control from behind you, like the back mount.

White belts often panic and flail, creating openings or exacerbating the bad position due to a lack of understanding of leverage and structure.

The core principle is to create space and re-establish a defensive structure using your limbs as levers and your hips as a base.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Opponent secures Seatbelt Grip (one arm over shoulder, one under armpit): Your goal is to break this grip.
    2. Tuck your chin to your chest: This protects your neck and prevents a rear-naked choke and limits head movement.
    3. Create a 'frame' with your arm opposite the choking arm: Place your forearm across their chest, aiming to create distance.
    4. Hips angle slightly towards the side of the choking arm: This shifts your weight and makes it harder for them to maintain control.
    5. Drive your elbow into their hip on the side of their choking arm: This is a primary lever to break their connection to your back.
    6. Simultaneously, pull your shoulder away from their choking arm: Use your free arm to create separation.
    7. Once a small gap is created, shrimp your hips away and turn your body to face them: Aim to land in a guard position (closed guard is ideal for white belts).
    8. Secure your guard by closing your legs: Ensure your ankles are crossed and your knees are tight to their hips.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Jerking your head backward to escape: This can hyperextend your cervical spine, leading to whiplash or nerve damage. Instead, tuck your chin and use your frame.
    • Allowing the opponent to flatten you out completely: This makes it impossible to create space and increases the risk of choke submissions. Instead, maintain a tight posture and use your hips to create angles.
    • Reaching for their arms to defend: This often leads to giving up your back entirely or creating an opportunity for them to transition to a more dominant position. Instead, focus on breaking their grip and creating space with your frame.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Escape and Frame Drill (0% resistance): Practice shrimping your hips and creating a frame with your arms 20 times.
    2. Partner Drill - Grip Break and Frame (25% resistance): Your partner lightly secures a seatbelt grip. Practice breaking the grip and creating a frame without moving your hips significantly. 10 repetitions.
    3. Partner Drill - Frame and Hip Escape (50% resistance): Partner secures seatbelt. Practice creating frame, then shrimping hips away. Partner offers minimal resistance. 10 repetitions.
    4. Partner Drill - Full Escape to Guard (75% resistance): Partner secures seatbelt and attempts to maintain back control. You practice the full sequence of breaking grip, framing, shrimping, turning, and closing guard. 5 repetitions.
    5. Controlled Sparring - Focus on Back Defense (90% resistance): Spar with a partner whose primary goal is to secure your back. Your goal is to defend and escape to guard. 3 rounds of 2 minutes.
    6. Live Sparring (100% resistance): Integrate back defense into your regular rolling. Focus on recognizing when your back is taken and applying the learned techniques.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent secures a seatbelt grip while you are on your hands and knees.
    • Opponent attempts to transition to back mount from a scramble.
    • You are taken down and the opponent immediately tries to establish back control.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The Frame and Shrimp: As described above, create a frame with your arm, angle your hips, and shrimp to create space to turn into your opponent.
    • The Wall Walk: If you are against a wall, use the wall for support to drive your hips up and away, creating space to turn.
    • The "Escape to Turtle" (Advanced for White Belt, but good to be aware): If unable to establish guard, prioritize getting to your hands and knees (turtle position) to prevent immediate submissions and reset.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when someone has my back in BJJ?

    Neck pain often occurs when you resist a choke by arching your neck backward or by allowing your head to be pulled too far in one direction. The correct defense is to tuck your chin tightly to your chest to protect your airway and cervical spine, and then use your arms to create a frame and your hips to create space, rather than relying on neck strength.

    Q: How do I defend my back against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise mechanics are even more crucial. Focus on using your frame to create maximum distance, and your hip escape to shift their weight off balance. Do not try to muscle your way out; instead, use their size against them by creating angles and exploiting the space you generate.

    Q: When should I try to escape their grip versus just moving my hips in BJJ back defense?

    You must do both simultaneously. The frame (using your arm) is to break their initial grip and create immediate space. Simultaneously, you need to angle your hips and shrimp to move your body out of the line of attack. One without the other will likely fail; they are integrated movements.

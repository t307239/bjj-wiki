Back Defense begins when an opponent has secured hooks and control from behind you, aiming to choke or submit. It's about regaining a safe positional base.

White belts often panic, leading to stiff movements and predictable escapes that expose them further. This reaction is the opposite of what's needed.

The key insight is to use your opponent's weight and momentum against them by creating frames and rotating your body efficiently.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Opponent has hooks: your hips are on the mat, their legs are inside yours.
    2. Opponent's chest is on your upper back, head near your shoulder.
    3. Establish an 'underhook' by reaching your arm across their body, securing their hip with a gable grip (palms clasped).
    4. Create a frame: place your free hand on their shoulder or neck, pushing slightly away.
    5. Rotate your hips TOWARDS the side of your underhook, driving your knee into their hip.
    6. As you rotate, bring your free leg (the one not framing) to your chest, creating space.
    7. Explode your hips up and to the side, driving your underhook arm through their body as you shrimp out.
    8. Once clear, immediately establish a guard (closed guard or half guard) or scramble to your feet.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Trying to stand up immediately without creating space: This can strain the lumbar spine and neck as the opponent pulls you back down, potentially causing disc compression.
    • Stiffly pushing on the opponent's head or shoulders: This can lead to shoulder impingement or AC joint sprains if the opponent drives forward, trapping your arm.
    • Turning directly into the opponent's chest: This allows them to maintain their dominant angle and can result in a knee or elbow injury to your own face or ribs.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip rotation and frame creation without a partner. Focus on the sequence of underhook, frame, hip turn, and shrimp. (50 reps each side)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Have a partner gently hold position. Execute the escape sequence. Focus on timing and body mechanics. (20 reps each side)
    3. Light resistance drilling: Partner applies minimal pressure. Focus on clearing their hooks and establishing guard. (15 reps each side)
    4. Controlled sparring (25% resistance): Attempt Back Defense in a controlled roll. Partner offers passive resistance, allowing you to practice the escape.
    5. Controlled sparring (50% resistance): Partner applies more active, but predictable, pressure. Focus on fighting for the escape. (5 minute rounds)
    6. Live rolling (75-90% resistance): Attempt Back Defense against a resisting opponent, focusing on successful escapes and transitions.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent secures both hooks and is driving forward.
    • Opponent has an underhook and is attempting to flatten you out.
    • You feel your base is compromised and they are close to a choke.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Underhook and Hip Escape: Secure an underhook on the side you want to escape to. Drive your knee into their hip and shrimp out, creating space to establish guard.
    • Frame and Rotate: Use your arms to create frames against their chest and head. Rotate your hips towards your underhook side, creating a wedge to break their control.
    • Body Triangle Escape (if applied): If the body triangle is locked, focus on breaking the grip on your legs by creating frames and then executing the hip escape and underhook.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try to defend the back in BJJ?

    Neck pain often results from trying to stiffly resist the opponent's head control or choke setup. Instead of pushing directly against their head, focus on creating space with your frames on their shoulders and chest. Rotate your hips to break their posture and unwind your neck, rather than using direct muscular force against their head.

    Q: How do I defend the back against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is critical. Focus on using their weight against you by rotating your hips sharply. When creating frames, aim to create angles that disrupt their base. Your underhook and the drive of your knee into their hip are your primary tools for generating the power needed to escape their superior mass.

    Q: When is the best time to try and escape the back mount in BJJ?

    The ideal time to escape the back mount is *before* the opponent fully secures their choke or establishes a dominant body triangle. Look for opportunities when their hooks are present but their chest pressure isn't fully overwhelming. Actively create frames and initiate your escape sequence as soon as you feel their control tightening.

Baseball Choke: A Biomechanically Accurate BJJ Guide for White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

The Baseball Choke is initiated from the closed guard, aiming to secure a submission by compressing the carotid arteries. White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength instead of precise leverage.

The key to a successful Baseball Choke is understanding the rotational force generated by your hips and the correct angle of your torso relative to your opponent's.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure a deep cross-collar grip with your right hand on your opponent's left lapel, thumb inside the lapel. Your left hand grips the same lapel on the opposite side, thumb outside.
    2. Arch your lower back and drive your hips upward, creating space between your bodies.
    3. Simultaneously, pull your opponent's head down and slightly to your left by driving your right elbow downwards and across your body.
    4. Rotate your right forearm inwards, creating a pinching motion against your opponent's carotid artery and the underside of their jaw.
    5. Maintain a tight closed guard, keeping your knees squeezed around your opponent's torso to prevent them from posturing up.
    6. Shift your weight slightly to your right, angling your hips to generate torque, and ensure your left shoulder is pressing into your opponent's upper chest.
    7. Continue the inward rotation of your right forearm and the downward pull of your opponent's head until they tap.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to pull the lapel straight up without hip drive: This strains the shoulder joint, potentially causing a rotator cuff tear as the humerus is forced into an unnatural position against the glenoid cavity.
    • Squeezing the lapel with an open palm instead of a focused grip: This distributes pressure inefficiently, leading to a weak choke and potential strain on the metacarpophalangeal joints from overextension.
    • Letting your hips drop immediately after initiating the choke: This removes the essential upward leverage, forcing you to compensate with excessive arm strength, which can lead to elbow hyperextension and ligament damage.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip arch and torso rotation motion without a partner, focusing on the feeling of generating torque. (50 reps)
    2. Solo drill: Practice the grip sequence and the inward forearm rotation motion, simulating the choke. (50 reps)
    3. Partner drill (no resistance): Practice the entire sequence with a compliant partner, focusing on correct body positioning and grip placement. (10 reps)
    4. Partner drill (light resistance - 25%): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the setup and execution of the choke. (5 reps per side)
    5. Partner drill (medium resistance - 50%): Your partner actively tries to defend, but allows you to practice the mechanics. (5 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling (90%): Attempt the Baseball Choke in a controlled sparring situation, focusing on timing and transition. (3 rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is posturing up in your closed guard, creating a slight gap between your bodies.
    • When your opponent is trying to stack you from closed guard, bringing their weight forward.
    • When your opponent is defending a triangle choke, and you can transition to the lapel grip.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Posture Up and Escape Guard: If the choke is not immediately tight, the opponent should immediately try to straighten their posture, widening the base and creating space to escape the guard.
    • Grip Break and Hip Escape: The defender can attempt to break the collar grip by prying the fingers open or by shrimping their hips away to relieve pressure on the neck.
    • Shielding the Neck with the Shoulder: The defender can attempt to tuck their chin and bring their shoulder forward to protect the carotid artery from the choking arm.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Baseball Choke?

    Wrist pain in the Baseball Choke typically arises from gripping the lapel too tightly with an open palm or by attempting to generate all the choking force with wrist flexion. Instead, focus on a strong, thumbless grip on the lapel and use your forearm and shoulder to drive the rotation. Ensure your wrist remains in a neutral or slightly extended position to avoid undue stress.

    Q: How can I use the Baseball Choke against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paramount. Ensure you achieve a deep cross-collar grip and secure your hips tightly around their waist to control their base. The key is to use your hip elevation and torso rotation to create the choking angle, rather than relying on pure strength to pull them down. Focus on the inward rotation of your forearm and the downward pull of their head simultaneously.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Baseball Choke from closed guard?

    The optimal time to attempt the Baseball Choke is when your opponent tries to posture up or is leaning forward, creating a slight space. This allows you to arch your back and drive your hips up, initiating the crucial upward leverage. Avoid attempting it when they are completely flattened out or have their weight heavily on top of you, as this makes it difficult to generate the necessary rotational force.

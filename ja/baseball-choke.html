Baseball Choke BJJ Technique Guide for White Belts

The Baseball Choke is typically initiated from the closed guard position, aiming to secure a submission by compressing the carotid arteries.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of control over their body mechanics and an over-reliance on brute strength rather than leverage.

The key insight is using your opponent's posture and your body's rotational momentum to generate the choking pressure, not just pulling with your arms.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish an "S" grip with your right hand on the opponent's left lapel, thumb inside, fingers outside.
    2. Your left hand secures a deep "baseball" grip on the same lapel, palm facing your opponent, fingers curled around the fabric.
    3. Align your hips so they are perpendicular to your opponent's torso, creating a strong base.
    4. Drive your left shoulder into your opponent's chest, creating a slight angle and breaking their posture forward.
    5. Begin to sit up by driving your hips towards the ceiling, simultaneously pulling your left elbow down and across your body.
    6. Rotate your torso counter-clockwise, maintaining the "S" grip pressure with your right hand while the left hand acts as a lever.
    7. As you complete the rotation, your opponent's neck will be trapped between their own shoulder and your forearm, and the lapel will tighten across their carotid artery.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Grip Placement: Gripping too high on the lapel can lead to your opponent easily posturing up, and can strain your own shoulder joint by forcing an unnatural external rotation.
    • Correct: Grip deep, near the sternum, for maximum leverage and control.
    • Excessive Arm Pulling: Yanking with your arms without hip and torso rotation strains your biceps and triceps, and is inefficient for choking.
    • Correct: Focus on sitting up and rotating your body, using arm pulls to complement the rotation.
    • Allowing Opponent to Posture Up: If your hips are not angled correctly or you don't drive your shoulder into their chest, they can straighten their back.
    • Correct: Maintain a strong base with perpendicular hips and use your shoulder to break their posture before initiating the choke.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Grip & Rotation Drill: Practice the "S" and "baseball" grips on a dummy or pillow, and practice the torso rotation motion without an opponent. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    2. Static Closed Guard Grip Setup: Partner lies on their back, you are in closed guard. Practice establishing the grips and feeling the lapel tension. (5 reps each side, 0% resistance)
    3. Static Closed Guard Posture Break: With partner in closed guard, practice driving your shoulder into their chest and breaking their posture. (5 reps each side, 10% resistance)
    4. Controlled Baseball Choke Attempt: From closed guard, attempt the Baseball Choke with a compliant partner who offers light resistance to posture. Focus on the rotation and grip. (10 reps each side, 25% resistance)
    5. Live Closed Guard Choke Attempts: In rolling, actively look for opportunities to set up the Baseball Choke from closed guard. Focus on proper execution and grip retention. (5 minutes, 50% resistance)
    6. Full Rolling Application: Integrate the Baseball Choke attempts into your live rolling sessions, accepting when it's defended and learning from the experience. (10 minutes, 75% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is postured up in your closed guard and you have control of their lapels.
    • Opponent attempts to stack you from closed guard, creating an opportunity to trap their head.
    • You have just swept or transitioned and the opponent is still recovering their base.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Posture Up and Grip Break: Opponent quickly straightens their back, creating space. Simultaneously, they use their free hand to peel your choking hand off their lapel, and the other hand to push your shoulder away.
    • Head and Arm Escape: If the choke is being applied, the defender can tuck their chin tightly and drive their head forward, creating space to peel the choking arm off.
    • Guard Pass Attempt: If the choke is not fully secured, the opponent can attempt a guard pass by creating a strong base and driving forward to break the closed guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do the Baseball Choke from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs if you are gripping the lapel too tightly with an overextended wrist or if you are relying solely on wrist strength. Ensure your "baseball" grip is firm but not tense, with your fingers curled naturally around the lapel. Focus on using your forearm and the rotation of your torso to create the choke, rather than just bending your wrist.

    Q: How do I apply the Baseball Choke effectively against a much larger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paramount. Focus on breaking their posture *before* you attempt the choke by driving your shoulder into their chest and using your hips to create an angle. Your rotation needs to be sharp and decisive. The "baseball" grip provides excellent leverage; ensure it's deep on the lapel to maximize the choking pressure.

    Q: Is the Baseball Choke a submission I should try to hit every time I'm in closed guard?

    No, the Baseball Choke is a specific submission that requires certain conditions to be met. Attempting it when your opponent is not properly postured or has strong control of your legs will likely be ineffective and can leave you in a vulnerable position. Focus on establishing control and breaking posture first, then look for the opportunity.

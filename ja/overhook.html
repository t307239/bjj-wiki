The Overhook is a dominant positional control, typically achieved from side control or mount, where your arm wraps around the opponent's back, elbow to elbow.

It's often misunderstood by white belts who treat it as a static pin, losing the dynamic pressure that makes it effective.

The key is to use your overhooking arm to create a strong connection and drive your shoulder into the opponent's shoulder, disrupting their base and ability to escape.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side control, establish a cross-face with your non-overhooking arm, pressing your forearm against their jawline, angling your head towards their head.
    2. Your overhooking arm's elbow should be close to their hip.
    3. Begin to drive your shoulder into their upper chest, creating a slight forward lean.
    4. As you drive forward, your overhooking arm snakes around their back, aiming to connect your elbow to your wrist.
    5. Simultaneously, your hips should drive forward and slightly to your right (if overhooking their left side), creating a strong base.
    6. Your chest should be pressed firmly against their upper back, preventing them from turning in.
    7. Maintain head-to-head connection, preventing them from creating space by looking up.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly reaching too far for the overhook without driving your shoulder forward can hyperextend your shoulder joint, risking a rotator cuff tear. Maintain connection by driving your shoulder first.
    • Trying to 'pull' the opponent's back to you with the overhook, rather than driving your body into them, can strain your bicep and elbow. Focus on forward pressure and hip extension.
    • Allowing your hips to stay too far back while attempting the overhook creates a gap, enabling the opponent to shrimp or bridge effectively and increasing the risk of your knee or ankle being trapped in their guard if they manage to recover guard.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the shoulder drive and arm wrap motion on an empty mat, focusing on the feeling of connection. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Partner lies on their side. Focus on establishing the overhook with correct body mechanics. (25 reps each side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner actively tries to resist the movement slightly. Focus on maintaining the connection and driving. (50 reps total, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled positional sparring: Start from side control, work to establish the overhook, and hold for 10 seconds. Partner attempts to escape. (5 rounds, 50% resistance)
    5. Controlled positional sparring: Work to establish the overhook and then transition to a submission attempt (e.g., arm triangle). (5 rounds, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling: Focus on implementing the overhook in scrambles and transitions, aiming to maintain control. (3 x 5-minute rounds, 90-100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When you have established side control and the opponent is facing away from you.
    • When transitioning from mount to side control and they are trying to turn into you.
    • When the opponent attempts a guard pass and you can catch their back or side.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Opponent turns into you: Immediately drive your shoulder into their shoulder and use your overhook to prevent them from completing the turn. Focus on hip pressure and head control.
    • Opponent shrimps away: Use your overhooking arm to maintain connection and drive your hips forward, negating their space creation. Your non-overhooking arm can frame against their hip.
    • Opponent attempts to underhook your arm: If they manage to get an underhook, you must immediately switch your overhook to an underhook on their other side, or disengage and reset to prevent being swept or controlled.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to get an overhook in BJJ?

    Your shoulder likely hurts because you are reaching for the overhook without first establishing a strong connection with your shoulder and chest. This hyperextends the joint. Instead, focus on driving your shoulder into their upper chest and back, then allow your arm to wrap naturally as your body moves forward. This uses your body's structure, not just arm strength.

    Q: How can I get an overhook against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise body mechanics are crucial. Don't try to muscle it. Focus on driving your shoulder into their upper torso and creating a tight, connected base with your hips. Use your head to control their head angle and prevent them from turning into you. Your overhooking arm should aim for their back, elbow to elbow, creating a 'seatbelt' like control, then drive your weight forward.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to a submission from an overhook position in BJJ?

    The optimal time to transition to a submission from an overhook is when you feel the opponent's structure begin to break down, meaning they are unable to effectively defend your pressure. This often happens when they are trying to turn away from you or are struggling to regain their base. Look for opportunities like an arm-triangle choke or a kimura when they expose a limb trying to escape your control.

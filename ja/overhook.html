The Essential Overhook for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The overhook is a dominant position where you secure one of your opponent's arms with your arm passing over theirs, typically from guard or side control.

White belts often struggle because they rely on brute strength or lack precise frame control, leading to inefficient mechanics and injury.

The key is to use your body's leverage and bone structure to control their limb and posture, not just muscle power.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish a two-on-one grip on your opponent's right arm: your right hand grips their right wrist (palm-to-palm), your left hand grips their right bicep.
    2. Shift your hips to your left, creating a 45-degree angle between your pelvis and your opponent's torso.
    3. Bring your left knee across your opponent's chest, aiming to place it inside their right armpit.
    4. Extend your right leg, pushing your opponent's right hip away to create space and break their posture.
    5. As you extend your right leg, simultaneously drive your left shoulder into their chest, creating a strong frame.
    6. Secure your left hand by gripping your own right bicep (or wrist) to create a tight "figure four" grip around their arm.
    7. Maintain a stable base by keeping your right foot planted on the mat, ensuring your weight is distributed over your hips and left shoulder.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Overextending your arm to grab the overhook: This can hyperextend your elbow joint, causing ligament damage (e.g., UCL tear).
    • Correct: Keep your elbow bent to 90 degrees and use your shoulder and hip to drive into their arm.
    • Tensing your neck and shoulders excessively: This can lead to muscle strains in the neck and upper back, and can also restrict breathing.
    • Correct: Relax your neck and shoulders, focus on driving with your core and hips.
    • Allowing your opponent to straighten their arm: If they manage to straighten their arm, it can create a powerful leverage point for them to escape or attack your arm.
    • Correct: Maintain constant pressure with your shoulder and keep your grip tight, preventing arm extension.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip shift and knee-over-chest movement with no resistance (10 reps each side).
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice establishing the overhook from closed guard, focusing on grip and hip angle (10 reps each side).
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Your partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to focus on the weight transfer and frame creation (10 reps each side, 25% resistance).
    4. Controlled positional sparring: Start from closed guard, work to establish the overhook and transition to a sweep or submission. Partner defends passively (5 rounds, 50% resistance).
    5. Positional sparring with dynamic defense: Your partner actively defends the overhook establishment and attempts to pass. Focus on maintaining the overhook position (5 rounds, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Incorporate the overhook into your rolling. Focus on maintaining the position and looking for opportunities to advance (3 rounds, 90% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is postured up in your closed guard, creating an opening for an arm isolation.
    • When you have successfully controlled one of your opponent's arms during a guard pass attempt.
    • When transitioning from side control to a more dominant position like mount or back control.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): If your opponent has an overhook on your arm from guard, immediately shrimp your hips away to create space and break their control.
    • Arm Posture and Frame: If your opponent attempts an overhook, actively posture up and use your free arm to create a frame against their shoulder, preventing them from isolating your limb.
    • Grip Break: If they have secured the overhook, focus on breaking their grip by targeting their thumbs or the wrist with your free hand, and then immediately returning your arm to a safe position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to get an overhook in BJJ?

    Wrist pain often occurs if you're gripping too tightly with your fingers or if your wrist is bent at an extreme angle. Ensure your grip is firm but not rigid, and try to keep your wrist in a neutral or slightly extended position. Focus on using your forearm and shoulder to drive the overhook, rather than solely relying on wrist strength.

    Q: How can I get an overhook against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are crucial. Instead of trying to muscle your way in, focus on creating angles with your hips and using your legs to off-balance them. A well-timed hip escape followed by driving your shoulder into their chest as you secure the overhook can be very effective, even against larger opponents.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from an overhook to a submission in BJJ?

    The ideal time to transition to a submission is when you have successfully broken your opponent's posture and they are unable to defend effectively. This often occurs after you've used the overhook to control their arm and create an angle, making them vulnerable to attacks like an armbar or triangle choke. Look for moments when their weight is compromised.

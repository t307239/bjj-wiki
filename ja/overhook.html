The Overhook is a dominant positional control, typically achieved from guard or side control, to limit your opponent's mobility and set up attacks.

White belts often fail by using raw strength instead of leverage, leading to wasted energy and vulnerability.

The key is to use your elbow as a fulcrum, creating a strong lever with your arm to control their posture and hip movement.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting from Closed Guard: Secure a strong grip on your opponent's collar with your right hand, pulling their head towards your chest.
    2. Right Arm Insertion: With your left arm, reach *underneath* their right armpit, aiming to hook your elbow past their shoulder blade.
    3. Elbow Fulcrum: Bend your left elbow to 90 degrees, creating a powerful pivot point against their upper back.
    4. Hip Angle: Simultaneously, angle your hips to your left, creating a tight frame and preventing them from posturing up.
    5. Weight Distribution: Drive your left shoulder slightly forward and down, distributing your weight onto their upper back and shoulder.
    6. Grip Refinement: Your left hand can now grip your own right bicep or wrist for an even tighter control, creating a "figure four" structure.
    7. Leg Action: Maintain a strong closed guard, squeezing your knees to control their hips and prevent them from creating space.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Elbow Placement: Reaching too far across the body and hooking the opponent's waist instead of their upper back. This can strain the rotator cuff of the shoulder due to internal rotation under pressure.
    • Straight Arm Extension: Trying to "muscle" the overhook with a straight arm, leading to hyperextension of the elbow joint or strain on the triceps if the opponent resists forcefully.
    • Lifting with the Spine: Attempting to lift the opponent using your lower back muscles while maintaining the overhook, risking lumbar disc injury.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Isolation (0%): Practice the arm motion of reaching under the armpit and hooking your elbow, focusing on the feeling of creating the fulcrum. 10 reps.
    2. Partner Drills - No Resistance (25%): Your partner stands passively. Practice establishing the overhook from closed guard, focusing on hip angle and elbow placement. 5 sets of 5 reps.
    3. Partner Drills - Light Resistance (50%): Your partner offers mild resistance to you establishing the overhook. Focus on maintaining the frame and hip angle as they try to create space. 5 sets of 5 reps.
    4. Guard Retention Drill (75%): Your partner attempts to pass your guard while you focus on maintaining the overhook. They will try to break your grips and posture. 3 rounds, 2 minutes each.
    5. Positional Sparring - Guard (90%): Start from closed guard. The goal is to establish the overhook and prevent your partner from passing. No submissions allowed initially. 3 rounds, 3 minutes each.
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Incorporate the overhook into your regular sparring sessions, looking for opportunities to establish and utilize it. Focus on maintaining control and setting up transitions.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is posturing up from your closed guard, creating a space to attack.
    • Opponent is attempting to stack pass your guard, allowing you to hook their upper back.
    • You have successfully transitioned to side control and want to secure a dominant over-under position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Defending the Overhook Insertion: If your opponent attempts to insert their overhook, immediately "frame" with your forearm against their elbow, preventing them from getting past your shoulder. Simultaneously, shrimp your hips away to create space and break their angle.
    • Breaking the Overhook Grip: If the overhook is established, use your free hand to "peel" their hooking arm away by gripping their wrist or forearm and pulling it towards your midline. This disrupts their leverage.
    • Posturing Up: If you are on the bottom and they have an overhook, focus on "crunching" your posture by bringing your chest to your knees and tucking your chin. This makes it harder for them to control your upper body with the overhook.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to get an Overhook in BJJ?

    This is likely due to overextending your arm or forcing the hook by rotating your shoulder internally against resistance. Ensure your elbow is bent at approximately 90 degrees, creating a strong fulcrum point. Focus on driving your shoulder forward and down into their upper back, rather than simply reaching with a straight arm.

    Q: How can I get an Overhook against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Instead of trying to lift them, focus on their posture and hip control. Angle your hips sharply away from their hooking arm, and use your legs to squeeze and disrupt their base. Your elbow should be the pivot point, not brute strength.

    Q: When is the best time to use an Overhook in BJJ, and what should I do next?

    The overhook is excellent for controlling posture and limiting your opponent's mobility, particularly when they are trying to posture up or stack pass. Once established, it can be used to set up sweeps like the scissor sweep or hip bump sweep, or to transition to dominant positions like side control or mount.

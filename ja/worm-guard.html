Precise Worm Guard Technique for White Belt BJJ

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

Worm Guard is an open guard position initiated from the guard, aiming to control the opponent's legs and hips to create sweep or submission opportunities.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of leverage and frame, leading to predictable movements and loss of position.

The key insight is using your body as a lever, specifically the angle of your hips and the placement of your shin to create off-balancing forces.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, break opponent's posture by pulling their collar and sleeve (same side) with your hands. Achieve a seated guard posture.
    2. Untuck one leg and step your foot onto the opponent's hip, creating a frame with your shin parallel to the floor, knee pointing slightly upwards.
    3. Simultaneously, thread your opposite arm *under* the opponent's leg (the one on the side of the foot on hip), reaching for their far ankle or the cuff of their pants.
    4. Drive your hips *into* the opponent, creating a strong base and pushing their weight away from you.
    5. Secure a grip on the opponent's ankle or pant cuff with your underhooking arm, ensuring your thumb is facing away from you.
    6. Place your free foot on the opponent's opposite hip, creating a second frame and maintaining distance.
    7. Use your shin on the hip to control their base, and your underhooking arm to pull their leg, creating an off-balancing angle. Your hips should be angled slightly towards the side of the leg you have hooked.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to pull the opponent forward with only arm strength without hip drive: This can strain the shoulder joint by hyperextension or lead to rotator cuff tears.
    • Letting the opponent's leg freely pass your body when establishing the underhook: This risks a knee hyperextension or ligament tear in your knee as their leg can buckle it.
    • Keeping your supporting foot too close to your hips when framing on the hip: This provides a weak base and can lead to your knee collapsing inwards, risking meniscus tears or ACL strain.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the sequence of untucking the leg, stepping on the hip, and reaching for the ankle, focusing on hip movement and shin placement. (50 reps)
    2. Solo drill: Practice transitioning between frames on both hips while maintaining a seated guard. (50 reps)
    3. Partner drill (no resistance): Partner stands in front of you. Practice establishing the Worm Guard grips and frames, focusing on hip angles and shin pressure. (20 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner gently resists your attempts to establish the guard. Focus on maintaining frames and preventing them from passing. (5 minutes)
    5. Positional sparring: Start from seated guard. Focus only on establishing and maintaining Worm Guard for 1 minute, then reset. (5 rounds)
    6. Live rolling: Attempt Worm Guard techniques during rolling, prioritizing control and proper mechanics over immediate sweeps or submissions. (5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is posturing up from your closed guard, creating space to open your guard.
    • When the opponent is attempting to pass your guard and their weight is forward, allowing you to attack their legs.
    • When you have successfully controlled one of the opponent's legs and are looking to create an attacking angle.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: If the opponent stacks you, drive your hips back and under their weight, using your shin on hip to create space and potentially transition to X-guard or sweep.
    • Leg Pummeling: If the opponent tries to pummel their leg through your underhook, focus on maintaining grip integrity and using your hips to create space, or switch to a different guard.
    • Hip Escape and Re-guard: If the opponent gets a strong grip on your arm or leg, escape your hips and re-establish a more stable guard position, such as closed guard or half guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to put my foot on the opponent's hip for Worm Guard?

    This often happens if your knee is collapsing inwards when you place your foot on the hip. Ensure your shin is parallel to the floor and your knee is pointing slightly upwards. Drive your hips into the opponent to create a strong frame. If pain persists, check your hip mobility and consider strengthening exercises for your hip abductors.

    Q: How can I use Worm Guard effectively against a much larger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on using your hips to create angles and off-balance them. Your shin on hip acts as a powerful lever. Do not rely on brute strength; instead, use your underhook to control their leg and your hips to push their weight away. Prioritize maintaining distance and control over immediate attacks.

    Q: When is the right time to switch from closed guard to Worm Guard?

    The optimal time to transition from closed guard to Worm Guard is when the opponent creates space by posturing up. As they extend their legs and lean forward, you can open your guard and immediately step your foot onto their hip. This timing prevents them from easily passing and sets up the Worm Guard mechanics for sweeps or submissions.

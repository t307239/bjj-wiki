Worm Guard: The White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

PURPLE★★★★☆Advanced
Purple帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 紫帯昇格要件

Worm Guard is an advanced open guard position initiated from a seated or supine stance, aiming to control the opponent's legs and hips for sweeps and submissions.

White belts often struggle with Worm Guard due to a lack of body awareness and a tendency to rely on brute strength, leading to inefficient mechanics and injury.

The core principle of Worm Guard is creating a strong, dynamic frame with your legs and hips to off-balance and manipulate the opponent's base, utilizing leverage over power.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Start from a seated guard, opponent in front of you.
    2. Secure a collar grip with your right hand (near opponent's left collar) and a sleeve grip with your left hand (opponent's right sleeve).
    3. Place your right shin across the opponent's left hip crease, ensuring your knee is slightly higher than your hip.
    4. Hook your left foot behind the opponent's right knee, creating a "figure four" with your legs around their hips.
    5. Shift your hips to your left, creating a 45-degree angle relative to the opponent's centerline.
    6. Extend your right leg slightly, pushing against the opponent's hip to create space and break their base.
    7. Simultaneously, use your left foot hook to pull the opponent's knee towards you, initiating a sweep or transition.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending your knee without hip rotation: This can hyperextend your opponent's knee or cause a direct knee-on-knee impact injury, damaging the MCL or ACL.
    • Gripping too tightly on the collar without proper frame: This puts excessive strain on your own shoulder and wrist joints, risking rotator cuff tears or carpal tunnel syndrome.
    • Allowing opponent to flatten your guard by keeping hips square: This compromises your leverage and can lead to your own hips being stacked, risking lumbar spine compression injuries.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg and hip positioning with no opponent, focusing on smoothness and control. (50 reps per side)
    2. Partner drilling (no resistance): Opponent stands passively, you execute the guard entry and hip shift. (25 reps per side)
    3. Light resistance drilling: Opponent offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the connection and control. (50 reps per side)
    4. Flow rolling (25% resistance): Incorporate Worm Guard into light sparring, focusing on maintaining the position and attempting one sweep or submission per round.
    5. Flow rolling (50% resistance): Increase intensity, focusing on maintaining the Worm Guard against a more active opponent and executing techniques with intent.
    6. Live rolling (75% resistance): Apply Worm Guard in a full sparring scenario, aiming for successful transitions and submissions, accepting occasional failures.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent attempts to pass your closed guard by squaring their hips.
    • Opponent is postured up slightly, creating a gap to enter your legs.
    • You are in a broken closed guard or half guard and can transition to a more dynamic open guard.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass Defense: If the opponent stacks, immediately drive your left foot into their hip and use your right leg to push their knee away, creating space to recover your guard or transition to X-guard.
    • Knee-to-Elbow Pass Defense: If the opponent tries to bring their knee to your elbow, widen your base and use your collar grip to pull their head down, while simultaneously using your leg hook to control their knee.
    • Leg Drag Defense: If the opponent attempts a leg drag, tighten your leg hook and use your hip angle to turn into them, creating a tight frame that prevents them from isolating your leg.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to grip for Worm Guard?

    Wrist pain often stems from gripping too tightly with a bent wrist or gripping the collar with your palm facing upwards. To fix this, maintain a neutral or slightly extended wrist and grip the collar with your thumb on the outside and fingers inside, creating a "cupped" hand. This distributes the pressure more evenly across your forearm and reduces strain on the wrist joint.

    Q: How can I use Worm Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes paramount. Focus on creating a strong hip angle (45 degrees) and using your legs to control their base, not to lift them. Your right shin on their hip should act as a wedge, and your left foot hook behind their knee should be tight. Use your body weight and hip movement to off-balance them, rather than trying to overpower them.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from a standard guard to Worm Guard?

    The optimal time to transition is when the opponent is slightly postured up, creating a small gap between their hips and your legs. This allows for a clean entry of your shin and foot. Avoid attempting Worm Guard when the opponent is already in a deep pass position or has completely flattened your guard, as the entry becomes much more difficult and risky.

Worm Guard is an open guard position initiated from the guard, aiming to control the opponent's hips and legs while creating sweeping or submission opportunities.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body awareness and poor weight distribution, leading to instability and injury.

Success hinges on maintaining a tight, active frame with your legs and hips, preventing the opponent from establishing a strong base and pressure.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a seated or half-guard position, place your right foot on the opponent's left hip crease, ankle facing upwards. This is the 'worm hook'.
    2. Simultaneously, wrap your left arm under the opponent's right thigh, gripping their right pant leg near the knee with your left hand. This is the 'leg grip'.
    3. Your right arm reaches across your body and secures a grip on the opponent's left collar or lapel with your right hand. This is the 'collar grip'.
    4. Maintain a slight inward rotation of your right hip, creating a wedge against the opponent's body, preventing them from posturing up.
    5. Keep your left knee pointed slightly towards the ceiling, actively pulling the opponent's leg towards your chest to maintain distance and control.
    6. Your right leg remains relatively straight but flexible, ready to adjust your base or initiate sweeps.
    7. Shift your weight onto your left hip, creating a strong base and allowing your right leg to extend and 'kick' the opponent's base away, creating space for sweeps or submissions.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending your right leg straight and stiffly can hyperextend the knee joint, leading to ACL or MCL tears.

    The correct movement is to keep the leg slightly bent and use it as a lever, not a rigid post.

    • Gripping the opponent's pant leg too loosely with your left hand allows them to easily step out or pass your guard, risking ankle or knee torque injuries.

    Maintain a firm grip by cupping the pant fabric with your palm and fingers, keeping the opponent's leg close.

    • Trying to 'pull' the opponent down with your collar grip without establishing hip control can lead to your own base collapsing, risking spinal compression or shoulder dislocations.

    Focus on using your hip and leg pressure to control their base before attempting to pull.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice establishing the Worm Guard hooks and grips on a dummy or pillow. Focus on the hip and leg angles. (50 reps)
    2. Assisted drill: Partner stands passively. Practice entering Worm Guard and adjusting grips, focusing on maintaining the hip wedge. (25 reps per side)
    3. Light resistance drill: Partner offers minimal resistance, attempting to step out. Focus on maintaining leg and collar control. (50% resistance, 20 reps per side)
    4. Dynamic entry drill: Partner attempts to pass. Practice entering Worm Guard and immediately using hip pressure to create space. (75% resistance, 15 reps per side)
    5. Sweep progression drill: Enter Worm Guard and practice a basic sweep (e.g., pendulum sweep) against light resistance. (90% resistance, 10 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling: Implement Worm Guard defensively during rolling, focusing on maintaining the position and transitioning to sweeps. (100% resistance, 2 minute rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is posturing up from your closed guard, creating an opportunity to open and establish a leg hook.
    • When the opponent is attempting to pass your open guard and their weight is distributed forward, allowing you to secure leg and hip control.
    • When you are in a half-guard situation and can transition to a more dynamic open guard by hooking a leg and controlling the hip.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: The opponent drives their weight forward, attempting to collapse your guard by stacking you. Counter by immediately releasing the collar grip, widening your base with your right leg, and using your left leg to push their hip away.
    • Knee Slice Pass: The opponent attempts to drive their knee across your body. Counter by retracting your right leg, bringing your knees together to form a tight guard, and using your arms to create frames and push their knee away.
    • Hip Escape and Grip Release: The opponent tries to break your grips and escape. Counter by maintaining active leg pressure, using your hips to shrimp and create space, and being prepared to transition to a different guard if the Worm Guard grip is compromised.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my right hip hurt when I try to do the Worm Guard?

    Your right hip may hurt due to improper hip angling and excessive internal rotation without proper support. You are likely trying to force the position by pushing your opponent away with your leg alone, rather than using your hip as a stable wedge. Ensure your right hip is angled slightly inward, creating a solid base, and that your left leg is actively pulling their leg in to maintain control before attempting to create space with your right leg.

    Q: How can I use Worm Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise timing are crucial. Focus on using your left leg to actively control their base by pulling their leg towards your chest, preventing them from easily driving their weight forward. Your right leg should be used to create distance and off-balance them, rather than trying to muscle them. Prioritize securing a strong collar grip to manage their posture before they can fully engage their weight.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from closed guard to Worm Guard?

    The optimal time to transition from closed guard to Worm Guard is when the opponent attempts to posture up or break your guard by posturing. As they extend their body, you can open your guard and immediately use your right foot to hook their hip and your left arm to grab their pant leg, creating the initial Worm Guard structure before they can reset their base effectively.

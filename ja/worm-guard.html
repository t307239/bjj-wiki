The Worm Guard is an open guard position initiated from the guard, aiming to create off-balancing and submission opportunities by controlling the opponent's leg and hip. White belts often struggle because they lack a foundational understanding of hip movement and weight distribution, leading to easily passed guards. The core insight is leveraging your hip rotation to create dynamic angles and control space, rather than relying on static grips.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a closed guard or half guard, establish a "worm hook" by placing your left foot on the opponent's right hip crease, with your knee pointing slightly towards the ceiling. Your right leg remains free or hooks the opponent's left leg.
    2. Simultaneously, your right hand grips the opponent's left ankle or pant cuff, and your left hand grips the opponent's right collar or shoulder.
    3. Rotate your hips 45 degrees to your left, creating a "C" shape with your body and pushing your left knee away from the opponent's center line.
    4. Extend your right leg slightly, using the ankle grip to "pull" the opponent's leg across your body, breaking their base.
    5. As their weight shifts, use your left collar/shoulder grip to "push" their upper body away and off balance.
    6. Drive your left hip towards the opponent's right hip, creating a tight connection and limiting their ability to posture up or shrimp.
    7. Execute sweeps like the "helicopter sweep" by driving your right foot through their legs and using your hip rotation to lift and roll them, or transition to submissions like a triangle choke by bringing your left leg over their head.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly "pulling" with your arm to break posture: This can lead to elbow hyperextension, damaging the ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) or causing a biceps tear. The correct movement is to use hip rotation and shoulder pressure to create distance and off-balance.
    • Letting your knee collapse inwards when establishing the "worm hook": This puts excessive stress on the ACL and meniscus, risking a tear. Maintain a strong "C" shape with your leg, with the knee pointing slightly up.
    • Arching your back excessively to create space: This can lead to lumbar strain or disc injury. Instead, focus on driving your hips forward and creating angles with your body rotation.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill (0% resistance): Practice the hip rotation and leg hook placement with no opponent. Focus on creating the "C" shape and feeling the hip angle change. 20 reps each side.
    2. Partner drill - light resistance (25% resistance): With a partner lying on their back, practice establishing the worm hook and grip. Focus on the initial off-balancing motion without attempting a sweep. 10 reps each side.
    3. Partner drill - controlled movement (50% resistance): Practice the initial setup and a single sweep (e.g., helicopter sweep) slowly with controlled resistance from the partner. Focus on completing the sweep motion. 5 reps each side.
    4. Partner drill - moderate resistance (75% resistance): Practice the full Worm Guard sequence, including transitions to a basic submission attempt (e.g., triangle choke setup). Partner offers mild defense. 5 reps each side.
    5. Live drilling - positional sparring (90% resistance): Start from a specific guard position (e.g., closed guard) and attempt to establish Worm Guard. Partner actively defends passes. 3 rounds, 3 minutes each.
    6. Live rolling (100% resistance): Integrate Worm Guard into regular sparring, focusing on maintaining the position and seeking submission opportunities or sweeps. 5 rounds, 5 minutes each.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is postured up and has their weight forward, creating an opening to attack their base.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard by stepping their legs wide, exposing their hips and ankles.
    • When you feel your opponent is too heavy or strong to maintain a closed guard, needing a more dynamic open guard.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass: If the opponent drives their weight forward and tries to "stack" you, immediately drive your right foot into their hip and push their leg across your body, creating space to shrimp out or transition to a single leg X guard.
    • Leg Cut Pass: If the opponent attempts to "cut the corner" with their leg, use your worm hook to "pull" their hips towards you while simultaneously "pushing" their upper body away with your other hand, forcing them to reset or opening up a sweep.
    • Knee Slice Pass: If the opponent attempts a knee slice, maintain your hip angle and use your worm hook to prevent them from advancing their knee. Drive your hips into them and use your free leg to create distance or a sweep opportunity.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to grip for Worm Guard?

    Wrist pain often stems from a "death grip" and improper hand positioning. Instead of gripping the opponent's collar or ankle with your fingers clenched tightly, aim for a "power grip" where your thumb wraps around the material. Ensure your wrist is neutral or slightly extended, not bent backwards. Focus on using your forearm and shoulder to create pressure, not just your fingers.

    Q: How can I use Worm Guard against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is key. Focus on creating steep angles with your hips, making yourself a smaller target. Use your worm hook to actively "pull" their weight across your body, disrupting their base. Your grips should aim to control their structure, not their mass; a collar grip combined with an ankle grip can be very effective at controlling their posture and preventing them from simply crushing you.

    Q: When is the right time to transition from closed guard to Worm Guard?

    The ideal transition occurs when the opponent is postured up and their weight is forward, creating an opening. If they are too low and tight, attempting Worm Guard can lead to them easily passing. Look for moments where they try to "break" your closed guard by pushing down on your knees; this is your cue to open your guard and establish the worm hook, using the momentum of their break to initiate your control.

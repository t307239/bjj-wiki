Body Triangle BJJ: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Body Triangle is a control position, typically initiated from closed guard, designed to restrict opponent's movement and set up submissions. It applies constant pressure and limits their ability to posture up.

White belts often struggle because they lack the foundational body mechanics, leading to inefficient control and potential injury. They focus on squeezing rather than strategic weight placement.

The core principle is creating a stable, constricting platform with your legs, allowing your hips to do the work, not just your quadriceps. This transfers your weight effectively.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure opponent's torso with your arms, hips low to the mat.
    2. Untangle one leg, stepping your foot behind the opponent's knee on the same side.
    3. Bring your other leg over the opponent's hip, crossing your ankle over the shin of the trapped leg.
    4. Lock your feet together by placing the instep of your free foot into the crook of your trapped knee.
    5. Ensure your hips are angled approximately 45 degrees away from the opponent's torso.
    6. Drive your heel of the trapped leg down towards your own glute, creating a strong wedge.
    7. Shift your weight onto your hips, pressing your sternum into your opponent's chest to maintain pressure.
    8. Maintain a strong grip on the opponent's collar or shoulders to prevent them from posturing.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Knee Extension: A white belt may fully extend their trapped leg, hyperextending the knee joint. This can cause ligament tears (ACL/MCL) or meniscus damage by placing excessive stress on the knee capsule.
    • Excessive Spinal Flexion: Trying to squeeze the triangle with rounded back instead of hip-driven pressure strains the lumbar spine. This can lead to disc compression and muscle spasms.
    • Foot on Shin Instead of Knee: Placing the foot on the opponent's shin rather than behind their knee sacrifices the biomechanical advantage. This allows the opponent to straighten their leg, creating space and potentially injuring your ankle or Achilles tendon.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Mobility Drill: Perform hip opening and closing exercises for 2 minutes, focusing on the range of motion needed to lock the triangle. 100% resistance.
    2. Body Triangle Lock Drill: On your back, practice locking and unlocking the body triangle on your own legs 20 times. 0% resistance.
    3. Static Body Triangle on Partner: With a compliant partner on their back, practice establishing the body triangle and holding it. Partner remains still. 10 reps, 25% resistance.
    4. Body Triangle with Hip Escape: From the locked body triangle, practice performing a hip escape to create an angle and then returning to the triangle. 10 reps per side, 50% resistance.
    5. Controlled Body Triangle with Escape: Partner attempts to escape the body triangle (e.g., by turning in). You maintain the triangle and adjust. 10 rounds, 75% resistance.
    6. Live Rolling - Body Triangle Focus: During rolling, attempt to establish and maintain the body triangle for at least 10 seconds before transitioning or being swept. 5-minute rounds, 90% resistance.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is in your closed guard and their weight is distributed evenly.
    • Opponent is attempting to posture up from your closed guard, creating space to insert your leg.
    • After successfully transitioning from side control or mount to guard, and the opponent is still on their hands and knees.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: Opponent drives their weight forward, stacking their hips onto your shoulders, attempting to break the triangle by driving your hips down. To counter, maintain strong grips and drive your heels towards your glutes, creating a wedge.
    • Knee to Chest: Opponent drives one knee into your chest/sternum, attempting to create space to break your leg lock. To counter, maintain tightness and use your free hand to control their head or shoulder, preventing them from creating leverage.
    • Hip Escape and Turn In: Opponent hip escapes away and turns their body into the trapped leg, attempting to relieve pressure. To counter, use your free leg to push their hip away and maintain your 45-degree angle, re-establishing pressure.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my hip hurt when I try to do the body triangle in BJJ?

    Your hip pain is likely due to improper hip angulation. You are probably trying to lock the triangle with your hips square to the opponent. Instead, angle your hips 45 degrees away from them and drive your heel of the trapped leg towards your own glute. This biomechanical shift allows your pelvis to support the weight, not just your hip flexors.

    Q: How can I use the body triangle effectively against a much larger BJJ opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on maximizing the constricting pressure. Ensure your foot is locked firmly into the crook of your knee, creating a solid cage. Use your grips on their collar and shoulders to pull their upper body down, preventing them from using their size to simply push through. Your hips should be actively driving *into* them, not just sitting back.

    Q: The body triangle can be a valuable tool for beginners if learned with correct biomechanics. While it offers good control, the risk of injury is higher if performed incorrectly, especially to your knees and back. Focus on the precise foot placement and hip angulation described in this guide to mitigate risks and build a solid foundation.

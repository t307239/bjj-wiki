Body Triangle BJJ: Your First Step to Positional Dominance

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Body Triangle is a positional control technique primarily initiated from Closed Guard, aiming to constrict the opponent's torso and limit their movement for submissions or transitions.

White belts often struggle because they focus on squeezing with leg strength alone, neglecting the crucial hip and core engagement required for stability.

The key insight is to use your hips to actively drive into the opponent's hip socket, creating a stable, inescapable wedge that leverages your entire body's structure.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From Closed Guard, establish a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand and a sleeve grip (same-side sleeve) with your left hand.
    2. Drive your hips forward, lifting your opponent's weight onto your lap while simultaneously bringing your right knee across their chest.
    3. Secure your right foot by tucking it behind your left shin, creating a figure-four leg lock around your opponent's waist.
    4. Extend your left leg, placing your left foot firmly against your right shin, locking the triangle shape around their torso.
    5. Actively squeeze your knees together, but more importantly, drive your right hip bone into their hip bone, creating a powerful fulcrum.
    6. Your weight should be shifted forward, over your opponent's hips, not resting on your heels.
    7. Maintain a tight frame by keeping your back straight and your elbows tucked in, preventing them from posturing up or creating space.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect knee pressure: Forcing your right knee directly into the opponent's sternum can compress their diaphragm, causing discomfort and potentially leading to rib injuries or breathing difficulties. Instead, aim to drive your hip into their hip socket.
    • Over-reliance on leg squeeze: Squeezing purely with leg adductors without hip drive can strain the hamstring and groin muscles, leading to tears. Focus on hip-driven pressure and pelvic tilt.
    • Neck hyperextension: When attempting to finish a submission from the Body Triangle, arching your back excessively to create leverage can strain your lumbar spine. Maintain a neutral spine and use your hips for power.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Drill (0% resistance): Practice the leg-locking motion without an opponent. Focus on the hip drive and foot tuck. 20 repetitions.
    2. Partner Drill - Static Hug (25% resistance): Your partner lies on their back. You practice entering the Body Triangle, focusing on hip placement and locking the legs. Partner remains passive. 10 repetitions.
    3. Partner Drill - Light Pressure (50% resistance): Partner tries to create minimal space by lightly pushing. You maintain the Body Triangle with hip drive. Partner applies no counter-pressure. 10 repetitions.
    4. Partner Drill - Hip Escape Resistance (75% resistance): Partner attempts to hip escape or create frames. You practice adjusting your hip angle and maintaining the squeeze. 5 repetitions.
    5. Flow Roll - Guard Retention (90% resistance): Practice maintaining the Body Triangle position against a partner who is actively trying to pass your guard or escape. Focus on transitions. 5 minutes.
    6. Live Rolling (100% resistance): Integrate the Body Triangle into live sparring. Focus on achieving and maintaining the position, then transitioning to submissions or sweeps. 5 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have your opponent in your Closed Guard and they are postured up.
    • When your opponent attempts a guard pass and you can invert or transition to a strong hip control.
    • As a setup for submissions like the Triangle Choke or Armbar from Closed Guard.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee-on-Belly Transition: If the opponent attempts to stack you, drive your left knee into their hip and roll to your right, coming up to Knee-on-Belly.
    • Hip Escape and Frame: The opponent can try to hip escape to create space. You must immediately drive your hips forward and maintain the wedge, preventing their escape.
    • Postural Break and Submission: The opponent can try to break your posture by reaching for your arms or head. Use this to your advantage by pulling them down and securing a submission like a Triangle Choke.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my hip hurt when I try to do a Body Triangle in BJJ?

    Hip pain often results from trying to force the lock solely with leg strength. The Body Triangle requires your hips to drive forward and create a stable wedge against your opponent's hip socket. Ensure you're actively using your glutes and core to drive your right hip bone into their hip bone, rather than just squeezing your knees together.

    Q: How can I use the Body Triangle against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise mechanics are paramount. Focus on driving your hips forward to off-balance them and create a tight wedge. Use your right foot to hook your left shin, then drive your left heel into your right shin to lock the triangle. Your core and hip drive will be your primary tools for control, not raw leg strength.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from Body Triangle to a submission in BJJ?

    The ideal time to transition to a submission is when your opponent is trapped and their posture is broken, making them unable to defend effectively. Once the Body Triangle is locked and you have a stable hip connection, look for opportunities to secure a Triangle Choke by attacking their neck or an Armbar by isolating an arm. Ensure you maintain hip pressure throughout the transition.

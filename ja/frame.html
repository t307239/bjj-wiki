Mastering the BJJ Frame: A White Belt's Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Frame is a defensive posture used to create space and prevent an opponent from advancing their position or applying submissions. It’s typically initiated from guard or when being passed.

White belts often fail because they lack the precise body mechanics, relying on brute strength or incorrect angles, leading to ineffective defense and injury.

The core concept is using your limbs as rigid levers, controlled by your core, to push and redirect your opponent’s weight and momentum away from your body.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish forearm-to-forearm grip on the opponent's biceps, thumbs pointing up.
    2. Simultaneously, place your feet on the opponent's hips with your soles flat, knees bent at 90 degrees.
    3. Shift your hips back slightly, creating a 45-degree angle between your torso and the opponent's torso.
    4. Extend your arms, keeping elbows slightly bent, pushing directly into the opponent's biceps.
    5. Arch your back slightly, engaging your glutes and core to drive your hips forward.
    6. Apply pressure with your feet on the hips, aiming to drive them away from your torso.
    7. Maintain a wide base with your feet on the hips, preventing them from collapsing inward.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Straightening arms completely and pushing with only hands: This hyperextends the elbow joint, risking ligament tears (ulnar collateral ligament).
    • Bending at the hips and letting the opponent stack: This collapses the frame, allowing the opponent to pass, and can lead to knee or ankle injuries from awkward pressure.
    • Gripping too loosely or only on the gi material: This allows the opponent to easily break the grip, negating the frame and potentially leading to armbar injuries if the arm is left exposed.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice extending and retracting arms, and pushing hips out while lying on your back, focusing on core engagement. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): Partner lies on their back, you practice establishing frames and pushing them away, focusing on grip and body angle. (10 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner gently tries to advance their position, you practice maintaining frames and hip movement. (10 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner applies light pressure to pass, you focus on creating space with frames and hip escapes. (10 reps per side)
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner applies moderate pressure to pass, focus on using frames to reset guard or create distance. (5 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Focus on implementing frames whenever an opponent tries to pass your guard or advance position. (1-minute rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When an opponent is attempting to pass your guard (e.g., knee slice, torreando).
    • When an opponent is postured up in your guard and trying to break your grip.
    • When you are in side control and need to create space to recover guard.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Guard Recovery: Use frames to push opponent's hips away, creating space to bring your knees back in and re-establish closed guard.
    • Hip Escape: Use frames in conjunction with hip movement to shrimp out from under an opponent in side control or mount.
    • Creating Distance for Submission Attempts: Use frames to push opponent away, then immediately transition to a sweep or submission.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my elbow hurt when I try to Frame in BJJ?

    Elbow pain often occurs when you fully hyperextend your arm, straightening it completely. The frame should be a rigid lever, not a straight line. Maintain a slight bend in the elbow, engaging your triceps and shoulder to create a stable structure. Focus on pushing with your entire arm and shoulder, not just your hand.

    Q: How do I Frame against a much bigger BJJ opponent when they are passing my guard?

    Against a larger opponent, the frame becomes even more crucial. Instead of just pushing with your arms, use your legs on their hips as your primary frame. Keep your feet wide and press your heels into their hip bones. Simultaneously, use your forearms to control their biceps or shoulders, pushing their weight to the side and creating angles to escape or recover guard.

    Q: When should I stop using Frames and try to attack in BJJ?

    Frames are primarily defensive tools. You should transition from framing to attacking when you have successfully created significant space and an advantageous angle. For example, after framing to push an opponent away and break their posture, you might immediately transition to a sweep like the scissor sweep or a submission like a triangle choke if they overcommit.

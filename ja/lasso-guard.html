Lasso Guard originates from open guard, aiming to control an opponent's arm and posture for sweeps and submissions.

White belts often struggle due to lack of fundamental hip control and frame strength.

The key is to use your own body as a lever, creating angles and preventing your opponent from establishing a strong base.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Sit with your back on the mat, knees bent, feet flat. Your opponent is in front of you.
    2. Secure a collar grip on your opponent's gi with your right hand, palm facing you.
    3. Simultaneously, thread your left arm *under* your opponent's right arm, reaching for their right sleeve grip.
    4. With your left hand, establish a strong sleeve grip on your opponent's right sleeve, palm facing down.
    5. Now, bring your left leg up and over your opponent's right arm, positioning your shin across their upper back/shoulder blade.
    6. Your left foot should hook *behind* your opponent's right elbow, creating tension.
    7. Simultaneously, drive your hips slightly forward and to your left, creating a strong frame with your left leg.
    8. Your right hand (collar grip) pulls your opponent's collar down and slightly to your right, breaking their posture.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to pull the opponent's arm directly towards you with your left arm while your leg is in lasso position: This can hyperextend your opponent's elbow, risking ligament tears (UCL/MCL).

    The correct movement is to use your leg to create the lasso tension and your body to angle them, not brute arm force.

    • Allowing your opponent to stack you by driving their weight straight down into your hips without maintaining hip angle: This can cause lower back compression injuries.

    Maintain a slight forward hip thrust and keep your body angled away from their direct line of pressure.

    • Gripping the sleeve too loosely or allowing it to slip: This compromises the lasso's structure and can lead to your arm being trapped or attacked.

    Ensure a firm, consistent sleeve grip throughout the process.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg over, hook behind elbow, and hip angle movement without a partner. (10 reps per side)
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): Partner stands passively. Practice entering the lasso guard, securing grips, and establishing the leg hook. (5 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to posture control. Focus on maintaining the lasso connection and breaking their posture. (5 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner attempts to pass or posture up. Focus on maintaining the lasso and using it to create angles for a sweep attempt. (5 reps per side)
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner actively tries to pass or remove the lasso. Focus on defensive framing and re-establishing the lasso if lost. (3 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Implement Lasso Guard in a rolling scenario, focusing on control and sweep opportunities. (2-minute rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is posturing up while you are in open guard, creating an opportunity to trap their arm.
    • When your opponent tries to pass your guard by stepping their leg over your hips, allowing you to intercept their arm.
    • When your opponent is applying pressure from the top and you need to create space and control their posture to transition.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stacking Pass: Opponent drives their weight directly forward, attempting to crush your hips. Maintain your hip angle and use your free leg to push their hip away, creating space.
    • Arm Release: Opponent attempts to pull their trapped arm out by straightening it. Use your body to maintain the angle and your collar grip to disrupt their posture, preventing them from creating leverage.
    • Knee Slice Pass: Opponent drives their knee across your body. Circle your hips away from the incoming knee and use your lasso leg to control their hip, preventing them from flattening you.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to get into Lasso Guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs if you're gripping the sleeve too tightly with your fingers extended, or if you're trying to pull the opponent's arm with just your forearm muscles. Ensure your grip is firm but relaxed, and focus on using your whole body's leverage, especially your hips and the lasso leg, to create the control, rather than isolated wrist strength.

    Q: How do I use Lasso Guard against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, the biomechanics of Lasso Guard become even more critical. Focus on creating sharp angles with your hips and using your lasso leg to control their hip mobility, preventing them from simply driving forward. Your collar grip should be used to break their posture and pull them off balance, making their size less of an advantage.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from Closed Guard to Lasso Guard?

    The optimal transition from Closed Guard to Lasso Guard occurs when your opponent attempts to posture up significantly. As they extend their arms to create space or break your grip, this is your opportunity to open your guard, bring your leg over their arm, and secure the lasso grip while they are off balance.

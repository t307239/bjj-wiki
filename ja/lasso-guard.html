Lasso Guard originates from open guard, aiming to control one of your opponent's arms and legs simultaneously to set up sweeps and submissions.

Many white belts struggle by overextending or creating insufficient structure, leading to broken grips and passes.

The core principle is creating a tight, constricting frame with your arm around their arm, anchored by your leg, preventing them from posting or posturing.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, break your opponent's posture by pulling them slightly forward, creating space.
    2. Establish a collar grip with your right hand, palm facing upwards, thumb inside the collar.
    3. Simultaneously, with your left arm, reach across and secure a grip on your opponent's right sleeve, specifically at the wrist or forearm.
    4. Swing your right leg up and over your opponent's right arm, aiming to place the back of your right knee near their right shoulder socket.
    5. As your right leg comes over, immediately use your left arm (holding the sleeve) to pull their arm across your body, creating a "lasso" effect.
    6. Tighten your right leg around their arm by flexing your hamstring and gluteal muscles, while simultaneously pulling their sleeve grip closer to your chest.
    7. Adjust your hips, tilting them slightly to your left, creating a stronger frame and preventing your opponent from easily posturing up or stepping their leg out.
    8. Maintain a strong frame with your left forearm against their bicep to further control their arm's angle and prevent them from straightening it.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending your right leg too far forward, placing excessive hyperextension stress on your knee's ACL and PCL ligaments, leading to potential tears. The correct movement is to keep the knee slightly bent and focus on hip flexion and hamstring engagement.
    • Gripping the opponent's sleeve too loosely or with an open palm, allowing them to easily pull their arm out and break the lasso. This can lead to shoulder impingement if you try to force the grip. Always secure a firm grip, ideally around the forearm or wrist, with your thumb wrapped.
    • Attempting to "pull hard" with your lasso arm while your hips are flat, which disconnects your base and allows the opponent to stack or pass. Instead, use your hips to create leverage and pull their arm into your body's structure.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg swing and grip acquisition motion without an opponent. (50 reps per side, 0% resistance).
    2. Partner drilling (static): Your partner stands, you establish Lasso Guard from closed guard. Focus on grip and leg placement. (20 reps per side, 10% resistance).
    3. Partner drilling (light movement): Partner offers minimal resistance to posture. Practice maintaining the lasso and hip angle. (30 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Transition drilling: Practice entering Lasso Guard from a failed closed guard or when your partner attempts to pass. (25 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Sweep attempts (controlled): With a compliant partner, practice initiating a sweep from Lasso Guard. Focus on the sweep mechanics. (20 reps per side, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling (positional sparring): Start in closed guard and attempt to establish Lasso Guard. Focus on maintaining the guard and setting up sweeps/attacks. (5 rounds, 90% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent maintains a strong, upright posture from your closed guard, making it difficult to attack directly.
    • When your opponent attempts to "stack" you from closed guard, creating an opportunity to swing your leg over their arm.
    • When transitioning from closed guard to open guard, and you can quickly secure the lasso before they establish their base.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Posture Up and Grip Break: The opponent can attempt to straighten their posture by driving their hips back and using their free arm to break your sleeve grip, then immediately step their leg out.
    • Shoulder Pressure and Stack: The opponent can drive their shoulder into your chest and attempt to "stack" you, forcing your hips flat and breaking the lasso. This requires immediate hip escape and frame creation.
    • Leg Escape and Base Reinforcement: The opponent can attempt to pull their trapped arm out by stepping their leg back and widening their base, forcing you to re-establish the lasso or transition.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to Lasso Guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs from gripping the sleeve too loosely or with an open palm. This allows the opponent's force to travel up your arm and into your wrist, causing hyperextension or strain. Ensure a firm grip around their forearm or wrist, with your thumb wrapped securely, and use your body's structure, not just arm strength, to control their limb.

    Q: How do I Lasso Guard against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on precise hip angles to create space, preventing them from simply crushing you. Use your leg as a strong frame, keeping the knee slightly bent and flexing your hamstring to create tension. Your collar grip should pull them into your frame, not just pull them towards you.

    Q: When should I transition from Lasso Guard to another guard?

    You should transition if your opponent successfully breaks your lasso grip or establishes a strong, stable base that prevents you from generating leverage. If they manage to isolate one of your legs or start passing effectively, it's time to switch to a more defensive guard like half-guard or re-establish closed guard.

Lasso Guard BJJ: A White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

Lasso Guard is an open guard position where one leg wraps around the opponent's arm, creating a powerful control point. It aims to break down their posture and set up sweeps or submissions.

Many white belts struggle by relying on brute strength or poor body alignment, leading to ineffective control and injury. Understanding the leverage is crucial.

The key insight is using your hip and leg as a fulcrum to control the opponent's posture, not just the arm itself.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From open guard, establish a deep collar grip with your right hand (if right-legged Lasso).
    2. With your left hand, secure a sleeve grip on the opponent's right arm.
    3. Swing your right leg up, aiming to place the outside of your shin across the opponent's right shoulder and upper chest.
    4. Simultaneously, pull the opponent's right arm across their body towards your left hip, creating tension.
    5. Your left foot should be flat on the mat, creating a base, with your knee pointing slightly away from the opponent.
    6. Tighten your right leg's grip by flexing your hamstring and glute, drawing your heel towards your own buttock.
    7. Use your hips to shrimp slightly left, angling your body to create a strong lever against the opponent's shoulder and arm.
    8. Maintain a strong collar grip to prevent them from posturing up, and keep your left sleeve grip to manage their arm's angle.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending your Lasso leg: This can hyperextend the opponent's shoulder joint, risking rotator cuff tears or dislocations.
    • The correct movement is to keep the Lasso leg bent and actively flexed, using your hamstring to create pressure.
    • Over-extending your own back trying to pull the opponent down: This puts immense strain on your lumbar spine, risking disc herniation or muscle pulls.
    • Instead, use your hips to shrimp and create the angle for leverage, rather than a direct pulling motion with your upper body.
    • Letting your Lasso leg fall slack and allowing the opponent to posture: This creates an opening for them to pass and can lead to knee or ankle injuries if they step over.
    • Actively engage your hamstring and glute to maintain constant tension on the Lasso leg, keeping it tight around their arm and shoulder.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling (0%): Practice the leg and hip movement of establishing the Lasso guard without a partner. Focus on the feel of your shin across the shoulder. (20 reps)
    2. Partner drill - Gripping (25%): With a compliant partner, practice establishing the collar and sleeve grips and swinging your leg into the Lasso position. Partner offers minimal resistance. (10 reps)
    3. Partner drill - Leg placement and tension (50%): Focus on correctly placing your shin and creating tension with your hamstring and glute. Partner resists slightly by trying to pull their arm out. (15 reps)
    4. Partner drill - Hip angle (75%): Practice shrimping and angling your hips while maintaining the Lasso. Partner actively tries to posture up, but allows you to maintain control. (20 reps)
    5. Guard retention with Lasso (90%): Partner attempts to pass the guard, and you use the Lasso to maintain distance and control, setting up a sweep. (3 minutes, 5 rounds)
    6. Live rolling (100%): Implement Lasso Guard during rolling, focusing on maintaining the position and attempting sweeps or submissions. (5 minutes, 2 rounds)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is postured up in your closed guard, trying to break your grips.
    • Opponent is attempting to pass your open guard from a distance, giving you space to establish the leg hook.
    • You have successfully transitioned from another guard (e.g., spider guard) and see an opportunity to secure the Lasso.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stacking Pass: Opponent drives forward, trying to stack you. Counter by shrimping your hips out and turning your body away, keeping the Lasso leg tight to prevent them from collapsing on you.
    • Arm Drag to Pass: Opponent attempts an arm drag on the Lassoed arm. Counter by pulling their far arm across your body and using your Lasso leg to control their posture, transitioning to a sweep or submission.
    • Standing Up and Pulling the Arm Out: Opponent tries to pull their arm free by standing. Counter by maintaining a strong collar grip and using your Lasso leg to pull their arm across your body, initiating a sweep.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to Lasso Guard?

    Wrist pain in Lasso Guard often stems from gripping too tightly with your hand in a pronated (palm down) position or from the opponent resisting by pulling their arm straight. To fix this, ensure your collar grip is firm but not death-like, and try to cup the collar with your thumb wrapped around. When establishing the Lasso, focus on creating leverage with your leg and hip, rather than solely relying on a powerful wrist pull.

    Q: How can I use Lasso Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is even more critical. Focus on using your hips to create angles and break their posture. Instead of trying to pull them down directly, use your Lasso leg to hook their shoulder and pull their arm across their body, forcing them to lean into your frame. Your opposite foot should be firmly planted on their hip to maintain distance and create a strong base for your lever.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from Closed Guard to Lasso Guard?

    The ideal time to transition from Closed Guard to Lasso Guard is when the opponent is postured up and attempting to break your guard. As they push away to create space, you can open your guard, swing one leg up to secure the Lasso, and simultaneously establish a collar grip. This transition capitalizes on their forward pressure to create the necessary space for your leg to enter.

ブラジリアン柔術のストレートアームバーを極める

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This technique is primarily initiated from closed guard, aiming to isolate and hyperextend the opponent's elbow joint for a submission.

White belts often struggle due to insufficient control, leading to their opponent escaping before the arm is properly isolated or the correct angle is achieved.

The key is maintaining hip control and creating a "figure four" grip with your legs to prevent the opponent from posturing up or turning their arm.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure your opponent's right arm with your left arm's bicep across their tricep and your left hand gripping your right wrist (figure four arm grip).
    2. Simultaneously, your right leg hooks behind your opponent's left shoulder, and your left leg hooks behind their right hip, creating a "figure four" leg grip.
    3. Shift your hips slightly to your left, angling your body at approximately 45 degrees away from your opponent's trapped arm.
    4. Bring your right knee towards your chest, ensuring it stays tight against your opponent's back to prevent them from posturing up.
    5. With your left hand, reach across and grip your own right shin, further tightening your leg control and stabilizing your base.
    6. Extend your hips upwards, driving your pelvis towards your opponent's armpit while simultaneously pulling your right heel towards your glutes.
    7. Maintain constant pressure with your legs to keep the opponent's body anchored and their arm extended, applying the hyperextension at the elbow joint.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly attempting to "pull hard" on the opponent's arm without proper hip and leg control can lead to the opponent escaping by turning their body or "stacking" you, risking a shoulder impingement or rotator cuff strain.
    • Rushing the hip shift and not establishing a strong leg "figure four" can allow the opponent to straighten their arm, potentially causing a biceps tear or elbow ligament damage if not controlled.
    • Failing to keep your right knee tight to the opponent's back while extending your hips allows them to "bridge" their hips and escape the submission, potentially causing a low back strain for you.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the hip and leg "figure four" positioning and the arm "figure four" grip without a partner. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the entire sequence of establishing grips and body positioning. (20 reps)
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to your grip establishment and hip movement. Focus on smooth transitions. (15 reps)
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner attempts to defend by posturing up or turning slightly, requiring you to adjust your control. (10 reps)
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner actively attempts to escape, focusing on maintaining your control and applying the submission mechanics. (5 reps)
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Attempt the Straight Armbar during rolling, prioritizing correct mechanics over immediate submission. (3 attempts per roll)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have your opponent in closed guard and they attempt to "stack" you, creating an opportunity to isolate an arm.
    • When your opponent extends an arm to create space or frame against your chest from closed guard.
    • When transitioning from a failed sweep or guard pass attempt, you can catch an opponent's arm.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Escape by "stacking": If the attacker's hips are not tight, bridge your hips and drive your shoulders into their chest to break their leg control and escape.
    • "Turn" into the attacker: If the attacker's hip angle is not sufficient, turn your body towards their hips, releasing the pressure on your elbow and potentially reversing the position.
    • "Bite" the elbow: Control the attacker's controlling arm with your free hand and pull your elbow down towards your hip, creating a tighter "frame" and preventing hyperextension.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my elbow hurt when I try to do a Straight Armbar from closed guard?

    Your elbow likely hurts because you are not properly isolating the arm. This often happens when your leg "figure four" is loose, allowing your opponent to straighten their arm and put pressure on the joint. Ensure your hips are angled correctly and your legs are creating a tight "box" around their torso and arm to prevent them from escaping.

    Q: How can I effectively apply a Straight Armbar against a much bigger and stronger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, precise mechanics are paramount. Focus on "slicing" your hips to the side (45-degree angle) to create leverage, and ensure your "figure four" leg grip is extremely tight, especially your knee against their back. Use their weight against them by driving your hips upwards as you pull your heel towards your glutes.

    Q: When should I transition to a Straight Armbar instead of trying another sweep from closed guard?

    You should consider transitioning when your opponent "posts" an arm to defend your sweep or pass attempt, or when they try to "stack" you. These actions often leave an arm extended and vulnerable. Instead of continuing with a potentially low-percentage sweep, use the opportunity to isolate that arm for the submission.

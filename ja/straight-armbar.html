Master the Straight Armbar in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This technique, applied from closed guard, aims to hyperextend the opponent's elbow joint, forcing a tap.

White belts often rush, failing to control the opponent's posture and hips, leading to escapes or failed submissions.

The key is isolating the arm and creating a fulcrum with your hips, leveraging your body weight against their elbow.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure an "over-under" grip on the opponent's arm: your right hand grips their left wrist, your left hand grips their left bicep. Your body is slightly to their left.
    2. Untuck your right leg and swing it over their head, keeping your right foot on the mat for base. Your left leg remains hooked inside their right leg.
    3. Immediately sit up, pulling your opponent's posture down towards your chest. Your hips should be elevated off the mat.
    4. Shift your weight to your left hip, bringing your right hip closer to their shoulder. Your left leg acts as a pivot point.
    5. Swing your right leg over their face, securing a "grapevine" with your ankles crossed behind their back. Your right knee should be near their ear.
    6. Extend your hips upwards and slightly back. This action creates the leverage against their elbow.
    7. Maintain your grip: right hand continues to control the wrist, left hand provides upward pressure on the bicep, preventing them from straightening their arm.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Rushing the hip extension before isolating the arm: This can cause a "pop" in the opponent's shoulder or elbow if the arm is not properly secured, leading to ligament tears (e.g., rotator cuff or UCL).
    • Not controlling the opponent's posture: If the opponent remains upright, they can stack you, potentially injuring your own spine or neck as your base collapses.
    • Gripping too loosely or releasing early: This allows the opponent to pull their arm out, negating the submission and potentially leading to a wrist or elbow hyperextension injury if they yank it out forcefully.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling of leg and hip movement from closed guard without a partner (5 reps per side).
    2. Partner drilling of the grip setup and leg over the head without resistance (10 reps per side, 10% resistance).
    3. Drilling the hip elevation and extension with a compliant partner, focusing on the feeling of leverage (15 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Practicing the full technique with a partner who offers light defense, focusing on maintaining control and hip position (20 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Incorporating the armbar into positional sparring from closed guard, allowing for escapes and counters (5 minutes, 75% resistance).
    6. Attempting the armbar during live rolling, prioritizing technique and safety over completion (10 minutes, 90% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent postures up significantly from your closed guard, creating an opening.
    • After successfully sweeping or transitioning to a dominant position where you can isolate an arm.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard and leaves an arm exposed.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stacking: The opponent drives their weight forward, attempting to crush you and straighten their arm. You counter by keeping your hips low and resisting the forward pressure, or by releasing the armbar and reguarding.
    • Arm Pull-out: The opponent attempts to pull their arm out by straightening it and rotating their wrist. You counter by tightening your grip and hip extension, or by transitioning to a different submission like a triangle choke if they expose their neck.
    • Re-guarding: The opponent escapes the armbar by bridging and rolling or by creating space. You counter by maintaining your connection and either re-securing the armbar or transitioning to a dominant side control or mount.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do a Straight Armbar from closed guard?

    Wrist pain often occurs if your grip on the opponent's wrist is not secure. Ensure your palm is flat against their palm and your fingers are wrapped firmly around their wrist. If you are gripping their gi, it reduces control and can lead to twisting forces on your own wrist when attempting to extend.

    Q: How can a white belt successfully apply a Straight Armbar against a bigger opponent from closed guard?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on leverage and hip elevation. Don't try to overpower them. Instead, use your hips to create a strong fulcrum against their elbow. Ensure their posture is broken down before initiating the hip extension, and keep your legs tight around their head and body to prevent them from using their size advantage.

    Q: Is it okay to 'pull hard' to finish the Straight Armbar, or should I just extend my hips?

    You should never 'pull hard' with your arms to finish an armbar. The submission comes from the extension of your hips, creating a lever with your body against the opponent's elbow. Your grips are for control and to prevent them from escaping; the force should originate from your core and hips, not your arms.

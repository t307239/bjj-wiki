Mastering the Double Under Pass: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Double Under Pass is a fundamental guard pass initiated from standing or kneeling, aiming to secure a dominant side-control position by controlling both of the opponent's legs with your arms.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body awareness, leading to ineffective pressure and potential injury when attempting to force the pass.

The key insight is using your head and shoulder pressure to drive through the opponent's legs, not just your arms, creating a powerful forward momentum.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a kneeling or staggered stance, establish a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a cross-sleeve grip with your left hand on the opponent's gi.
    2. Lower your hips by bending your knees to approximately 90 degrees, ensuring your pelvis is directly over your knees.
    3. Drive your head down and slightly to the left, making contact with the opponent's upper chest/shoulder area.
    4. Simultaneously, step your right foot forward, positioning it slightly ahead of your left foot, creating a staggered base.
    5. With your left arm, reach through the opponent's legs and secure a grip on their far hip or pants, aiming for the hip bone for maximum leverage.
    6. Drive your hips forward and down, transferring your weight into the opponent, aiming to flatten them onto their back.
    7. As you drive, pull with your right hand (collar grip) to break their posture and use your left arm (hip grip) to control their lower body's rotation.
    8. Continue driving forward, allowing your right leg to follow your left, creating a forward-moving base and securing side control as you pass.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly driving forward with straight legs: This can hyperextend the knees, causing ligament damage (ACL/MCL) or patellar tendonitis. The correct alternative is to maintain bent knees throughout the drive.
    • Reaching too deep with the arm for the hip grip without head pressure: This exposes the shoulder to potential injury from armbars or kimuras, and also weakens the passing leverage. Maintain head and shoulder pressure first, then secure the grip.
    • Trying to 'pull' the opponent's legs apart with the arms: This is biomechanically inefficient and can strain the elbows and biceps, risking tendonitis. Instead, use your body weight and head pressure to drive *through* their legs.

    ドリル段階

    1. Shadow drilling: Practice the footwork, hip movement, and head pressure without a partner. Focus on maintaining a low center of gravity. (10 reps, 0% resistance).
    2. Mirror drilling with a partner holding a neutral guard: Partner maintains a passive guard. You practice the grips, hip drive, and head pressure, feeling the contact. (10 reps, 25% resistance).
    3. Partner provides light resistance: Partner attempts to maintain their guard but does not actively resist the pass. Focus on driving through and securing side control. (15 reps, 50% resistance).
    4. Partner actively tries to recompose guard: Partner makes minor adjustments to their guard. You focus on maintaining pressure and adjusting your drive to overcome their slight movements. (20 reps, 75% resistance).
    5. Controlled sparring with a focus on passing: You are allowed to attempt guard passes, including the double under. Partner defends, but the goal is to pass, not to submit. (5 minutes, 90% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the double under pass into your regular sparring rounds, looking for opportunities to apply it naturally. (5 minutes, 100% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is actively trying to close their guard but their knees are slightly apart.
    • When the opponent attempts an open guard sweep and their legs become momentarily accessible.
    • When the opponent is posturing up from a closed guard, creating a small window to attack the legs.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Inside Heel Hook: When the opponent drives forward, they can grip your ankle, step over your hip, and attack the heel hook. The defense is to immediately bring your knee towards your chest and create distance.
    • Re-guarding with a Hip Escape: If the pass is slow, the opponent can shrimp their hips away, creating space to bring their knee back in and re-establish guard. The defense is to maintain tight pressure and prevent the hip escape.
    • Toreando Counter: If the passer's base is too narrow, the opponent can use their legs to push the passer's hips away and bridge, potentially sweeping or re-guarding. The defense is to keep your base wide and maintain forward pressure.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Double Under Pass?

    Knee pain often stems from hyperextending your legs during the forward drive. Ensure your knees remain bent at approximately 90 degrees throughout the pass, like a sprinter's starting stance. This keeps your weight low and protects your knee joints from excessive stress and potential ligament damage.

    Q: How can I effectively use the Double Under Pass against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is key. Focus on controlling their posture with your collar grip and driving your head into their chest to break their base. Instead of trying to muscle through, use your body weight and a tight hip grip to pivot and drive *around* their legs, rather than directly through them. Ensure your hips are lower than theirs.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate the Double Under Pass in BJJ?

    The optimal time to initiate the Double Under Pass is when the opponent's legs are slightly separated, and their base is compromised, such as during an attempt to close their guard or transition between guards. Avoid attempting it when their guard is tightly closed, as you'll be met with strong resistance and risk getting stuck.

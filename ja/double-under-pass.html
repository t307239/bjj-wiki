Double Under Pass: The White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This pass begins from a standing or kneeling position when the opponent attempts to establish closed guard or half guard.

It aims to bypass the opponent's legs entirely and achieve side control or mount.

White belts often struggle by trying to force the pass with raw strength, neglecting proper weight distribution and hip pressure.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Secure a staggered stance with your lead foot slightly forward of the opponent's hip.
    2. Establish a strong cross-collar grip with your lead hand and a cross-body grip (e.g., on the opponent's hip or belt) with your trail hand.
    3. Drop your hips low, ensuring your weight is distributed over your lead foot and trail knee, which should be on the mat.
    4. Initiate a slight backward pull on the collar grip while simultaneously driving your lead shoulder forward and down towards the opponent's hip.
    5. As the opponent's guard opens slightly, step your trail leg forward, placing your knee on the mat parallel to your lead knee.
    6. Simultaneously, drive your hips forward and down, tucking your head to the side of the opponent's chest.
    7. Pierce through the opponent's legs with your chest and hips, maintaining chest-to-chest pressure and driving forward into side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Lunging forward with straight legs: This can hyperextend the knee joint, leading to ACL or MCL tears.
    • Gripping too high on the collar and pulling straight up: This can strain the shoulder joint and rotator cuff, causing impingement.
    • Allowing the opponent to maintain a strong hip connection: This prevents hip penetration and can lead to lumbar spine strain when attempting to force movement.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo movement: Practice the hip-lowering and shoulder-driving motion without a partner (20 reps).
    2. Partner drill (static): Opponent lies on their back, you practice the grip and hip penetration without guard engagement (10 reps per side).
    3. Partner drill (dynamic, no resistance): Opponent presents a loose guard, you execute the pass with minimal resistance (15 reps per side).
    4. Partner drill (light resistance): Opponent attempts to frame and resist slightly, you focus on maintaining pressure and hip drive (25 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    5. Flow rolling: Integrate the pass into controlled rolling scenarios, focusing on timing and anticipation (5 minutes, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the pass during full sparring, prioritizing technique over outcome (10 minutes, 90% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent has a loose or broken closed guard.
    • When the opponent attempts to establish a weak half guard.
    • When the opponent is attempting to shrimp away and create space.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Frame with forearms: Opponent places forearms between their hips and your chest, pushing you away to maintain distance.
    • Hip escape and re-guard: Opponent shrimps their hips to the side, creating space to re-establish guard.
    • Underhook and bridge: Opponent secures an underhook on your arm and bridges to create an escape angle.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Double Under Pass?

    Your knee pain likely stems from entering the pass with straight legs and insufficient hip penetration. When you lunge forward without bending your knees and driving your hips, your knees absorb the impact and can hyperextend. To fix this, always drop your hips low and ensure your knees are bent and close to the mat as you drive through.

    Q: How can I use the Double Under Pass against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on timing and leverage rather than brute force. Instead of trying to force your way through, wait for a slight opening in their guard. Utilize your collar grip to break their posture and create that opening, then drive your hips forward with precise weight transfer. Speed and angled entry are more crucial than raw power.

    Q: I feel like my Double Under Pass is too slow and my opponent always recovers. What am I missing?

    The key to a fast Double Under Pass is the immediate transition of weight and the 'piercing' motion of your hips. As you drive your shoulder forward, your hips must follow closely behind, almost like a ramrod. Don't pause after the initial drive; continue to push your hips through their legs while maintaining chest-to-chest contact to prevent them from regrabbing their guard.

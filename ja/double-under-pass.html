Double Under Pass: A White Belt's Biomechanically Precise Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Double Under Pass is a fundamental guard passing technique initiated from a standing or kneeling posture against an opponent in closed guard. It aims to secure control of both of the opponent's legs to break their guard and advance to side control.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of weight distribution and hip pressure, leading to predictable and easily countered movements. They tend to rely on raw strength rather than efficient leverage.

The core mechanical principle is to create a strong, low base and drive your hips forward and down, forcing the opponent's legs to become irrelevant by controlling their base and mobility.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Assume a low, staggered stance with knees bent and hips below shoulder level. Your lead foot should be slightly ahead of your opponent's hip line.
    2. Secure an underhook on the opponent's left thigh with your right arm, gripping their pant cuff or ankle, palm facing up.
    3. Simultaneously, secure an underhook on the opponent's right thigh with your left arm, gripping their pant cuff or ankle, palm facing up.
    4. Drive your chest down onto the opponent's thighs, keeping your back straight and head up, ensuring your hips are lower than theirs.
    5. Initiate a forward hip drive, pushing your pelvis towards the opponent's hips while simultaneously stepping your lead foot (right foot) slightly to the side, creating a diagonal path.
    6. As your hips drive forward, begin to slide your underhook arms further up the opponent's legs, aiming for a grip just above the knee.
    7. Continue the forward hip drive, transferring your weight onto your lead leg and stepping your trail leg (left foot) forward to secure a strong side control position, maintaining chest-to-chest pressure.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect hip angle: Driving hips up and back instead of forward and down. This hyperextends the lumbar spine, risking a disc injury. The correct movement is a forward hip drive, keeping the spine neutral.
    • Over-reliance on arm strength: Pulling with arms only, leading to bent elbows and shoulder strain. This can cause rotator cuff tears or biceps tendonitis. The correct movement utilizes full body weight and hip drive.
    • Knees collapsing inward: Allowing knees to buckle inwards while driving forward. This puts excessive torque on the knee joints, risking ACL or MCL tears. Maintain a stable base with knees tracking over the toes.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Drive Drill: From a kneeling position, practice driving hips forward and down, mimicking the hip movement of the pass. (10 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Grip and Hip Drive: From a kneeling position facing a partner, practice securing the pant grips and performing the hip drive. Partner remains static. (10 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Controlled Progression: Partner is on their back with knees bent. You, kneeling, secure grips and drive hips, stepping one foot forward to simulate the pass. Partner offers minimal resistance. (10 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Half-Speed Pass: Partner in closed guard. You, kneeling, execute the Double Under Pass at half speed, focusing on grip integrity and hip drive. Partner attempts to maintain guard. (5 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Full Speed Pass (Controlled): Partner in closed guard. Execute the Double Under Pass at full speed. Partner actively attempts to defend but does not actively counter with submissions. (5 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling: Attempt the Double Under Pass during live rolling against a resisting opponent. Focus on applying the learned biomechanics. (5 minute rounds, 100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent's knees are tightly locked in closed guard, creating a strong platform.
    • When the opponent attempts to shrimp their hips away, creating space.
    • When the opponent's legs are slightly separated or extended, making them more vulnerable.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Retention: If the passer's hips are too high or their base is unstable, the opponent can bring their knees back in to re-establish closed guard or attempt a sweep.
    • Reguarding: If the passer fails to establish dominant chest pressure, the opponent can use their feet on the passer's hips to create space and re-establish guard.
    • Underhook Escape: If the passer's grips are weak, the opponent can use their arms to peel the passer's grips off their legs and either escape to the side or re-establish guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try the Double Under Pass?

    This often occurs because you are extending your lumbar spine excessively by arching your back instead of driving your hips forward. Your pelvis should remain tucked and your spine neutral as you push your hips downwards and forwards into the opponent's legs. Imagine scooping them forward with your hips.

    Q: How can I effectively perform the Double Under Pass against a much larger and heavier opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision become paramount. Focus on getting your hips significantly lower than theirs before initiating the pass. Secure tight pant grips and drive your chest into their thighs to prevent them from using their weight to push you away. Your hip drive needs to be explosive and direct, aiming to get your hips underneath their center of gravity.

    Q: When is the best time to transition from a failed Double Under Pass attempt?

    If your opponent successfully defends by bringing their knees back in or establishing a strong frame, do not force the pass. Immediately transition to a standing base or a knee-slide setup. If they attempt a sweep, focus on maintaining your base and balance to defend the sweep before re-attempting a pass or moving to a dominant position.

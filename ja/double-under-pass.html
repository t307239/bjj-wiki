Double Under Pass: A White Belt's Biomechanically Accurate Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Double Under Pass is a fundamental guard pass initiated from a standing or kneeling position against an opponent's closed guard.

White belts often struggle by rushing, failing to establish proper frames, and neglecting hip pressure, leading to ineffective passes and potential injury.

The key is to break the opponent's posture and control their legs with your arms, allowing you to drive forward with your hips and chest.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Opponent is in closed guard. Your knees are inside their hips, feet flat on the mat, creating a base.
    2. Secure a deep collar grip with your right hand, pulling their head towards your chest to break posture.
    3. Simultaneously, secure a deep cross-collar grip with your left hand, reinforcing posture control.
    4. Insert your right arm under their left leg, gripping your own left bicep (or wrist if possible).
    5. Insert your left arm under their right leg, gripping your own right bicep (or wrist).
    6. Square your hips to the mat, ensuring your pelvis is directly in front of their pelvis.
    7. Drive your hips forward and down, transferring your weight from your knees to your chest pressing into their sternum.
    8. Maintain head control and continue driving forward, aiming to get your chest to their chest and hips to their hips.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Rushing forward with your head down: This can cause hyperextension of the neck or cervical spine injury if the opponent shrimps and creates space.
    • Correct action: Maintain head up and chest pressure, driving with your hips.
    • Gripping too loosely on the collars: This allows the opponent to easily improve their posture and resist the pass.
    • Correct action: Establish deep, secure collar grips, digging your knuckles into their sternum.
    • Letting your knees drift wide: This creates an opening for the opponent to attack your legs or recover guard.
    • Correct action: Keep your knees relatively close together, driving them forward.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the grip acquisition and arm insertion sequence without a partner. (10 reps)
    2. Partner drill (passive): Partner maintains a relaxed closed guard. Practice the complete pass mechanics with 0% resistance.
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner provides light hip pressure and attempts to close guard if it opens. (25% resistance, 5 reps)
    4. Partner drill (medium resistance): Partner actively attempts to maintain guard and create frames. (50% resistance, 5 reps)
    5. Live drilling: Attempt the pass in a rolling scenario with a partner who is actively defending. (75% resistance, 2 min)
    6. Full rolling: Integrate the Double Under Pass into live sparring scenarios. (100% resistance, 5 min)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent's guard is closed and their posture is somewhat broken or they are reaching.
    • Opponent attempts to open their guard but doesn't create enough distance or angle.
    • You have established dominant cross-collar grips and can control their upper body.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Scissor Sweep: If the passer drives forward without maintaining hip control, the defender can flatten their legs, bring one knee to the passer's hip, and use the other leg to sweep.
    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): If the passer's weight is too far forward, the defender can shrimp their hips away to create space and re-establish guard or transition to another position.
    • Knee Tap Sweep: If the passer drives too deep without a secure frame, the defender can bring one knee to the passer's hip and use their leg to trap the passer's knee, sweeping them to the side.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my neck hurt when I try the Double Under Pass?

    Neck pain often results from hinging at the neck instead of driving with the hips and chest. When you insert your arms, your head can get trapped if the opponent pulls you forward. Always maintain an active grip on the collars, keeping your head up and driving your sternum into their sternum. This creates a strong frame and prevents hyperextension.

    Q: How do I do the Double Under Pass against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on breaking their posture aggressively with deep collar grips before attempting to pass. Once your arms are under their legs, use your body weight and leverage by driving your hips forward and down, rather than trying to lift them. Think of it as pinning their legs to the mat with your forearms while your hips drive past their hips.

    Q: When is the best time to transition to a different pass if the Double Under Pass isn't working?

    If the opponent successfully frames with their elbows or knees to prevent your hips from driving through, it's a sign to transition. If they are strong on their frames and you cannot break them, consider releasing the leg grips and transitioning to a Knee Slice Pass or Torreando Pass, using your established collar grips to maintain control as you shift your weight.

Elevator Sweep: The Precise BJJ Technique for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Elevator Sweep is initiated from closed guard and aims to invert your opponent, bringing their hips off the mat to sweep them. It's a fundamental sweep that leverages hip and leg mechanics.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of coordinated hip extension and reliance on upper body strength, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury. They also often fail to establish a strong base for the sweep.

The core mechanical principle is using your leg as a lever to elevate and then 'walk' your opponent's hips upwards, creating an unbalanced position for the sweep. This requires precise timing of hip and leg movements.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a double-sleeve grip on your opponent's gi, placing your hands just above their elbows. Ensure your knuckles face upwards.
    2. Sit up straight, keeping your back against the mat. Your knees should be drawn up towards your chest, creating a tight closed guard.
    3. Initiate a slight upward pull with your right arm sleeve grip while simultaneously driving your left knee forward and slightly upwards towards your opponent's left hip bone.
    4. As your left knee drives forward, rotate your pelvis to the left, creating a fulcrum. Your hips should now be angled approximately 45 degrees to the right.
    5. Simultaneously, extend your right leg, placing your right foot firmly on your opponent's left hip bone. This foot acts as a pivot point.
    6. Drive your left hip upwards forcefully, using your left knee as the primary lifting mechanism. Your entire body's weight should transfer towards elevating their hips.
    7. As their hips lift, pivot on your right foot on their hip, rotating your body to your right. Your left leg continues to drive upwards, lifting their weight.
    8. Complete the sweep by driving your opponent over your right leg as they become unstable, ending up in your guard or side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly attempting to lift opponent's hips by 'shrugging' shoulders: This places excessive strain on the cervical spine and can lead to neck injury (ligament sprains). The correct movement involves hip extension and pelvic tilt.
    • Driving knee directly into opponent's stomach/ribs: This can cause internal organ damage or rib fractures. The knee should drive towards the hip bone to create leverage for elevation.
    • Over-extending legs while opponent is postured: This can lead to knee hyperextension and ACL/MCL tears. Maintain control of your limbs and only extend as the opponent's weight is being lifted.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice sitting up, establishing double sleeve grips, and the leg extension/hip drive motion without a partner (50 reps).
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the grip establishment, hip angle, and leg elevation without any resistance (20 reps per side).
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to grips and allows controlled hip elevation. Focus on the body mechanics and weight transfer (30 reps per side).
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to prevent the sweep by posturing up but allows you to get the initial hip lift. Focus on maintaining the sweep mechanics under light pressure (40 reps per side).
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner actively defends by posturing and attempting to break grips. Focus on completing the sweep through the entire sequence (50 reps per side).
    6. Light rolling (90% resistance): Implement the Elevator Sweep in a controlled rolling environment, focusing on timing and execution against a resisting opponent (5 minutes, 3 attempts per roll).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent is postured up with their weight on their feet, creating a gap between their hips and the mat.
    • Opponent is attempting to pass your guard and their weight is forward, making their hips vulnerable.
    • Opponent has heavy hips and is trying to 'smash' your guard down, creating an opportunity to lift.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass Defense: If opponent attempts to stack, immediately widen your base and drive your hips into their chest, preventing them from getting their weight over yours. Your knees should remain tight.
    • Grip Break: If opponent breaks your sleeve grips, immediately transition to a pants grip or attempt to re-establish control. Do not allow your guard to be opened.
    • Hip Escape: If opponent is successfully lifting your hips and you are losing balance, perform a strong hip escape to the side, creating space and resetting your base. Drive your elbow into the mat.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Elevator Sweep?

    Wrist pain often occurs from gripping too tightly or with the wrong angle. Ensure your grip is on the opponent's sleeve just above the elbow, with your knuckles facing upwards. Avoid wrapping your thumb around the sleeve excessively. Focus on using your forearm and shoulder to drive the pull, not just your wrist, and relax your grip slightly when not actively pulling.

    Q: How do I perform the Elevator Sweep against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes even more critical. Focus on establishing a very tight closed guard and use your leg as a powerful lever. The key is to get your knee *under* their hip bone and drive upwards with your entire body's force, rather than trying to lift with your arms. A strong pelvic tilt and rotation are essential to unbalance their weight.

    Q: When is the best time to try the Elevator Sweep from closed guard?

    The optimal time is when your opponent is postured up and their weight is primarily on their feet, creating a slight gap between their hips and the mat. This allows you to get your lifting knee underneath their hips effectively. Avoid attempting it when they are already smashing your guard down with their weight, as this makes lifting their hips extremely difficult and can lead to a failed sweep.

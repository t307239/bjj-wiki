エレベータースイープ: 白帯のための生体力学

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate

📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This technique originates from closed guard, aiming to elevate your opponent and sweep them over your hips. It's a powerful sweep when executed correctly.

White belts often fail by muscling the sweep, neglecting hip movement, or losing their base, leading to instability and potential injury.

The core principle is using your hips as a fulcrum and your legs as levers, creating an upward and forward momentum against a stable base.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting Position: Closed guard. Opponent’s hips are close to yours.
    2. Grip 1: Secure a grip on the opponent's left collar with your right hand. Your thumb should be inside the collar.
    3. Grip 2: Secure a grip on the opponent's left sleeve (near the wrist) with your left hand. Your palm should face upwards.
    4. Hip Elevation: Tighten your grip on the collar and sleeve. Simultaneously, drive your hips upwards off the mat, creating space.
    5. Leg Action (Right): Uncross your right leg, placing your right foot on the opponent's left hip bone. Keep your knee slightly bent.
    6. Leg Action (Left): Bring your left leg up, hooking behind the opponent's left knee pit. Your left shin should be perpendicular to the mat.
    7. Sweep Execution: Simultaneously push off the opponent's hip with your right foot while pulling down and slightly forward on their collar and sleeve with your hands. Drive your hips further upwards.
    8. Post-Sweep Transition: As the opponent falls, maintain control and transition to mount or side control, keeping your hips low.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hip Drive: Tensing your back and trying to lift your opponent solely with your arms. This can strain your lower back ligaments and cause muscle tears.
    • Knee Extension During Sweep: Fully straightening your left leg and then attempting to push. This puts excessive hyperextension stress on the knee joint, risking ACL or meniscus tears.
    • Over-Rotation of the Spine: Twisting your torso violently to generate power. This can lead to disc compression and ligament damage in the lumbar spine.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Elevation Drill: Practice lifting your hips off the mat from closed guard, holding for 3 seconds. (50 reps, 0% resistance).
    2. Guard Open and Hook Drill: Practice opening guard, placing your right foot on the hip and hooking behind the knee with your left leg. (50 reps, 0% resistance).
    3. Simulated Sweep Motion: From closed guard, perform the hip elevation and leg motion without an opponent, focusing on the smooth transition. (50 reps, 0% resistance).
    4. Partner Drill (Passive): With a compliant partner, practice the full Elevator Sweep motion. They offer no resistance. Focus on correct body mechanics. (20 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    5. Partner Drill (Light Resistance): Your partner offers light weight distribution, making it slightly harder to lift your hips. Focus on adjusting your leverage. (20 reps per side, 50% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling (Controlled): Attempt the Elevator Sweep during controlled rolling. Focus on timing and recognizing opportunities. (5 attempts per round, 75% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is postured up with their weight too far forward in your closed guard.
    • When you feel your opponent is trying to stack you, creating an opportunity for upward lift.
    • When your opponent has a weak base and is leaning heavily on one side.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard Pass: As you attempt to elevate, the opponent can immediately drop their weight and attempt a pass (e.g., knee-slice pass) by driving their knee across your body.
    • Stacking Defense: The opponent can prevent the sweep by driving their weight downwards forcefully, straightening their legs and pushing their hips into yours.
    • Hip Escape and Base: The opponent can hip escape to the side, breaking your leg hooks and creating a wider base to resist the sweep.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my left knee hurt when I try the Elevator Sweep in BJJ?

    This often happens if you fully extend your left leg while hooking behind the opponent's knee and then try to sweep. This hyperextends the knee joint. Instead, maintain a slight bend in your left knee throughout the sweep and focus on driving your hips upwards and pulling their collar down.

    Q: How can I perform the Elevator Sweep against a much larger and heavier BJJ opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on creating a larger gap with your hip elevation. Use your right foot on their hip as a powerful wedge to push them away, and your collar grip to pull their upper body down and off-balance. Leverage is key; don't try to overpower them.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate the Elevator Sweep from closed guard?

    The ideal time is when your opponent is postured up and leaning forward, creating an opening for you to lift. If they are very tight and stacked, it's harder. Look for moments when they are reaching for a grip or shifting their weight, as this creates instability.

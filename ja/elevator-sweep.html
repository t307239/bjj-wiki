This technique starts from closed guard and aims to elevate your opponent's hips to pass their legs and achieve a dominant top position.

White belts often struggle by trying to muscle the sweep rather than using leverage, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The core principle is creating a fulcrum with your hips and using your legs to lift, mimicking a lever's mechanical advantage.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grips: Secure a double-sleeve grip on your opponent's gi sleeves, pulling them towards you.
    2. Hip Elevation: Simultaneously, drive your hips upwards off the mat, creating space between your torso and theirs.
    3. Leg Placement: Hook your right foot (if sweeping to your left) deep into your opponent's left hip crease.
    4. Left Leg Action: Swing your left leg up and over your opponent's right hip, aiming to create a hook.
    5. Leverage: Maintain tension on the sleeve grips while driving your hips further up.
    6. Lift and Rotate: Using your hooked legs as a lever, drive your opponent's hips upwards and to their left.
    7. Transition: As their hips lift, drive your own hips forward and to their left, transitioning to a top position.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hip Drive: Attempting to lift with your back muscles instead of your glutes and hamstrings. This can cause lumbar strain or disc injury by hyperextending the spine.
    • Gripping Too High: Grabbing the opponent's shoulder or bicep instead of the gi sleeve. This prevents proper leverage and can lead to wrist or elbow hyperextension if they resist forcefully.
    • Legs Crossing Prematurely: Extending your legs too early or without sufficient hip elevation. This can lead to your knees or ankles being trapped and twisted, risking ligament tears (ACL, MCL).

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Elevation & Leg Hook Drill (0% resistance): Practice lifting hips and placing hooks without a partner. 10 reps each side.
    2. Partnered Grip & Hip Lift (25% resistance): Partner lies on their back, you practice grips and hip elevation. 10 reps.
    3. Controlled Sweep (50% resistance): Partner assists by lightly resisting. Focus on the lift and hook mechanics. 5 reps each side.
    4. Elevator Sweep with Light Resistance (75% resistance): Partner actively tries to prevent the sweep but without full power. 5 reps each side.
    5. Elevator Sweep with Moderate Resistance (90% resistance): Partner applies typical defensive pressure. Focus on timing and body positioning. 3 reps each side.
    6. Live Rolling (100% resistance): Attempt the Elevator Sweep during rolling. 5 attempts.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent has their knees close together in your closed guard.
    • When your opponent attempts to posture up forcefully, creating an opportunity to lift.
    • When your opponent tries to stack you, as this often leaves their hips vulnerable.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: If the opponent starts to lift, immediately drive your weight forward and attempt to pass their guard by stacking them.
    • Hip Escape and Re-Guard: If the sweep is failing, quickly hip escape to create space and re-establish your guard, ideally in open guard.
    • Leg Posture Control: If the opponent attempts to hook your hips, maintain a strong base and use your hands to push their hips away, preventing the hook from fully engaging.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Elevator Sweep in BJJ?

    Wrist pain often occurs if you are gripping too high on the opponent's arm or relying solely on finger strength. Ensure you are gripping the gi sleeve firmly with your entire hand, and use your biceps and back to pull, not just your wrists. Keep your wrists straight and avoid bending them backwards.

    Q: How can I perform the Elevator Sweep against a much heavier BJJ opponent?

    Against a heavier opponent, the key is leverage and timing, not brute force. Focus on creating a significant hip elevation to break their base *before* attempting the lift. Use your legs to hook deeply into their hip crease and under their thigh for maximum leverage. A well-timed sweep is more effective than a prolonged struggle.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate the Elevator Sweep from closed guard?

    The optimal time is when your opponent's weight is forward, making them susceptible to being lifted. This often happens when they are trying to posture up, or when they lean in to pass your guard. Avoid attempting it when they are already heavily stacked or have a very wide base.

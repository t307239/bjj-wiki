The Elevator Sweep is initiated from closed guard, aiming to invert your opponent and sweep them to your guard.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of leverage and relying on brute strength.

The core principle is creating an off-balance moment by lifting and pivoting on a single point of contact.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand, palm facing upwards, thumb inside the collar.
    2. Simultaneously, secure a sleeve grip on your opponent's left sleeve with your left hand, palm facing downwards.
    3. Sit up, extending your hips slightly, and bring your left knee to your chest, tucking it inside your opponent's right leg.
    4. Rotate your pelvis to your right by pivoting on your left hip.
    5. With your right hand, pull the collar upwards and slightly to your left, creating upward tension.
    6. With your left hand, pull the opponent's left sleeve towards your left shoulder, creating forward tension.
    7. As you pull and rotate, lift your opponent's weight by straightening your right leg and extending your hips, driving their right leg upwards.
    8. Continue the pivot on your left hip, bringing your right leg across their body to complete the sweep to your guard.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly gripping the collar with fingers outside or palm down can lead to a wrist sprain from the opponent's resistance. Always grip with thumb inside and palm up for maximal control.
    • Trying to lift the opponent by straightening both legs simultaneously without hip rotation can hyperextend the knees. Focus on pivoting on one hip and extending one leg.
    • Sweeping without a proper collar grip can result in losing control and allowing the opponent to posture up. Maintain a strong, consistent collar pull throughout the movement.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the grip acquisition and body rotation motions on an imaginary opponent for 10 repetitions per side.
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the grip sequence and hip pivot. Focus on smooth transitions for 10 repetitions per side.
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to grips and movement. Execute the sweep. 10 repetitions per side.
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to resist the sweep but allows for movement. Focus on generating leverage. 10 repetitions per side.
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner actively defends the sweep. Focus on timing and maintaining grips under pressure. 10 repetitions per side.
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Attempt the Elevator Sweep during rolling. Focus on successful execution and recovery if the sweep is not completed. 5 attempts per round.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • Opponent has their weight forward and their base is not wide.
    • Opponent attempts to posture up from your closed guard.
    • You have established a strong cross-collar and sleeve grip.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee-to-Chest Defense: If the sweep is initiated, immediately bring both knees to your chest and try to create a frame with your forearms to prevent the lift. Maintain a tight base.
    • Hip Escape and Base Reset: If you feel the lift starting, perform a hip escape to your side and immediately widen your base to regain balance. Do not let your leg get elevated.
    • Grip Break and Posture Up: Quickly break the opponent's collar and sleeve grips, then drive your weight forward to posture up. This negates their leverage.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Elevator Sweep?

    Your wrist is likely hurting because you are gripping the opponent's collar incorrectly, with your palm facing down or fingers exposed. This exposes your wrist to a painful hyperextension when the opponent resists or pulls back. Always grip the collar with your palm facing up and thumb inside for a secure, wrist-friendly hold.

    Q: How can I perform the Elevator Sweep against a much bigger and heavier opponent in BJJ?

    Against a heavier opponent, focus on the biomechanical advantage of leverage rather than pure strength. Ensure your collar grip is deep and your sleeve grip is tight to control their posture. The key is to create a significant off-balance by driving their weight onto their hips and then using your hip rotation and leg extension to lift. Do not try to muscle them; instead, use their weight against them by elevating one of their legs.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Elevator Sweep from closed guard?

    The Elevator Sweep is most effective when your opponent is leaning forward, attempting to posture up, or has a narrow base. If they are too upright or have a very wide, stable base, the sweep will be much harder to execute. Look for moments of imbalance and actively create those opportunities by pulling and pushing at the right times.

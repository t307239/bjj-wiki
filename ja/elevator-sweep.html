This sweep originates from closed guard, aiming to invert your opponent and create an advantageous position for you to come up.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength or jerky movements, leading to loss of balance and potential injury.

The core principle is creating a stable base while efficiently lifting and rotating your opponent's weight, leveraging your hips.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure a double sleeve grip on your opponent's arms, palms facing inward, thumbs outside.
    2. Place your left foot on your opponent's right hip bone, creating a strong frame.
    3. Simultaneously, place your right foot on your opponent's left hip bone, ensuring your heels are dug in.
    4. Hook your opponent's right leg with your left shin (inside of your shin against their outer thigh).
    5. Arch your lower back and drive your hips upwards towards the ceiling, lifting your opponent's hips off the mat.
    6. As your opponent's hips lift, pull their right arm down with your left hand and push their left arm away with your right hand.
    7. Simultaneously rotate your body counter-clockwise, bringing your right knee to the mat and stepping your right foot forward to establish side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect hip lift: Trying to lift by extending your legs straight up, without arching your back, can strain your lower back.
    • Correct movement: Arch your back and drive your hips with your glutes and core, using your legs as levers.
    • Premature rotation: Turning your body before fully lifting the opponent’s weight leads to losing your base and falling.
    • Correct movement: Ensure the opponent’s hips are elevated before initiating the body rotation.
    • Slipping foot on hip: Your foot slides off the opponent's hip during the lift, causing you to lose leverage and potentially fall forward.
    • Correct movement: Dig your heels into the hip bones and maintain constant pressure, using the elastic band of your foot.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Lift Drill (0% resistance): From closed guard on the mat, practice the hip arch and lift motion 20 times.
    2. Solo Frame and Lift Drill (0% resistance): From closed guard, practice placing feet on hips and lifting hips 20 times.
    3. Partnered Foot Placement and Hip Lift (25% resistance): Your partner lies on their back, you practice closing guard, placing feet on hips, and lifting their hips 15 reps.
    4. Partnered Arm Control and Lift (50% resistance): With partner in closed guard, practice sleeve grips, foot placement, and hip lift. Partner offers minimal resistance to the lift 15 reps.
    5. Full Elevator Sweep Drill (75% resistance): Practice the entire sweep motion with a compliant partner, focusing on correct weight transfer and rotation. 10 reps per side.
    6. Controlled Live Rolling (90% resistance): Attempt the Elevator Sweep during controlled rolling. Focus on execution, not necessarily success. 5 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is postured up with their weight distributed forward.
    • When your opponent tries to stack you from closed guard.
    • When your opponent’s hips are relatively close to yours.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The Stack Pass: If your opponent attempts the sweep, they should immediately drive their chest into your chest and stack your weight down, preventing the lift.
    • Leg Release and Posture: The defender can quickly release their grip on one sleeve and use that arm to push off your hip or face, breaking your base and regaining posture.
    • Switching to Half Guard: If the sweep is initiated but not completed, the defender can quickly bring their knee inside your leg to establish half guard, neutralizing the sweep attempt.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to do the Elevator Sweep?

    This pain often comes from trying to lift with your back muscles alone, rather than engaging your glutes and core to drive your hips. Ensure you're arching your lower back effectively and using your legs as levers to generate upward force from your hips.

    Q: How can I Elevator Sweep a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes even more critical. Focus on the precise placement of your feet on their hip bones, digging your heels in firmly. The upward hip drive must be explosive and timed with a strong pull on their sleeves to break their base before rotating.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt an Elevator Sweep from closed guard?

    The ideal timing is when your opponent is slightly postured up, meaning their weight is more on their feet and less on their hips. If they are too low, the sweep will be difficult; if they are too high and stacked, you risk getting squashed. Look for that moment of slight forward weight distribution.

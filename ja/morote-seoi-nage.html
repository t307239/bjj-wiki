Precise Morote Seoi Nage Technique for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Morote Seoi Nage is a powerful two-handed shoulder throw, typically initiated from a standing grip-fighting scenario. It aims to off-balance your opponent and take them down to the mat.

Many white belts struggle because they rely on brute strength rather than proper leverage and body mechanics. This leads to wasted energy and potential injury.

The key insight is to use your hips and the opponent's own momentum against them by creating a strong, stable base and rotating your body with precision.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grips: Secure a deep right cross-collar grip with your left hand and a right sleeve grip (at the wrist or forearm) with your right hand. Your right elbow should be tucked inside the opponent's right elbow.
    2. Stance: Adopt a stable, slightly staggered stance with your feet shoulder-width apart. Your knees should be slightly bent, maintaining a low center of gravity.
    3. Entry: Step your right foot forward and slightly to the side, aiming to get your right hip closer to the opponent's left hip. Your body should be angled at approximately 45 degrees to the opponent.
    4. Weight Transfer (Initial): Begin to transfer your weight onto your lead (right) leg. Simultaneously, pull the opponent's collar grip towards your chest and their sleeve grip towards your right shoulder.
    5. Hip Engagement: Drop your hips by bending your knees further, lowering your center of gravity below the opponent's. Your right hip should now be positioned to drive into the opponent's left side.
    6. Rotation and Lift: Rotate your torso counter-clockwise, driving your right hip into the opponent. As you rotate, lift their sleeve grip upwards and across your body, using your right shoulder to support their upper back.
    7. Completion: Continue the rotation and hip drive to unbalance the opponent forward and over your right shoulder. Maintain your grips and base throughout the throw to ensure a controlled takedown.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Neck Strain: Grabbing the collar too high or yanking the head upwards can hyperextend the opponent's neck, risking cervical spine injury. Always maintain control of their body's center of gravity, not just the head.
    • Knee/Ankle Sprain: Stepping incorrectly into the throw (e.g., foot too far forward or inward) or failing to maintain a bent-knee base can lead to ankle rolls or knee ligament tears when weight shifts.
    • Lower Back Injury: Attempting to lift the opponent solely with your arms and back, rather than using your hips and legs to drive and rotate, can cause lumbar strain or disc injury. Focus on hip power and body rotation.

    ドリル段階

    1. Grip and Stance Practice (Solo): Practice securing the collar and sleeve grips and establishing the correct 45-degree angle stance. Repeat 20 times.
    2. Shadow Morote Seoi Nage (No Partner): Execute the entire sequence of grips, entry, hip drop, rotation, and lift without a partner, focusing on the biomechanics. Repeat 15 times.
    3. Controlled Entry with Resistance (Light Partner, 25%): Partner stands passively. You practice the entry, hip placement, and initial weight transfer. Partner offers minimal resistance. Repeat 10 reps per side.
    4. Controlled Throw with Light Resistance (Partner 50%): Partner offers slight resistance to your entry. You execute the throw, focusing on proper hip drive and rotation. Partner helps absorb the fall. Repeat 10 reps per side.
    5. Controlled Throw with Moderate Resistance (Partner 75%): Partner actively resists your grip fighting and entry. You must apply the technique with good timing and leverage. Repeat 5 reps per side.
    6. Live Rolling (Sparring, 90-100%): Attempt Morote Seoi Nage during live rolling, focusing on applying the learned mechanics against a resisting opponent. Focus on successful entries and controlled takedowns, not just completion. Aim for 3-5 successful attempts per roll.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is posturing upright and you have established strong collar and sleeve grips.
    • When your opponent is leaning forward slightly, creating an opportunity for an off-balancing throw.
    • After successfully defending a guard pass attempt and transitioning to a standing grip-fighting scenario.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • The "Oosoto Gari" Counter: If the opponent attempts Morote Seoi Nage and you feel the weight shift, immediately step your left foot back and wide, dropping your weight low. Simultaneously, sweep their lead leg with your right leg in an Oosoto Gari motion, using their forward momentum against them.
    • The "Switch Base and Pull" Counter: As they commit to the throw, quickly switch your base to a wide, low stance. Use your sleeve grip to pull their upper body down and across, and your collar grip to push their head away, disrupting their balance and throwing mechanics.
    • The "Forward Roll" Counter: If the throw is initiated and you are unable to counter with a leg sweep, tuck your chin to your chest and perform a forward roll over their shoulder, landing in a safe position and potentially transitioning to a scramble.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try Morote Seoi Nage?

    Shoulder pain often occurs if you are trying to lift the opponent with your arm strength alone, rather than using your hips and core rotation. Ensure your right elbow is tucked, and you are driving your hips forward and rotating your torso; your shoulder acts as a pivot point for their upper body, not the primary lifting mechanism.

    Q: How can I perform Morote Seoi Nage against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on getting your hip deeper and lower than theirs, creating a fulcrum. Instead of trying to lift them directly, use their forward momentum from their own pressure or a slight pull. Your rotation should be strong and precise, turning your body into the throw.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt Morote Seoi Nage in a grappling match?

    The ideal time is when your opponent is slightly off-balance and you have established dominant grips. This often happens during transitions, after defending a submission, or when resetting from a scramble. Avoid attempting it when your opponent is perfectly balanced or actively resisting with a strong base.

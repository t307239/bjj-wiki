Morote Seoi Nage: A Biomechanically Precise BJJ Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Morote Seoi Nage is a powerful shoulder throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to unbalance and take your opponent down.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength or poor weight distribution, leading to ineffective attempts.

The key is using your opponent's forward momentum against them by creating a strong, stable base and driving through their center of mass.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Establish a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand, palm facing upwards, thumb inside the lapel. Simultaneously, secure your opponent's left sleeve with your left hand, gripping the fabric at the wrist bone.
    2. Entry: Step your left foot forward, slightly angled, placing it outside your opponent's right foot. Your hips should be angled roughly 45 degrees towards your opponent's right side.
    3. Weight Transfer (Uke): Shift your body weight onto your right leg, bending your knee and lowering your hips. Your left leg remains slightly bent, ready to pivot.
    4. Posture Break: With your collar grip, pull down and slightly forward on your opponent's lapel, breaking their posture and drawing their weight forward.
    5. Sleeve Control: Use your left hand on their sleeve to guide their arm across your body and towards your right hip.
    6. Hip Engagement: Drive your right hip into your opponent's lower abdomen/pelvis area. Your right hip should be lower than your opponent's, creating a strong fulcrum.
    7. Rotation and Throw: As you drive your hip, rotate your body counter-clockwise, bringing your opponent over your right hip. Extend your right leg and push off the mat to complete the throw, landing in a controlled side control position.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to lift your opponent solely with arm strength: This can lead to elbow hyperextension, causing UCL (ulnar collateral ligament) tears or strains.
    • Incorrect hip angle and insufficient hip drive: This results in a weak throw and potential lower back strain from twisting without proper support.
    • Overextending your neck during the throw: This can cause cervical spine strain or whiplash, especially if the opponent resists or falls awkwardly.
    • Gripping too loosely on the sleeve or collar: This allows the opponent to escape easily and can lead to wrist sprains if you are pulling against a strong resistance without a firm grip.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Shadow Drilling (0% resistance): Practice the footwork, hip movement, and arm positioning without a partner. Focus on smooth transitions. (20 reps)
    2. Partnered Mirror Drilling (0% resistance): Stand opposite a partner and mirror their movements as you execute the steps. Focus on grip and body alignment. (15 reps)
    3. Controlled Entry and Hip Placement (25% resistance): Partner stands passively. You practice entering, gripping, and sinking your hips into their abdomen. Focus on the feeling of engagement. (10 reps per side)
    4. Assisted Throwing (50% resistance): Partner helps slightly by leaning in. You practice the full motion, focusing on the weight transfer and rotation. Partner should not actively resist. (10 reps per side)
    5. Controlled Throwing with Light Resistance (75% resistance): Partner offers mild resistance and works with you to execute the throw. Focus on timing and commitment. (5 reps per side)
    6. Live Takedown Attempts (90% resistance): During sparring, attempt Morote Seoi Nage when the opportunity arises. Focus on applying the learned mechanics and not forcing. (5 attempts per round)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is posturing up and you have a strong collar grip.
    • When your opponent is leaning forward, creating an opportunity to unbalance them.
    • During a clinch exchange where you can secure the grips and initiate the movement.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Uchi Mata (Inner Thigh Throw) Defense: If the opponent attempts Morote Seoi Nage, step your right foot back and slightly to the side, creating distance. Simultaneously, use your left arm to block their right arm's path and your right arm to push their head away, breaking the throw.
    • Otoshi (Slipping the Throw): As the opponent begins to rotate, anticipate the throw and step out to their side (your left). Simultaneously, lower your base and use your arms to push away, creating space to disengage or transition to a guard.
    • Countering with a Double Leg Takedown: If you feel the opponent's hip drive strongly, you can immediately lower your level and shoot for their legs, securing a double leg takedown as they commit to their throw.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my elbow hurt when I try Morote Seoi Nage?

    Your elbow likely hurts due to hyperextension, which happens when you try to lift your opponent using only your arm strength instead of driving with your hips and legs. Ensure your grip is firm and your primary force comes from your body's rotation and hip engagement, keeping your arm in a stable, slightly bent position throughout the throw.

    Q: How do I do Morote Seoi Nage against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on using their weight and momentum against them. Secure a deep collar grip and pull down strongly to break their posture. Get your hip in *very* low and drive through their center of mass, using leverage rather than brute strength. A lower entry and a powerful hip rotation are crucial for generating enough force.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt Morote Seoi Nage in BJJ?

    Morote Seoi Nage is most effective when your opponent is actively posturing up or leaning forward, as this provides the forward momentum you need to unbalance them. It's also a good option when you've secured a strong collar and sleeve grip and can control their upper body to initiate the sequence. Avoid attempting it when your opponent is already low and stable.

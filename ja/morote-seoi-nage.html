morote背負い投げ：BJJのテイクダウンガイド

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック

Morote Seoi Nage is a dynamic throwing technique, typically initiated from a standing collar and sleeve grip. It aims to off-balance your opponent and take them down to the mat.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of coordinated body movement and a tendency to rely on brute force, leading to ineffective attempts and potential injury.

The key insight is using your opponent's forward momentum against them while creating a strong, stable base with your hips and legs.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Establish a strong cross-collar grip with your right hand (for a right-handed throw) and a standard sleeve grip on your opponent's left sleeve with your left hand. Your grips should be firm but not tense.
    2. Footwork: Step your right foot slightly forward and to the side, creating a staggered stance. Your left foot should be behind your right, with your feet shoulder-width apart for a stable base.
    3. Entry: Simultaneously, drive your right shoulder into your opponent's chest, just below their collarbone. Your hips should rotate inwards, turning your body perpendicular to your opponent.
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your right leg. Your opponent's weight will naturally be on their front leg due to your initial grip and entry.
    5. Leverage: Pull their sleeve grip towards your right hip while simultaneously pushing your right shoulder into their chest. This creates a strong lever.
    6. Rotation and Lift: Rotate your hips further and drive upwards with your right leg, lifting your opponent onto your right hip.
    7. Completion: Continue the rotation and forward momentum, guiding your opponent over your hip and onto the mat. Maintain control of their sleeve and collar throughout the throw.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to lift with arms alone: This places excessive strain on the elbow and shoulder joints, potentially leading to ligament tears (e.g., UCL tear in the elbow, rotator cuff tear in the shoulder) due to hyperextension or sudden twisting forces. The correct method involves using your entire body, especially the hips and legs, to generate lifting power.
    • Incorrect hip rotation: Forcing a hip rotation without proper weight transfer can twist the lumbar spine or knee, leading to muscle strains or ligament damage (e.g., medial collateral ligament tear in the knee). Your hips must rotate in conjunction with your body's forward drive and your opponent's weight distribution.
    • Dropping your base too low: Bending your knees excessively without maintaining proper hip alignment can create instability and expose your knees to impact or twisting injuries if the throw is not completed cleanly. Keep your base strong and your hips low but stable.

    ドリル段階

    1. Shadow Drilling (0% resistance): Practice the footwork, grip, and body rotation motions without a partner for 5 sets of 10 repetitions each.
    2. Grip Fighting and Entry Practice (25% resistance): With a partner standing, practice establishing grips and entering the throw without attempting the actual throw. Focus on the initial body mechanics. 5 sets of 8 repetitions.
    3. Controlled Entry and Hip Placement (50% resistance): With a partner standing, practice the entry, hip rotation, and placement of your opponent onto your hip, but without the full lift or throw. Focus on feeling the weight distribution. 5 sets of 8 repetitions.
    4. Assisted Throw (75% resistance): Your partner stands passively, allowing you to execute the full Morote Seoi Nage with minimal resistance. Focus on clean mechanics. 5 sets of 5 repetitions.
    5. Light Resistance Drilling (90% resistance): Practice with a partner offering light resistance, allowing them to react slightly to your movements. This simulates a more realistic scenario. 5 sets of 5 repetitions.
    6. Live Rolling (100% resistance): Integrate Morote Seoi Nage attempts into live rolling scenarios, focusing on timing and commitment. Aim for 3 successful attempts per rolling session.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • Opponent is leaning forward: When your opponent is pushing forward or has a forward lean, their weight is already compromised for a throw.
    • Establishing strong grips: After successfully securing both collar and sleeve grips, creating the necessary control.
    • Opponent is off-balance: If you notice a slight imbalance in your opponent's stance, indicating an opening.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stalling and maintaining base: If your opponent attempts Morote Seoi Nage, immediately widen your base and plant your feet firmly. Drive your hips back to resist the pull and rotation. This prevents them from establishing leverage.
    • Breaking grips and circling out: Simultaneously work to break your opponent's grips on your collar and sleeve. As you break their grips, circle your hips away from their pulling arm and step your feet to create distance.
    • Counter-throw (e.g., Osoto Gari): If your opponent commits heavily to the throw, you can attempt a counter-throw. As they drive forward, use their momentum to step your leg back and execute a powerful outer reaping throw like Osoto Gari.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try Morote Seoi Nage?

    Shoulder pain often occurs if you are trying to muscle the throw or if your entry is too upright. When you drive your shoulder into your opponent's chest, ensure your hips are rotating inwards and you are creating a strong base with your legs. The lift should come from your hips and legs, not solely from your arm pulling upwards, which can strain the rotator cuff.

    Q: How can I perform Morote Seoi Nage against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on their balance and leverage. Use their forward momentum against them by timing your entry precisely. Instead of trying to lift them directly, focus on rotating your hips and driving your shoulder into their chest to off-balance them. Your goal is to get them onto your hip, using their size to your disadvantage by making them fall over you.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt Morote Seoi Nage from a standing position?

    The optimal time to attempt Morote Seoi Nage is when your opponent is actively trying to advance or is leaning slightly forward. This forward pressure provides the necessary momentum that you can redirect. Establishing dominant collar and sleeve grips is crucial, as this control allows you to break their posture and initiate the off-balancing action effectively.

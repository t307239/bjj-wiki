Morote Seoi Nage: The Essential BJJ Takedown for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Morote Seoi Nage is a powerful shoulder throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to unbalance and take your opponent down to the mat.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of coordinated weight transfer and relying on brute strength rather than leverage.

The key is to create a strong pulling and turning action using your hips and core, not just your arms.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip opponent's right lapel with your left hand, thumb inside the lapel fold.
    2. Grip opponent's right sleeve cuff with your right hand, ensuring a firm wrist grip.
    3. Step your left foot forward, angling your body 45 degrees to your opponent's right.
    4. Drop your hips by bending your knees, lowering your center of gravity below your opponent's.
    5. Rotate your torso counter-clockwise, pulling your opponent's right arm across their body with your left hand.
    6. Swing your right arm under their armpit, driving your right shoulder into their chest, just below their collarbone.
    7. Pivot on your left foot, bringing your right foot around to face your opponent's left.
    8. Extend your legs and drive upwards with your hips, simultaneously pulling their lapel grip down and their sleeve grip towards you, completing the throw.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to lift with arms alone: This strains the elbows and shoulders, risking rotator cuff tears or bicep tendonitis from improper leverage.
    • Incorrect hip angle: If hips are too high or not rotated, the shoulder will hit their torso improperly, risking rib fractures or lower back hyperextension for both parties.
    • Gripping too loosely on the sleeve: This allows the opponent to easily slip their arm out, leading to a loss of balance and potentially a knee injury as you stumble forward.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the footwork, hip rotation, and arm movements without a partner (10 reps).
    2. Partner static drill: With a compliant partner, practice the grips and body mechanics without resistance (25% resistance, 10 reps).
    3. Partner assisted drill: Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the weight transfer and throw (50% resistance, 10 reps).
    4. Controlled drilling: Practice the throw with a partner who understands the technique and provides controlled defense (75% resistance, 5 reps).
    5. Sparring with focus: During sparring, actively look for opportunities to attempt Morote Seoi Nage, focusing on execution over success (90% resistance, 3 attempts).
    6. Live rolling: Integrate Morote Seoi Nage into live rolling with full resistance, aiming for successful execution (100% resistance, ongoing attempts).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • Opponent is postured up in your guard, creating a standing clinch opportunity.
    • Opponent is leaning forward, presenting an open upper body for a shoulder throw.
    • During a tie-up where you can establish strong lapel and sleeve grips.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Against Morote Seoi Nage: If the opponent initiates the throw, quickly step your right foot back and widen your base, creating a strong base to resist the pull.
    • If the opponent gets their shoulder in: Immediately sprawl your hips back and attempt to grab their head or arm, preventing them from driving through.
    • If you feel yourself being lifted: Break your own grips and attempt to land on your side, minimizing impact and avoiding a direct fall onto your back.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try Morote Seoi Nage?

    Your shoulder likely hurts because you are trying to lift your opponent with your arms instead of using your hips and core to generate the throwing motion. This places excessive strain on the shoulder joint. Ensure your hips are lower than your opponent's and that you are driving upwards through your legs and torso, not pulling with your arm strength alone.

    Q: How do I Morote Seoi Nage against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on using their size against them. Your goal is to get *under* their center of gravity. Lower your hips significantly by bending your knees deeply, and ensure your shoulder drives into their chest, not just their arm. The key is breaking their base and using their forward momentum against them through precise rotation and hip drive.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt Morote Seoi Nage in BJJ?

    The most opportune moments are when your opponent is leaning forward, creating a natural forward momentum that you can redirect. This often occurs when they are trying to pass your guard or during a standing clinch where they are not maintaining a solid base. Look for opportunities where their weight is on the balls of their feet.

Morote Seoi Nage: A Precise BJJ Takedown Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Morote Seoi Nage is a powerful one-arm shoulder throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to unbalance and take down your opponent.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of body awareness and relying on brute strength instead of leverage.

The key is to use your hips and core to generate rotational force, making your opponent feel weightless and easy to throw.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Right-hand secures opponent's left lapel at the collarbone. Left-hand secures opponent's right sleeve at the wrist.
    2. Stance: Feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, maintaining a stable base.
    3. Entry: Step your right foot forward, slightly angled, bringing your hips closer to your opponent's left hip.
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your left foot, lowering your center of gravity.
    5. Rotation: Rotate your hips and torso counter-clockwise, pulling your opponent's left arm across your body.
    6. Leverage: Drive your right shoulder into your opponent's upper chest/sternum, using your lapel grip to elevate and control their arm.
    7. Throw: Continue the hip rotation and body pivot, extending your right leg back and lifting with your lapel grip while your left arm guides their sleeve, projecting them forward and over your right hip.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Hyperextension: Incorrect pivoting on the lead leg can cause the knee joint to hyperextend when driving forward, potentially damaging the ACL or meniscus.
    • Correct: Maintain a slight bend in the lead knee and pivot on the ball of the foot.
    • Lower Back Strain: Attempting to lift the opponent solely with arm strength and an upright posture can overload the lumbar spine, leading to muscle strains or disc compression.
    • Correct: Utilize hip rotation and a lowered center of gravity to create leverage, minimizing reliance on pure arm power.
    • Shoulder Impingement: Forcing the throwing shoulder into the opponent's chest without proper hip alignment can cause the humerus to impinge on the rotator cuff tendons.
    • Correct: Ensure your hips are in position to receive the opponent's weight and drive your shoulder into their sternum as a pivot point, not a direct impact point.

    ドリル段階

    1. Shadow Drilling (0%): Practice the footwork, hip rotation, and arm movements without a partner. Focus on correct body angles and weight transfer. (20 reps)
    2. Static Grip and Pivot (25%): Hold grips with a compliant partner, focusing only on the hip rotation and body pivot. Partner stands passively. (15 reps each side)
    3. Controlled Entry and Pivot (50%): Partner offers light resistance. Practice entering, gripping, and pivoting, but no actual throw. Focus on maintaining connection and balance. (10 reps each side)
    4. Assisted Throw (75%): Partner allows you to execute the throw with minimal resistance, helping with balance. Focus on completing the rotation and feeling the throw. (10 reps each side)
    5. Light Resistance Throw (90%): Partner offers moderate, realistic resistance. Focus on breaking their balance and executing the throw smoothly. (5 reps each side)
    6. Live Rolling Application (100%): Attempt Morote Seoi Nage during live sparring, prioritizing technique over success. Focus on timing and execution. (Incorporate as opportunities arise)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is leaning forward, creating an opportunity to break their posture.
    • When you have established a strong cross-collar and sleeve grip.
    • When your opponent attempts a forward movement or is slightly off-balance.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Uchi Mata (Inner Thigh Throw): If the opponent attempts Morote Seoi Nage, step your right foot to the outside of their right foot, lift your left leg and sweep their right leg, driving forward with your hips.
    • Osoto Gari (Major Outer Reaping): As they enter, step your left foot back and to the side of their right foot, then sweep their right leg with your left leg while driving your hips into their right side.
    • Snap Down: If they attempt to enter, use your sleeve grip to pull their arm down sharply, breaking their balance forward and potentially creating an opening for a follow-up.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try Morote Seoi Nage?

    Your shoulder likely hurts due to improper biomechanics. Instead of using your hips and core to rotate and throw, you are likely relying too much on arm strength to drive your shoulder into your opponent. This can cause impingement or strain. Ensure your hips are rotating and you are driving *through* your opponent's sternum as a pivot point, not just impacting them.

    Q: How do I Morote Seoi Nage against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on lowering your center of gravity by bending your knees significantly. Use your hip rotation to get your body *under* their center of mass. Your lapel grip needs to be tight to pull their weight onto you, and your sleeve grip helps control their arm's leverage. The throw becomes more of a pivot and unbalancing act than a pure lift.

    Q: The ideal time is when your opponent has committed to a forward movement or has a broken posture, leaning towards you. This is often after they attempt a guard pass or when you've successfully pulled them into a clinch. Avoid attempting it when your opponent is squared up or has a strong base, as they will be much harder to unbalance.

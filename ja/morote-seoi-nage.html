Morote Seoi Nage is a powerful forward throw initiated from a standing clinch, aiming to unbalance and take your opponent down.

White belts often struggle by relying on brute strength instead of proper body mechanics, leading to failed attempts and potential injury.

The core insight is to use your opponent's forward momentum against them by dropping your weight and rotating your hips.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Initiate with a strong cross-collar grip on your opponent's gi lapel with your dominant hand and a sleeve grip on their gi sleeve with your non-dominant hand. Your elbow should be at approximately a 90-degree angle.
    2. Step your non-dominant foot forward, slightly angled towards your opponent, creating a base width approximately shoulder-width apart. Your hips should be moving towards your opponent's left side (if you are right-handed).
    3. Simultaneously, lower your hips by bending both knees, sinking your base. Your opponent's weight should now be shifted forward onto their toes.
    4. Rotate your body counter-clockwise, bringing your right hip (if right-handed) closer to your opponent's sternum. Your right elbow should remain tucked in, close to your body.
    5. Drive forward with your legs and hips, using your body as a lever. Your right arm (holding the collar grip) will act as the pivot point.
    6. As you rotate, pull your opponent's sleeve grip towards your right hip, while simultaneously guiding their collar with your left hand to control their upper body.
    7. Continue the rotation and forward drive, hinging at your hips and extending your legs. Your opponent will be lifted onto your right shoulder and then thrown forward over your hip as you complete the rotation.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect hip rotation: Lunging forward with straight legs and forcing the throw. This can strain your lower back ligaments and cause knee hyperextension for the opponent.
    • Over-reliance on arm strength: Pulling solely with your arms and not sinking your hips. This can lead to elbow tendonitis in your arms and a lack of leverage, potentially resulting in a failed throw and a fall onto your own head.
    • Improper grip breaking by opponent: If your opponent resists by pulling back strongly, and you continue to pull forward with your arms while your hips are not aligned. This can cause a shoulder dislocation for you or a neck injury for the opponent if they are pulled awkwardly.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo movement drill: Practice the hip rotation and body posture without a partner. Focus on sinking your hips and rotating your torso. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Grip and step drill: With a compliant partner standing, practice establishing the grips and taking the initial forward step and hip angle. (25 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Controlled entry drill: Partner stands passively. You practice the step, hip drop, and initial rotation, guiding them forward slightly. (25 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Weight transfer drill: Partner offers slight forward pressure. You practice sinking your hips and rotating, focusing on feeling their weight shift. (25 reps, 50% resistance)
    5. Throw completion drill: Partner offers moderate resistance. You execute the full motion, aiming for a controlled throw. (25 reps, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling integration: Attempt the technique during live rolling, focusing on timing and execution rather than outcome. (5-minute rounds, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is leaning forward, creating an opening for a forward throw.
    • When you have secured a strong cross-collar and sleeve grip, allowing for control.
    • When you have successfully pulled your opponent into you, breaking their base.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Snap-down counter: If the attacker attempts Morote Seoi Nage and you feel the hip rotation, quickly snap their collar grip down while stepping your lead leg back. This breaks their posture and can lead to a sweep or a better position.
    • Leg defense/Single leg counter: If the attacker drops their hips and begins to rotate, grip fight their sleeve and attempt to sprawl your hips back, simultaneously shooting for a single leg takedown. This disrupts their throw and allows you to regain control or attempt your own takedown.
    • Inside trip counter: As they rotate and drive, anticipate the throw and step your inside leg (closest to them) across their path. Simultaneously, use your grips to pull them forward and off balance, tripping them with your leg.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try Morote Seoi Nage?

    Your shoulder likely hurts because you are using excessive arm strength and not dropping your hips low enough. When you try to throw with just your arms, you place immense strain on the shoulder joint and rotator cuff. Ensure you are bending your knees deeply and rotating your hips to generate power, allowing your arms to guide the opponent rather than force the throw.

    Q: How can I use Morote Seoi Nage against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on timing and leverage over strength. Wait for them to commit to a forward movement or a push. Your goal is to get *under* their center of gravity by sinking your hips extremely low. Use their forward momentum by stepping in deep and rotating your hips powerfully, using their size against them as they overcommit.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt Morote Seoi Nage from the clinch?

    The optimal time is when your opponent is slightly off-balance and leaning forward, perhaps after they've attempted their own forward pressure or a less committed movement. Look for moments where their weight is naturally on their toes. Securing a strong cross-collar grip and controlling their sleeve is crucial for setting up the throw effectively.

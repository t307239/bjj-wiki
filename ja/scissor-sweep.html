The Scissor Sweep is performed from closed guard, aiming to invert your opponent's base and sweep them over your legs.

White belts often hesitate, fearing they'll be stacked or injured, leading to a lack of commitment and poor execution.

The key is creating a strong base with your hips and using your legs as levers, not just pushing with your feet.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a closed guard. Secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a left sleeve grip with your left hand.
    2. Bring your right knee across your opponent's chest, placing your right foot on their left hip bone.
    3. Shift your weight onto your left hip, creating a strong base. Your hips should angle slightly towards your opponent's left side.
    4. Lift your right leg and swing it over your opponent's left arm and head, aiming to hook their left shoulder.
    5. Simultaneously, extend your left leg straight, pushing off their right hip with your left foot.
    6. Use your right leg's hook around their neck/shoulder and your left leg's push to create leverage.
    7. Drive your hips forward and upward, arching your back slightly to unbalance them and initiate the sweep.
    8. As they fall, transition to mount by bringing your right knee to the mat and stepping your left leg over their body.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending your legs simultaneously without hip engagement can strain your knees and ankles, leading to ligament tears. Always use your hips to create the angle and leverage first.
    • Pushing directly with your feet against the opponent's hips without a strong collar grip can cause your arms to get stacked, leading to shoulder impingement or rotator cuff tears. Maintain control of their upper body to prevent this.
    • Attempting to sweep by solely pushing your opponent's hips away with your feet, while keeping your hips flat on the mat, puts excessive pressure on your lower back and can cause lumbar strain. Focus on lifting your hips off the mat and angling them.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo: Practice closing your guard and establishing grips without a partner. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    2. Solo: Practice the leg movement of bringing your right knee across, hooking the shoulder, and extending the left leg. (5 minutes, 0% resistance)
    3. Partner Drill: With a compliant partner, practice establishing grips and executing the hip angle and leg motion. (10 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Partner Drill: Practice the full sweep motion with a partner who offers minimal resistance, focusing on hip drive. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Partner Drill: Practice the sweep against a partner who attempts to posture up and resist slightly. Focus on maintaining control and adjusting your base. (5 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling: Attempt the Scissor Sweep during sparring, prioritizing correct mechanics over completion. (3 rounds, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is postured up and their weight is forward, making them susceptible to being off-balanced.
    • When you have secured a strong cross-collar and sleeve grip, allowing for good control.
    • When your opponent is trying to pass your guard from a close distance, and you can trap their arm and head.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: The opponent widens their base and drives their hips forward, stacking you. To counter, maintain your collar grip and use your legs to push their hips away, creating space to hip escape.
    • Knee Slice Pass: The opponent brings their knee inside your guard to pass. Immediately close your guard or transition to a different guard like half guard to prevent the pass.
    • Hip Escape to Guard Recovery: If the sweep is failing and you are losing control, immediately hip escape to regain your guard or transition to a safer position like half guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Scissor Sweep?

    Knee pain during the Scissor Sweep typically occurs when you extend your legs simultaneously without proper hip engagement. Your knee joint is not designed for forceful, outward rotation under direct pressure. Ensure you are driving your hips forward and angling your body first, using your legs as levers, not just pushing them straight out.

    Q: How can I do the Scissor Sweep against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on the hip angle and the hook around their neck/shoulder. Instead of trying to overpower them, use their weight against them by getting your hips underneath their center of gravity. A strong collar grip is crucial to control their posture and prevent them from simply crushing you.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Scissor Sweep in a BJJ match?

    The optimal time to attempt the Scissor Sweep is when your opponent is postured up and their weight is forward, making them unstable. If they are leaning in to pass your guard, and you can isolate an arm and their head with your legs, this creates an excellent opportunity. Avoid attempting it when they are low and heavy, as this makes them harder to sweep.

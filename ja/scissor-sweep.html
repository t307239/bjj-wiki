This technique starts from the closed guard and aims to reverse the opponent's position, placing you on top.

White belts often fail by muscling the sweep instead of using leverage and proper body mechanics.

The core principle is creating a strong base with your hips and using your legs as levers to unbalance your opponent.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure a cross-collar grip with your right hand and a right sleeve grip with your left hand.
    2. Place your right foot on the opponent's left hip, angling your hips 45 degrees to the left.
    3. Bring your left leg up and over the opponent's right leg, placing your left foot on their right hip.
    4. Extend your right leg, pushing off their left hip to create space and break their posture.
    5. Simultaneously, pull with your collar and sleeve grips, driving their upper body towards your left side.
    6. Shift your weight onto your left hip, creating a fulcrum.
    7. Sweep your right leg across their body, using your left leg's base on their hip to generate the unbalancing force.
    8. As they fall, bring your right leg down and secure a strong base with your hips on the mat, transitioning to side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to lift the opponent with your arms while their weight is forward: This can cause shoulder impingement and rotator cuff tears due to excessive external rotation and abduction.
    • Extending your legs too forcefully without proper hip angling: This can lead to hyperextension of the knee joint, potentially damaging the ACL or PCL.
    • Arching your back excessively to generate power: This places undue stress on the lumbar spine, leading to potential disc herniation or facet joint strain.
    • Not establishing a solid base after the sweep: This can result in your opponent recovering guard or transitioning to a better position, leaving you vulnerable.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the leg and hip movements without a partner, focusing on the sequence and range of motion (50 reps).
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Execute the sweep with a compliant partner, focusing on grip placement and weight transfer (20 reps per side).
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the connection and timing (30 reps per side).
    4. Controlled drilling: Partner actively tries to defend, but allows the sweep to be completed for learning purposes (50% resistance, 15 reps per side).
    5. Flow rolling: Incorporate the sweep into light rolling, focusing on opportunities and successful execution (75% resistance, 5 minutes).
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the sweep during a regular roll, applying full technique and strategy (100% resistance, 10 minutes).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • Opponent is postured up in your closed guard, with their weight forward.
    • Opponent is trying to pass your guard by stacking or pressuring heavily.
    • You have established strong cross-collar and sleeve grips.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape & Base Adjustment: If the sweep is initiated, the defender can hip escape their hips away from the direction of the sweep and widen their base to resist the unbalancing force.
    • Posture Up & Grip Break: The defender can attempt to break the collar and sleeve grips by driving their elbow into their own chest and pulling their arm across their body.
    • Knee Shield & Guard Recovery: The defender can bring their knees up to create a knee shield, preventing the sweep leg from fully crossing and allowing them to re-establish their base.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try the Scissor Sweep?

    This often happens when you try to lift your opponent with your arms instead of using your legs and hips as levers. Your shoulder joint is being forced into an unstable position, leading to impingement. Focus on using your right leg to push off their hip and your hips to create the sweep, keeping your arms in a more controlled pulling motion.

    Q: How can I Scissor Sweep a much larger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Ensure your cross-collar grip is high and your sleeve grip is secure to control their posture. Angle your hips significantly (45 degrees is a good starting point) and use your legs to create a strong fulcrum. Your goal is to unbalance their weight, not to overpower them physically.

    Q: When is the best time to initiate the Scissor Sweep from closed guard?

    The ideal moment is when your opponent is postured up and leaning forward, creating an opportunity to break their balance. If they are too far back or have a very strong, upright base, the sweep will be much harder. Look for moments when they are trying to advance their position or apply pressure, as this often creates the necessary opening.

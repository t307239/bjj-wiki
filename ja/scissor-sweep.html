Scissor Sweep: Your First BJJ Sweep

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The Scissor Sweep originates from closed guard, aiming to unbalance your opponent and transition to a dominant top position. It leverages leg mechanics to create leverage.

White belts often fail by trying to muscle the sweep or neglecting fundamental body positioning. They focus on the legs in isolation, ignoring the crucial role of the upper body and hips.

The key mechanical insight is using your hips to drive forward while simultaneously creating a fulcrum with your leg, effectively lifting and turning your opponent over.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure opponent's left sleeve with your right hand (collar grip is also acceptable if available and safe).
    2. Secure opponent's left pants cuff (above the knee) with your left hand, creating a strong anchor point.
    3. Hook your right foot (inside of opponent's left thigh) and place your left foot on the mat, slightly behind your hips, creating a base.
    4. Angle your hips to your right by lifting your hips off the mat and driving them forward towards your opponent's left hip.
    5. Simultaneously, extend your right leg straight, using your left foot on the mat as a pivot point.
    6. Pull your opponent's left sleeve towards your left shoulder while pushing their left pants cuff away from you, creating rotational force.
    7. As your opponent's weight shifts, drive your hips further forward and slightly lift your right leg, sweeping their left leg out from under them.
    8. Immediately follow with hip movement to establish a dominant top position (e.g., side control or mount).

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Applying excessive upward force to the opponent's sleeve without hip drive: This can strain your rotator cuff and shoulder ligaments due to the shearing force.
    • Extending your right leg too forcefully and straight without hip rotation: This can hyperextend your knee or strain your hamstring if the opponent resists unexpectedly.
    • Allowing your hips to drop flat to the mat instead of lifting and driving: This removes your base and makes you vulnerable to your opponent's hips driving into you, risking lower back compression.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice hip elevation and rotation on the mat without a partner. Focus on the feeling of driving hips forward and pivoting on the foot. (20 reps)
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): With a willing partner on their knees, practice the grip-taking and initial hip movement. Partner offers no resistance to the sweep. (10 reps each side)
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner attempts to lightly resist the hip drive and leg extension. Focus on maintaining control of grips and smooth hip movement. (10 reps each side)
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to defend by keeping their base and avoiding being swept. Focus on maintaining leverage and adapting your hip angle. (10 reps each side)
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Partner actively attempts to counter the sweep by shifting weight and creating frames. Focus on committing to the sweep once the opportunity arises. (10 reps each side)
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Attempt the Scissor Sweep in a rolling scenario, prioritizing technique over outcome. Focus on executing the biomechanics precisely. (3-5 attempts)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When opponent is posturing up from your closed guard, creating space between your hips and theirs.
    • When opponent attempts to pass your guard by posturing heavily, making their weight distribution unstable.
    • When opponent's weight is unevenly distributed, leaning too far onto one leg.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape and Frame: If the sweep is initiated, the defender can immediately hip escape to their right and frame with their right forearm against the attacker's hip, preventing the forward drive.
    • Base and Weight Shift: The defender can widen their base and shift their weight onto their left leg, making it heavier and harder to sweep. They can also try to plant their left foot firmly on the mat.
    • Grip Break and Posture Up: The defender can attempt to break the sleeve and pant grips by pulling their limbs inwards and straightening their posture, thus regaining balance and stability.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Scissor Sweep?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you are gripping too tightly with your fingers and not using your forearm and shoulder to drive the pull. Instead of just squeezing, think of using your entire arm as a lever, with your wrist in a neutral position. Imagine pulling the sleeve towards your shoulder, not just with your hand.

    Q: How can I Scissor Sweep a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on timing and leverage over brute strength. You must elevate your hips effectively and create a strong fulcrum with your sweeping leg. Ensure your grips are secure and your hip drive is explosive. Emphasize turning their weight against them rather than trying to lift them directly.

    Q: When should I actually try to do the Scissor Sweep and not just hold closed guard?

    The Scissor Sweep is best attempted when your opponent is posturing up and their weight is not firmly planted on both feet. Look for moments when they are leaning forward or to one side, as this instability is your primary opportunity. If they are tightly controlling your hips and maintaining a low, stable base, other guard options or sweeps might be more effective.

The Scissor Sweep is a fundamental sweep initiated from closed guard, aiming to invert your opponent's base and create an opportunity to take top position.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of leverage and relying on brute strength rather than precise body mechanics.

The key is to use your legs to create a fulcrum, breaking your opponent's posture and initiating a controlled rotation.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a strong two-on-one grip on your opponent's right sleeve with your left hand, and their right collar with your right hand. Your hips should be slightly elevated off the mat.
    2. Hook your right leg around your opponent's right leg, placing your foot on their left hip. Your left leg should be positioned to control their left hip or waist.
    3. Shift your weight to your left hip, creating a strong base. Your opponent's weight should be distributed over your hips.
    4. Drive your right knee towards your opponent's left hip, while simultaneously extending your left leg to push their left hip away.
    5. As your opponent attempts to re-establish balance, arch your back and lift your hips, using your right leg as a pivot point.
    6. Simultaneously, scissor your legs by bringing your right leg across your opponent's body and extending your left leg outwards. This action forces their weight to shift dramatically.
    7. As their weight shifts off-balance, immediately drive your hips forward and shrimp your body to the side, transitioning to your left side to secure top position.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to 'pull' your opponent over with arm strength alone can strain your shoulder joint (rotator cuff or labrum), as you're not using your legs for leverage. Instead, focus on hip elevation and leg extension to create the angle.
    • Forcing the scissor motion by extending one leg while the other is still trapped can hyperextend or twist your knee, potentially damaging the ACL or meniscus. Ensure your hooking leg is securely positioned before initiating the scissor action.
    • Trying to 'jump' into the sweep without proper hip control and weight transfer can lead to your opponent falling on top of you, risking a knee or ankle injury from an uncontrolled fall. Maintain a stable base and controlled hip movement throughout.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg positioning and hip elevation without a partner. Focus on the feel of the scissor action. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Partner lies on their back, you are in closed guard. Practice the entire sequence of grips, hip movement, and leg scissor. (20 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (light control): Partner offers minimal resistance to your grips and hip movement. Focus on making the sweep happen. (25 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (controlled sweep): Partner actively tries to defend but allows the sweep to be completed. Focus on smooth transitions after the sweep. (30 reps per side)
    5. Live rolling (50% resistance): Attempt the sweep in live rolling, prioritizing correct mechanics over success. Tap if you make a mistake that would cause injury. (5 rounds x 3 minutes)
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Attempt the sweep with a resisting opponent, focusing on timing and finishing the sweep cleanly. (5 rounds x 5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • Opponent's posture is upright and they are pressuring down into your closed guard.
    • Opponent has stacked you, creating space for your hips to elevate.
    • Opponent is trying to pass your guard by standing up and reaching for your legs.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass Defense: If your opponent attempts a stack pass, immediately bring your knees together, trapping their arms between your legs, and drive your hips forward to break their posture and create space to reset.
    • Hip Escape to Guard Recovery: If the sweep is failing and your opponent is posturing up, perform a strong hip escape to create distance and re-establish a strong closed guard or transition to an open guard.
    • Leg Pummeling: If your opponent tries to control your legs to prevent the sweep, actively pummel your legs to regain dominant positioning, such as hooking their leg or creating space to shrimp out.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Scissor Sweep in BJJ?

    Your knee might be hurting because you're forcing the scissor motion without proper alignment. This often happens when you extend one leg powerfully while the other is still trapped or poorly positioned, putting excessive stress on the ligaments like the ACL. Ensure your hooking leg is firmly in place and your supporting leg is creating a stable base before initiating the scissor action to avoid hyperextension or twisting.

    Q: How can a white belt Scissor Sweep a much bigger and heavier opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage becomes even more critical. Focus on your hip elevation and creating a strong fulcrum with your hooking leg. Instead of trying to lift their weight, aim to break their posture and shift their base using precise angles. Drive your knee into their hip and use your other leg to push their opposite hip away, making their weight unstable and easier to rotate.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt a Scissor Sweep from closed guard?

    The optimal time to attempt a Scissor Sweep is when your opponent is leaning into your guard, trying to break your posture. This pressure provides the necessary resistance and weight distribution for your sweep. If they are too far away or have already established a strong stacked position, the sweep will be much harder to execute effectively.

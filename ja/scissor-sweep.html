The Scissor Sweep is initiated from closed guard, aiming to reverse position and achieve a dominant top position. It leverages leg entanglements and hip movement to unbalance your opponent.

White belts often struggle because they lack the precise body control and connection needed, leading to ineffective or dangerous attempts. They may focus too much on brute strength rather than leverage.

The key insight is using your legs as levers to create a fulcrum with your hips, driving your opponent's weight away from their base and onto their side.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, establish a strong two-on-one grip on your opponent's left sleeve with your right hand, and their left collar with your left hand. Your opponent's left leg is between your legs.
    2. Bring your right foot to the outside of your opponent's left hip, creating a frame. Your left leg remains hooked inside their left thigh.
    3. Angle your hips 45 degrees to your right, away from your opponent's trapped leg. Your base should be narrow, with your knees close together.
    4. Use your right hand to pull your opponent's left arm across their body towards your right hip. Simultaneously, use your left hand on the collar to create upward tension.
    5. Extend your right leg, driving your right foot into your opponent's left hip with moderate pressure. This creates leverage.
    6. As your right leg extends, begin to simultaneously bring your left knee forward and slightly upward, tucking it towards your chest. This motion initiates the scissor action.
    7. Simultaneously, drive your hips forward and to your right, creating a rotational force. Your extended right leg acts as the pivot point, while your knee action lifts and rotates their body.
    8. Continue the hip drive and leg scissor motion, pushing your opponent's weight over their left shoulder. Your goal is to break their base and fall to your right side, landing in side control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to lift your opponent solely with your arms: This can strain your shoulder and elbow joints, leading to dislocations or sprains, as the force is not applied through a stable base.
    • Extending your legs too widely and forcefully without hip engagement: This can hyperextend your knees or cause adductor strains in your groin, as your legs are not supported by a strong core and hip structure.
    • Allowing your hips to remain flat on the mat: This prevents effective leverage and can lead to your opponent posturing up or passing your guard easily, and may strain your lower back if you try to force the sweep without proper body alignment.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling (no opponent): Practice the hip angle, leg extension, and knee tuck motion with no resistance. Focus on the feeling of creating a frame and leverage. (10 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice the entire sequence of grips, hip movement, and leg action. Partner remains still. (15 reps)
    3. Light resistance drill (25%): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the timing of the pull and hip drive. Focus on maintaining your frame. (20 reps)
    4. Controlled resistance drill (50%): Partner actively tries to defend but allows you to complete the sweep motion. Focus on adjusting your angle and hip pressure based on their defense. (25 reps)
    5. Semi-live drill (75%): Partner applies moderate resistance, reacting to your movements. Focus on completing the sweep or transitioning if they defend effectively. (30 reps)
    6. Live rolling (100%): Attempt the Scissor Sweep during sparring. Focus on recognizing opportunities and applying the learned mechanics under pressure. (5 attempts per round)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is postured up, creating space between your guards.
    • When your opponent is leaning heavily on one side, making them unstable.
    • When your opponent is trying to stack you, as this often leaves them exposed to a sweep.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape and Base Adjustment: If the sweep is initiated, immediately hip escape to your left and widen your base. This breaks the scissor action and allows you to regain balance.
    • Leg Pummeling: Quickly bring your free leg (your right leg in this example) inside your opponent's attacking leg to create a stronger base and prevent the scissor effect.
    • Posture Up and Grip Release: If the sweep is developing, focus on regaining your posture and releasing your opponent's grips to neutralize the leverage.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Scissor Sweep in BJJ?

    Knee pain often stems from overextending your leg or forcing the sweep without proper hip engagement. When you extend your right leg to sweep, ensure your hips are angled and driving forward. If your hips are not moving, your knee joint bears excessive torsional stress. Focus on rotating your hips and using your extended leg as a lever, not a pushing force.

    Q: How can I Scissor Sweep a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are paramount. Do not attempt to lift them. Instead, focus on controlling their posture with your grips (collar and sleeve are essential) and using your hip angle to create a fulcrum. Drive your hips forward and to the side of their trapped leg, using your extended leg to unbalance their weight distribution, aiming to make them fall sideways.

    Q: When is the best time to use the Scissor Sweep from closed guard?

    The Scissor Sweep is most effective when your opponent's weight is not evenly distributed, often when they are postured up and leaning forward, or when they are trying to stack you. These positions create an imbalance that your leg lever can exploit. Avoid attempting it when they are tightly controlling your legs or have a very wide base.

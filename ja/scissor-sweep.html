BJJ Scissor Sweep Technique Guide for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Beginner
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

This technique starts from the closed guard position, aiming to reverse the opponent's position and establish top control. It's a fundamental sweep for beginners.

Many white belts struggle because they rely on brute strength or hope rather than precise body mechanics. They often overextend or fail to create the necessary angles.

The key insight is to use your legs as levers, creating an off-balancing force by controlling one leg and using the other to drive your hips away and upward.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From closed guard, secure an overhook grip on your opponent's right arm with your left arm, placing your bicep across their chest. Your right hand grips their right sleeve near the wrist.
    2. Simultaneously, reach your right leg *across* your opponent's body, hooking your right foot behind their left knee. Your left leg remains on the inside of their left thigh.
    3. Angle your hips 45 degrees to your left. Your base should be narrow, with your knees close together.
    4. Begin to sit up, driving your left knee towards your opponent's hip. Your opponent's right arm is still controlled by your overhook.
    5. As you sit up, use your right leg to 'scissor' the opponent's left leg, pulling it towards your chest while pushing your hips away from them.
    6. Simultaneously, begin to drive your opponent's right shoulder down and away with your overhook, creating a lever.
    7. As your opponent begins to fall to their right, transition your right leg to a position that blocks their hip and begin to pass to side control, maintaining your left knee on their hip for control.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending your right leg and pushing your hips forward while your opponent is still stable can hyperextend your knee joint, leading to ACL or MCL tears.
    • Gripping the opponent's sleeve too tightly with your right hand without proper hip rotation can cause wrist sprains or carpal tunnel aggravation.
    • Attempting to 'pull' the opponent over with your arms alone, neglecting the hip drive, puts excessive strain on your shoulders and can lead to rotator cuff injuries.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the leg movements and hip angles without a partner. Focus on the 'scissor' action and hip dissociation. (10 reps each side)
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): Opponent lies on their back. You are in closed guard. Practice the grip acquisition and the full sweep motion, with the opponent offering no resistance. (10 reps each side)
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Opponent maintains a very loose base, allowing you to initiate the sweep with minimal effort. Focus on timing your hip drive. (10 reps each side)
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Opponent actively tries to resist by keeping their base tight, but allows you to practice the setup and initial sweep attempt. (10 reps each side)
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Opponent defends the sweep more actively, requiring you to adjust your angles and timing. Focus on finishing the sweep and transitioning to side control. (5 reps each side)
    6. Live rolling (90% resistance): Apply the sweep in a controlled sparring session, focusing on execution and adaptation to their defense. (3-5 attempts per round)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent's weight is on their heels, making it difficult for them to maintain a strong base.
    • When the opponent's hips are close to yours, allowing for effective leg entanglement.
    • When the opponent has a loose grip on your guard, or is posturing up too high.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): If you feel the scissor sweep initiate, immediately shrimp your hips away from the direction of the sweep, widening your base and breaking the entanglement.
    • Stacking: The defender can stack their weight forward onto the attacker's hips, collapsing the guard and preventing the sweep. This involves driving your chest down onto the attacker's hips.
    • Leg Recovery: If the sweep is partially successful, the defender can attempt to bring their trapped leg back inside their own guard or establish a heavy base to prevent the full sweep and transition.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try the Scissor Sweep?

    This often happens when you push your hips forward too aggressively without properly 'scissoring' the opponent's leg. This can hyperextend your knee. Ensure your right leg hooks behind their knee and you actively pull that leg towards your chest while pushing your hips away, creating a controlled lever action.

    Q: How do I Scissor Sweep a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on *leverage* and *timing* over strength. Ensure your overhook grip is tight and your right leg is deeply hooked behind their knee. Use your hips to create the angle, then drive your hips away and up, initiating the fall. Your goal is to unbalance them, not overpower them.

    Q: When should I attempt the Scissor Sweep versus other sweeps from closed guard?

    The Scissor Sweep is excellent when the opponent is posturing up or has their weight slightly forward. If they are very low and heavy, a Hip Bump Sweep might be more effective. If they are very far away, a Pendulum Sweep could be a better option. Observe their posture and base.

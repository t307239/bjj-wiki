This technique starts from closed guard and aims to reverse the position, placing you on top.

Many white belts fail because they rely on brute strength instead of leverage.

The key mechanical insight is using your legs to create a fulcrum and hip rotation.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish a strong closed guard by securing your ankles behind the opponent's back. Your shins should be perpendicular to the mat.
    2. Grip the opponent's right sleeve with your left hand and their left collar with your right hand. Pull them slightly forward to break their posture.
    3. Bring your right knee across the opponent's chest, aiming for their left shoulder. Your right hip should now be slightly elevated off the mat.
    4. Extend your left leg straight, placing your left foot on the opponent's right hip. This creates a base and prevents them from posturing up.
    5. Shift your weight onto your right hip, initiating a rotation. Your right leg acts as a lever against their hip.
    6. Simultaneously, drive your left foot into their hip while pulling their collar and sleeve towards you and slightly upwards.
    7. As you rotate your hips, your right leg will scissor over their body, lifting and sweeping them to their left.
    8. Immediately follow by driving your hips forward and establishing a top control position, such as side control, by placing your left knee on the mat and your right knee over their hips.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to lift the opponent with your arms alone, without hip rotation: This can strain your shoulder joint (glenohumeral joint) due to excessive abduction and external rotation. The correct movement involves driving your hips and using your legs as levers.
    • Extending your knee joint forcefully against resistance: This can lead to ligament damage in the knee (ACL, MCL tears) if the opponent resists with their own leg. Maintain a slight bend in your knee and focus on the scissor motion.
    • Arching your lower back excessively to create space: This puts undue stress on your lumbar spine and can cause muscle strains or disc issues. Keep your core engaged and focus on hip movement for leverage.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the leg and hip movement without a partner, focusing on the scissor motion and hip elevation. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a partner lying down, practice the grip establishment, hip elevation, and scissor motion. Focus on smooth execution. (25 reps)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance, allowing you to feel the sweep mechanics. Focus on driving the hips and leg extension. (25 reps per side)
    4. Half-resistance drill: Partner actively tries to defend by posturing up or bridging, but not with full strength. You focus on maintaining grips and executing the sweep. (20 reps per side)
    5. 75% resistance drill: Partner attempts to defend more actively, requiring more precise timing and body mechanics from you. Focus on resetting if the sweep is not clean. (15 reps per side)
    6. Live rolling: Attempt the scissor sweep during rolling with 100% resistance. Focus on recognizing opportunities and executing with proper technique, not just trying to finish.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is postured up with their weight forward, making their base unstable.
    • When the opponent tries to pass your guard by stacking or pressuring forward.
    • When you have secured a strong two-on-one grip (sleeve and collar) and can control their posture.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Posture Up and Base Wide: If the sweep is initiated, the defender should immediately try to straighten their back and widen their base by placing their feet flat on the mat, preventing the sweepers' hips from elevating.
    • Hip Escape and Reguard: The defender can hip escape away from the direction of the sweep, creating space and allowing them to potentially regain a better guard or pass.
    • Knee Shield/Block: The defender can bring their opposite knee (the one not being swept over) to block the sweeper's hip, preventing the necessary leverage and rotation for the sweep.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to scissor sweep?

    Your knee might hurt if you are trying to force the sweep by straightening your leg completely against resistance. This can hyperextend the knee or cause shearing forces. Ensure you maintain a slight bend in your knee throughout the motion and focus on rotating your hips and using your foot on the opponent's hip as a fulcrum, rather than just pushing with your leg.

    Q: How can I scissor sweep a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is even more critical. Focus on breaking their posture down significantly with your grips, pulling them close. Elevate your hips as high as possible and use your leg on their hip as a strong point of leverage, combined with a powerful hip rotation to create the sweep. Don't try to lift them; aim to unbalance them.

    Q: When should I stop trying to scissor sweep and switch to another technique?

    You should switch if the opponent successfully defends by posturing up and widening their base, or if they manage to get their knee inside your guard. Continuing to force a failed sweep can lead to you being swept or losing your guard. Recognize the defense, reset your guard, and look for a new opportunity or transition to a different technique.

The Essential Underhook for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The underhook is a fundamental control position, typically established from guard or during transitions, aiming to secure dominance and initiate attacks.

White belts often struggle with underhooks due to a lack of understanding of leverage, leading to ineffective grips and exposed vulnerabilities.

The core principle is using your shoulder and elbow to create a powerful lever against your opponent's torso, establishing superior positional control.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From side control or guard, approach your opponent's torso. Your lead leg should be slightly bent and your hips angled towards their hip.
    2. Insert your lead arm (e.g., right arm) underneath their armpit, aiming to place your bicep flush against their ribs.
    3. Your opponent's arm should be on the outside of your bicep. Your elbow should be tucked, creating a tight frame against their body.
    4. Simultaneously, drive your shoulder into their chest or shoulder blade, initiating a slight forward pressure.
    5. Your hips should be low, almost touching the mat, providing a stable base and preventing them from posturing up.
    6. Your free hand (e.g., left hand) can grip their far collar, their far sleeve, or even secure your own bicep for a "seatbelt" grip.
    7. Maintain a slight head pressure, directing your forehead towards their neck or upper chest, preventing them from creating space.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly driving your head into your opponent's face while attempting an underhook can lead to neck hyperextension or whiplash, damaging cervical ligaments. The correct movement involves driving your shoulder into their torso with head pressure directed *along* their body line, not into their face.
    • Overextending your arm to reach for the underhook without proper hip engagement can expose your shoulder to an armbar or kimura, causing rotator cuff tears. Ensure your hips are low and close, and your elbow is bent and tucked, creating a strong frame.
    • Trying to force an underhook by shrugging your shoulders and lifting your opponent can strain your back muscles (erector spinae) and lead to disc compression. Instead, use your hips and legs to create leverage, driving your shoulder into their base.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the underhook entry motion 20 times per side, focusing on hip angle and arm placement, with 0% resistance.
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Your partner stands passively. Practice entering the underhook and establishing the grip 10 times per side, with 0% resistance.
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Your partner offers minimal resistance to the underhook entry. Practice 10 reps per side, focusing on maintaining the frame against their light push, with 25% resistance.
    4. Positional sparring (guard retention): Start in closed guard. Your partner attempts to pass. Focus on establishing an underhook to prevent the pass or reset guard. 5 rounds, 5 minutes each, with 50% resistance.
    5. Positional sparring (side control escape): Start in side control with your partner. Focus on achieving an underhook to shrimp out and regain guard. 5 rounds, 5 minutes each, with 75% resistance.
    6. Live rolling: Integrate the underhook into your rolling. Aim to establish it when possible and use it to control or transition. 5 rounds, 5 minutes each, with 90-100% resistance.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is posturing up from your guard, creating an opening for your arm.
    • During transitions from open guard to closed guard or vice-versa, when space is momentarily created.
    • When defending a guard pass, securing an underhook can disrupt their base and allow for a guard recovery.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • "Over-Under" Exchange: If your opponent secures an underhook, you secure an overhook (arm over their shoulder) to neutralize their leverage. Your elbow should be over their shoulder, and your grip can be on their far hip or pants.
    • Hip Escape and Frame: If they have an underhook and are driving into you, immediately hip escape away and use your free arm to frame against their head or shoulder, creating space to re-establish your guard or posture up.
    • "Shrimping" with Underhook: From bottom side control or when they have a strong underhook, shrimp your hips away and use the underhook to help create a wedge, allowing you to bring your knee inside and recover guard.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to get an underhook against a bigger opponent?

    You are likely overextending your arm and not using your hips effectively. Against a larger opponent, your hips must be low and driving into their base. Instead of reaching with your arm, think of driving your shoulder and bicep under their armpit while simultaneously lowering your hips. This uses your body weight and leverage, not just arm strength, protecting your shoulder joint.

    Q: How do I get an underhook if my opponent is already framing tightly on my head?

    You need to break their frame first. Use your forearm on the side of their framing arm to create a small amount of space, then quickly insert your underhook. Alternatively, you can use a "head-and-arm" control: get your head on one side of their neck and your arm under their armpit simultaneously. This allows you to control their posture and create the opening for a full underhook.

    Q: When should I use an underhook from the bottom in BJJ?

    From the bottom, an underhook is primarily used for defense and guard retention. If your opponent is trying to pass your guard, securing an underhook on their hip or torso disrupts their balance and prevents them from advancing. It also helps you "pull" them into your guard or create space to shrimp out and recover your guard if they have established side control.

Underhook BJJ: The White Belt Biomechanical Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The underhook is a dominant position primarily achieved from guard (closed, open, half) or during transitions, aiming to control your opponent's posture and create attack angles.

White belts often struggle with the underhook by relying on raw strength or poor body positioning, leading to ineffective control and vulnerability.

The core mechanical insight is leveraging your body's structure to drive into the opponent, creating a tight frame with your shoulder and arm.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a base (e.g., guard), lower your hips towards the mat, increasing your base width slightly.
    2. Drive your shoulder into the opponent's torso, aiming to get your armpit to their hip crease.
    3. Secure a "collar grip" on the opponent's far-side lapel with your bicep against their neck or a "seatbelt grip" over their shoulder.
    4. Your other arm secures a "cross-collar grip" on their near-side lapel or "sleeve grip" on their opposite arm.
    5. Drive your hips forward and slightly up, creating a wedge with your shoulder and chest.
    6. Maintain a "tight frame" by keeping your elbows tucked and your forearms engaged.
    7. Distribute your weight so your hips are heavy, preventing the opponent from easily shrimping or posturing up.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly "pulling" with the arm to create the underhook: This strains the rotator cuff and shoulder capsule, risking a labral tear.

    The correct movement is to drive the shoulder and torso into the opponent, using the arm as a hook.

    • Overextending the elbow when trying to secure the grip: This hyperextends the elbow joint, potentially damaging the ulnar collateral ligament (UCL).

    The correct movement is to keep a slight bend in the elbow, maintaining a strong frame.

    • Arching the back excessively to "cling" to the opponent: This puts undue stress on the lumbar spine, risking facet joint strain or disc issues.

    The correct movement is to drive with the hips and core, keeping the spine neutral.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the hip drive and shoulder placement motion without a partner. (20 reps)
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): Partner stands passively. You practice securing the underhook from a starting position (e.g., guard). (10 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Partner offers light resistance, allowing you to feel the pressure and frame. Focus on maintaining the underhook. (10 reps per side)
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to break your underhook. Focus on maintaining your frame and weight distribution. (10 reps per side)
    5. Live rolling (75% resistance): Practice securing the underhook during positional sparring. Focus on control, not submission. (3 rounds, 2 mins each)
    6. Live rolling (90-100% resistance): Integrate underhook attempts and maintenance during full sparring. (5 rounds, 3 mins each)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • From closed guard when the opponent postures up, creating space to drive your shoulder in.
    • When your opponent attempts a guard pass, allowing you to secure an underhook on their hip or torso.
    • During scrambles and transitions where you can quickly establish body control.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • "Elbow to Knee" defense: If the opponent has an underhook on your torso, drive your knee towards your elbow on that side, creating a frame to break their grip and create space.
    • "Shrimping Away": If the opponent has an underhook and is driving into you, shrimp your hips out to their side, creating distance and breaking their pressure.
    • "Posting and Bridging": If the opponent has a strong underhook and is controlling your posture, post on their far arm and bridge your hips up and away to disrupt their base and create an opportunity to escape.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to get an underhook in BJJ?

    Shoulder pain often occurs when white belts rely on "pulling" with their arm instead of driving their shoulder and torso into the opponent. This creates excessive strain on the rotator cuff. To fix this, focus on lowering your hips and driving your shoulder into their body, creating a solid frame with your bicep and chest.

    Q: How can I get an underhook against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is key. Instead of trying to match their strength, focus on using your hips and core to drive into their structure, making their size a disadvantage. Secure grips on their collar or sleeve to help control their posture, and always aim to get your shoulder pit to their hip crease for maximum leverage.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt an underhook in BJJ, and when should I avoid it?

    The underhook is best attempted when your opponent's posture is compromised, or when they are trying to pass your guard. It's a powerful tool for controlling their base and setting up sweeps or submissions. Avoid attempting an underhook if your opponent has a very strong frame or is already in a dominant passing position, as it can leave you exposed.

Underhook: The Foundation of BJJ Control for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

The underhook is a dominant position typically achieved from guard or side control, aiming to establish superior body control and initiate offensive actions like sweeps or passes. White belts often struggle by over-relying on upper body strength instead of proper hip engagement, leading to ineffective control and vulnerability.

The core biomechanical insight is that the underhook is not just an arm position; it's a connection that leverages your hips and core to drive your opponent's weight and structure offline, creating opportunities for advancement.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a kneeling or seated position (e.g., closed guard bottom or side control bottom), identify the opponent's arm nearest to your head.
    2. Drive your chest forward, slightly angling your hips to the side of the opponent's head you are approaching.
    3. Insert your dominant arm (e.g., right arm) *under* the opponent's armpit, reaching towards their opposite shoulder blade.
    4. Secure a grip on the opponent's far shoulder blade or the fabric of their gi on their upper back, ensuring your bicep is tight against their ribcage.
    5. Simultaneously, drive your forearm and elbow into the opponent's side, creating a strong frame against their torso.
    6. Engage your core by slightly tucking your tailbone and flattening your lumbar spine, creating a stable base.
    7. Transfer your weight forward onto your hips, driving them towards the opponent's centerline while maintaining the underhook grip and frame.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly reaching for the underhook: Extending the arm fully and straightening the elbow while the opponent applies pressure can lead to a hyperextended elbow joint, potentially damaging the ulnar collateral ligament (UCL).
    • Using only arm strength: Trying to pull the opponent into an underhook without hip engagement causes strain on the shoulder joint and rotator cuff muscles as you attempt to create leverage solely with your arms.
    • Sloppy hip placement: Failing to drive hips forward and instead letting them fall back opens your base and allows the opponent to easily disengage or pass your guard, risking a knee or ankle injury if you fall off-balance.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Engagements: Practice driving your hips forward while maintaining a simulated underhook grip against a wall or mat. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Partnered Underhook Entry (Static): With a compliant partner in your guard, practice entering the underhook without any movement from them. Focus on hip angle and arm insertion. (20 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    3. Underhook Framing and Hip Drive: From guard, have your partner gently try to pass. Focus on establishing the underhook and driving your hips to create space and prevent the pass. (30 reps, 25% resistance)
    4. Underhook Sweep Setup: Practice entering the underhook from guard and executing a basic sweep (e.g., scissor sweep) with minimal resistance from your partner. (20 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Side Control Underhook Control: From bottom side control, practice securing the underhook and using your hips and frame to prevent your partner from escaping or establishing dominant positions. (30 reps, 75% resistance)
    6. Live Rolling (Guard Retention): Focus on maintaining your guard and using underhooks defensively when your partner attempts to pass. (5 minutes, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is in your closed guard and you want to create space for a sweep or submission.
    • When you are in side control and want to prevent your opponent from posturing up or escaping.
    • When you are defending a guard pass and need to re-establish a dominant position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Overhook Establishment: If your opponent attempts an underhook, immediately counter by securing an overhook on the same side. This creates a cross-face pressure and limits their ability to drive hips forward.
    • Framing with Forearm: If your opponent inserts an underhook, quickly place your forearm between their bicep and ribs, creating a strong frame to prevent their hip drive and maintain distance.
    • Hips Away and Base Adjustment: If your opponent achieves a strong underhook, immediately create distance by pushing your hips away and widening your base, making it harder for them to leverage your structure.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try to get an underhook in BJJ?

    Shoulder pain often stems from trying to muscle the underhook with your arm alone, rather than using your hips and core. This leads to overextension of the shoulder joint and strain on the rotator cuff. To fix this, focus on driving your hips forward and angling your body to get under the opponent's arm, rather than just reaching. Ensure your elbow is bent and you're using your forearm to create a tight frame.

    Q: How do I get an underhook against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are more critical than strength. Instead of trying to force an underhook head-on, use your hips to create angles and get to the side of their body. Look for moments when they are slightly off-balance or have their weight forward. Drive your hips into their side and use a tight frame with your forearm to prevent them from re-centering their weight, then secure the grip on their back.

    Q: When is the best time to go for an underhook from the bottom of closed guard?

    The optimal time to go for an underhook from closed guard is when your opponent is posturing up and their weight is slightly forward, creating a small window of opportunity. As they try to create space or advance their position, you can use your legs to slightly shift your hips and insert your arm under their armpit. This allows you to break their posture and set up sweeps like the scissor sweep or hip bump sweep.

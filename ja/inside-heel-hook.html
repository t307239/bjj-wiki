This technique targets the opponent's heel from specific leg entanglement positions, aiming to hyperextend the knee or twist the ankle.

White belts often rush, fail to secure proper positioning, and apply pressure incorrectly, leading to injury.

The key is controlling the opponent's hip and isolating the heel with precise body alignment and leverage.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a 50/50 guard or similar position, establish a "honeyhole" grip: your forearm locks the opponent's hamstring, and your other hand grips their heel from the outside, thumb pointing towards the sole.
    2. Drive your hips down and slightly to your right, creating a fulcrum point with your right hip on the opponent's buttock.
    3. Extend your left leg straight, pushing your hips away from the opponent to create tension on their heel.
    4. Keep your left knee pointed towards the ceiling to prevent inward rotation of the opponent's leg.
    5. Your right shin should be tight against the opponent's ankle, preventing it from rotating freely.
    6. Apply pressure by arching your back and driving your hips forward while maintaining the "honeyhole" grip.
    7. The opponent's heel is now trapped and will experience torque when you increase hip extension.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Heel Grip: Gripping the side of the heel instead of the "honeyhole" allows the foot to rotate, leading to uncontrolled twisting.
    • Damage: Tibia and fibula fractures, or severe ligament tears in the ankle and knee.
    • Correct: Secure the heel with your forearm on the hamstring and your hand on the heel, thumb towards the sole.
    • Leg Extension without Hip Control: Extending your legs without anchoring your hips to the opponent's creates slack and allows them to escape or counter.
    • Damage: Potential for knee hyperextension in the wrong direction for you, or shoulder/neck strain from uncontrolled movement.
    • Correct: Drive your hips down and into the opponent's buttock before extending.
    • Knee Valgus Pressure: Allowing your supporting knee (the one not directly on the opponent's buttock) to collapse inward exposes your own knee to injury.
    • Damage: Medial collateral ligament (MCL) tear or meniscus damage.
    • Correct: Keep your supporting knee pointed slightly outward and maintain a wide base.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Isolation (0%): Practice the "honeyhole" grip and hip drive motion without a partner, focusing on feel and body alignment. (20 reps).
    2. Static Partner (25%): Partner lies on their back, you secure the Inside Heel Hook position without any resistance. Focus on grip and hip placement. (10 reps per side).
    3. Passive Resistance (50%): Partner allows you to establish the position and applies minimal defense (no active pulling or bridging). Focus on achieving the correct leverage. (10 reps per side).
    4. Controlled Resistance (75%): Partner actively tries to defend by posturing up or turning their hips, but doesn't aggressively counter. Focus on maintaining control and applying pressure. (8 reps per side).
    5. Simulated Live (90%): Start from a common entry position (e.g., 50/50 guard) and attempt the submission against a resisting partner who is trying to escape but not actively attack. (5 reps per side).
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Attempt the technique in a live rolling scenario, understanding that it may not always be successful. Focus on positional awareness and safety. (2-minute rounds, focus on 1 attempt per round).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have achieved a solid 50/50 guard, with both competitors' legs intertwined.
    • When the opponent attempts to stack you from a leg entanglement, creating an opportunity to isolate their heel.
    • During transitions from open guard where the opponent's leg is exposed and you can quickly secure the "honeyhole" grip.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • The "Heel Hook Escape" (Knees to Chest): As the opponent attempts the "honeyhole" grip, quickly bring both knees to your chest, tucking your heels under your buttock. This shortens the lever arm and prevents the heel from being isolated.
    • The "Hip Escape and Turn" (Escape to Guard): If the opponent secures the "honeyhole" grip but hasn't fully extended, bridge your hips away and turn your body towards your trapped leg. Aim to create space to untangle your leg and return to a guard position.
    • The "Stomp and Posture Up" (Defensive Posture): If you feel the pressure building and can't escape, stomp your free foot on the mat and drive your hips up. This can sometimes break the grip or create enough discomfort to force the opponent to release.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try to do an Inside Heel Hook even if I don't finish it?

    This often occurs when you're not properly controlling the opponent's hip with your own. Without this crucial hip-to-hip connection, your body alignment is compromised, and you might inadvertently torque your own ankle or knee as you try to apply pressure. Ensure your hips are driving down and slightly into the opponent's buttock before you extend your legs.

    Q: How can I safely practice the Inside Heel Hook against a bigger opponent as a white belt?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on precision and leverage over brute force. Start by only drilling the setup and grip acquisition with 0-25% resistance. Once you can consistently secure the position, progress to passive partners (50%) who understand the importance of not resisting too hard. Never attempt to crank or force the submission; always tap early if you feel any discomfort.

    Q: Is it okay for white belts to learn Inside Heel Hooks, or is it too dangerous?

    It is essential for white belts to learn the fundamentals of leg locks, including the Inside Heel Hook, but with extreme caution and under direct supervision. The danger lies in incorrect application and lack of awareness. By focusing on biomechanically accurate movements, understanding the specific grips, and practicing with controlled partners, white belts can learn the technique safely and effectively.

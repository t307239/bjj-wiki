The Inside Heel Hook is a submission targeting the opponent's knee, primarily initiated from a 50-50 guard or similar leg entanglement. It aims to hyperextend the knee joint by applying rotational force to the heel.

White belts often fail due to a lack of understanding of the precise body mechanics and a tendency to force the movement, increasing injury risk for both parties.

The key mechanical insight is isolating the opponent's heel and creating a strong "figure four" grip while simultaneously collapsing your body weight onto their leg, generating torque through your hips and core.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a 50-50 guard, secure your opponent's heel with your arm, placing your bicep deep in their ankle pit. Your forearm should cradle the Achilles tendon.
    2. Grip your own shin with your opposite hand, creating a "figure four" lock around the opponent's ankle. Ensure your grip is firm on your own leg, not the opponent's.
    3. Position your hips so they are perpendicular to the opponent's leg, creating a strong fulcrum. Your hips should be elevated, not flat on the mat.
    4. Drive your shoulder into the opponent's thigh, creating upward pressure and preventing them from hip escaping.
    5. Collapse your chest towards your own knee on the side of the trapped leg. This pulls the opponent's heel into your bicep.
    6. Initiate the finish by driving your hips forward and upward, away from the opponent's knee. Simultaneously, pull your own shin towards your chest.
    7. The rotational force is generated by the combined action of your hip drive and the "figure four" grip, causing the heel to twist internally.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Grasping the opponent's foot loosely: This can lead to the foot slipping out, causing the attacker to lose position and potentially injure their own knee by overextending.
    • Not securing the "figure four" grip on your own shin: This results in a weak lock, allowing the opponent to escape and potentially causing your own wrist or elbow to hyperextend.
    • Forcing the finish with upper body strength: This is biomechanically inefficient and can lead to shoulder or back injuries for the attacker, while also risking knee ligament tears for the defender if not applied with control.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the "figure four" grip and hip elevation without a partner. Focus on the feeling of isolating the ankle and creating the correct hip angle. (50 reps)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a compliant partner, practice securing the Inside Heel Hook from 50-50. Focus on precise grip placement and body positioning. (20 reps per side)
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to hip escape. Focus on maintaining hip pressure and the correct body collapse. (15 reps per side)
    4. Controlled submission drill: Partner "taps" when they feel significant pressure. Focus on smooth transitions and controlled application. (10 reps per side)
    5. Scenario drill (live rolling, limited): Start from 50-50 and attempt the Inside Heel Hook. Partner can defend lightly but must tap immediately if caught. (5 rounds, 2 minutes each)
    6. Live rolling (controlled): Attempt the Inside Heel Hook during live rolling, prioritizing safety and control. Tap early if in danger. (10 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have achieved a stable 50-50 guard with control of the opponent's heel.
    • When the opponent attempts to stack or pressure you heavily from the 50-50 position.
    • When the opponent's leg is extended, making it more vulnerable to rotational force.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape and Re-Guard: As the attacker secures the "figure four," immediately hip escape to create space and attempt to recover your guard or transition to a different position.
    • Straightening the Attacker's Leg: If the attacker tries to collapse their body, attempt to straighten your own leg to relieve pressure and prevent the torque.
    • "Heel Out" Defense: If the attacker has a weak grip, try to "kick" your heel out of their bicep and ankle pit by driving your knee forward and away.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try the Inside Heel Hook?

    Wrist pain often occurs when the "figure four" grip is not properly secured on your own shin, or when you are gripping the opponent's leg too directly. Ensure your forearm is deep in their ankle pit and your grip is on your own tibia, not their foot. This transfers the pressure to your stronger leg bones and muscles, not your delicate wrist joint.

    Q: How can I finish the Inside Heel Hook against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise body mechanics are crucial. Focus on elevating your hips significantly to create a strong fulcrum and driving your shoulder into their thigh to prevent them from posturing up. The "collapse" of your chest towards your knee is vital to pull their heel into your bicep and generate the necessary torque. Do not rely on raw strength; use their size against them by controlling their limb's extension.

    Q: The Inside Heel Hook is best attempted when you have already established a dominant leg entanglement like the 50-50 guard. It's particularly effective when your opponent is trying to pass your guard or is in a compromised leg position themselves. Avoid forcing the transition if you don't have secure control of their leg, as this opens you up to counters.

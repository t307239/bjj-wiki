インサイドヒールフック：白帯のための生体力学的ガイド

The Inside Heel Hook is a leg lock attacking the opponent's heel from the inside of their leg, aiming to hyperextend the knee and ankle joints.

White belts often fail due to uncontrolled movements and a lack of understanding of joint mechanics, leading to injury.

The key mechanical insight is to isolate the opponent's heel and apply rotational pressure while keeping your own body stable.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a 50-50 guard or similar position, secure the opponent's ankle with your armpit, creating a "figure-four" grip with your own arms around their leg.
    2. Place your opponent's heel between your elbow and your torso, ensuring your forearm presses directly against the back of their heel.
    3. With your hips, create a perpendicular angle to your opponent's leg, ensuring your torso is not directly aligned with their limb.
    4. Extend your legs, straightening your own knee to create tension and prevent your opponent from easily escaping.
    5. Drive your hips forward and slightly up, creating a fulcrum point at your hip.
    6. Apply pressure by squeezing your arms together, pulling your opponent's heel into your armpit.
    7. Simultaneously, slightly lift your hips and push your chest towards your opponent's thigh to increase the rotational force on their heel.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly gripping the opponent's foot and ankle, applying pressure to the instep instead of the heel: This can cause severe hyperextension of the metatarsals and tarsals, leading to fractures and ligament tears in the foot.
    • Allowing the opponent's knee to bend excessively while applying the lock: This can create shearing forces on the ACL and MCL, resulting in ligament tears.
    • Not maintaining a stable base and allowing your own body to twist with the opponent's leg: This can lead to your own knee or hip joints being put in dangerous positions, risking sprains or tears.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the grip and body positioning on a dummy or a willing partner without resistance (0% resistance) - 20 reps.
    2. Partner drilling with no resistance: Focus on executing the steps precisely with a cooperative partner (0% resistance) - 10 reps per side.
    3. Light resistance drilling: Partner offers minimal resistance to the leg isolation and grip (25% resistance) - 10 reps per side.
    4. Controlled submission drilling: Partner defends passively, allowing you to work through the entire submission sequence (50% resistance) - 8 reps per side.
    5. Technical sparring: Focus on entering the position and attempting the submission against light, predictable defense (75% resistance) - 5 minutes.
    6. Live rolling with submission focus: Attempt the Inside Heel Hook during sparring, prioritizing safety and technique over completion (90% resistance) - 5 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is in a 50-50 guard and their heel is accessible.
    • When you have successfully transitioned to a position where you can isolate one of their legs.
    • During scrambles where you can quickly secure a leg entanglement.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Escape by "stacking" your weight onto the opponent's hips, driving your chest into their thigh to relieve pressure on the heel.
    • "Heel disconnect": If your opponent's grip is not fully secure, try to pull your heel out by rotating your foot outwards and away from their armpit.
    • "Leg-over defense": If the opponent is attempting the lock from a 50-50, you can try to bring your free leg over their hips to create space and potentially roll out.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my ankle hurt when I try to do an Inside Heel Hook?

    Your ankle pain likely stems from not isolating your opponent's heel correctly. The pressure should be on the calcaneus (heel bone) and applied rotationally. If you are applying pressure to the arch or the ball of the foot, you are risking injury to your own foot's structure. Ensure your forearm is firmly against the back of their heel and your hips are creating a stable base.

    Q: How can I safely practice Inside Heel Hook against a bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on precision and leverage over brute strength. Ensure you achieve a tight "figure-four" grip and isolate their heel effectively before initiating the hip drive. Prioritize controlling their leg and preventing them from bridging or posturing up. Use your hips to generate power and maintain your own stable base to avoid being overpowered.

    Q: What is the main difference between an Inside and Outside Heel Hook for BJJ white belts?

    The primary difference lies in the angle of attack on the opponent's leg. An Inside Heel Hook attacks the heel from the same side as your body, typically from a 50-50 or similar entanglement, and applies rotational pressure inwards. An Outside Heel Hook attacks the heel from the opposite side of your body, often from a saddle or inside sankaku position, and applies rotational pressure outwards. For white belts, understanding the specific grip and body positioning for each is crucial to avoid injury.

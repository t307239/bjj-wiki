Inside Heel Hook: A White Belt's Biomechanically Accurate Guide

BROWN★★★★★Expert
Brown帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 茶帯昇格要件

The Inside Heel Hook is a powerful leg lock that targets the opponent's knee and ankle. It is typically initiated from a 50-50 guard or a position where you have control of both of the opponent's legs.

White belts often rush this technique, forgetting the precise control needed. This leads to missed submissions and potential injury to yourself or your partner.

The core principle is isolating the heel and creating rotational torque on the knee joint by controlling the ankle and shin.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Assume a 50-50 guard position, ensuring your hips are on the same side as the opponent's hips and your knees are pointed away from each other.
    2. Secure an 'S-Grip' around the opponent's ankle: place your thumb on the inside of their heel and your fingers wrapping around the outside of their heel.
    3. Place your secondary hand on the opponent's shin, just below the knee joint, with your palm facing their leg.
    4. Drive your hips forward, creating a slight bridge, while simultaneously pulling the opponent's heel towards your body.
    5. Rotate your hips 90 degrees counter-clockwise (if attacking the opponent's right leg), ensuring your knees remain pointed away from each other.
    6. Apply pressure by extending your hips and drawing the opponent's heel further into your armpit, creating a reaping motion on their knee.
    7. Maintain a tight S-Grip and shin control, preventing the opponent from rotating their foot or leg out of the lock.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly applying pressure by 'kicking' your hips forward: This can cause hyperextension or torsion of your own knee ligament (ACL/MCL) if the opponent resists by twisting their leg.
    • Correct: Maintain a controlled hip drive and extension, focusing on drawing the opponent's heel into your armpit, not on raw power.
    • Gripping the opponent's foot too loosely, allowing it to slip: This can lead to the opponent escaping and you losing control, potentially resulting in a scramble where your own ankle or knee is vulnerable.
    • Correct: Maintain a firm S-Grip and focus on securing the heel firmly before initiating the extension.
    • Allowing your own knees to collapse inwards towards the opponent: This compromises your base and can lead to your own knee joint being twisted or injured by the opponent's leg entrapment.
    • Correct: Actively push your knees away from each other, maintaining a wide and stable base.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling (0% resistance): Practice the S-Grip and shin control on a dummy or your own leg. Focus on hip movement and rotation. 10 reps.
    2. Partner drill, no resistance (0% resistance): From 50-50 guard, practice securing the grips and executing the hip drive and rotation. Partner remains passive. 5 reps per side.
    3. Partner drill, light resistance (25% resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to grip acquisition and hip movement. Focus on maintaining control. 5 reps per side.
    4. Partner drill, controlled submission attempt (50% resistance): Partner attempts to defend the lock, but allows you to apply pressure up to the point of tap. Focus on smooth execution. 5 reps per side.
    5. Live drilling, positional control (75% resistance): Start in 50-50 guard with the goal of finishing the Inside Heel Hook. Partner actively defends but does not resist leg entanglement. 3 rounds x 2 minutes.
    6. Live rolling, full resistance (90-100% resistance): Implement the Inside Heel Hook as a natural progression from established leg entanglement positions. Focus on timing and opportunity. 5 rounds x 3 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • From 50-50 guard when the opponent's heel is exposed and their knee is oriented away from you.
    • When transitioning from a failed sweep or guard pass attempt where you have secured control of the opponent's leg.
    • During scrambles where you manage to isolate one of the opponent's legs in a favorable position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Straightening the attacked leg and creating space by pushing your hips away: This reduces the torque on the knee joint and can help relieve pressure.
    • Rotating your hips towards the attacker and attempting to 'stack' them: This shifts weight and can break the attacker's structure, making the lock harder to finish.
    • 'Kneebar Escape' by reaching for the attacker's far leg and attempting to create a counter-entanglement: This forces the attacker to address a new threat and may allow you to escape.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do the Inside Heel Hook?

    Your knee might hurt due to improper biomechanical alignment during the lock. If you are over-extending your hips or allowing your own knees to collapse inwards, you are placing undue stress on your knee joint. Ensure your hips drive forward in a controlled manner and your knees are actively pushed away from each other to maintain structural integrity.

    Q: How do I Inside Heel Hook against a bigger opponent who has strong leg defense?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on precision and leverage rather than pure strength. Secure a very tight S-Grip on the heel, ensuring no space between their heel and your hand. Use your hip extension to create torque, and maintain a strong shin control to prevent them from rotating their leg. Timing is crucial; attack when their weight is compromised.

    Q: You generally cannot directly attack an Inside Heel Hook from a standard closed guard due to the lack of leg control and exposed structure. The Inside Heel Hook is primarily a submission from leg entanglement positions like the 50-50 guard. You would typically transition from closed guard to a position that allows for leg entanglement, such as a sweep or a guard pass defense that leads to a scramble.

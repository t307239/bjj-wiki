Inside Heel Hook: A Biomechanically Precise Guide for BJJ White Belts

BROWN★★★★★Expert
Brown帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 茶帯昇格要件

The Inside Heel Hook is a powerful leg lock targeting the knee and ankle, often initiated from a 50-50 guard or similar leg entanglement. It aims to hyperextend the opponent's knee and internally rotate their ankle for submission.

White belts often struggle due to a lack of understanding of proper limb control and insufficient hip engagement. This leads to ineffective attempts and potential injury from uncontrolled movements.

The key is isolating the opponent's heel and using your hips to create rotational torque on their ankle and knee joint, not brute strength.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Starting Position: Opponent's legs are trapped, ideally in a 50-50 guard position where both competitors have control of each other's heel. Your left leg is over their right leg, and your right leg is under their left leg.
    2. Grip 1 (Far Leg): Secure a deep grip on the opponent's right pant leg, just above the ankle, with your left hand. Your thumb should be inside the pant leg, fingers outside.
    3. Grip 2 (Near Leg): With your right hand, cup the opponent's right heel from the outside. Your palm should be on the heel, fingers wrapped around the Achilles tendon area.
    4. Hip Angle: Drive your left hip down and towards the opponent's hips, creating a strong perpendicular angle between your torso and their legs.
    5. Weight Transfer: Shift your body weight onto your hips, pressing them into the mat. Your chest should be close to their thigh.
    6. Knee Control: Use your right knee to control the opponent's right knee, preventing them from straightening their leg. Your right shin should be across the front of their knee.
    7. Pulling Motion: With your left hand, pull the opponent's right pant leg towards your chest, drawing their heel closer.
    8. Pushing Motion: Simultaneously, push their heel away from you with your right hand, creating rotational pressure. Your right hand's movement is a slight outward push and internal rotation of your wrist.
    9. Completion: The combination of pulling their pant leg and pushing/rotating their heel will cause their ankle to internally rotate and their knee to hyperextend, leading to the submission.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect Hip Engagement: A white belt might lift their hips vertically instead of driving them perpendicularly. This fails to create rotational torque and can strain the lower back and hip flexors.
    • Gripping Too Loosely: Grabbing the pant leg loosely or not securing the heel firmly. This allows the opponent to escape the grip and can lead to wrist hyperextension or shoulder strain when trying to compensate.
    • Knee Pushing Instead of Controlling: Pushing the opponent's knee forward with your own knee, rather than using your shin to block and control its movement. This can lead to your own knee ligaments being stretched or torn if the opponent extends their leg forcefully.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Movement: Practice driving your hips down and perpendicular to an imaginary opponent's legs for 30 seconds. No resistance (0%).
    2. Solo Gripping and Pulling: Practice securing the pant grip and heel grip on a dummy or your own leg, then practice the pulling motion. 10 repetitions per side (0%).
    3. Partnered Isolation (No Resistance): Partner lies on their back, you establish the 50-50 position. Practice the grips and hip drive without any pressure. 5 repetitions per side (0%).
    4. Partnered Isolation (Light Resistance): Partner offers minimal resistance to the leg isolation. Focus on securing grips and initiating the hip drive. 10 repetitions per side (25%).
    5. Controlled Submission Attempts: In a controlled rolling environment, attempt the Inside Heel Hook with your partner offering light defense. Focus on correct mechanics, not finishing. 5 attempts per side (50%).
    6. Live Rolling (Controlled Environment): Attempt the Inside Heel Hook during live rolling with a partner who understands leg lock rules and will tap early. Focus on applying the mechanics correctly. 3 attempts per side (75-90%).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have successfully established a 50-50 guard.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard and their legs become accessible.
    • When you are in a position where you can isolate one of your opponent's legs, such as a deep half guard transitioning to a 50-50.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Leg Escape (Straightening): The opponent attempts to straighten their trapped leg by pushing their hips away and driving their heel forward. To counter, maintain a tight grip on the heel and pant leg, and use your hips to resist the straightening motion.
    • Re-guarding: The opponent attempts to bring their free leg back to establish guard. To counter, use your free arm to block their free leg or maintain pressure to prevent them from regaining guard.
    • Rolling Out: The opponent attempts to roll out of the submission by rotating their body. To counter, maintain control of the heel and pant leg, and follow their rotation with your hips, keeping the pressure consistent.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to do an Inside Heel Hook from 50-50?

    Wrist pain often occurs when you're trying to generate all the rotational force through your wrist alone. Instead, focus on using your entire body. Secure a firm grip on the pant leg with your thumb inside and cup the heel with your palm. Drive your hips down and perpendicular to your opponent's leg, then use a slight internal rotation of your wrist combined with pulling the pant leg towards you to create the submission torque.

    Q: How can I perform an Inside Heel Hook against a much bigger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precision are paramount. Focus on securing a very deep pant grip, almost at the ankle bone, and a firm heel cup. Your primary tool will be your hip drive; ensure your hips are perpendicular to their leg and you are driving down into the mat. Avoid trying to muscle them; instead, use their size against them by controlling their limb's rotation with your body's leverage.

    Q: When is the best time to switch from an Outside Heel Hook to an Inside Heel Hook?

    The transition is usually dictated by your opponent's defensive movements. If you're attempting an Outside Heel Hook and your opponent turns their foot inward to relieve pressure, their heel will naturally move towards your body, creating an opportunity for you to switch to an Inside Heel Hook. This often happens naturally as you adjust your body and grips to maintain control of their leg.

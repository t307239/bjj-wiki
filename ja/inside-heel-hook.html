This technique targets the opponent's ankle from a controlled leg entanglement, aiming to hyperextend the knee or invert the ankle for submission.

White belts often rush, losing control of the opponent's body and creating dangerous positions for themselves and their partner.

The key is isolating the opponent's leg and controlling their hip movement to create the necessary torque on the ankle.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a 50-50 guard or similar leg entanglement, secure your opponent's heel with your armpit, creating a "figure-four" grip with your own forearm across their shin. Your bicep should be pressing into the back of their knee.
    2. Control the opponent's hip by placing your outside leg (the one not entangled with their leg) across their pelvis, with your knee pointed towards their armpit. This prevents them from turning into you.
    3. Shift your weight onto your hips, driving your base into the mat. Your hips should be directly underneath the opponent's knee joint, not to the side.
    4. With your free hand, grip your own shin or wrist to create a strong, compact pulling mechanism. This intensifies the pressure on the heel.
    5. Initiate the submission by extending your hips away from the opponent while simultaneously pulling your own shin towards your chest. This action drives their heel into your armpit and creates the rotational force.
    6. Maintain a stable base with your non-entangled leg, keeping your knee on the mat or slightly elevated to maintain hip control.
    7. The pressure should be felt primarily on the opponent's ankle and heel, not directly on their knee or shin.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect hip angle: A white belt might place their hips too far to the side of the opponent's knee, leading to a "banana" shape in the opponent's leg. This can cause a severe MCL (medial collateral ligament) tear in the knee due to excessive valgus stress.
    • Incomplete leg isolation: Failing to fully isolate the opponent's leg and allowing their hips to rotate freely. This can result in the opponent escaping the entanglement and causing a knee-on-belly position, potentially leading to a twisted knee or ACL (anterior cruciate ligament) injury.
    • Overextension of the ankle: Applying pressure too quickly or with excessive force, leading to a sprained or torn ankle ligament (e.g., ATFL - anterior talofibular ligament) due to uncontrolled inversion or eversion.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice the figure-four grip and hip control positioning with no resistance (10 reps each side).
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): From 50-50 guard, practice securing the heel, hip control, and the finishing hip extension and pull motion. Focus on body mechanics (5 reps each side).
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Opponent lightly resists hip rotation. Focus on maintaining hip control and creating the proper finish mechanics (10 reps each side).
    4. Partner drill (controlled resistance): Opponent attempts to defend by turning their hips or pulling their leg. Focus on maintaining control and applying the submission with controlled pressure (15 reps each side).
    5. Live rolling (limited submission): Practice the setup and entry into the Inside Heel Hook from the 50-50 guard. Tap immediately if you feel too much pressure or lose control (2 minutes, 50% resistance).
    6. Live rolling (full submission): Attempt the Inside Heel Hook with proper technique and controlled finishing pressure. Focus on safety and tapping early if in danger (3 minutes, 75% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When you have achieved a stable 50-50 guard, ensuring your opponent's leg is isolated and controlled.
    • When your opponent attempts to stack you from a leg entanglement, creating an opportunity to invert and attack their heel.
    • When you have successfully switched from a failed guard pass attempt into a leg entanglement that allows for heel hook access.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape and Turn-in: The opponent can perform a strong hip escape away from the attacker's hips while simultaneously turning their body into the attacker. This elongates the attacking leg and reduces the torque on the ankle.
    • Leg Release and Base Up: The opponent can use their free leg to push off the attacker's hip or body and simultaneously pull their attacked leg free. They then focus on establishing a solid base and transitioning to a guard pass.
    • "The Turtle": The opponent can roll onto their stomach into a turtle position, tucking their legs underneath them. This effectively removes the ankle from the attacking angle and creates a safer position to disengage.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Inside Heel Hookの練習中に足首が痛くなるのはなぜですか？

    Inside Heel Hookの練習中に足首が痛くなるのは、主に足首の過度な回外（外側にひねる動き）や回内（内側にひねる動き）が原因です。これは、相手の足首を正確に捉えきれていない、または自分の腰の角度が不適切である場合に起こりやすいです。痛みを避けるには、相手の踵をしっかりと腕で固定し、自分の腰を相手の膝の真下に置くように意識してください。足首ではなく、膝関節の伸展と足首の回旋を連動させることが重要です。

    Q: Inside Heel Hookをより大きな相手に対してどのように極めますか？

    より大きな相手に対してInside Heel Hookを極めるには、相手の体重とパワーに頼るのではなく、より洗練されたテコの原理を利用する必要があります。相手の腰の動きを完全に封じることが最重要です。相手の腰を自身の腰でしっかりとコントロールし、相手が回転して逃げられないようにしてください。そして、相手の膝裏に自身の腕を深く差し込み、自分の脛を引く力を最大限に利用して、相手の足首に正確な回転力を加えることが鍵となります。

    Q: Inside Heel Hookのセットアップで、相手の腰をコントロールするのが難しいのですが、どうすれば良いですか？

    Inside Heel Hookのセットアップで相手の腰のコントロールが難しい場合、それは相手があなたの腰の近くにいるか、またはあなたの腰が相手の足から離れていることが原因です。相手の腰をコントロールするには、まず相手の足にしっかりと絡みつき、その状態を維持しながら、自分の腰を相手の膝の真下、あるいはわずかに相手の腰側に寄せるように移動させます。相手の腰に自身の片足を跨がせるようにすることで、相手の回転を効果的に防ぎ、Inside Heel Hookを極めるための安定した土台を作り出すことができます。

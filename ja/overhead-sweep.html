This sweep originates from closed guard, aiming to invert your opponent and take top position. It's a fundamental transition to gain a dominant advantage.

White belts often struggle with the Overhead Sweep due to a lack of coordinated hip and shoulder movement. They try to muscle it rather than using leverage.

The core principle is using your hips to elevate your opponent's base and then initiating a rotational unbalancing action, creating an opening for the sweep.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Establish two grips: left hand on opponent's right collar, right hand on opponent's right sleeve. Keep elbows tucked close to your body.
    2. Lift your hips off the mat by contracting your glutes and lower back, creating space under your hips.
    3. Simultaneously, pull the collar grip downwards and slightly across your body, and push the sleeve grip upwards and away.
    4. As your hips rise, drive your left knee forward between your opponent's legs, aiming for their hip crease.
    5. Rotate your torso counter-clockwise, using your right leg as a pivot point on the mat.
    6. At the apex of your hip lift and rotation, extend your right leg upwards, pushing against your opponent's right hip.
    7. Continue the rotational momentum, driving your opponent over their own shoulder as you post your left foot on the mat to stand up or transition to mount.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to lift the opponent purely with arm strength: This can lead to shoulder impingement or rotator cuff tears due to excessive strain on the glenohumeral joint.
    • Extending the knees forcefully while your hips are still low: This risks hyperextending the knee joint, potentially damaging the ACL or meniscus.
    • Arching the lower back excessively to create height: This can cause lumbar strain or disc compression by placing undue stress on the spinal erector muscles and intervertebral discs.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo hip elevation and knee drive: Practice lifting your hips and driving your left knee forward without a partner. (50 reps, 0% resistance)
    2. Partnered grip and hip lift: With a compliant partner, practice establishing grips and lifting your hips. (20 reps, 0% resistance)
    3. Controlled sweep attempt with partner: Execute the full sweep motion with a partner who provides minimal resistance and helps you recover if you lose balance. (15 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Overhead Sweep entry against light pressure: Your partner attempts to resist slightly by keeping their base solid, focusing on the hip elevation and rotation. (10 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Overhead Sweep against active defense: Your partner tries to counter the sweep by posturing up or shrimping away, forcing you to adapt your timing. (10 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling application: Integrate the Overhead Sweep into positional sparring with full resistance. (Focus on attempting 3-5 times per round, 100% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent has established a strong cross-collar grip on your closed guard.
    • When your opponent is postured up, creating an opportunity to break their posture further.
    • When your opponent attempts to pass your guard by driving their weight forward.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Guard pass retention: If your opponent anticipates the sweep and starts to drive their weight forward to pass, switch to a leg drag pass by securing their far leg and pulling it across your body.
    • Stacking defense: If your opponent tries to stack you, immediately tuck your chin to your chest and drive your hips into them, creating frames with your forearms to prevent pressure on your neck and spine.
    • Re-guarding: If the sweep is unsuccessful and your opponent is in a dominant position, focus on re-establishing guard by shrimping out and creating space to bring your knees back between you.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my shoulder hurt when I try the Overhead Sweep?

    Your shoulder likely hurts because you are trying to lift your opponent solely with your arms. The Overhead Sweep relies on hip elevation and core rotation, not arm strength. Ensure your hips are high off the mat before initiating the pull, and use your body's leverage, not just your biceps and shoulders.

    Q: How can I perform the Overhead Sweep against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and timing are even more critical. Focus on getting your hips as high as possible to create a fulcrum. Your grip on the collar should be tight and pull their head down towards your chest, breaking their posture before you attempt the sweep. Use your legs to drive their hip away.

    Q: When is the best time to attempt the Overhead Sweep in BJJ?

    The Overhead Sweep is most effective when your opponent's weight is forward and they have given you an opportunity to break their posture. This often occurs when they are trying to apply pressure, attempt a pass, or have just settled into your closed guard. Look for moments when their base is slightly compromised.

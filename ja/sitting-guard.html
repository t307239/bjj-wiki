Sitting Guard: The Biomechanically Sound Approach for White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Sitting Guard is a fundamental open guard position initiated from a seated stance, aiming to control the opponent's legs and hips to set up sweeps and submissions.

Many white belts struggle due to a lack of understanding of leverage, leading to an easily passed guard.

The key is creating a stable base and framing with your arms and legs to maintain distance and prevent the opponent from collapsing your structure.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Begin seated with knees bent and feet flat on the mat, hips slightly elevated.
    2. Grip opponent's right pant leg cuff with your left hand (cross-grip), and opponent's left pant leg cuff with your right hand (cross-grip).
    3. Extend your legs, creating a frame between your shins and the opponent's torso or hips.
    4. Maintain a slight posterior pelvic tilt to keep your base stable and prevent forward pressure.
    5. Keep your elbows tucked to your sides, reinforcing the frame with your forearms.
    6. Adjust your foot placement on the mat behind your hips to control distance and mobility.
    7. If the opponent advances, shift your hips to the side to create angles and maintain your frame.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Sitting with an upright, unsupported back: This hyper-extends the lumbar spine, risking muscle strains and disc compression.

    Correct: Maintain a slight posterior pelvic tilt, engaging your core for spinal support.

    • Gripping too high on the opponent's pants (e.g., knee area): This allows the opponent to easily unhook your feet and pass.

    Correct: Grip securely at the pant cuff, maintaining a consistent frame.

    • Extending legs straight without hip connection: This creates a weak, easily broken frame, leaving your hips vulnerable to stacking.

    Correct: Keep a slight bend in your knees and connect your shins to the opponent's body.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice sitting up, extending legs, and returning to the starting position for 2 minutes (0% resistance).
    2. Solo drill: Practice gripping pant legs and extending legs, focusing on frame creation for 2 minutes (0% resistance).
    3. Partner drill: With a compliant partner, practice establishing Sitting Guard and maintaining the frame for 5 repetitions per side (25% resistance).
    4. Partner drill: Practice transitioning between Sitting Guard and other basic guards (e.g., closed guard) with a compliant partner, 5 repetitions per side (50% resistance).
    5. Partner drill: Practice basic sweeps from Sitting Guard (e.g., scissor sweep) with a semi-compliant partner, 5 successful attempts (75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling: Apply Sitting Guard against a resisting opponent, focusing on maintaining your frame and attempting sweeps/submissions (100% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When the opponent is standing or in a kneeling position directly in front of you.
    • When the opponent attempts a takedown and you can sit back to establish your guard.
    • As a transition from other guard positions if your opponent is pressuring forward too heavily.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Stack Pass: The opponent attempts to drive their weight forward, collapsing your guard. Maintain your frame by pushing with your legs and shifting your hips to the side.
    • Leg Drag: The opponent isolates and pulls one of your legs across their body. Immediately re-establish your frame with the other leg and use your grips to control their hips.
    • Knee Slice Pass: The opponent drives their knee across your body. Create space by pushing with your hips and using your framing legs to block their knee.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to do Sitting Guard?

    Your lower back pain likely stems from an anterior pelvic tilt, causing your spine to arch excessively. To fix this, consciously engage your glutes and abs to create a posterior pelvic tilt, flattening your lower back against the mat. This stabilizes your core and protects your lumbar spine.

    Q: Against a larger opponent, your primary focus should be on creating a strong frame and using your legs to control distance. Grip their pants at the ankles and extend your legs fully, using your shins to push against their hips. Continuously shift your hips to create angles, making it harder for them to drive their weight directly into you.

    Q: Sitting Guard is definitively an open guard. Unlike closed guard where your ankles are locked around the opponent's waist, in Sitting Guard your legs are extended, creating frames and allowing for more dynamic movement and transitions to different open guard positions.

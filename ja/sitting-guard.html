Sitting Guard BJJ: The Ultimate White Belt Guide

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Sitting Guard is a fundamental guard position initiated from a kneeling or seated stance, aiming to control your opponent's base and create sweep or submission opportunities. White belts often struggle by collapsing their structure or overextending, losing their frames and inviting passes.

The key mechanical insight is establishing a strong, upright posture with your hips as the anchor, using your legs and arms to create angles and distance, not just to hold on.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Start from a kneeling position, facing your opponent. Sit up, lifting your hips slightly off the mat.
    2. Place your feet on your opponent's hips, with knees bent at approximately 90 degrees. This creates your primary frame.
    3. Grip your opponent's sleeves or collar with both hands. For sleeve grips, aim for the wrist area for maximum control.
    4. Maintain an upright torso, engaging your core. Your spine should be neutral, not rounded or hyperextended.
    5. Keep your elbows tucked in, creating a tight structure. Avoid letting your arms flail or extend away from your body.
    6. Distribute your weight evenly, with your hips providing a stable base. Avoid leaning too far forward or backward.
    7. Use subtle hip movements to create angles. Shifting your hips to one side (e.g., left) allows you to attack the opposite side (e.g., right).

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Knee Valgus Collapse: Extending knees inward while opponent applies pressure. This can damage the ACL and MCL ligaments in the knee. Maintain knees pointing slightly outward, wider than hip-width, especially when framing.
    • Lumbar Hyperextension: Arching your lower back excessively when trying to create space. This strains the lumbar spine, leading to pain and potential disc issues. Keep your core engaged and spine neutral, using your hips for movement, not your back.
    • Elbow Hyperextension: Straightening your arm completely and locking the elbow under pressure. This can cause ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) damage or biceps tendonitis. Maintain a slight bend in your elbows and use your frames actively, not passively.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Escapes (0%): Practice sitting up, lifting hips, and moving hips side-to-side without a partner. 10 reps each side.
    2. Partnered Framing (25%): Partner stands in front. Practice establishing foot frames on hips and maintaining an upright posture. 5 sets of 30 seconds.
    3. Grip Fighting (50%): With partner standing, practice establishing sleeve or collar grips and maintaining frames while they attempt to break grips. 5 sets of 1 minute.
    4. Guard Retention (75%): Partner attempts to pass guard. Focus solely on maintaining frames and hip position to prevent the pass, not on attacking. 5 rounds of 2 minutes.
    5. Sweep Practice (90%): Execute a basic sweep (e.g., scissor sweep) from Sitting Guard with a resisting partner who is actively defending. 5 reps per sweep.
    6. Live Rolling (100%): Implement Sitting Guard during live rolling, focusing on maintaining position and attempting sweeps or submissions. 3 rounds of 5 minutes.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is in front of you, standing or kneeling, and within your leg reach.
    • When you are on your back and have successfully defended a guard pass attempt.
    • To transition from a collapsed guard to a more active, structured position.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Knee-to-Chest Frame: If opponent pressures forward, drive your knees towards their chest and use your arms on their shoulders to create a strong frame, pushing them away.
    • Hip Escape and Re-Guard: If opponent attempts to pass to one side, immediately hip escape away from their pressure and re-establish your leg frames, potentially transitioning to closed guard or half guard.
    • Stand Up and Grip Fight: If opponent is very heavy and pressure passing, you can sometimes stand up into them (if safe) to break their posture and initiate a grip fight to regain control.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my wrist hurt when I try to grip fight in Sitting Guard?

    Wrist pain often stems from gripping too tightly with your fingers extended and then having that grip broken by an opponent's strong counter-force. Instead, focus on a relaxed but secure grip, keeping your wrist in a neutral position (not bent excessively backward or forward). Imagine you are holding a bird; you don't want to crush it, but you don't want it to fly away either. Use your forearm and bicep to support the grip, not just your fingers.

    Q: How do I maintain Sitting Guard against a much bigger opponent who is trying to smash pass me?

    Against a larger opponent, your frames become even more critical. Focus on keeping your knees wide and your hips active to create space, preventing them from collapsing your guard. Use your arms to create a strong barrier on their shoulders or biceps, always maintaining a slight bend in your elbows to absorb impact. Your goal is not to hold them still, but to constantly create angles and prevent them from establishing a dominant, forward pressure.

    Q: When should I transition out of Sitting Guard, and what are common mistakes?

    You should transition out of Sitting Guard when your opponent is posturing up strongly, making it difficult to maintain frames, or when they have successfully initiated a strong pass. A common mistake is trying to force a sweep or submission when the position is compromised, leading to a breakdown of your guard. Instead, recognize when to disengage, reset your hips, and transition to a more advantageous guard like closed guard or even a defensive turtle, rather than getting passed.

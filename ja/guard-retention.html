BJJ Guard Retention: The White Belt's Essential Defense

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Guard retention starts when your opponent attempts to pass your guard, aiming to prevent them from establishing a dominant position like side control.

White belts often fail by reacting passively, allowing the opponent's weight and leverage to crush their hips and legs.

The core principle is creating and maintaining frames with your limbs to control distance and angles, forcing the opponent to re-engage their pass attempt.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Opponent attempts to pass your closed guard. Immediately bring your knees to your chest, creating a tight "box" with your shins.
    2. Establish "forearm frames": Place your forearms firmly against the opponent's biceps, just above the elbow joint. Your elbows should be tucked close to your body.
    3. "Shrimp" your hips: With your feet flat on the mat, push off the mat and move your hips to the side, away from the direction the opponent is trying to pass. This creates space.
    4. "Scissor" your legs: As you shrimp, extend one leg to block the opponent's hip and the other leg to block their knee. This prevents them from advancing their hips or knees past your legs.
    5. "Re-hook" your guard: Once space is created, use your extended legs to re-establish your closed guard by bringing your feet back around the opponent's hips and locking your ankles.
    6. "Control the distance": Maintain constant pressure with your forearms against their biceps and your knees towards their chest. Never let your limbs go completely straight.
    7. "Hip angle adjustment": If the opponent tries to pass to your right, shrimp your hips to your left and create frames on their right bicep and left hip. Your body should form a diagonal line, not a straight one.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly "kicking out" your legs: This can hyperextend your knees, potentially tearing the ACL or MCL. Always maintain a "bent knee" posture.
    • Overextending your arms to "push" the opponent: This can lead to elbow hyperextension injuries or shoulder dislocations if the opponent uses your force against you. Maintain frames with bent elbows.
    • Letting your hips get "flattened": If your hips are flat on the mat, the opponent can easily stack you and pass. Always maintain a "bucketed" hip position, with your knees pulled towards your chest.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo "Shrimping" Drill: Practice shrimping your hips side-to-side for 30 seconds, focusing on hip movement and knee tuck. 10 reps, 0% resistance.
    2. Solo "Frame" Drill: Lie on your back, practice placing forearm frames on imaginary opponent's biceps and scissor-blocking hips/knees. 20 reps, 0% resistance.
    3. Partner Drill (No Resistance): Partner lies on their back, you attempt to pass their guard. They focus *only* on maintaining frames and shrimping to retain guard. 5 reps each, 0% resistance.
    4. Partner Drill (Light Resistance): Partner attempts to pass guard with very light pressure. You focus on retaining guard using frames and shrimping. 5 reps each, 25% resistance.
    5. "Guard Retention Scenarios": Partner attempts a specific pass (e.g., Torreando). You practice retaining guard for 15 seconds, then reset. 5 reps each, 50% resistance.
    6. Live Rolling (Guard Retention Focus): Roll with a partner, with the goal being *only* to retain your guard for the entire round. Your partner tries to pass. 3 rounds, 75% resistance.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When an opponent's knee enters your closed guard, threatening to break it open.
    • When an opponent drives forward with their chest and shoulders, trying to "smash" your legs.
    • When an opponent attempts to "grip break" your closed guard by pulling your legs apart.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Forearm & Shin Frames: Create "L" shapes with your arms and legs to push the opponent's weight and body away. Keep your elbows and knees bent.
    • Hip Escapes (Shrimping): Move your hips perpendicular to the opponent's direction of pressure, creating space to re-establish your guard.
    • "Re-Hooking" the Guard: Once space is made, quickly bring your feet back around the opponent's hips and lock your ankles to close the guard again.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to keep my guard closed?

    Your lower back pain likely stems from over-arching your spine to compensate for a lack of hip flexion. Ensure your knees are pulled tightly towards your chest, creating a "scooped" or "bucketed" pelvic tilt. This engages your core and allows your hips to act as a natural cushion, rather than relying on your lumbar spine.

    Q: How do I retain my guard against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on using your frames as levers to create micro-spaces and off-balance them. Your forearms against their biceps and shins against their hips are crucial. Use explosive hip escapes (shrimping) to create just enough room to re-hook your guard, rather than trying to push their entire weight away.

    Q: When should I actively try to retain my guard versus just letting them pass and resetting?

    You should actively retain your guard whenever the opponent is *within* your guard and has not yet achieved a dominant passing position (like side control). Letting them pass without resistance allows them to accumulate points and progress to more dangerous positions. Aim to re-establish your guard immediately upon feeling pressure indicating a pass is imminent.

Guard Retention: The Foundation for BJJ White Belts

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Guard retention starts from being passed and aims to re-establish your guard position. White belts often lose guard by reacting passively or using insufficient framing.

The single most crucial mechanical insight is creating frames with your forearms and hips to dictate distance and angles, preventing knee-to-hip connection.

This prevents the opponent from collapsing your legs and passing.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Opponent attempts to pass your guard (e.g., knee-slice-pass). Immediately establish frames with both forearms extended, palms facing the opponent's hips/thighs.
    2. Create a 90-degree angle between your torso and the opponent's attacking leg by shifting your hips to that side.
    3. Your opposite leg (the one not being attacked) should drive its knee towards the opponent's hip, creating a wedge.
    4. Grip control: Use a sleeve grip on the opponent's attacking arm, pulling it towards you to break their posture.
    5. Maintain a wide base with your feet on the mat or hips, ensuring your pelvis is elevated off the mat.
    6. If the opponent advances, shrimp your hips away, maintaining forearm frames to keep them at bay.
    7. Re-establish closed-guard by bringing your heels to your hips and locking your ankles if distance is recovered.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly tucking your chin and rounding your upper back: This exposes your neck to chokes and can lead to cervical spine hyperextension if the opponent stacks, damaging ligaments and discs.
    • Overextending your arms to push the opponent: This places excessive strain on the elbow joint's ligaments, risking sprains or tears, and offers no stable frame.
    • Allowing the opponent's knee to connect with your hip: This collapses your guard structure, risking hip impingement injuries and allowing easy guard passes.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice shrimping, hip escapes, and forearm framing against an imaginary opponent for 5 minutes, 3 sets.
    2. Partner drill (0% resistance): Opponent lies down, you practice establishing frames and shrimping away. 10 reps each side.
    3. Partner drill (25% resistance): Opponent applies light pressure, you practice maintaining frames and recovering guard. 10 reps each side.
    4. Partner drill (50% resistance): Opponent attempts basic guard passes (e.g., knee-slice), you focus solely on retention. 5 minutes.
    5. Partner drill (75% resistance): Opponent actively tries to pass, you work to prevent the pass and re-establish guard. 5 minutes.
    6. Live rolling (90-100% resistance): Focus entirely on guard retention for 3 minutes, without attempting submissions. 3 rounds.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When an opponent is actively trying to pass your guard, especially with forward pressure.
    • When you feel your guard structure beginning to break down (e.g., opponent's knee close to your hip).
    • As a reactive measure when an opponent initiates a leg-entanglement sweep or pass.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Forearm Framing and Hip Escape: Extend forearms to create space, then shrimp hips away to reset distance and angles, re-establishing leg frames.
    • Knee-to-Hip Wedge: Drive the knee of your non-attacked leg into the opponent's hip, creating a strong structural wedge to prevent them from collapsing your guard.
    • Grip Fighting for Control: Secure sleeve or collar grips to control the opponent's posture and break their connection to your body, making it harder for them to advance.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my elbow hurt when I try to keep my guard as a white belt?

    Your elbow likely hurts because you are overextending your arm to push the opponent directly. This turns your forearm into a lever that can easily bend under pressure, straining the ligaments. Instead, focus on creating a rigid frame with your entire forearm and elbow, keeping your elbow slightly bent and your shoulder engaged to absorb force.

    Q: How do I retain my guard against a much bigger and stronger opponent in BJJ?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and angles become even more critical. Focus on creating frames with your forearms and hips to disrupt their base and prevent them from using their weight. Use your legs to create wedges and shrimping to create space, making them work harder to maintain pressure. Your goal is to use their weight against them by controlling their posture and balance.

    Q: When should I focus on guard retention versus trying to sweep or submit?

    Guard retention is your primary defensive posture and should be prioritized whenever your guard is under immediate threat of being passed. If the opponent is successfully breaking down your structure or advancing past your legs, attempting a sweep or submission is premature and likely to fail, leading to a worse position. Master retention first, then integrate offensive techniques.

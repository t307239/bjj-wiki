Guard Retention: The White Belt's Essential BJJ Defense

WHITE★★☆☆☆Intermediate
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Guard Retention starts from a bottom position, aiming to prevent your opponent from passing your guard and achieving a dominant position.

White belts often fail due to a passive mindset and lack of understanding of leverage, leading to predictable movements.

The single key insight is creating a strong, mobile frame with your limbs to control distance and re-establish guard.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Opponent attempts a pass (e.g., knee-slice). Immediately flatten your back to the mat, engaging your core.
    2. Place your forearms firmly against your opponent's hips, palms facing inward, creating a "frame" at a 90-degree angle to your body.
    3. Your knees should be slightly bent and tucked towards your chest, forming a protective barrier.
    4. If your opponent is pressuring forward, drive your hips slightly upwards and push your forearms outwards, widening your base.
    5. As your opponent shifts their weight, move your hips laterally (sideways) to match their movement and maintain the frame.
    6. Use your feet to hook your opponent's hips or biceps to create grips, preventing them from closing the distance.
    7. If they are on your right side, move your hips to your left and frame with your right arm. If they shift to your left, move hips right and frame with left arm.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly bracing your arms: Extending arms fully and passively allows the opponent to break your frame and drive through, potentially hyperextending your elbow joint or damaging your shoulder ligaments.
    • Stiff-arming without hip movement: Rigidly pushing outwards without moving your hips leaves you static and vulnerable to being swept or passed. This can lead to knee or hip joint strain from uncontrolled twisting.
    • Allowing opponent to grip your legs: Not actively controlling distance or framing allows your opponent to secure leg grips, leading to a strong base for passes. This can result in ankle or knee ligament damage if you resist passively.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Escape and Frame Drill: Practice hip escapes (shrimping) from your back while maintaining a strong forearm frame against an imaginary opponent. 50 reps.
    2. Partner Assisted Frame Drill (0% Resistance): Partner lies on their back. You practice placing and holding frames against their hips, moving hips laterally as they gently push. 10 reps per side.
    3. Partner Guard Retention vs. Gentle Pressure (25% Resistance): Partner attempts to slowly drive their hips into your frame. Focus on maintaining the frame and hip movement. 5 mins.
    4. Partner Guard Retention vs. Slow Pass Attempt (50% Resistance): Partner attempts a slow knee-slice pass. Practice framing and shrimping to keep them away. 5 mins.
    5. Partner Guard Retention vs. Controlled Pass Attempt (75% Resistance): Partner attempts a pass with moderate speed and control. Focus on re-establishing guard or maintaining space. 5 mins.
    6. Light Sparring (90% Resistance): Focus purely on guard retention. If the pass is successful, reset to guard. 3 rounds.

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When your opponent is attempting to pass your guard from a standing or kneeling position.
    • When your opponent has established side control or mount and you are trying to re-establish guard from the bottom.
    • When an opponent is pressuring into your closed guard and trying to break your posture.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Hip Escape (Shrimping): Move your hips away from the pressure while maintaining your frame, creating space to re-establish guard.
    • Framing with Forearms/Elbows: Create a rigid barrier with your arms against the opponent's hips or shoulders to prevent them from advancing.
    • Leg Hook Grips: Use your feet to hook the opponent's hips or biceps, providing leverage to push them away and control distance.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my back hurt when I try to retain my guard against a heavier opponent?

    Your back may hurt because you are relying on lumbar extension rather than core engagement and hip movement. When a heavier opponent pressures, your spine bears the brunt. Engage your abdominal muscles to create a strong core unit and use your hips to absorb and redirect their weight through hip escapes (shrimping), not by arching your back.

    Q: How do I retain my guard if my opponent is much bigger and stronger than me?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise framing are paramount. Focus on creating a solid "frame" with your forearms against their hips or shoulders, angled to push their weight away. Use your hip escapes to constantly shift your base and prevent them from settling their weight on you. Never try to match their strength directly; instead, use their momentum against them by creating angles.

    Q: When should I focus on guard retention versus trying to sweep or submit?

    Guard retention is the foundational defense. You should prioritize guard retention whenever your opponent is actively trying to pass your guard or achieve a dominant position. Sweeps and submissions are secondary; if your guard is passed, your opportunities for offense drastically decrease. Focus on maintaining your guard first, then look for offensive opportunities once the immediate threat is neutralized.

BJJ ガードリテンション：白帯の基礎

白★★☆☆☆中級
White帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 白帯ガイド

Guard retention is the active defense against a guard pass, starting from your guard positions like closed guard or open guard. It prevents your opponent from establishing dominant positions like side control or mount.

White belts often fail due to a passive mindset, waiting to be passed rather than actively preventing it. They lack the understanding of how to create frames and use their body weight effectively.

The single key insight is using your limbs as levers and your hips as a base to create space and re-establish your guard before the pass is complete.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. Grip: Opponent's hip (pant grip) or wrist (double wrist grip).
    2. Frame: Place forearm between your chest and opponent's hip/shoulder.
    3. Hip Angle: Drive your hips away from the opponent's pressure, creating a 45-degree angle.
    4. Weight Transfer: Shift your weight onto your elbow and the hip that is driving away.
    5. Knee Direction: Keep your knees slightly tucked towards your chest, ready to re-establish guard.
    6. Leg Movement: Use your free leg to hook behind the opponent's leg or push their hip away.
    7. Core Engagement: Actively engage your abdominal muscles to maintain tension and stability.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Error: Grabbing opponent's gi with a loose grip and pulling them towards you.
    • Injury Risk: Torn shoulder or elbow ligaments due to uncontrolled pulling force, specifically the rotator cuff or UCL.
    • Correct Action: Maintain frames and push the opponent away, creating space to re-establish your guard.
    • Error: Allowing opponent to flatten you out by not creating hip space.
    • Injury Risk: Lumbar spine hyperextension or sacroiliac joint sprain from excessive arching.
    • Correct Action: Drive your hips away to create a 45-degree angle, keeping your back flat.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo Hip Escape & Frame Drill: Practice hip escapes with no opponent. Focus on creating space. (10 reps, 0% resistance).
    2. Partnered Frame Drill: Partner stands, you practice framing and pushing their hips away from a seated position. (10 reps, 25% resistance).
    3. Guard Retention Against Static Pass: Partner attempts a slow knee-slice pass. You focus on frames and hip escapes. (10 reps, 50% resistance).
    4. Guard Retention Against Slow Pass: Partner attempts a controlled torreando or knee-slice pass. Focus on re-establishing guard. (10 reps, 75% resistance).
    5. Guard Retention Against Controlled Pass: Partner attempts a pass with moderate speed and intent. Focus on preventing the pass. (10 reps, 90% resistance).
    6. Live Rolling - Guard Retention Focus: Spar with a partner, with the explicit goal of retaining guard for the entire round. (3 rounds, 100% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When an opponent attempts to pass your guard by flattening you out.
    • When an opponent is establishing a strong base to begin a pass.
    • When you feel your guard being broken and the opponent is moving towards side control.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Hip Escape & Frame: Drive your hips away from the opponent's pressure, simultaneously creating a frame with your forearm against their hip or shoulder to create space and prevent them from flattening you.
    • Knee Shield Re-establishment: Use your free leg to create a "knee shield" by placing your shin across the opponent's chest or hip, blocking their forward momentum and allowing you to adjust your hips.
    • Guard Pull (Controlled): If a pass is imminent, a controlled guard pull can be used to bring the opponent back into a closed guard or butterfly guard position, resetting the engagement.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my lower back hurt when I try to keep my guard against a bigger opponent?

    Your lower back pain is likely due to hyperextension. When a bigger opponent applies pressure, you might be arching your back to create space instead of using your hips. Focus on driving your hips *away* from their pressure and engaging your core to keep your spine neutral. This uses your pelvis as the primary moving structure, not your lumbar spine.

    Q: How do I stop my opponent from passing my guard when they have a strong grip on my pants?

    When your opponent has strong pant grips, your priority is to break those grips or neutralize their effect. Instead of pulling your legs out, use your hands to grip their wrists or forearms. Simultaneously, drive your hips away to create space, making their grip less effective. Then, use your knees to create frames and re-establish your guard.

    Q: When should I start thinking about Guard Retention, not just holding my guard closed?

    You should be thinking about Guard Retention the moment your opponent begins to apply pressure to pass. This means actively creating frames and space as they advance, not waiting until they are already past your knees or hips. The goal is to *prevent* the pass before it's almost complete, not to recover after it has happened.

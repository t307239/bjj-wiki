The 50/50 Guard is an open guard position where both competitors' legs are entangled in a specific manner, aiming to control the opponent's base and attack submissions. White belts often struggle due to a lack of body awareness, leading to uncontrolled entanglements and potential injury.

The core principle is creating a strong, unbreakable structure with your legs to deny the opponent's movement and establish leverage for sweeps or submissions.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a seated guard, establish a standard open guard grip: grip opponent's left pants leg with your right hand and their right pants leg with your left hand.
    2. Internally rotate your right hip and swing your right leg over the opponent's right leg, aiming to place your right shin across the opponent's left thigh.
    3. Simultaneously, bring your left leg to the inside of the opponent's right leg, hooking their right ankle with your left foot's instep.
    4. Your right shin should be perpendicular to the opponent's thigh, creating a 'figure-four' leg entanglement with your opponent.
    5. Your left foot's instep should be pushing against the opponent's right ankle bone, preventing them from easily stepping out.
    6. Maintain a posterior pelvic tilt (tuck your tailbone) to keep your hips close to the opponent and prevent them from stacking you.
    7. Use your grips on the pants to control the opponent's hip mobility and prevent them from posturing up effectively.
    8. Your right thigh should be pressing into the opponent's left thigh, creating a fulcrum for hip elevation.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrect knee rotation: A white belt might externally rotate their right knee when entering the 50/50, placing excessive torque on the ACL and MCL, risking a tear.

    The correct action is to internally rotate the hip and keep the knee tracking over the toes.

    • Overextending the ankle: Trying to force the entanglement by snapping the foot might hyperextend the ankle or talocrural joint, risking ligament sprains.

    The correct action is to use a controlled hook with the instep of the foot against the opponent's ankle bone.

    • Allowing the opponent to stack: Failing to maintain hip pressure and a posterior pelvic tilt allows the opponent to drive their weight forward, compressing the lower back and potentially injuring the lumbar spine.

    The correct action is to keep hips close and actively use your legs to create space and prevent the stack.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice leg entanglement without a partner. Focus on the correct hip and leg angles. (5 minutes, 0% resistance).
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): Partner stands. You practice entering the 50/50 and establishing grips. Partner remains static. (5 reps per side, 0% resistance).
    3. Partner drill (light resistance): Partner stands, gently resists your leg movements. Focus on maintaining the entanglement and grips. (5 reps per side, 25% resistance).
    4. Guard retention drill: Partner attempts to pass your guard from 50/50. You focus on maintaining the entanglement and preventing them from breaking it. (3 minutes, 50% resistance).
    5. Submission drill: From a stable 50/50, practice a specific submission like a heel hook (if permitted and with strict control). Partner offers minimal defense. (5 reps per side, 75% resistance).
    6. Live rolling (controlled): Roll with a higher-ranked belt or experienced partner who understands the 50/50. Focus on maintaining the position and executing techniques without injury. (3 minutes, 90% resistance).

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard by stepping over your legs.
    • When you are in a broken open guard and can isolate one of the opponent's legs.
    • When transitioning from a closed guard or half guard and the opponent's base is unstable.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass Defense: If the opponent attempts to stack, immediately drive your right shin into their hip and use your left foot to push their ankle away, creating space and returning to a more stable guard.
    • Sweeping the Leg: If the opponent is posturing up, use your right leg as a hook on their left thigh and your left foot to push their right ankle. Drive your hips up and to the left to sweep them over.
    • Transition to Ashi Garami: If the opponent tries to pass by stepping around, use your right leg to control their hip and your left leg to secure their ankle, transitioning to a more dominant leg entanglement position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get into 50/50 Guard?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are not properly rotating your hip internally when entering the entanglement. This causes your knee to twist outwards, putting dangerous stress on the ligaments. Focus on leading with your hip and keeping your knee aligned with your foot throughout the movement.

    Q: How do I use 50/50 Guard against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, prioritize maintaining tight control with your grips on their pants and using your legs to create a strong frame. Actively push their hip away with your right shin and use your left foot to control their ankle. Your goal is to prevent them from using their weight to crush you and to create enough leverage for a sweep.

    Q: When should I try to attack a submission from 50/50 Guard as a white belt?

    As a white belt, your primary focus in 50/50 Guard should be on understanding the position and its mechanics, not solely on submissions. Only attempt submissions when you have a completely stable and controlled entanglement, and your opponent is not actively defending or posturing up. Prioritize safety and proper technique over rushing a submission.

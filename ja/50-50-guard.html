50/50 Guard BJJ: A Biomechanically Precise Guide for White Belts

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

The 50/50 Guard is initiated from a position where both practitioners' legs are intertwined, specifically with both of your legs controlling one of your opponent's legs. It aims to create a strong platform for leg attacks or sweeps by isolating a single limb.

White belts often struggle because they focus on 'holding on' rather than controlling the opponent's structure and weight distribution. This leads to losing the leg entanglement or exposing themselves to passes.

The key insight is to use your hips and core to generate leverage against the opponent's hip and knee joint, creating an unresolvable entanglement and limiting their mobility.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a seated or kneeling position, secure a deep inside leg hook with your right leg around the opponent's right thigh, your heel digging into their hip crease.
    2. Simultaneously, place your left foot on the opponent's left hip, creating a frame to prevent them from stacking you.
    3. Grip the opponent's right ankle with your right hand, ensuring a firm palm-to-shin grip, thumb pointing upwards.
    4. With your left hand, secure a cross-grip on the opponent's right pants, just above the knee, to control their leg rotation.
    5. Shift your hips to your right, creating a 45-degree angle relative to the opponent's hips, driving your weight into their right hip.
    6. Maintain a strong posterior pelvic tilt to keep your lower back safe and engage your glutes and hamstrings.
    7. Your left leg should remain extended, pressing into their hip, and your right knee should be tucked towards your chest to maintain tightness.
    8. The opponent's right leg is now trapped between your right thigh and your right arm, with your left leg controlling their hip.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to 'pull' the opponent's leg towards you with your arms while your hips are not properly angled: This can hyperextend your own elbow or shoulder joint, leading to ligamentous tears.
    • Letting your hips fall flat to the mat while in the entanglement: This removes your base and leverage, allowing the opponent to stack or pass easily, and can put undue stress on your knee joint if they twist.
    • Over-extending your trapped leg to create space: This can lead to a knee ligament sprain (MCL/LCL) if the opponent forcefully rotates their leg away or attempts a pass.
    • Releasing the ankle grip prematurely while still entangled: This allows the opponent to escape the entanglement and potentially pass your guard, exposing your hips to injury.
    • Forcing the leg entanglement when the opponent has already passed your initial guard: This can lead to awkward joint positions and potential knee or ankle injuries for both parties.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drill: Practice entering the 50/50 Guard from a closed guard break scenario, focusing on the leg entanglement and grip acquisition. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    2. Partner drill (no resistance): With a willing partner, practice achieving the 50/50 Guard from a neutral position, focusing on hip angle and grip security. (10 reps per side, 0% resistance)
    3. Assisted drill: Partner provides light resistance, allowing you to work the hip shift and leg control. Focus on maintaining the entanglement. (5 reps per side, 25% resistance)
    4. Controlled entry drill: Partner allows you to enter the 50/50 Guard from a specific position (e.g., opponent trying to pass your guard). Focus on maintaining the position. (5 reps per side, 50% resistance)
    5. Submission entry drill: From the 50/50 Guard, practice transitioning to a heel hook or ankle lock, with the partner offering mild defense. (5 reps per side, 75% resistance)
    6. Situational rolling: Start rolling with the specific goal of achieving and maintaining the 50/50 Guard for a set time or until a submission is achieved or defended. (3 rounds, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When an opponent attempts to pass your open guard by stepping over your legs, creating an opportunity to trap one leg.
    • During a scramble where you find yourself in a position where both legs are near one of the opponent's legs.
    • When you are defending a pass from your guard and can transition into the entanglement as they commit their weight.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass Defense: If the opponent attempts to stack you, drive your left foot into their hip with force, extending your leg and simultaneously pulling their trapped ankle towards your chest with your right arm. Maintain a tight hip angle.
    • Escape from Entanglement: If the opponent achieves the 50/50 Guard on you, immediately drive your hips forward and away from their trapped leg, using your free leg to push their hip. Grip fight to break their grips on your ankle and pants.
    • Guard Pass Against 50/50: If you are in the 50/50 Guard and the opponent is trying to attack your leg, grip fight their attacking arm and leg. Use your free leg to push their opposite hip and create space to transition to a more dominant position like side control or mount.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to enter the 50/50 Guard from closed guard?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are forcing the entanglement without proper hip alignment. When breaking closed guard, ensure your hips are angled towards the side you are attacking, and your entering leg hooks the opponent's thigh deeply before you commit to the full 50/50 structure. Avoid letting your knee go into an awkward valgus or varus stress position.

    Q: How can I use the 50/50 Guard effectively against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is paramount. Focus on using your hips to control their weight distribution, not just your arms. Drive your hips into their hip and create a strong frame with your free leg to prevent them from easily crushing you. Your primary goal is to isolate their leg and prevent them from posturing up or driving forward effectively.

    Q: Is the 50/50 Guard a safe position for white belts to practice leg attacks?

    The 50/50 Guard itself is a position of mutual entanglement, but the leg attacks from it carry significant risk for white belts. It's crucial to learn the position and control first, with minimal resistance, before attempting submissions. Focus on understanding the biomechanics of the entanglement and escapes before actively pursuing heel hooks or ankle locks to minimize injury.

50/50 Guard BJJ: A White Belt's Biomechanical Blueprint

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

The 50/50 Guard is entered from an open guard or half guard position, aiming to control both of the opponent's legs for submission or sweep opportunities.

White belts often struggle due to poor weight distribution and uncontrolled limb entanglement, leading to escapes or injury.

The core concept is creating a stable tripod with your legs and hips, preventing the opponent from stacking or easily passing.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a knee-shield or open guard, secure a Double-Unders grip on the opponent's right leg, gripping both the ankle and the pant cuff just above the knee.
    2. Extend your own right leg, placing your right foot on the opponent's left hip bone, creating a frame.
    3. Hook your left foot around the opponent's right ankle, ensuring your left shin is perpendicular to their shin bone.
    4. Simultaneously, bring your right leg over the opponent's right knee, trapping it between your right shin and your left thigh.
    5. Shift your hips to your left, creating a base with your left hip on the mat, while your right hip is elevated.
    6. Maintain a strong frame with your right foot on their hip and pull their trapped leg towards your chest to break their base.
    7. Crucially, keep your left knee pointed slightly towards the ceiling to prevent them from stacking you directly.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to 'pull' the opponent's leg without proper hip rotation: This can hyperextend your own knee or cause a ligament tear (ACL/MCL) as the force is applied directly to the joint.
    • Allowing the opponent to stack directly onto your head and shoulders: This can lead to neck injuries (whiplash) or shoulder dislocations if you are unable to support their weight.
    • Gripping the opponent's ankle with only your hand without leg control: This allows them to easily pull their leg out, and you risk a wrist sprain or dislocation if they apply pressure sideways.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg entanglement and hip positioning without a partner. Focus on smooth transitions between positions. (50 reps each side)
    2. Partner drilling (static): Opponent stands still. Focus on executing the entry and securing the 50/50 position. (20 reps each side)
    3. Partner drilling (light resistance): Opponent offers minimal resistance, allowing you to practice the hip shift and leg control. (15 reps each side)
    4. Controlled rolling (0% resistance): Practice entering 50/50 from various open guard positions with a partner who understands the drill and offers no active defense. (5 minutes)
    5. Controlled rolling (25% resistance): Partner attempts to post and resist the entanglement, but does not actively pass or submit. (5 minutes)
    6. Controlled rolling (50% resistance): Partner actively tries to pass or escape the 50/50 position, allowing you to practice maintaining control and transitioning to sweeps or submissions. (5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent is postured up and has their weight forward, making them susceptible to leg entanglements.
    • When transitioning from a failed sweep or guard pass attempt, and you find yourself in a position to isolate a leg.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your open guard by stepping over your legs, creating an opportunity to trap them.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Stack Pass Defense: If the opponent attempts to stack, maintain your left knee pointed upwards and use your right foot on their hip to push them away, creating space to reset.
    • Leg Pull Escape: If they try to pull their leg out, maintain a strong grip on both the ankle and pant cuff and use your hips to drive their leg back into the 50/50 position.
    • Sweeping to Mount/Side Control: Once the 50/50 is secured, use your hips and the trapped leg to generate a sweep, aiming to land in a dominant control position.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get into 50/50 Guard?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are twisting it in an unnatural direction without proper support. When you trap their leg, ensure your own shin is perpendicular to their shin and your hips are aligned to absorb the force. Avoid letting your knee buckle inwards or outwards.

    Q: How can I use 50/50 Guard against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on using your hips and frames to prevent them from generating crushing pressure. Your right foot on their hip is crucial for creating space and preventing a direct stack. Utilize their weight against them by hip-shaking and angling your body to initiate sweeps.

    Q: Is 50/50 Guard a good position to stay in for a long time?

    No, 50/50 Guard is generally considered a transitional position. While it offers strong submission and sweep opportunities, staying too long without advancing can allow the opponent to establish a strong base or execute counters. The goal is to use it to create an immediate advantage.

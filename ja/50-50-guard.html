The 50/50 guard is an open guard position where you control both of your opponent's legs with your own legs, placing your hips and theirs in a mirrored configuration.

White belts often struggle due to uncontrolled hip movement and incorrect weight distribution, leading to passes or submissions.

The key is to create a stable, perpendicular frame with your legs to control hip rotation and prevent your opponent from easily unhooking or passing.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a seated guard or transition, secure a double-leg grab: grip both of your opponent's ankles with your hands, palms facing each other.
    2. Position your right leg over your opponent's left thigh, hooking your foot behind their knee joint, creating a fulcrum.
    3. Simultaneously, position your left leg to block your opponent's right thigh from moving forward, your shin pressing against their outer thigh.
    4. Drive your hips into your opponent's hips, creating a slight angle where your hips are not directly facing theirs.
    5. Maintain constant pressure with your right shin against the back of their left knee, preventing them from straightening their leg.
    6. Ensure your left shin is actively pushing their right thigh away, denying them hip escape.
    7. Keep your core engaged and your back relatively flat, avoiding rounding your spine, to maintain structural integrity.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending your knee on the controlling leg (left leg in the description above) can cause a hyperextension injury to your own knee's posterior cruciate ligament (PCL).
    • The fix is to maintain a slight bend in your knee and use your hip to drive your shin into their thigh, not just your leg muscles.
    • Gripping the opponent's pants too loosely and allowing them to pull their legs out leads to a loss of control and potential knee or ankle strain if you react improperly.
    • The fix is to grip their ankles firmly and use your legs to create a strong, unyielding frame.
    • Attempting to 'pull' your opponent's legs towards you without proper hip connection and angling can strain your lower back and shoulders.
    • The fix is to use your hips to drive into them, creating the 50/50 structure, rather than just arm strength.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice establishing the 50/50 guard from a seated position without a partner, focusing on leg and hip placement. (10 reps)
    2. Static partner drill: With a compliant partner, practice securing the 50/50 guard from a closed guard or knee-on-belly position, focusing on grip and frame. (25% resistance, 5 reps each side)
    3. Controlled transition drill: Work with a partner to transition into the 50/50 guard from various common guard positions, with the partner offering minimal resistance to escapes. (50% resistance, 3 reps each side)
    4. Submission entry drill: Practice transitioning from the 50/50 guard into a specific submission (e.g., ankle lock), with the partner providing light resistance to the setup. (75% resistance, 2 reps each side)
    5. Guard retention drill: Your partner attempts to pass your 50/50 guard, and you focus solely on maintaining the position and preventing the pass. (90% resistance, 2 minutes)
    6. Live rolling: Implement the 50/50 guard during rolling, focusing on establishing and maintaining the position, and looking for submission opportunities. (100% resistance, 5 minutes)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    • WHEN TO ATTEMPT:
    • When your opponent is attempting to pass your open guard and their legs are within reach.
    • When you have successfully swept or transitioned to a position where you can isolate one or both of your opponent's legs.
    • As a proactive guard retention strategy when your opponent is pressuring forward and you want to disrupt their base.
    • PRIMARY COUNTERS:
    • Against an opponent trying to stand up: Immediately drive your hips into theirs, extending your right leg to block their hip and your left leg to keep their other leg away, creating a stable base.
    • Against an opponent trying to unhook your leg: Use your free hand to grip their hip or thigh, and use your core to pull them back into your frame, preventing them from creating space to escape.
    • Against an opponent trying to stack you: Create a strong frame with your legs and hips, ensuring your shins are actively pushing into their thighs and blocking their hip movement. Simultaneously, use your arms to push their shoulders away, creating distance.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to hold the 50/50 Guard?

    Knee pain in the 50/50 guard typically arises from hyperextending your knee or applying twisting pressure to it. This happens when you rely too much on your leg muscles to create the frame instead of using your hip and core to drive your shin into the opponent's thigh. Ensure your knee has a slight bend and your hips are actively engaged to create a stable, perpendicular structure.

    Q: How do I use the 50/50 Guard effectively against a much larger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage and precise angles are paramount in the 50/50 guard. Focus on driving your hips into their hips to create a strong base and use your legs to create a perpendicular frame, preventing them from using their weight to simply push through you. Secure firm ankle grips and use your shin pressure to control their knee and thigh, limiting their mobility.

    Q: The optimal time to transition to a submission from the 50/50 guard is when you have successfully stabilized the position and your opponent is unable to effectively escape or pass. Look for moments when they are trying to posture up, post their hands, or shift their weight in a way that exposes a limb, like their ankle or knee, for a submission attempt.

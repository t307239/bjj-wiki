50/50 Guard BJJ: A White Belt's Biomechanical Guide

BLUE★★★☆☆Intermediate
Blue帯テクニック
📖 完全ガイドを見る → 青帯ガイド

The 50/50 Guard is an open guard position where both competitors' legs are entangled in a mutual leg lock threat. It aims to isolate and attack one of the opponent's legs, or to create a stable base for sweeps.

White belts often struggle with 50/50 Guard because they lack the proprioception and control over their own limbs. They may overcommit to attacks or fail to maintain proper structure, leading to sweeps or submissions against them.

The key insight is establishing a superior hip angle and controlling the opponent's base by anchoring their hips, preventing them from standing or posturing effectively.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From a seated position, as the opponent passes your guard, sit up and secure a double-ankle grip on their legs, with your forearms wrapping around the outside of their shins.
    2. Immediately shift your hips to your left (if attacking the opponent's right leg), creating a 45-degree angle relative to their hips.
    3. Drive your right knee towards the opponent's left hip, creating a wedge and preventing them from posturing up.
    4. Hook your left foot behind the opponent's right knee, keeping your heel close to their glute for maximum control.
    5. Secure your right foot on the opponent's left hip, acting as a post and controlling their base width.
    6. With your left hand, grab your own right shin, creating a strong triangular frame around the opponent's leg.
    7. Maintain constant pressure with your right knee into their hip, forcing their weight onto their heels and off-balance.
    8. To initiate a sweep or submission, drive your hips forward and slightly upwards, while simultaneously pulling their trapped leg towards you.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Incorrectly extending your knee while in 50/50 Guard: This can cause hyperextension of the knee joint (ACL/MCL tear) if the opponent applies pressure or a counter-sweep. The correct movement is to keep a slight bend in your knee, acting as a controlled spring.
    • Applying a leg lock without proper control of the opponent's hips: This can lead to your opponent stepping over your head or bridging out, resulting in a knee-bar or ankle lock against you. Always ensure your hips are angled and their base is compromised before attacking.
    • Allowing the opponent to achieve a strong base by standing up: If the opponent stands and drives their weight down, it can put immense pressure on your entangled leg, risking a knee injury. Maintain hip connection and frame with your knee.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo drilling: Practice the leg entanglement and hip angle shift without a partner. Focus on smooth transitions and correct grip placement. (10 reps each side)
    2. Static drilling with a compliant partner: Partner lies on their back. You practice entering the 50/50 Guard, securing grips, and establishing the correct hip angle. (5 reps each side, 25% resistance)
    3. Controlled entry and sweep attempts: Partner offers minimal resistance to your guard entry. You attempt to execute a sweep from the 50/50 Guard. (10 reps each side, 50% resistance)
    4. Controlled submission attempts: Partner defends passively. You practice setting up and applying a basic leg lock (e.g., straight ankle lock) from the 50/50 Guard. (10 reps each side, 50% resistance)
    5. Positional sparring (starting from 50/50 Guard): Start with the 50/50 Guard established. Partner attempts to pass or escape. You focus on maintaining the position and attempting sweeps/submissions. (5 rounds, 75% resistance)
    6. Live rolling (starting from neutral): Aim to establish the 50/50 Guard from open guard or during a guard pass attempt by the opponent. (5 rounds, 90% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard and their legs are within reach.
    • When you are defending a guard pass and can transition to entangle their legs.
    • When you are in open guard and want to create a specific leg entanglement for attack or sweep.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Standing Escape: If the opponent has you in 50/50, they may try to attack your leg. Immediately drive your hips towards them, creating space, and attempt to stand up by posting your free foot and driving forward. This breaks the entanglement.
    • Hip Switch and Re-Guard: As the opponent enters 50/50, if you feel your leg is compromised, switch your hips aggressively in the opposite direction of their attack and use your free leg to re-establish a guard (e.g., butterfly guard). This requires quick hip movement.
    • Leg Drag Counter: If the opponent attempts to lock down your leg in 50/50, use your free leg to hook their hip and drag their leg across your body, transitioning to a leg drag position. This immediately disrupts their control and opens up new attack angles.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to do the 50/50 Guard?

    Your knee likely hurts due to a lack of proper knee flexion and hyperextension. When you're in the 50/50 Guard, your knee should maintain a slight bend, acting like a shock absorber. Overextending your knee or allowing the opponent to apply direct, uncontrolled pressure against a straight leg can cause ligament strain. Always keep a micro-bend and focus on controlling the opponent's hip and base instead of solely relying on leg pressure.

    Q: How do I do the 50/50 Guard against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, focus on controlling their base and hip angle rather than overpowering them. Use your forearms to create a strong frame around their shins, preventing them from posturing up. Drive your knee into their hip to off-balance them, and use your free leg to hook their far leg, further limiting their mobility. Your goal is to make them feel unstable and vulnerable, allowing you to initiate sweeps or attacks with precision, not brute force.

    Q: When is the best time for a white belt to try the 50/50 Guard?

    For a white belt, the best time to attempt the 50/50 Guard is when the opponent is actively trying to pass your guard and their legs are within your immediate reach. Specifically, when they are committing to a knee-cut or toreando pass, their legs are often exposed. Instead of letting them pass, you can use this moment to quickly sit up, entangle their legs, and establish the 50/50 position. Avoid attempting it from a completely static or defensive position where you have no momentum.

The 50/50 Guard is a leg entanglement position where both practitioners have a grip on each other's legs, resembling a "50/50" split of control.

White belts often struggle by applying excessive, uncontrolled force, leading to knee and ankle injuries due to poor structural alignment.

The key to the 50/50 Guard is establishing a superior hip angle and controlling the opponent's base to limit their mobility and create submission opportunities.

目次

    グリップ・生体力学

    1. From Closed Guard, break the opponent's posture by pulling their collar with your right hand and their sleeve (same side) with your left hand.
    2. Shift your hips to your left, creating a 45-degree angle relative to your opponent's hips.
    3. Swing your right leg over the opponent's left leg, aiming to place your right shin across their left thigh, just above the knee.
    4. Simultaneously, grip the opponent's left pants cuff with your left hand, and their left ankle with your right hand.
    5. Drive your left hip into the opponent's left hip, forcing them to shift their weight onto their right leg.
    6. Your right leg should be locked around the opponent's left leg, preventing them from easily stepping out.
    7. Your body should be angled, with your chest facing their left hip, creating a strong base and leverage point.
    8. Maintain a tight grip on the pants and ankle, ensuring no space for them to escape their leg.

    ⚠️ 白帯の注意点

    • Attempting to 'pull hard' on the opponent's leg without establishing a superior hip angle: This can hyperextend the opponent's knee or ankle, causing ligament tears (ACL, MCL, or ankle sprains) due to uncontrolled rotational force.
    • Allowing the opponent to establish a strong base by keeping your hips squared: This leaves your own knee vulnerable to being trapped and twisted, leading to meniscus tears or ACL damage.
    • Gripping the opponent's leg too low (e.g., calf instead of thigh/ankle): This provides insufficient control and leverage, allowing the opponent to easily pass or apply pressure that strains your knee or hip.

    ドリル段階

    1. Solo movement: Practice the hip shift and leg swing motion without a partner, focusing on smooth transitions. (50 reps)
    2. Static partner drill: Partner lies on their back, you enter 50/50 from Closed Guard. Partner offers no resistance. Focus on grip and hip angle. (5 sets of 30 seconds)
    3. Light resistance entry: Partner offers mild resistance to posture break and hip shift. Focus on maintaining grips and angle. (5 sets of 30 seconds)
    4. Controlled entry and position hold: Partner tries to posture up slightly or move their hips. You maintain the 50/50 position. (5 sets of 45 seconds)
    5. Entry with sweep attempt: Partner offers light resistance. You enter 50/50 and attempt a basic sweep (e.g., scissor sweep from 50/50). (5 sets of 3 reps per sweep)
    6. Live rolling: Apply the 50/50 Guard entry and attempt to maintain or transition to a submission. (2 x 3-minute rounds, 75% resistance)

    使うタイミング・カウンター

    WHEN TO ATTEMPT:

    • When the opponent has their legs too close together, creating an opportunity to entangle.
    • When the opponent attempts to pass your guard by stepping their legs inside yours.
    • Immediately after breaking the opponent's posture from Closed Guard, before they can fully re-establish it.

    PRIMARY COUNTERS:

    • Opponent Hip Escape: If the opponent hips away, immediately follow their movement with your hips to maintain the entanglement, or release and reset to Closed Guard.
    • Opponent Stacking: If the opponent stacks, widen your base by pushing their hips away with your free hand and simultaneously driving your hips forward to create space.
    • Opponent Leg Release: If the opponent pulls their leg out, use your free hand to grab their opposite leg or collar to prevent them from passing to side control.

    よくある質問・トラブル

    Q: Why does my knee hurt when I try to get into 50/50 Guard?

    Your knee likely hurts because you are not establishing the correct hip angle. When you enter 50/50, your hips should be at approximately a 45-degree angle to your opponent's hips. If your hips are squared, the rotational forces applied when entangling the leg can put excessive stress on your knee ligaments, causing pain.

    Q: How do I use 50/50 Guard against a much bigger opponent?

    Against a larger opponent, leverage is key. Focus on controlling their hip and ankle with precise grips. Instead of trying to 'pull' them, use your hips to create an angle and subtly shift their weight onto their supporting leg. This makes it easier to off-balance them and initiate sweeps or submissions, as their mass is less of an obstacle.

    Q: Should I always try to sweep or submit immediately from 50/50 Guard?

    Not necessarily. For white belts, the primary goal when entering 50/50 Guard is to establish control and prevent the opponent from passing. Once secure, you can then choose to work for a sweep or submission. Rushing into an attack without proper positional control can lead to losing the entanglement and giving up your guard, or worse, creating an injury risk.
